#dc-ladder
> **The one ladder every difficulty hangs on — an anti-drift adjudication reference.** Today the DM picks a number out of the air when the player tries something uncertain, and the same act ("climb the wall," "pick the lock," "lie to the guard") drifts from DC 12 one session to DC 18 the next. This pins each rung to a fixed constant **and** to concrete, Genesis-native example tasks so the DM finds the nearest rung and uses *that* DC instead of inventing one — the same challenge resolves the same way across a long campaign. This is a **reference, not a roll table** (no dice — the first column is named, so the compiler skips it; it is served to the DM via the digest). The six rungs and their numbers are the standard 5/10/15/20/25/30 difficulty constants; every example task is original Genesis content (authored 2026-06-28). Cross-references the per-tool DCs in `[[Tool Proficiency Uses#^tool-proficiency-uses]]` and the hazard scaffold in `[[Hazard Severity#^hazard-severity]]`.

**How to use it.** Picture the act. Ask *who could do this and how reliably?* — that names the rung. Then add the relevant modifier (proficiency, advantage from a clever approach, disadvantage from poor footing or pressure). Per the DM Charter `§6`, **keep the DC hidden by default** — call for the roll ("roll Athletics") and never recite the number unless asked. **Roll only when failure is both possible and interesting**; a trivial act on a calm day just succeeds. When two rungs both fit, let the *circumstances* break the tie — a still room is Easy, the same task in a gale is Hard.

| Rung | DC | Who clears it, and how often | Example tasks (Genesis-native) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Very Easy | 5 | Almost anyone, almost always — you only roll because the scene is tense or the stakes are sharp. | Shoulder open a swollen door · recall a rumor every child in town knows · haggle a market-stall down a copper · keep your feet on a wet plank bridge · spot the only lit window on a dark street. |
| Easy | 10 | A capable adult on a normal day; failure means you were rushed, rattled, or unlucky. | Climb a knotted rope · catch the gist of an overheard argument in your own tongue · calm a spooked but un-panicked horse · read whether a beggar is lying about being hungry · find dry tinder after a light rain. |
| Medium | 15 | The everyday bar for a trained character — the default for "this is a real test." | Scale a rough stone wall with handholds · pick a sound, ordinary lock · convince a wary guard you belong here · track a wounded deer over a half-day-old trail · stay upright on an icy ledge in a rising wind. |
| Hard | 20 | An expert performing near their best, or anyone doing something genuinely difficult under pressure. | Free-climb a sheer, rain-slick cliff · forge a noble's seal you've only glimpsed once · talk an executioner into staying his axe · recall a heresy struck from every official record · leap a chasm wider than you are tall and land running. |
| Very Hard | 25 | The peak of mortal skill — only a master, and only on a good day, with the wind right. | Scale a wet tower by the mortar-seams alone · cross a frayed rope over a gorge in a gale without a hand free · sway a fanatic off the cause they'd die for · pick a vault lock built to defeat exactly your craft · catch a falling companion by the wrist mid-fall. |
| Nearly Impossible | 30 | Legend territory — what bards sing about. Most characters cannot attempt it; success is a story by itself. | Climb a glassed, frictionless wall with bare hands · forge a document that fools the people who wrote the original · turn a sworn assassin against the master who raised them, in one conversation · recall a true name no living tongue still speaks · thread a sprint across collapsing stones a half-step ahead of the fall. |
