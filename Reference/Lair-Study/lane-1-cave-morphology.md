## What was looked at

Four origin families plus the drawings that describe them:

- **Karst** — Mammoth Cave sinkhole entrances (`L1-01`, `L1-02`), dripstone chambers
  (`L1-09` Reeds Cave, `L1-10` Shatter Cave), rimstone gour terraces (`L1-11`), breakdown
  (`L1-15` Giant's Coffin, `L1-16` Standing Rocks).
- **Lava tube** — collapsed skylight from outside and from beneath (`L1-05`, `L1-06`),
  bore interiors (`L1-07` Lava River Cave, `L1-08` Thurston).
- **Sea cave** — mouth read from inside (`L1-03`, `L1-04`), slot corridor (`L1-13` Piper's
  Hole), tidal floor with boulder litter (`L1-14`).
- **Ice** — glacier cave shell (`L1-12`).
- **Survey drawings** — GB Cave plan (`L1-17`), 1869 Nevada plan+section (`L1-18`), 1889
  Waitomo gallery plan (`L1-19`), USGS Devils Hole section (`L1-20`).

The survey drawings did most of the topological work. Photographs are good at *surface* and
*light*; only the surveys show what a cave actually is as a graph.

## Findings

**1. A cave is a descending chain, not a floor plan.** GB Cave (`L1-17`) carries spot
elevations from **791/788 at the entrance down to 396** at the far dig — the whole system is
one monotonic descent, and every named chamber sits on that descent. The 1869 Nevada
plan+section (`L1-18`) shows the same thing in elevation: chamber, drop, chamber, drop.
This is the single strongest support for the banked "rooms CHAIN" ruling — but it adds a
constraint the ruling does not yet carry: **the chain has a direction, and it is down.**

**2. Chambers are widenings on passage; passage is the default state.** In both GB Cave and
Waitomo (`L1-19`), the drawn line is a *ribbon* that occasionally swells. Chambers are named
("Main Chamber", "Great Chamber", "Bedding Chamber", "The Grand Cavern"); the connecting
ribbon usually is not, or gets a texture-name ("mud passage", "ooze passage", "white
passage", "rhumba alley", "the gorge", "devil's elbow", "the bridge", "the squeeze"). The
*organic portal* vocabulary Adam banked is exactly the vocabulary real cavers use.

**3. Ceiling height is the property a chamber is identified by.** Waitomo (`L1-19`) labels
almost every space by roof height — "ROOF 8 FT HIGH", "35 FT HIGH", "THE GRAND DOME 50 FT
HIGH", "THE LITTLE DOME 40 FT HIGH", "FLOOR 14 FT ABOVE STREAM". Within *one* system, roof
height swings 8 ft → 50 ft. Cave chambers are not distinguished by footprint; they are
distinguished by how far up the dark goes.

**4. The topology is a tree with a small number of honest loops.** GB Cave explicitly labels
a "loop" and an "oxbow"; the degus burrow plan in Lane 2 shows the same shape at animal
scale. Everything else terminates. The dead ends are *named as dead ends* —
"Disappointment Chamber", "Bertie's Pot", "dig". A cave graph is: one entrance, a spine,
two or three loops, and several proud dead ends.

**5. Breakdown is structure, not litter.** `L1-15` is a single house-sized block that
divides the passage; `L1-06` is a rubble cone under a skylight that *is the stair*;
`L1-20` (USGS section) labels a wedge of "BREAKDOWN" choking the middle of a vertical
shaft. Collapse debris does three jobs — it blocks, it ramps, and it becomes the room
divider. It is never even scatter. This corroborates the guard post's "rocks are relational
clusters" rule and gives it a cave-specific reading: **breakdown is a connector or a
blocker, and always has a causal parent above it.**

**6. The mouth is a silhouette event with essentially no gradient.** `L1-03` and `L1-04`
photograph the mouth from inside: the aperture is blown to white/sky, and everything within
the cave is *pure black*, including the ceiling directly overhead. There is no soft falloff
band. The "mouth daylight" the banked deck ruling assumes is real, but it is a hard-edged
aperture, not a gradient — a bright hole punched in black, with a short lit apron on the
floor and nothing else.

**7. Skylights make one lit island and no ambient.** `L1-05`/`L1-06` (Indian Tunnel):
daylight through a collapsed roof lands as a single lit patch on the rubble cone. Ten metres
either side is unreadable. If Genesis wants a "nature provides light" case, the honest
version is **one lit island with a hard boundary**, not a room-wide brightening.

**8. Bores keep a constant section; floors do not.** Lava tubes (`L1-07`, `L1-08`) have an
almost machined shell — a rounded triangle or ellipse held for the whole length, with a flow
ledge scribed at a consistent height on the wall — yet the *floor* is angular breakdown
blocks. The "smooth vs claw-cut vs melted" bore-finish parameter is a **shell** property.
The floor is always debris. Do not let the shell parameter reach the floor.

**9. Water is a level datum, not a pond.** `L3-01` (flooded adit, Lane 3) and `L1-20`
(Devils Hole section) both show it: standing water cuts an absolutely level line across an
utterly irregular section, and everything below that line is a different world. Flowing
water is a different noun again — `L1-11`'s rimstone gours are terraced *dams*, a stack of
shallow steps, and `L1-19` shows the Waitomo Stream threading through the whole lower series.
Water in a lair is either a **datum plane** (flooding, siphon, sump) or a **thread**
(channel, gour terrace). Never a decorative puddle.

**10. Karst and mine entrances are near-invisible from the surface.** `L1-01` and `L1-02`
are photographs *of* famous cave entrances and you can barely find them — they read as
shallow wooded depressions with a rock lip. `L3-06` (Lane 3, collapse cone) is the same
picture with a mine underneath. This is real support for **mishap entry**: the fiction of
falling into a lair is not a contrivance, it is the ordinary way these voids present.

**11. Ice is a counter-family: one shell, no furniture.** `L1-12` is a single sweeping
translucent vault with a stream, a moraine bank, and *no* dripstone, no breakdown clusters,
no ledges. If ice is on the origin roll, it should suppress most of the shell-set vocabulary
rather than re-skin it.

**12. Small chambers are legitimate and common.** `L1-09` (Reeds Cave "Easter Chamber") is
described by its own photographer as "quite small, a matter of metres." The banked one-off
room case is not a degenerate minimum; it is the ordinary size of a decorated cave chamber.

## Where this pressures the banked rulings

- **"Rooms chain"** — held, and strengthened: add *direction* (descending) and *chamber =
  widening on a passage*, not "rooms connected by doors."
- **"The causal light law"** — held, and sharpened: the evidence says light events are
  hard-edged islands (mouth apron, skylight patch, lamp pool), not gradients. The law's
  presentation card should be about **island boundaries**, not falloff curves.
- **"The ledge deck"** — the geology that would supply a ledge is present (flowstone
  terraces `L1-11`, flow ledges `L1-08`, breakdown cones `L1-06`), but *none* of the twenty
  photographs shows a continuous walkable rim around a chamber. The rim in nature is
  fragmentary. See the founder packet.
- **"Scale axis: one-off chamber ↔ pocket ↔ full dungeon"** — held. GB Cave is a full
  dungeon-length identity; Reeds Cave's Easter Chamber is the one-off room. Both are real.
