*p. 279 MM 2024*

Skeletons are the most common form of undead in the world — simple, cheap to animate, and tireless. They are not truly alive in any meaningful sense. They have no hunger, no fear, no ambition. They simply obey, executing the last command given to them with robotic literalness until they are destroyed or their animating magic fades. A skeleton ordered to "guard this hall" will do exactly that until it crumbles to dust — even if the person who gave the order died a century ago.

This makes them excellent cannon fodder but unreliable sentinels. They cannot adapt, report back, or make judgment calls. A clever party can exploit this: skeletons won't pursue beyond their assigned area, won't raise an alarm, and won't respond to a threat they weren't specifically ordered to engage. The tragedy of a skeleton is that most were once ordinary people — soldiers, miners, plague victims — stripped of everything except their bones and a crude magical compulsion.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Skeletons in an area signal deliberate undead cultivation, not spontaneous haunting.

- **Animated, Not Wild:** Unlike ghouls, skeletons don't arise from grief or trauma. Someone *made* them. Finding a cache of skeletons implies a necromancer's workshop, a cult's sanctum, or an old ritual site that hasn't been dismantled.
- **The Uniform Dead:** Skeletons raised from a single source often share physical traits — all the same height, all missing the same teeth, all wearing fragments of the same uniform. A DC 12 History or Investigation check can date the original bodies and identify where the victims came from.
- **Lingering Orders:** A skeleton still executing an absurd task (guarding an empty vault, patrolling a collapsed corridor) is a clue that whoever animated it never came back to issue new orders. Something happened to the necromancer.

### d10 Skeleton Encounters (Tone Variance Table)

Skeletons work in nearly any campaign register — they can be chilling, darkly comic, or a statement about mortality depending on how they're played.

| d10 | Encounter Premise | Campaign Tone / Mechanics |
|:----|:------------------|:--------------------------|
| **1** | **The Last Orders** | **Melancholy:** A skeleton is found still diligently patrolling a corridor, torch-bracket to torch-bracket. A DC 12 History check reveals the uniform matches a garrison that was wiped out 80 years ago. It simply never received the order to stop. |
| **2** | **The Bone Orchestra** | **Humorous / Surreal:** A necromancer's apprentice, practicing their craft, accidentally enchanted the wrong corpses. The skeletons aren't aggressive — they're mechanically playing instruments, badly, in an endless loop. |
| **3** | **The Ambush Pattern** | **Tactical:** Three skeleton archers are positioned in the rafters, ordered to fire at anyone who enters the room. The melee skeletons below are bait. |
| **4** | **The Old Soldier's Name** | **Character Moment:** One skeleton is wearing a signet ring that a PC's family would recognize. The PC must decide whether to destroy it or attempt to retrieve the ring. |
| **5** | **The Collapsing Formation** | **Action:** A group of skeleton warriors marches in a tight formation using shields to create a wall. They advance slowly and relentlessly — the PCs must break the formation or give ground. |
| **6** | **The Bone Trap** | **Puzzle / Suspense:** The skeletons have been arranged to trigger a rockfall or portcullis if disturbed. Perception DC 14 to notice the tripwires connected to their feet before engaging. |
| **7** | **The Necromancer's Diary** | **Investigation:** The room contains a still-active skeleton at a writing desk, its bony fingers dragging a dry quill across a blank page in an endless loop. The necromancer ordered it to "write everything down" — and it has been. Thousands of pages of nothing. |
| **8** | **The Fresh Batch** | **Horror:** These skeletons are newly made — the flesh hasn't fully decomposed yet. The PCs can recognize faces. Someone in this dungeon has been raising locals. |
| **9** | **The Siege Engine** | **High Stakes:** A giant skeleton — raised from the bones of an ogre or troll — is being used as a battering ram by smaller skeletons, smashing through a reinforced door the party needs intact. |
| **10** | **The Bone Storm** | **Dramatic:** A necromancer is destroyed mid-combat, causing every skeleton in the room to shatter simultaneously — spraying bone fragments as a final hazard (DC 13 Dex save, 2d6 piercing, half on success). |

### d10 The Ossuary Scene (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Maker's Mark** | Every skeleton in the room bears a small arcane rune carved into the inside of their left femur. A DC 13 Arcana check identifies it as a binding sigil from a specific necromantic school — giving a name or organization. |
| **2** | **The Mismatched Bones** | One "skeleton" is actually assembled from the bones of four different humanoids. It is noticeably asymmetrical — one arm longer, one leg from a larger creature. The creator was sloppy or running low on materials. |
| **3** | **The Clothes Pile** | A corner of the room holds a careful pile of clothing, shoes, and personal effects — neatly folded. Whoever made these skeletons stripped the bodies before animating them, suggesting familiarity with the process. |
| **4** | **The Kill Mark** | Each skeleton has a small notch cut into one rib. Thieves' cant or a DC 14 Investigation check reveals it's a tally system — this necromancer was keeping count. |
| **5** | **The Animal Skeleton** | Mixed in with the humanoid skeletons is an animated dog or wolf skeleton. Whoever animated these creatures had an emotional attachment to a pet, or used animals as practice. |
| **6** | **The Old Coin** | A coin pouch still hangs from one skeleton's belt — untouched. The coins are dated 40 years ago. These skeletons predate the current dungeon's occupants, which means there are two different necromancers' work here. |
| **7** | **The Pointing Hand** | One skeleton stands facing a specific wall, both arms raised, finger bones pointing directly at a seam in the stonework. A secret door. The skeleton was ordered to point at it and never stopped. |
| **8** | **The Dueling Pair** | Two skeleton warriors are found standing perfectly still, locked in mid-combat, weapons crossed. They were ordered to "practice fighting" and have frozen in a stalemate. Neither will break stance unless attacked. |
| **9** | **The Missing Skull** | Several skeletons are missing their skulls entirely. A DC 14 Arcana check reveals that decapitated skulls can be enchanted as communication relays — someone has been harvesting them for a ritual. |
| **10** | **The Name Carved in Bone** | On close inspection of one skeleton, names are scratched into the inside of its ribcage — dozens of them. The creature carved these names during whatever torture created it. Each name is a potential NPC, victim, or quest thread. |

### Flaming Skeleton

_Medium Undead, Lawful Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 15
- **Initiative:** +2 (12)
- **Hit Points:** 65 (10d8 + 20)
- **Speed:** 30 ft.
- **STR:** 10 (+0) | **DEX:** 14 (+2) | **CON:** 15 (+2) | **INT:** 6 (-2) | **WIS:** 8 (-1) | **CHA:** 5 (-3)
- **Vulnerabilities:** Bludgeoning
- **Immunities:** Fire, Poison; Exhaustion, Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft.; Passive Perception 9
- **Languages:** Understands Common plus one other language but can't speak
- **Challenge:** 3 (700 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Death Burst.** When the skeleton dies, it explodes. Dexterity Saving Throw: DC 12, each creature in a 10-foot Emanation originating from the skeleton. Failure: 14 (4d6) Fire damage. Success: Half damage.
- **Illumination.** The skeleton sheds Bright Light in a 15-foot radius and Dim Light for an additional 15 feet.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack.** The skeleton makes two attacks, using Flame Scepter or Hurl Flame in any combination.
- **Flame Scepter.** Melee Attack Roll: +4, reach 5 ft. Hit: 5 (1d6 + 2) Bludgeoning damage plus 3 (1d6) Fire damage.
- **Hurl Flame.** Ranged Attack Roll: +4, range 60 ft. Hit: 7 (1d10 + 2) Fire damage.
