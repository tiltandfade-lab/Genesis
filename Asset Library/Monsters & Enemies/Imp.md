*p. 163 MM 2024 (Imp); p. 241 MM 2024 (Quasit)*

Imps and quasits are the lowest-rung fiends — small, treacherous, and contractually bound to serve spellcasters who summon or bind them. The distinction is infernal versus abyssal: imps serve devils and the ordered hierarchy of the Nine Hells, while quasits serve demons and the screaming chaos of the Abyss. They are otherwise similar in role — familiars, spies, messengers, and reluctant assassins for masters who have traded some measure of their soul for a magical servant.

The crucial thing about imps and quasits in a campaign: they are *not* loyal. They are bound. The distinction matters enormously. An imp will serve its master competently, efficiently, and without visible complaint — because the binding compels it. The moment the binding weakens, the moment its master is distracted, the moment an imp believes it can escape without consequences, it will act entirely in its own interests. This might mean betraying its master to the party. It might mean stealing and running. It might mean alerting the party to a trap — if the alternative is being destroyed alongside its master.

Quasits are more chaotic and less controlled than imps. A quasit familiar is a liability as much as an asset — it will speak at wrong moments, provoke enemies, and pursue its own amusement during combat if it thinks it can escape the consequences. Warlocks and dark cultists often prefer the relative reliability of an imp.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

An imp or quasit in a dungeon is a sign of deliberate fiendish compact.

- **The Familiar's Absence:** A spellcaster who is killed without their familiar appearing means the familiar has fled (and may be reporting the party's capabilities to a higher power). A dead master whose familiar is still nearby means the binding is unusually strong — or the familiar was abandoned.
- **The Claw Scratch Pattern:** Imp claws leave distinctive three-toed marks at a specific height — about 1.5 feet off the ground, when the imp is skulking along walls. Quasit marks are similar but slightly smaller and more erratic. DC 13 Arcana to recognize the pattern as fiendish.
- **The Whisper Network:** NPCs near a dungeon might mention hearing whispered voices or laughter at night — something watching from the dark. This is usually an imp or quasit performing surveillance for its master.

### 1. Imp

A small devil with venom, limited shapeshifting, and enough infernal cunning to serve as an effective spy and familiar.

_Tiny Fiend (Devil), Lawful Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 13
- **Hit Points:** 10 (3d4 + 3)
- **Speed:** 20 ft., Fly 40 ft.
- **STR:** 6 (−2) | **DEX:** 17 (+3) | **CON:** 13 (+1) | **INT:** 11 (+0) | **WIS:** 12 (+1) | **CHA:** 14 (+2)
- **Skills:** Deception +4, Insight +3, Persuasion +4, Stealth +5
- **Damage Resistances:** Cold; Bludgeoning, Piercing, and Slashing from nonmagical attacks that aren't silvered
- **Damage Immunities:** Fire, Poison
- **Condition Immunities:** Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 120 ft., passive Perception 11
- **Languages:** Infernal, Common
- **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Devil's Sight:** Magical darkness doesn't impede the imp's Darkvision.
- **Magic Resistance:** The imp has Advantage on saving throws against spells and other magical effects.

**Actions**

- **Sting (Bite in Beast Form):** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 5 (1d4 + 3) piercing damage, and the target must make a DC 11 Constitution saving throw, taking 10 (3d6) poison damage on a failed save, or half as much on a successful one.
- **Invisibility:** The imp magically turns invisible until it attacks, or until its concentration ends. Any equipment the imp wears or carries is invisible with it.
- **Shapechanger:** The imp can use its action to polymorph into a beast form that resembles a rat (speed 20 ft.), a raven (20 ft., fly 60 ft.), or a spider (20 ft., climb 20 ft.), or back into its true form. Its statistics are the same in each form, except for the speed changes noted. Any equipment it is wearing or carrying isn't transformed.

### 2. Quasit

A small demon of pure chaos — useful as a familiar if the master can tolerate its erratic behavior, dangerous if it ever escapes control.

_Tiny Fiend (Demon), Chaotic Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 13
- **Hit Points:** 7 (3d4)
- **Speed:** 40 ft.
- **STR:** 5 (−3) | **DEX:** 17 (+3) | **CON:** 10 (+0) | **INT:** 7 (−2) | **WIS:** 10 (+0) | **CHA:** 10 (+0)
- **Skills:** Stealth +5
- **Damage Resistances:** Cold, Fire, Lightning; Bludgeoning, Piercing, and Slashing from nonmagical attacks
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison
- **Condition Immunities:** Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 120 ft., passive Perception 10
- **Languages:** Abyssal, Common
- **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Magic Resistance:** The quasit has Advantage on saving throws against spells and other magical effects.

**Actions**

- **Claws (Bite in Beast Form):** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 5 (1d4 + 3) piercing damage, and the target must make a DC 10 Constitution saving throw or take 5 (2d4) poison damage and be Poisoned for 1 minute. The target can repeat the saving throw at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.
- **Scare (1/Day):** One creature the quasit can see within 20 ft. must succeed on a DC 10 Wisdom saving throw or be **Frightened** until the end of the creature's next turn.
- **Invisibility:** The quasit magically turns invisible until it attacks, or until its concentration ends.
- **Shapechanger:** The quasit can use its action to polymorph into a beast form resembling a bat (speed 10 ft., fly 40 ft.), a centipede (40 ft., climb 40 ft.), or a toad (40 ft., swim 40 ft.), or back into its true form. Statistics are the same in each form.

### d10 Imp and Quasit Encounters (Tone Variance Table)

| d10 | Encounter Premise | Campaign Tone / Mechanics |
|:----|:------------------|:--------------------------|
| **1** | **The Master's Eyes** | **Surveillance:** The imp is invisible, following the party at a safe distance, reporting their actions to its master through telepathic link. The party can detect it with *detect magic* or by noticing it keeps triggering small sounds or Perception checks DC 16. |
| **2** | **The Betrayal Offer** | **Dark Deal:** The imp approaches the party openly, having decided its master is losing. It offers information — the dungeon layout, its master's weaknesses, the location of the MacGuffin — in exchange for its freedom. It will honor the deal exactly as stated. Not a word more. |
| **3** | **The Familiar Defender** | **Encounter Layer:** A cult spellcaster's quasit familiar uses Scare on the party's most Wisdom-vulnerable member at the start of combat, then turns invisible and keeps using Invisibility to avoid retaliation while the caster focuses on the real fight. |
| **4** | **The Venomous Nuisance** | **Attrition:** An imp has been harassing the party for the last hour — Invisible, stinging one person, retreating, reappearing somewhere else. Nobody can reliably hit it. It's not trying to kill anyone. It's playing with them. |
| **5** | **The Escaped Familiar** | **Investigation Trigger:** The party finds a dead spellcaster, binding circle broken. The familiar escaped when its master died. It's been free for three days. A quasit free for three days has caused chaos in this dungeon — other creatures are displaced, objects are moved, a trap was set off "for fun." |
| **6** | **The Master's Secrets** | **Information Cache:** An imp that has spent years with a master knows everything. Absolutely everything. If captured (DC 13 Athletics to grab a visible imp; it can't go invisible while grappled), it can be interrogated. It will lie badly, then tell the truth under continued pressure, then try to make a deal, then try to threaten, then ultimately provide exactly what the party needs in exchange for its freedom. |
| **7** | **The Shapechanger Confusion** | **Social Trick:** The imp has been posing as a rat for three days, following the party. It has been listening. It knows their plans, their weaknesses, and one significant personal secret per party member. When it reveals itself, it leads with the secret to establish dominance in the negotiation. |
| **8** | **The Scare Chain** | **Action Economy Disaster:** The quasit uses Scare on the wizard. The wizard fails and is Frightened. The Frightened wizard breaks concentration on the buff spell maintaining the paladin's protection. The quasit turns invisible and lets the combat take its course. |
| **9** | **The Tormentor** | **Cult Scene:** The cultist interrogator is using a quasit as an "assistant" — the quasit's Scare ability breaks prisoners psychologically while the cultist asks questions. The quasit enjoys this work. The cultist barely controls it. |
| **10** | **The Free Agent** | **Independent Threat:** An imp or quasit that has been free of its binding for months has built a small domain for itself in the dungeon — convincing goblin servants it is a "devil lord" through illusions and Scare. It wants tribute. It is genuinely trying to establish a tiny infernal fiefdom in this dungeon. It will fight if its position is threatened. |

### d10 The Fiend's Mark (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Binding Circle** | A careful summoning and binding circle is scratched into the floor — the kind used to call and contain a quasit or imp. It's been used recently (the chalk is fresh). The binding is gone, which means either the creature was released or the master is still present and has the creature under direct control. |
| **2** | **The Three-Toed Prints** | Tiny three-toed claw marks on a window ledge or high shelf — at an angle that only makes sense for a creature that was roosting there, watching the room below. Something small has been observing this location regularly. |
| **3** | **The Cultist's Complaint** | A journal entry: "The familiar is impossible. It told Brother Veleth that I called him a fool — which I did, but not in front of it. I don't know how it heard. I don't know how it told him. I don't know if it's working for someone else." |
| **4** | **The Imp's Correspondence** | A tiny, neatly folded note in Infernal — a report sent by an imp to an infernal contact. A DC 14 Arcana or Language check translates it: a field report describing the dungeon's occupants, their numbers, their resources, and, critically, a recommendation about whether the infernal patron should "maintain the investment." |
| **5** | **The Frightened Prisoner** | A prisoner in the dungeon is more frightened than the circumstances seem to warrant — babbling, flinching, convinced that something invisible is still in the room with them. The quasit used Scare repeatedly over several hours. The psychological damage persists even without the creature being present. |
| **6** | **The Master's Demise** | A dead spellcaster with no visible cause of death. Their own familiar killed them — the binding must have been improperly maintained. Whether the familiar is still in this room, long fled, or now acting as a free agent in the dungeon is uncertain. |
| **7** | **The Invisible Witness** | An NPC insists they heard two voices in the room — one the cultist's, one a whispering, hissing voice that "seemed to come from the air." The cultist was receiving instructions from their imp familiar. The instructions came from someone else. |
| **8** | **The Contract Remnant** | A fragment of parchment — partially burned — bears the beginning of a fiendish pact. The party can see one party's obligations clearly. The signatory name on the cultist side is legible. A DC 15 Arcana check confirms the contract grants a named imp servitude in exchange for something the cultist wanted — power, knowledge, or protection. |
| **9** | **The Tiny Footprints** | In dust on a high shelf, tiny footprints — bipedal, clawed, the size of a large cat. They lead to a crack in the wall, too small for any humanoid. The imp has a hiding spot inside the stonework. If it's still in there, it's listening to this conversation. |
| **10** | **The Turned Coat** | A living informant in the dungeon — a cultist, a guard, a prisoner — has been giving information to an imp rather than the other way around. The imp promised them something in exchange. The imp has no authority to deliver what it promised, and it never intended to. |
