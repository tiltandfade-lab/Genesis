## Current Read

NPC Hook is structurally strong now. It has the situation shape:

`Band | Hook | Pressure/Clock | If Ignored`

The table should stay broad enough to include grounded pressure, creepy social trouble, absurd gag rows, and high-band realm-warping weirdness. The goal is not to sand off the strange parts. The goal is to keep each row playable and anchored on the rolled NPC.

Good NPC Hook rows usually answer:

- Why this NPC?
- Why now?
- What visible thing is happening?
- What player handle exists?
- What gets worse if ignored?
- What state bucket remembers the consequence?

## Row 172 - HOA Invasion, Keep And Escalate Weird/Fun

Current row:

| d200 | Band | Hook | Pressure/Clock | If Ignored |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 172 | Volatile | A lawn appeared in front of the NPC's cottage - perfect, striped, chemically green. Nobody planted it. A white fence followed. (Guarantees a leaky Suburb breach nearby - DM places it and logs codex/map.) | The lawn spreads yard by yard. Standards acquire fines, fines acquire enforcement (the committee smiles harder) - and the market square's ancient chaos of stalls wakes one morning zoned, painted taupe, and closed on alternating days. |

Ruling: keep. HOA invasion is an actual nightmare with fun escalation potential. Strengthen the NPC's relationship to the committee and make the player handles more concrete.

Escalation ideas:

- Notices become retroactive: violations dated before the lawn existed.
- Fines are paid in color, noise, pets, holidays, memories of disorder, or square footage.
- The committee cannot enter uninvited, but it can define "common areas."
- The newspaper describes tomorrow's infractions before they happen.
- Approved colors spread as a physical rule; noncompliant paint peels itself.
- The NPC is elected chair by mail they never opened.
- The committee smiles harder when contradicted, then sends casseroles, then liens, then enforcement.
- The first monster is not violent: it is a clipboard-bearing inspector who can make property lines true.
- Market chaos gets "regularized": stalls align, bargaining becomes a violation, music requires a permit, alleys become cul-de-sacs.
- A player can weaponize the HOA against worse forces by filing complaints, but every successful complaint strengthens jurisdiction.

## Place Generation Gaps Exposed By Row 154

Row 154 shows that Genesis may need more place-generation lanes, especially for places that are not ordinary static locations.

Useful future place table concepts:

- `Impossible Nearby Place` - locations that are adjacent but impossible, forgotten, folded, seasonal, or conditionally reachable.
- `Road Sign / Crossroads Omen` - signposts, milestones, boundary stones, ferries, bridges, toll gates, and directional weirdness.
- `Hidden Settlement` - hamlets, markets, camps, monasteries, inns, and districts missing from maps or memory.
- `Place Drift` expansion - places whose borders, routes, names, inhabitants, or legal status are changing over time.
- `Distant Word` expansion - rumors and reports of places that should not exist yet are affecting trade, travel, or politics.
- `Local Map Trouble` - maps, surveys, deeds, charters, and directions that disagree with geography.
- `Threshold Place` - places reached through doors, wells, cellars, crossroads, alleys, and other repeatable thresholds.

Possible family shape for impossible places:

`Band | Place | What Is Happening Now | Local Pressure | Player Handle | If Ignored`

This can reuse the existing `place` family rather than inventing a new contract immediately.

## Keep The Gags Doctrine

Absurd rows are allowed and useful. The table has 200 entries, and memorable tonal breaks help prevent the engine from becoming one long grim municipal ledger.

The rule for gag rows:

- The premise may be silly.
- The pressure should be playable.
- The ignored consequence should alter the social or physical situation.
- The NPC should remain awkwardly responsible, implicated, useful, or endangered.

Row 138 is the model problem: the sneeze can stay, but the fallout should create a playable shortage, released entity, blame economy, or social complication rather than only a visual oddity.
