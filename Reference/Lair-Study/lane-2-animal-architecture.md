## What was looked at

- **Entrances in the field** — badger sett mouth close-up (`L2-04`), sett on a stream bank

- **Water-gated architecture** — beaver lodge in its pond (`L2-07`).
- **Counter-example** — sectioned termite mound (`L2-08`).

## The typology sheet

The gopher section (`L2-02`) is the single most useful drawing in the study, because it is
labelled with function. Read literally it gives a five-part grammar that recurs across every
other burrow image:

| Part | Gopher label | What it is | Genesis reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| Working horizon | "feeding tunnel 6 in. to 8 in. below surface" | shallow, disposable, extends toward resources | the *approach*, not the lair — abandoned and re-dug constantly |
| Living horizon | "living tunnel 2 ft. to 4 ft. below surface" | the deep trunk run | the chained spine |
| Nest | "Nest" | one blind pocket off the trunk | the prize floor / occupant's own room |
| Larders | two "store house" pockets | blind pockets, plural, off the trunk | loot placement is *off* the spine, never on it |
| Plugged shaft | the black stippled vertical column at right | an emergency exit, kept filled | **BOLT_HOLE** — present, closed, openable |

Every functional room in that drawing is a **blind side-pocket**. Nothing is a corridor
room. The trunk is for movement; the pockets are for content.

## Findings

**1. Two depth horizons, not one.** Gopher (`L2-02`) draws them explicitly; degus (`L2-03`)
measures them (16–56 cm range on a single system); badger setts photograph as several mouths
at *different heights on one bank face* (`L2-05`). A warren is not a single-level maze — it
is a shallow working layer plus a deep living layer, connected by short climbs. This is a
concrete, buildable alternative to "chained rooms on one floor."

**2. Multiple entrances are the norm; one entrance is the exception.** `L2-03` shows one
system with three mouths; `L2-05` shows a bank "riddled with large holes"; the field
descriptions of setts routinely count dozens. Contrast Lane 1: a *cave* usually has one
entrance and a long tree behind it. **Origin predicts entrance count.** Dug-by-occupant →
many mouths. Found/karst → one mouth. That is a clean, rollable rule the current nature roll
does not yet express.

**3. The bolt-hole is real, and it is normally plugged.** The gopher drawing's vertical
shaft is drawn *filled* with spoil. Bolt-holes exist as latent geometry that the occupant
opens under pressure. For W4/secrets this is better than a hidden door: it is a **known
feature in a closed state**, which is a different and more interesting discovery than "there
was a secret here."

**4. The mouth is wider than it is tall, and it is dressed.** `L2-04`: a flattened dome
mouth with dragged leaf litter carpeting the sill and the throat, going instantly black about
half a metre in. The occupant's adaptation vocabulary Adam banked (nest, bone litter) starts
*outside* the threshold — the dressing is on the sill, not only in the nest.

**5. Spoil is the dug-origin tell.** `L2-06`: a fan of excavated earth below the mouth.
`L3-02`/`L3-06` (Lane 3) show the same fan at mine adits and run-ins. If Genesis rolls a dug
origin, the outside must carry a **spoil fan** or the origin does not read. That is one
cheap prop-class, not a new family.

**6. The underwater entrance is a real posture with a real cost.** `L2-07`: the beaver lodge
sits in open water with no visible door at all; the entrances are submerged, and the animal
has also dug an approach canal (visible as a dark channel through the grass mat). The lodge
is safe not because it is strong but because the *route* is gated. This is the strongest
argument in the study for the flooded-siphon posture: it is not a hazard dressing, it is an
**access class** — the interior is ordinary, the *approach* is the problem.

**7. Loops exist at animal scale too.** The degus plan (`L2-03`) contains an unambiguous
loop (the 33/36/38 triangle) alongside blind pockets. So the "loops beat dead ends" instinct
in the ledge ruling is not a game-design concession — it is what burrows actually do. But
loops are *rare and short*; most of the graph is still blind pockets.

**8. Chamber count is small.** Across `L2-02` and `L2-03`, functional chambers number **two
to four** per system, on a trunk of a few metres. Not a dozen. If Genesis's warren occupancy
generates chamber counts, the animal evidence argues for a low band with specialised roles,
not a large band with repeated rooms.

**9. Terminal chambers are differentiated by contents, not by shape.** In `L2-03` the
chambers are drawn identically and distinguished only by hatching — stippled = food store,
cross-hatched = nest material. The room shape carries nothing; the *litter* carries
everything. This is a direct argument that the lair's room identity should ride on
paint-first litter/scatter, exactly as the banked material demand already assumes.

**10. The insect colony is the anti-model.** `L2-08` is the counter-example on purpose: a
sectioned termite mound is a **porous solid** — thousands of millimetre voids in a dense
matrix, with no room a creature could stand in. If "warren/colony" is on the inhabitation
roll, it must be built at *vertebrate* scale (few big voids on a trunk) or it produces an
unplayable sponge. The colony read has to come from **population and litter**, never from
topology.

**11. The wasp-nest section gives the "one big void" case.** `L2-01`: a single spherical
void excavated in soil, one narrow throat from the surface, contents stacked as horizontal
tiers with pillars between, a distinct envelope wall, and a stone-strewn floor beneath. That
is a legible single-chamber lair with vertical layering — a good archetype for the one-off
room where the *interest is stacked upward* rather than spread horizontally.

## Gaps (declared)

- **Bear den**: Commons has essentially no CC-licensed interior imagery of a wild bear den;
  the term is dominated by place-names and a band. The apex-den case is therefore argued in
  this study from cave morphology (Lane 1) and from the BG3 owlbear layout (Lane 5), not from
  first-hand denning photographs. Flagging rather than papering over.
- **Raptor eyrie**: same — usable Commons results were 19th-century book plates of little
  morphological value, and modern platform-nest photographs are artificial structures. The
  eyrie posture in the nature roll is currently **unevidenced by this study**.
