| 11 | Grounded | The neighbor has been feeding the NPC's hound behind the fence for months — it's fat, glossy, and last week it followed the neighbor home and wouldn't come back. | 12 | Grounded | A crate of "farm tools" the NPC was paid to warehouse was seized on the road — and the manifest bears the NPC's mark beside a name they can't afford to know. | The seizing officer is either honest (bad: an investigation) or crooked (worse: an auction) — and the NPC must learn which before answering any summons. | The NPC gets named. | 13 | Grounded | The NPC's daughter came home with a carved wooden toy nobody gave her — the third gift this week, each left closer to the house. | The gifts are a countdown; the NPC recognizes the carving style and won't say from where. | A fourth gift appears inside the house. | 14 | Grounded | The NPC's kiln was sabotaged with real skill — nothing broken outright, everything set to fail slowly under load. | 15 | Grounded | The NPC is boarding windows in broad daylight and selling stock at a loss — they think tomorrow's grain announcement starts a riot, and their shop sits between granary and square. | The announcement comes at morning bell. | 16 | Grounded | The neighbor painted their fence a shrieking festival orange, visible from every window the NPC owns. Ward complaints went nowhere — the painter's brother sits on the ward committee. | The NPC has already bought lamp-black and a brush. | 17 | Grounded | The wagon the NPC's whole season rides on is nine days late. The pass reported clear, other traffic got through, no wreck was found. | The buyer holds the contract price only to week's end — and a rival has mysteriously just come into identical stock. | The buyer buys from the rival — the NPC's own goods, everyone quietly understands — and the carter's family starts asking the NPC where their father is. |
| 18 | Grounded | The saddler's wife and the ferryman have gotten careless. Half the market pretends not to know. The silence is being purchased, and accepting even one more makes it a sale. | The saddler learns last and learns publicly — and the NPC's long silence reads as betrayal. | 19 | Grounded | The quiet loan that got the NPC through last winter is called in full, years early — legal, per a clause they never read, invoked the same week the lender took on a new silent partner. | Fourteen days to produce principal already spent — and the new partner has been buying up debts all down the street. | The NPC signs over the deed "temporarily." Two more shops follow within the month. Somebody is assembling the street. |
| 20 | Grounded | The NPC found the second ledger. Their partner of twelve years has skimmed enough to buy the building across the way — the one that just sold to "a foreign buyer." | The partner boards the morning coach for a "buying trip" in three days, carrying the season's takings. | The coach leaves with the takings, the partner — and, the NPC discovers, the deed to their own premises. |
| 21 | Grounded | The NPC needs a strongbox moved across town without the watch, the guild, or their own family knowing. They're offering payment in favors — which says more than coin would. | Tonight, under festival noise, is the only window; after that, the person they're hiding it from is back in town. | The NPC moves it alone, is seen by exactly the wrong person, and ends up owing a favor far worse than the one they offered. |
| 22 | Grounded | An auditor is in the warehouse right now, and the NPC is stalling with wine — because bay nine holds goods the NPC agreed not to ask about, for a fee. | The auditor works alphabetically. Bay nine is two days out, and the fee-payer has made clear that "discovery" has consequences for the landlord. | Bay nine opens. The NPC learns what they were storing the same moment the auditor does — and "I never looked" convinces no magistrate, nor the fee-payer, who tidies loose ends. |
| 23 | Grounded | The NPC's reputation stands on the winter they killed the grey wolf — the story is bought drinks, the good table, their children's standing. The man who actually killed it just moved back to town, and drinks quietly at the end of the bar. | He hasn't said anything. He just watches the NPC over his cup whenever the story gets told — and the story gets told nightly. | One night he says it. Flat, detailed, checkable. Everything built on the story — marriage brokered, seat on the ward council, the children's matches — starts being re-examined at once. |
| 24 | Grounded | The NPC's signature — perfect, down to their favored ink — sits on a purchase agreement they never saw, for the old tannery by the marsh. | First payment on "their" agreement is due at month's end; disputing it means calling a notary a liar who swears he watched the signing. | The NPC pays rather than fight — until they learn what the tannery is used for. Then they're not a victim; they're an accomplice with a paper trail. |
| 25 | Grounded | The NPC is converting everything to small portable valuables and drilling the family on a rally point outside town. They won't say what they heard — only where: the counting-house. | Two other merchants quietly began their own fire sales this morning. Panic is a chain reaction, and the fuse is maybe a week. | Either the collapse comes and the NPC is the last solvent trader — instantly hated — or it doesn't, and the panic they started ruins the street anyway. |
| 26 | Grounded | The NPC is paid a town stipend to keep the flood-drain clear, and hasn't touched it since spring — it's silted to the grate. The sky has been wrong for two days. | Rain's coming, and the first inspection after any flood goes straight to the drain-keeper's log — which is filled in, in advance, in the NPC's tidy hand. | The rain lands, the low quarter floods, someone's cellar becomes a grave — and the falsified log turns simple laziness into a hanging matter. |
| 27 | Grounded | A local leader holds the NPC's letters — genuine, from a worse time — and the price of silence just changed from money to services. | The first service is small and due Sunday: leave the side gate unbarred. It's what the gate is for that the NPC can't stop imagining. | The gate stays unbarred. What comes through it is on the NPC's soul now — and the second service will be bigger, because it can be. |
| 28 | Grounded | The NPC keeps copies: ten years of manifests that don't match the council's published tariffs. Someone on the council has been shorting the town, and the NPC can prove who. | A council clerk just requested the NPC's "complete records" for a routine survey. Routine surveys don't come with a militia escort waiting outside. | Records surrendered, true copies hidden — and the NPC becomes the one person that councilor can't afford to leave standing. They both know it. |
| 29 | Grounded | Two greater houses are fighting through the NPC's small trade: one buys everything at double, the other blocks their cartage. The NPC hasn't picked a side. The town assumes they have. | The buying house wants exclusivity signed by market-day; the blocking house's factor "suggests" the warehouse insurance may lapse. | The NPC stays neutral, so both houses make an example of them instead — and their ruin becomes the story apprentices are told about neutrality. |
| 30 | Grounded | A noble's steward left a sealed schedule of "requisitions" — goods and labor, uncompensated, framed as loyalty owed. None of it is legal. All of it is enforceable. | The first requisition falls due in five days. The last holdout was convicted of an unrelated crime with remarkable speed. | The NPC complies and joins the steward's list of the reliable: quarterly becomes monthly, and the business becomes a serf in all but name. |
| 31 | Grounded | A rumor about the NPC arrived in town wearing details only two living people could know. The NPC is one of them. | The other left years ago on bad terms — and the rumor gains one new, truer detail with each telling, like it's being fed. | The final detail lands: the one that's an actual crime. | 32 | Grounded | An old scandal the NPC outlived is back in every mouth, told fresh — including the part everyone had mercifully forgotten, plus one part that never happened. | The NPC's daughter is being courted by a family that prizes reputation. | 33 | Grounded | The marriage tying the NPC's family to their supplier's family is failing loudly — last night's shouting carried three doors — and both trades are held together by that bed. | Next season is contracted on family rates that die with the marriage. | 34 | Grounded | The heir's horse came back saddled at dawn, blood on one stirrup; the NPC is saying "away on business" through their teeth. | Every hour of daylight the trail cools — and the NPC won't involve the watch because of where the heir was going. | A ransom note arrives tonight naming a price the NPC can only raise by selling out someone else. |
| 35 | Grounded | The NPC's hall was chosen for tonight's reconciliation dinner between two families with a body between them — chosen as neutral ground, which means whatever happens, happens under the NPC's roof. | Both families sent lists of who may not sit near whom, and both lists have a knife-shaped hole in the wording. | 36 | Textured | The survey map they sell copies of redraws one street every time it's unrolled — always the same street, always slightly shorter. | Three customers bought copies this week and went down that street. | 37 | Textured | The baker's apprentice is eating a tenth of every batch and swears it isn't him — he's gained a stone since spring and genuinely doesn't remember. | Last night he was found asleep standing in the neighbor's pantry, chewing. | 40 | Textured | The courier never arrived. His horse did — groomed, grain-fed, re-shod with iron from no forge within a hundred miles.
