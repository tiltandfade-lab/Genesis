# Genesis Backgrounds — native + standard archetypes (SRD-safe)
> The non-SRD character backgrounds for the L1 Sheet (Layer 1 of `CHAR-CREATION.md`), beyond the 4 SRD 5.2.1 ones (Acolyte / Criminal / Sage / Soldier). Two groups: **6 Genesis-native** (tied to world-genesis factions/settings) and **9 standard archetypes** (Charlatan … Urchin) rebuilt **IP-clean**. **Edit-source layer** — `genesis.html` runs a mirrored inline copy (`BACKGROUNDS` + `CG_BG`).
>
> **SRD-safe by construction:** each uses only an SRD origin feat (Alert / Magic Initiate [Cleric/Druid/Wizard] / Savage Attacker / Skilled) and SRD skills/tools, with **original mechanical packages and original prose** — the standard archetypes are *not* copied from the PHB (copyright protects the PHB's flavor text and its specific package designs; recreating the effect under your own wording is clean). The 4 SRD backgrounds keep their official packages.
>
> Each grants: the listed **feat**, **+2 / +1** to the first two of its three abilities (or +1/+1/+1), two **skill** proficiencies, and one **tool** proficiency. The d6 "I became…" table interlocks with the character's Personal-Decisions roll. 19 backgrounds total (4 SRD + 6 native + 9 standard).

## "I became…" tables — standard archetypes (d6, original prose)

### Charlatan
1. I learned young that a confident lie travels farther than an honest coin.
2. People want to be fooled; I only ever gave them what they were asking for.
3. I talked my way out of trouble so often the talking became a trade.
4. A grifter took me under their wing and taught me the long game.
5. Someone fleeced my family, so I learned the trick well enough to never be fooled again.
6. Poverty frightened me more than dishonesty, so I chose the dishonesty.

### Entertainer
1. My family sang for their supper, and I was born already knowing the chorus.
2. I could read a crowd's mood and turn it — laughter or tears, whichever paid.
3. I ran off after a traveling troupe and never looked back at the village.
4. I watched a performer hold a whole room once and knew that was the only thing worth doing.
5. I played the corners and the markets until people started learning my name.
6. A wandering player took me in and taught me to hold an audience in my hand.

### Folk Hero
1. My family taught me right from wrong, and I couldn't unlearn it.
2. I grew up on stories of heroes and ached to be more than ordinary.
3. When the moment came for someone to stand up, I found it was me.
4. Someone in my family was a doer and a wanderer; I wanted to be brave like them.
5. An old hermit said over my cradle that I'd do great things, and the words stuck.
6. I have always put myself between the strong and the people they'd trample.

### Guild Artisan
1. I was apprenticed young and learned a craft well enough to make it mine.
2. I did a guildmaster a quiet favor, and the favor became a place at the bench.
3. A relative held a guild seat and made room at the table for me.
4. My hands were always good with the work, so I went where the work was.
5. I wanted out of my old life, and a trade was the cleanest door.
6. A mentor taught me the craft, but only the guild could finish the teaching.

### Hermit
1. My name was ruined, so I went where no one knew it.
2. I am easiest in my own company, and the quiet is where I find things.
3. I never much liked the people I called friends, so leaving cost me little.
4. I felt I had to leave my old life behind, though some days I still mourn it.
5. I lost everyone and everything; going it alone was all that was left.
6. The rot in polite society turned my stomach, so I walked away from it.

### Noble
1. The family name is old and heavy, and carrying it fell to me.
2. My house was disgraced, and I mean to scrub the stain off our name.
3. We came by our title only recently, and it pitched me into a strange new world.
4. We hold a title but no glory; no one of my blood has earned it in generations.
5. My family is full of remarkable people, and I am desperate to deserve the name.
6. I intend to make my house wealthier and its reach longer than I found them.

### Outlander
1. I spent my youth in the wild places and loved how they ask nothing of you.
2. From the first I couldn't stand the stink of cities and fled to open country.
3. I learned what hunts in the dark beyond the fences, and swore to stand against it.
4. My people lived at the edge of the maps, and taught me to live there too.
5. After everything fell apart, I gave myself to the wilderness and let it remake me.
6. My family moved past the last fencepost, and I learned to belong to that country.

### Sailor
1. Press-ganged onto a deck, I served until I found a way to slip the ship.
2. I wanted to see what lay past the horizon, so I signed on as a deckhand.
3. A relative who lived by the tide took me out to learn the water.
4. I needed to vanish fast, and a hull bound for anywhere was the nearest door.
5. Reavers burned my home, and I took to the water to chase them.
6. There was nothing left for me on land, so I went to find my fortune at sea.

### Urchin
1. I struck out on my own to see the world and have looked after myself since.
2. I ran from a bad house and made my own way on the streets.
3. Something terrible took my village; I was the one small enough to survive it.
4. A thief gathered up the orphans, and we earned our keep with light fingers.
5. I woke on the street one day, hungry, with no memory of how I got there.
6. My parents died and left no one to mind me, so I raised myself.
