## 2. The promise and the invariants

### 2.1 Plain-English promise

**This is a place whose measurements were set by a body that isn't yours.**

The player walks into a space and can read, immediately and without being told, that the
door, the step, the shelf, the bones, or the ground were sized for something else. The
interesting part is not that the thing is big. The interesting part is working out what that
other body can do here that you cannot — and, crucially, what *you* can do here that *it*
cannot.

That last clause is the whole site. A place scaled to another body always leaves gaps that
body cannot reach. Those gaps are the player's advantage, and finding them is the game.

### 2.2 Proposed invariants

Recommendation classes per `GOLDEN-SITES-CATALOG.md`.

- **REQUIRED — THE TWO-SCALE LAW.** At least two represented body-scale contracts must have
  a consequential and different relationship to the same space. The larger body may be
  present, absent, historical or only the intended user, provided its scale still changes
  route capacity, reach, cover, access or interaction for play now. A cave with a dragon in it
  does not qualify unless those affordances differ; an inherited giant-built hall may qualify
  with no giant present.
- **REQUIRED — CONSEQUENTIAL SCALE.** Eligibility is an affordance test, not a fixed ratio.
  At least one aperture, route, reach, cover, traversal or interaction fact must resolve
  differently for the two scale contracts. Huge-versus-Medium (3 cells versus 1) is the
  recommended first proof because it is visually obvious and already represented by live
  spans, not because 3:1 is a universal threshold.
- **REQUIRED — the oversize must be reachable.** Whatever is out of scale must be something
  the player can stand on, climb, hide inside, shoot through, be blocked by, or be denied by.
  Scale that lives only in the ceiling is dressing, not this site. (FFT anti-rule 3.)
- **REQUIRED — unscaled reference is present.** A 2× piece is only legible beside a 1× piece.
  Every valid composition retains ordinary-scale ground, objects, or figures against which the
  oversize is measured. A uniformly doubled scene reads as an ordinary scene further away.
  (FFT anti-rule 2; qualifies `STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG.md`'s Site-11 matrix line.)
- **REQUIRED — the walkable cell stays 5 feet.** Site 11 never enlarges the tactical grid.
  Scale is expressed in the envelope, the pieces, and the apertures. (FFT anti-rule 1;
  agreed independently by the built record — the Tiryns gallery is one cell wide.)
- **REQUIRED — host identity remains independent.** An ordinary or anomalous substrate may
  carry the same ScaleContract. When substrate behavior matters, compose Site 12's shared
  contract; do not reroute or replace the host. (§1.5.)
- **DEFAULT — the scale has a named cause.** A builder, an intended occupant, a set of
  remains, or a present colossus explains the measurements. An unexplained big room is a big
  room.
- **DEFAULT — the small body's advantage is spatial, not statistical.** The player's edge
  comes from a route, aperture, or volume the large body cannot use — not from a bonus.
- **LICENSED — the player is the large body.** A Large PC, mount, polymorph, or controlled
  construct inverts which routes are legal. Legal only where the party actually fields such a
  body. See §7.4 and Founder Question 3.
- **LICENSED — hoard terrain.** Requires an actual accumulating agent with a reason and a
  span of time. A pile of coins on a floor is loot; a hoard is a landform. See §6.3.
- **VARIANT — each of the four relational modes** (§3.1) is a legal composition of the same
  family, ordered by build order rather than excluded.

### 2.3 What a rejected candidate looks like

A big cavern with a dragon asleep in it and treasure on the floor is **not** Site 11. It is
`dungeon-area-type` "Massive Cavern" (a live row, 2.24% of rolled rooms) hosting a
`walk-archetypes` "Dragon Lair" threat (a live archetype, 2.37% of dungeon walks) — which is
Site 7 with an occupant. It becomes Site 11 only when the dragon's body has *shaped* the
space: apertures it fits and you don't, ground it has packed and slid, a hoard that behaves
like a landform, ledges it uses that you must climb.

## 3. Identity, culture, circumstance — and the five-expression family

### 3.1 Site identity: four relational modes

The identity is the *relationship*, and it comes in four kinds. Each has a real-world
precedent, a signature geometry, and a different cost. Full evidence in
`../Reference/Mixed-Scale-Study-0727/lane-1-breadth-sweep.md`.

| mode | relationship | precedent | signature | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — BUILT BY** | a larger builder raised it; smaller bodies inherited it | Tiryns casemate galleries · Mycenae · Karnak hypostyle | inherited oversize — sills, treads, rails, lintels set to the wrong body | cheapest: existing kit at changed piece scale |
| **B — BUILT FOR** | a smaller builder raised it to house or serve a larger occupant | Hampi Elephant Stables | **the aperture pair** — a big-body opening and a small-body opening on the same room | one wall assembly; highest gameplay yield |
| **C — BUILT OUT OF** | the larger body *is* the structure | Mezhyrich mammoth-bone dwellings · Whale Bone Alley · whale-jaw arches | anatomy as bay system — ribs as slotted cover, jaw as arch, skull as chamber | cheap: existing post/arch sockets + bone material + irregular joints |
| **D — BUILT AROUND** | a colossal presence with a parasitic small-scale apparatus on it | Bamiyan · Leshan · ship-breaking yards | colossus-as-terrain; access visibly added and irregular | most expensive: new mass + new access rules |

### 3.2 Cultural expression

Culture decides *which mode a people reach for* and how they behave once inside it. Named
influence, not a lookup key:

- **Toward Mode A** — cultures that inherit and reoccupy: they wedge, infill, ramp, and rail
  the oversize rather than rebuild it. Their marks are additive and provisional. The Greeks
  reading Cyclopes into Mycenae is this posture, and it is a *religious* reading as much as a
  practical one.
- **Toward Mode B** — cultures with a working relationship to a large animal or ally:
  handlers, tethers, feed, mucking-out, and a permanent small-body service circulation. The
  Hampi hook in the ceiling and the mahout's rear door are both this.
- **Toward Mode C** — cultures that butcher at scale: the remains are a resource, the joints
  are lashed not framed, and the building doubles as a claim on the kill.
- **Toward Mode D** — cultures organised *around* a presence: pilgrimage, quarrying, salvage,
  or worship. The apparatus is bolted on, and its irregularity is the tell that the colossus
  came first.

Culture should change proportions, joints, marks, maintenance habits, and where the small
bodies are allowed — not merely palette. Coercion is never an automatic flavour of Mode B; a
tended beast and an enslaved one need different social facts.

### 3.3 Local circumstance

Which body (species, size class, count, alive/dead/petrified/absent) · how long ago · terrain
and available material · whether the large body is present, expected, or gone · who currently
uses the space and why · maintenance state · light ownership · whether anything is still being
*added* to the site (which decides whether a hoard is live or slumping).

### 3.4 Five-expression family

1. **Smallest believable — the one-object room.** An ordinary room with one oversized object
   in it, producing a doughnut playfield with sightline breaks and two ways around. FFT's
   `73 Inside of Windmill Shed` is the demonstration; the engine already rolls ~100 of these
   objects per 539 wilderness arrivals. One prop, no new geometry.
2. **Ordinary — the steading bay.** A Mode-B working building: repeated big-body bays with
   the aperture pair, a tethering point, feed and muck circulation, and an attendant's
   back-of-house. Hampi's eleven chambers at Genesis scale.
3. **Large or advanced — the inherited hall.** A Mode-A structure: oversized masonry with a
   human-scale passage threaded through its thickness, an ordinary-scale approach apron, and
   later small-body infill. Tiryns plus Nelveska.
4. **Degraded / occupied / repurposed — the carcass hall.** A Mode-C composition: a ribcage or
   hull whose bays are now rooms, with squatters, salvagers, or a shrine inside it. Ship-
   breaking economy or Mezhyrich domesticity, depending on the occupancy roll.
5. **Unusual but supported — the colossus site.** A Mode-D composition: a petrified or
   sleeping colossal body as the terrain itself, with a pinned irregular access route and a
   hoard landform at its base. This is the "dragon hoard-hall" the brief named, and it is
   deliberately last.

These five share one relational grammar: **a body sets the measurements; a second body works
around them; the leftover volumes are the game.**

## 4. The scene ladder, implementation order, and the Golden Seed

### 4.1 The scene ladder (how the *place* grows)

Rungs map to the L1–10 bands per TIER-SCOPE and the growth-ladder directive.

- **Rung A — the object.** One oversized thing in an otherwise ordinary space. Cover,
  elevation, and one interior volume. (L1–2.)
- **Rung B — the bay.** One big-body room with the aperture pair, an attendant route, and a
  working reason to exist. (L2–4.)
- **Rung C — the range.** Repeated bays plus a service spine, a yard, and a second body class
  actually present. (L4–7.)
- **Rung D — the domain.** A colossus or carcass at terrain scale with a hoard landform, a
  parasitic access network, and a local economy attached. (L7–10.)

### 4.2 Implementation order — PROPOSED: **B → A → C → D**

Deliberately different from the scene ladder, per the Site 5 precedent.

- **Rung B first** because it contains the smallest useful version of every load-bearing
  relationship: two body classes, two aperture classes, two routes with different capacities,
  and a reason for both bodies to be there. It is the only rung that proves the *mechanism*
  rather than the *look*.
- **Rung A second, by honest degradation** — remove the building and keep the object. This is
  the highest-volume live demand (§8.1) and it should be produced by stripping the proven bay,
  not by inventing a prop family.
- **Rung C third** — repetition and a service spine, which is recombination once B works.
- **Rung D last** because it is the only rung that needs genuinely new technology (colossus
  mass, pinned irregular access, hoard terrain) and it is the rung whose failure is most
  expensive.

**Learning rationale.** B teaches the aperture pair, route capacity by body class, and the
two-body camera problem. Once those hold, A is subtraction, C is repetition, and D is the
only real invention left.

**What stays unknown after B passes.** Hoard terrain. Colossus-as-terrain. Whether a
Gargantuan standee can be framed at all by the fixed production camera (§4.5). Mode C's bone
joints.

**Ladder links.** B promotes to C by repetition and to D by replacing the built envelope with
a body. B degrades to A by deleting the envelope. C degrades to B by deleting the spine. D
degrades to A by deleting the apparatus and keeping the body — which is exactly the
`256 Petrified Dragon` row the engine already rolls.

### 4.3 The Golden Seed — PROPOSED

**A working beast-bay range with a live attendant circulation and one bay that is
currently occupied.**

Composition:

- three to five repeated big-body bays under a continuous roof, sized to a Huge occupant;
- **the aperture pair** on every bay — a big-body working opening on the yard face, and a
  small-body service opening at the back;
- a back-of-house service passage linking the small openings, at 1 cell clear;
- a yard apron of ordinary-scale ground in front, with ordinary-scale objects for reference;
- one bay currently holding the large body; the rest empty, in use, or stored;
- a tethering point, feed store, water, and a muck route with somewhere real to go;
- an ordinary-scale approach that lets the player read the whole thing before committing.

It is the Golden Seed because it is the only composition that makes the two-body relationship
*mechanical* rather than scenic, and because every other rung is reachable from it by
subtraction, repetition, or substitution.

**It is not the family.** A beast bay proves the aperture pair and route capacity. It proves
nothing about hoards, colossi, bones, or inherited ruins.

### 4.4 Provisional grid hypotheses — NUMBERS ARE HYPOTHESES

Committed quantum (not a hypothesis): **1 cell = 5 ft; vertical `h` = 2.5 ft**
(`STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG.md` line 51; `src/engine/clay-room.js:782`).

Restated as real-world construction before each number: a stable needs a door the animal can
walk through without touching, a ceiling it can raise its head under, a floor it can turn on,
and a separate way in for the person who has to work around it while it is standing there.

| element | proposed start | derived from |
|---|---|---|
| big-body aperture (Huge occupant) | **3 cells / 15 ft clear width**, 4 h / 10 ft clear head | the SRD Huge space is 15 × 15 ft; the animal must pass without contact |
| big-body aperture (Gargantuan) | **4 cells / 20 ft clear width**, 6 h / 15 ft clear head | SRD Gargantuan space is 20 × 20 ft |
| small-body service aperture | **1 cell / 5 ft clear**, 2 h / 5 ft head | its capacity limit *is* its value; a Large body must not fit |
| bay depth (Huge) | **4 cells / 20 ft** | occupant space plus turning and working clearance |
| service passage | **1 cell clear**, and it must not admit a 2-cell body anywhere along its run | the whole point |
| yard apron | **≥ 3 cells deep of ordinary-scale ground** | the unscaled-reference invariant |
| Mode-A inherited tread | **1 h rise per tread** for the small body's added stair; **2 h** for the builder's own step | 2 h is a climb for a Medium body and a stride for the builder — the read *is* the number |
| hoard slope, stable | **1 h per cell (26.6°)** | below the loose-material repose band |
| hoard slope, unstable | **2 h per cell (45°)** | above it; slides when disturbed |

No number becomes binding until standee fit, collision, fixed-camera legibility, and actual
play choices agree. The first proof must show **four standee envelopes in one frame**: small,
six-foot human, Huge (3 cells), and Gargantuan (4 cells).

### 4.5 The standee and sprite constraint — the tray problem Site 11 first proves

Site 11 is the portfolio's first retained proof that routinely puts a solo-sheet titanic
sprite and a sixteen-per-sheet medium sprite in the same frame at the same time. Other hosts
may later consume the same `ScaleContract`. Two live consequences follow.

**(a) The engine currently holds two different answers for "how big is Gargantuan."**

```
src/ui/theater-sprites.js:180        SPRITE_SIZE_SCALE    gargantuan: 4    (SRD space ratio; billboard path)
src/ui/theater-standee-mount.js:105  interiorTacticalSpan gargantuan: 4    (cells)
src/ui/theater-boot.js:571           SIZE_SCALE           gargantuan: 2.2  (readability tune; cuboid/recipe path)
src/ui/blockwright.js:156            BW_SIZE_SCALE        Gargantuan: 3    (same as Huge; no distinct rung)
```

`theater-sprites.js`'s own comment says the split is deliberate. It is defensible everywhere
except here: a site whose entire identity is the ratio between two bodies cannot carry two
answers for what the ratio is. **No fix is proposed in this spec.** It is recorded because the
first clay proof will surface it on frame one.

**(b) The sheet economy holds pixels-per-foot roughly flat, then drops at the top.**

Adam's ruling, 2026-07-09, verbatim: *"creatures 9' and up need to be regened at 2x on sheets
with less sprites — 4 per sheet for the big ones, and the real badass titanic ones get their
own sheet."* Under the round-4 sheet map in `ART-DEPARTMENT.md`, assuming the fantasy style
block's own requirement that the subject fills its cell head-to-toe:

| band | sheet | cell | subject | px per foot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| medium (6 ft) | 1280×1600, 4×4 | 320×400 | 400 px | **≈ 67** |
| large (10 ft) | 1280×1600, 2×2 | 640×800 | 800 px | **≈ 80** |
| huge (15 ft) | 1600×1000, 1×2 | 800×1000 | 1000 px | **≈ 67** |
| gargantuan (24 ft) | 1024×1280 solo | 1024×1280 | 1280 px | **≈ 53** |

The 2× ruling is doing its job through Huge — the band holds at ~67 px/ft. It falls to ~53
px/ft at Gargantuan, roughly 0.8×. **The largest creature is the least detailed per foot,
which is the opposite of what a hero frame wants**, and Site 11 is where that inversion
becomes visible.

Two disclosures. First, `ART-DEPARTMENT.md` flags the round-4 generator as *superseded for new
production formatting* and routes new packets through `PRODUCTION-FORMAT.md` "after the
giant/titanic aspect tests rule exact ratios." Site 11 therefore has a **live dependency on an
unruled decision**. Second, giant/titanic `cellAspect: 4:6` is explicitly a `PROVISIONAL TEST
CANDIDATE` — and a coiled dragon over a hoard is a *landscape* subject, so portrait 4:6 is
probably the wrong cell for exactly the composition this site wants most.

This is Founder Question 3.

## 5. The deck — elevation as objective

Per the guard-post roof ruling's generalization, every site names its high ground.

**Site 11's deck is the body itself.** Not a roof, not a ledge — the back, shoulder, spine,
skull, or palm of the thing that set the measurements.

Ownership varies by mode, exactly as the lair's deck owner varies by occupancy:

| mode | the deck | who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| A — BUILT BY | an oversized sill, cap, or step: a climb for you, a stride for the builder | nobody — it is inherited furniture |
| B — BUILT FOR | the mounting block, tether platform, or bay roof from which handlers work | the attendants; the player can take it |
| C — BUILT OUT OF | the ribcage's spine, the skull's crown, the hull's deck | whoever holds the carcass |
| D — BUILT AROUND | the colossus's shoulder, wing-root, or palm — the engine's own rows say so (`238 Stone Hand`: "Palm creates a natural elevated platform"; `256 Petrified Dragon`: "Wings form ramps") | the colossus, if it is alive |

The deck is an *objective* because it is the only place from which the whole two-body
relationship is visible at once — and because in Mode D, reaching it may be the difference
between fighting a colossus and standing on one.

## 6. Operating circuits and factorized mutable state

Site 11 does not have a production chain like Site 5 or a custody chain like Site 6. It has
**four relational circuits**, each of which can change without repainting the site as
generically "giant."

1. **Body presence.** absent / expected / present / dead / petrified. Decides whether the
   scale relationship is live, remembered, or fossil.
2. **Access by class.** For every portal and route: which body classes may pass, and under
   what state (open / restricted / blocked / one-way / crawl-only). This is the circuit that
   makes the site playable.
3. **Attendance.** Who services the large body, when, and by which route. Shift, feed, muck,
   tether, and inspection each have a time and a path.
4. **Accumulation.** What is being added to the site and by whom — hoard, spoil, bone,
   salvage, offering. Decides whether the granular mass is live (flat working top, access
   ramp) or dead (slumped, skinned over, revegetating).

### 6.1 Factorized state — no single `giantState` switch

The script may show friendly labels ("the beast is in", "the hoard is unstable"), but those
derive from separate facts:

- **presence:** absent / expected / present / dead / petrified;
- **aperture state, per portal:** which classes pass, and open/restricted/blocked/crawl;
- **attendance:** staffed / skeleton / abandoned, plus current shift;
- **mass state:** static / shifting / flowing (§6.3);
- **integrity:** sound / strained / critical / failed — for inherited oversize masonry and for
  bone alike;
- **accumulation:** growing / static / being removed;
- **cause and recovery:** what changed each, and what could change it back.

One `mixedScaleState = dragon` switch is rejected for the same reason Site 5 rejected
`mineState = failing`: it makes every consequence simultaneous and removes the levers.

### 6.2 The aperture-pair circuit in detail

Every aperture carries a **class capacity** — the largest body span in cells that may pass —
and that capacity is a first-class runtime fact, not a collision accident. The two-aperture
building is what turns that fact into gameplay:

- the big-body opening is fast, obvious, and watched;
- the small-body opening is slow, hidden from the large body's reach, and **cannot be
  followed through**;
- a large body inside a bay can block, but not pursue.

That last line is the site's core fun. It is the same shape as Site 5's haul/manway split,
arrived at independently from a real building, which is why it is proposed as the first buy.

**The ruleset already agrees.** SRD 5.2.1's movement rules
(`Reference/SRD-Data/core-rules.md`, "Moving around Other Creatures") say a creature may pass
through the space of *"a creature that is two sizes larger or smaller than you"* — treating
that space as difficult terrain. So a Medium body may legally move through a Huge body's
space, and a Small body through a Large one's. That is not a Site 11 invention; it is the
existing rule, and it means **the two-body relationship already has mechanical teeth in the
combat layer before any geometry is built.** Medium (1 cell) to Huge (3 cells) is the smallest
gap at which this particular movement rule applies, which makes it a useful first-proof
calibration without turning it into a universal Site-11 threshold. (Whether
the SRD wording means *exactly* two sizes or *at least* two is a reading question the combat
layer already has to have settled; this spec does not resolve it and should not.)

### 6.3 Granular mass — PROPOSED shared terrain provider, with hoard as one use

Restated as real-world construction first: a heap of loose material stands at its angle of
repose. Steeper and it slides; shallower and it sits. Loose granular material sits broadly in
a 30°–40° band. Flowing granular material behaves like quicksand — an adult sinks knee-deep in
about 4 seconds and can be fully engulfed in about 20; extraction from waist-deep needs
body-weight plus roughly 600 lb of force, and fully buried can exceed 2,000 lb. Movement on a
steep face is itself an avalanche trigger.
(Sources in `../Reference/Mixed-Scale-Study-0727/lane-1-breadth-sweep.md`.)

Mapped onto the committed quantum:

| slope | rise | angle | proposed behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **stable** | 1 h per cell | 26.6° | difficult terrain; walkable; climbable at cost |
| **unstable** | 2 h per cell | 45° | any movement rolls a slide: the mover and anything on the face move one cell downslope; the face re-grades |
| **flowing** | any | — | licensed only where something is drawing the mass down (a breach, a chute, a moving body beneath). Entering costs footing on a short clock; leaving needs help or a hold |

Proposed shape facts, from `MS-11`'s read: a hoard that is still being *added to* has a **flat
working top and a graded access route**; a hoard nobody tends **slumps, rounds, and skins
over**. That is a free occupancy tell requiring no props.

Proposed material fact, from `MS-12`'s read: a real mass of loose gold and jewellery is dull,
mixed, size-varied, and matte in aggregate — not a uniform gleaming pile. Hoard material
should read as *aggregate*, with individual legible objects only at the surface and edges.

**Why terrain and not a prop.** As a prop it is loot wallpaper and Site 7 is already borrowing
"a bounded hoard-pile prop until site 11 mints hoard terrain properly." As a shared
`GranularMass` terrain provider it serves hoard, mine spoil, rubble, grain, salt, ash, scree
and bulk cargo without giving Site 11 exclusive ownership of a general mechanic. Site 11
sponsors the first hoard proof and consumes the same provider through its `ScaleContract`.
This is Founder Question 2.

## 7. Strategic levers and demonstrable plans

The site does not pass because the scale relationship is believable. It has to produce fun
plans.

### 7.1 Levers

- **Aperture control.** Opening, closing, blocking, or widening any aperture changes which
  bodies can go where. Blocking the big opening traps or excludes; opening the small one
  creates an escape a large body cannot follow into.
- **Body presence and position.** Where the large body is standing determines what is blocked,
  what is watched, and what is reachable. Moving it — by lure, feed, alarm, or provocation —
  is a spatial move, not a combat move.
- **Attendance timing.** Shift, feed, muck, and inspection change witnesses, open doors, and
  route traffic. The site's social access runs on this clock.
- **Mass manipulation.** Destabilising a hoard or spoil face, or drawing it down, changes
  footing, reachable ground, and what is buried.
- **The deck.** Reaching the body's high ground changes sightline, reach, and — where the
  body is alive — the entire encounter's shape.
- **Structural integrity.** Inherited oversize masonry and large bone both fail in
  characteristic ways: block displacement and slab fall for masonry, splitting and collapse
  for bone. Both are levers, and both must have a visible last-safe marker rather than a
  scripted collapse anywhere.

### 7.2 Three demonstrable plans for the Golden Seed, one noncombat

1. **The service route (noncombat).** Enter through the attendants' back-of-house at 1-cell
   clear, move behind the bays, reach the objective without ever entering the big body's
   space. Gives: no confrontation, full access to stores and records. Asks: slow, single-file,
   no cover, and total exposure if a handler is present. The large body cannot follow.
2. **The yard approach.** Cross the ordinary-scale apron and enter through a big-body opening.
   Gives: speed, room to fight, several exits. Asks: open ground, watched from the bays,
   and a large body free to move and block.
3. **The occupancy play.** Change where the large body is — feed, tether, release, alarm — and
   let its new position do the work of blocking, opening, or threatening. Gives: a lever no
   fight can match. Asks: the plan is only as good as your read of the animal, and a mistake
   puts a Huge body between you and the exit.

Every one of the three uses the same three spatial truths: aperture class, body position, and
attendant timing.

### 7.3 Rejection triggers for the first build

Reject or revise if: the small route is merely a slower version of the big route; the large
body has no position that matters; the aperture pair is decorative rather than capacity-bearing;
the scale is only visible in the ceiling; a changed seed erases the plan choice; the fixed
camera cannot show a Gargantuan standee and a Medium standee legibly in one frame; or an
ordinary standee blocks the only legal route.

### 7.4 The inversion — LICENSED

If the party fields a Large or bigger body, every capacity fact runs backwards: the service
route closes to them, the yard opens, and the small teammates gain routes the big one has just
lost. This is the single best stretch of the whole site because it re-uses every aperture and
capacity fact without one new asset — and it is the hardest thing on the standee and sprite
pipeline. Proposed: **Proof is small-side only**; the inversion is MVP work.

## 9. Generator contract

### 9.1 Inputs committed before layout

Relational mode (A/B/C/D) · the large body's identity, size class, count, and state

### 9.2 Semantic blueprint — named before scenery

- the **scale-setting body** and its span in cells;
- the **working body** and its span;
- every **aperture** with its class capacity and current state;
- every **volume** the large body occupies, and the leftover volumes it cannot reach;
- the **deck** and its access;
- routes, and for each route the largest span that may pass;
- any **granular mass**, its slope band, and its accumulation state;
- the unscaled-reference elements;
- attendance circuit nodes and times;
- three candidate plans and their tradeoffs;
- hooks, interaction anchors, and recovery verbs.

### 9.3 Generation order

1. Commit the two represented body-scale contracts, their spans, presence/history state, and
   the host program.
2. Choose the relational mode from the committed facts — never from a preferred look.
3. Place the scale-setting body's volume(s) first. Everything else is negative space around it.
4. Cut the big-body apertures to the large span; verify passage without contact.
5. Cut the small-body apertures and the service route; verify a 2-cell body cannot pass
   anywhere along its run.
6. Place the unscaled-reference apron and objects.
7. Place the deck and its access; in Mode D the access is added, irregular, and pinned.
8. Realise granular mass, slope band, and accumulation tells.
9. Reserve approaches, objectives, retreat, cover, quiet ground, and interaction space.
10. Validate the fixed camera against **all four standee envelopes in one frame**, plus
    collision, route capacity, light ownership, and changed states.
11. Dress the retained ids. Never move gameplay geometry to improve a beauty shot.

## 10. Rejection rules and the fallback ladder

### 10.1 Countable rejection checks

Reject the candidate before presentation when:

- fewer than two represented body-scale contracts have a consequentially different
  relationship to the space (`TWO-SCALE LAW`);
- no aperture, route, reach, cover, traversal or interaction affordance resolves differently
  between them (`CONSEQUENTIAL SCALE`);
- the oversize is not standable, climbable, enterable, shootable-through, or blocking;
- no unscaled reference is present in the fixed camera's frame;
- any walkable cell is not 5 ft;
- the small-body route admits a 2-cell body anywhere along its run;
- the big-body aperture does not clear the committed occupant span without contact;
- the large body has no position that changes what is reachable or watched;
- anomalous substrate behavior is consequential but no `SubstratePlan`/Site-12 composition is
  recorded;
- a granular mass exists with no accumulating agent and no reason;
- an unstable slope exists with no downslope destination;
- the fixed camera cannot legibly show a 4-cell standee and a 1-cell standee together;
- practical light has no owner, fuel, shift, or process (the causal light law);
- a changed seed preserves the picture by destroying the plan choice.

### 10.2 Deterministic fallback ladder

Try in order and record the relaxation:

1. move noncanonical dressing;
2. widen the yard apron rather than the apertures;
3. reduce the number of repeated bays while keeping at least one complete aperture pair;
4. drop the relational mode one rung of expense (D → C → B → A) while keeping both body
   classes present;
5. reduce the large body one size class only if the consequential affordance difference
   survives;
6. shorten the granular mass while preserving its slope decision;
7. reject and reroll.

The generator may never overlap standee bases, silently widen a service route to admit a large
body, delete the unscaled reference to fit the frame, or enlarge the tactical cell.

## 11. Culture, construction, and occupant cards

The first culture pair should change at least four of these without changing the committed
bodies:

- which relational mode the culture reaches for, and why;
- how additions attach to inherited oversize — wedged, ramped, railed, infilled, or bolted;

- whether remains are resource, relic, or taboo;
- what gets accumulated, by whom, and whether the pile is defended or shared;

**Coercion is never automatic.** A tended beast, a worked beast, and an enslaved giant need
different social facts, different marks, and different consequences.

## 12. Occupancy, hooks, lighting, context, and boundaries

### 12.1 Occupancy states

Body absent and expected back · body present and working · body present and hostile · body
present and indifferent · body dead and being processed · body petrified/fossil · attendants
without a body · squatters in a space built for a body · salvagers dismantling the body ·
pilgrims around the body · nobody at all, everything slumping.

### 12.2 Arrival hooks

Retrieve something from a volume only a small body can reach · move or free the large body ·
protect it · butcher it · prevent a butchering · a handler is missing · a hoard is being drawn
down and something under it is surfacing · an inherited hall's oversize masonry is failing on
someone · a small-body infill has blocked the animal's own route · the accumulation is somebody
else's property.

Every hook must touch at least one circuit (§6) and at least one map lever (§7.1).

### 12.3 Lighting and cutaway

- The causal light law applies unchanged: every light has an owner. A dark-sighted occupant
  motivates no practical fixture, and its volume may be genuinely dark.
- A big-body volume lit only for the attendants produces a characteristic read: light clings to
  the service edge and the big volume goes dark. That is a free tell and should be preferred to
  filling the space.
- Cutaway may omit the camera-facing roof, ceiling, or overhead mass — but **not** the piece
  that makes the scale legible. Site 11's cutaway rule: whatever the cutaway removes, the
  frame must still show one full-height oversize element next to one full-height ordinary
  element. Losing that is losing the site.

### 12.4 Rolled world-context projection

Context must explain how a body that size got here and what it does when it leaves: approach
routes wide enough for it, ground it has compacted, vegetation it has cleared or broken,
water it needs, and the settlement or wilderness that tolerates it. Near/mid/far may show the
continuation of a colossal mass, a quarry cut, a spoil or bone field, or the tracks. A painted
giant on the horizon may not stand in for a canonical occupant, and a painted route may not
replace a real connector.

Only direct visual nouns, physical properties, and visible processes project automatically,
per `GOLDEN-SITE-WORLD-CONTEXT-PROJECTION.md`. **No context receipt has been rolled for Site
11 yet** — that is part of the next demonstration, not a completed item.

### 12.5 Boundaries with other sites

- **Site 12 — Anomalous / Living / Mobile.** These are orthogonal stress contracts. Ask:
  (1) does body/object scale change a present affordance? If yes, apply `ScaleContract`;
  (2) does support, datum, mobility, anchoring or substrate state change play? If yes, apply
  `SubstratePlan`; (3) does Becoming/Intrusion change that substrate? If yes, apply the
  transformation. **`The Megastructure` clearly needs the substrate side** because its source
  says *"Anomaly / Ancient technology"* and *"non-euclidean walls shift"*. It also needs
  ScaleContract when differently sized routes or apertures are consequential. Neither answer
  replaces the Megastructure host.
- **Site 7 — Natural Lair.** Site 7 owns the lair interior grammar at ordinary scale, and its
  brief already says a dragon-lair occupancy may borrow a bounded hoard-pile prop "until site
  11 mints hoard terrain properly." This spec accepts that boundary and proposes to discharge
  the debt through the shared `GranularMass` provider (§6.3), sponsored by Site 11 and
  available to Site 7 as terrain rather than prop.
  A big cave with a dragon in it stays Site 7.
- **Site 9 — Contested Fortress.** Both are scale/relationship stress cases. Site 9's stress is
  *population and fronts* over a large defence; Site 11's is *body ratio*. A fortress built by
  giants and held by humans is Site 9 with a Site 11 transform.
- **Site 5 — Mine / Workshop.** Owns the production chain and the haul/manway split. Site 11's
  aperture pair is the same idea from a different premise; Site 5 remains the authority for
  production-flow grammar and Site 11 must not grow one.
- **Site 3 — Dormant/Abandoned** and **Site 8 — Layered Control** are transforms that apply
  over a Site 11 composition without changing its host.
- **Site 2 — Camp / Service.** The guest-family property covers humanoid squatters inside a
  Site 11 space; Site 11 does not grow a squatter vocabulary.

## 16. Founder questions — PROPOSED BUILD ORDERS, not A-or-B choices

Per THE GENERATOR PRINCIPLE, each records the build order, the learning rationale, and the
ladder links. All variants are already in the roller.

### FQ-11-1 — Which relational mode is built first?

**Proposed build order: B → A → C → D.**
**Learning:** B is the only mode that proves the *mechanism* (two body classes, two aperture
classes, two capacities) rather than the *look*. A is then subtraction, C is repetition, D is
the only remaining invention.
**Ladder links:** B degrades to A by deleting the envelope; B promotes to C by repetition and
to D by replacing the built envelope with a body; D degrades to A by deleting the apparatus
and keeping the body.
**Tension to be aware of:** the broad object screen (18.55% of wilderness arrivals) is all
Mode-A/object-scale candidate content, which argues for building A first. It does not prove
that every hit activates Site 11. The counter-argument is that A built
first teaches nothing reusable — it is a prop family, and we would then still have to discover
the aperture pair. **Recommendation: B first anyway**, with A landing immediately after by
degradation rather than by separate invention.
**Easy to revise?** Yes. The order costs sequencing, not exclusion.
**Needs to judge it:** the §15.5 section-and-plan pair.

### FQ-11-2 — Is hoard a shared granular terrain type or a prop?

**Proposed build order: shared `GranularMass` terrain first, prop as its degradation** —
built with the first Mode-D composition, not with Proof.
**Learning:** as a prop it is loot wallpaper and teaches nothing; Site 7's brief is already
carrying a placeholder prop and waiting. As shared terrain it discharges the Site 7 debt,
turns the dragon's floor into a decision, and reaches Sites 5, 3, and 10 as spoil, rubble,
grain and bulk without assigning a general terrain mechanic to one Golden number.
**Ladder links:** the prop version is a strict subset of the terrain version (a static mass
with no slope band), so terrain degrades to prop cleanly; the reverse does not hold.
**Easy to revise?** Downward, yes. Upward, no — a prop built first would be thrown away.
**Needs to judge it:** a clay capture of one mass at 1 h/cell and the same mass at 2 h/cell,
with a standee on each, so the stable/unstable read can be seen rather than argued.

### FQ-11-3 — The mixed-register frame: does Site 11 gate a sprite re-render?

**The situation.** Site 11 is the first retained proof that routinely frames a solo-sheet
titanic sprite beside a sixteen-per-sheet medium sprite. Under the round-4 sheet economy the band holds at
~67 px/ft from Medium through Huge and drops to ~53 px/ft at Gargantuan — the biggest creature
is the least detailed per foot. Separately, the engine currently carries two different answers
for the Gargantuan ratio (4× on the billboard path, 2.2× on the cuboid path), and the
giant/titanic `cellAspect` is an explicitly unruled `PROVISIONAL TEST CANDIDATE` whose portrait
4:6 default is probably wrong for a coiled dragon over a hoard, which is a landscape subject.
**Proposed build order:** build the Proof with the sprites as they stand, capture the mismatch
honestly at the production camera, and let the capture decide — rather than pre-emptively
re-rendering anything.
**Why it is a founder question and not an implementation choice:** whether a visible density
difference between the giant and the party reads as *wrong* or as *fine* is taste, and it is
the kind of thing that only becomes decidable in front of a real frame.
**What it changes later:** if the mismatch is unacceptable, Site 11 gates a re-render of the
Gargantuan/titanic band under a new `PRODUCTION-FORMAT.md` ratio — which is exactly the
decision `ART-DEPARTMENT.md` already says is pending.

## Architecture integration ruling — 2026-07-30

Scale is relational, not a uniform multiplier and not a site selector. Site 11 inherits
its host's battle-space mode, then compiles explicit body-relative apertures, routes,
cover, support, interaction, service, wear, and camera composition. At least two unlike
body profiles are mandatory.

`MEGAINTERIOR` describes the active spatial experience; `GIANT_LEGACY` additionally
requires explicit original-builder or original-occupant evidence. A colossal object,
dragon trace, or “megastructure” label alone is insufficient. The proof receipt names
original builder, original occupant, current occupant, each body envelope, and which
members are inherited, repaired, repurposed, or newly built at current scale.
