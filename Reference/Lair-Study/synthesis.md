## 1. The one-sentence result

Across geology, animal architecture, industrial voids, the FFT cave cohort, and the two named
touchstones, the lair converges on the same shape: **a descending chain of chambers, where
the chamber is a widening on a passage, the enclosure is terrain height rather than
architecture, the interest is on the vertical faces rather than the floor, light is a small
number of hard-edged islands, and the way in is almost never a door.**

## 3. Typology sheet — origin × posture × inhabitation × scale

Four independent axes. The evidence says they really are independent — which is the point,
because the banked nature roll already composes them.

### Origin (what made the void)

| Origin | Reads as | Ceiling | Entrances | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Karst | irregular chambers on a descending tree | absent/irregular | usually **one** | dripstone risers, gour terraces, breakdown |
| Lava tube | constant-section bore | absent/irregular | one + skylights | banded shell, flow ledge, rubble floor |
| Sea | slot corridor, taller than wide | absent | one, at water | flat wet floor, boulder litter, tidal datum |
| Ice | one sweeping shell | absent | one | *suppresses* the shell-set vocabulary — no dripstone, no clusters |
| Dug by occupant | trunk + blind pockets, two depth horizons | absent | **many** | spoil fan, bore-section constancy, dressed sill |
| Adopted void (mine/quarry) | galleries, big and regular | **flat, or a black overburden mass** | one adit + shafts | pillar rhythm, timber (if working), level flood water |
| Collapse void | a chamber opened from above | broken | a hole at the top of a debris fan | scree runs *into* the void |

**Two discriminators do all the origin work visually: ceiling regularity (Lane 3 finding 1,
LC-4) and entrance count (Lane 2 finding 2).** Everything else is dressing.

### Posture (how you get in and move)

Walk-in mouth · pit (the rim is the way out) · cliff/eyrie · flooded siphon (the *approach*
is the problem, not the room — L2-07) · skylight break · size-gated crevice.

### Inhabitation

Apex den · warren (**vertebrate scale only** — L2-08 is the anti-model) · larder lair · eyrie
*(unevidenced by this study)* · squatters (guest camp) · **uninhabited** (visually complete on
its own — Lane 3 finding 10).

### Scale

One-off chamber (real and common — L1-09) ↔ pocket lair (2–4 functional chambers, the animal
band — L2 finding 8) ↔ full dungeon-length identity (GB Cave: one entrance, a descending
spine, two loops, several named dead ends).

## 5. The transfer risk nobody has named yet

**LC-10.** FFT's cave maps are authored for **one camera rotation**; two of the four
rotations of a typical Deep Dungeon map are majority black backfaces, and FFT is fine with
that because it ships a fixed view. Genesis's walk/theater camera does not obviously inherit
that permission. Every one of the LC rules that buys cheapness — no ceiling, enclosure by
back mass, subtractive labyrinths, painted breakdown — is buying it *against a known
camera*. If the lair camera can orbit, the cave falls apart at the unfavoured angles and the
savings evaporate.

This is a `DERIVED` technical consequence, and it should be settled before any clay pass, not
after.

## 6. THE FOUNDER PACKET

Ten questions, plain English. Each is argued by evidence above; none is answered here.

**1. Should the room chain have a direction — always downward?**
Every real cave system in the study descends monotonically from its entrance to its far end.
Do you want Genesis lairs to inherit that (each link goes down, and going back up is going
out), or should a lair chain be free to wander level and even rise?

**2. Is the ledge one continuous rim, or a broken shelf with a gap you have to jump?**
Your named touchstone's shelf "divides narrowly in the middle" and crossing costs a jump. A
continuous rim gives a clean patrol loop; a broken one makes the high ground a commitment.
The evidence favours broken. Which do you want?

**3. Where does the loop live — on the ledge, or on the floor?**
The banked ruling puts "the route around" on the rim. In the owlbear cave the loop is at
floor level: two paths from the entrance that rejoin at the den from opposite sides, divided
by a stream. Floor loop plus a dead-end ledge is a different tactical puzzle from a rim
walkway. Pick one as the default.

**4. Should the way up to the ledge normally be a hidden, size-gated back door?**
In the touchstone, the shelf's own entrance is a crevice found on a Perception check that
only small creatures fit through — a bolt-hole, a search reward, and a party-composition gate
all at once. Real burrows carry the same noun (a plugged emergency shaft). Do you want the
bolt-hole promoted from "a secret sometimes" to "the standard way the high ground is
reached"?

**5. Is the causal light law a look, or a trade?**
Diablo 1's darkness bit because carrying more light let monsters notice you sooner — the
player chose between seeing and being seen. Right now the law says lights must have owners
and dark rooms stay dark, which reproduces the picture but not the decision. Do you want
carried light wired into the detection clock, or does it stay purely presentational?

**6. Do we ever model a cave ceiling?**
The FFT cave cohort models none — enclosure comes from a tall mass at the back of frame. But
the owlbear cave's best trick is shooting stalactites down, and that needs a ceiling to hang
from. Do we (a) never model one and lose that class of trick, (b) allow ceiling *props*
without a ceiling *surface*, or (c) model an overhead mass only in the mine/dug case, the way
FFT's own mine map does?

**7. Can the lair camera orbit?**
FFT gets its cheapness by authoring each cave for a single viewing angle; two of its four
rotations are mostly black. If our camera can be turned, none of that economy is available.
This is the biggest cost question in the study and it is a yes/no.

**8. Does the mine get its own room family, or do we scope "adopted void" to feral workings
only?**
A creature's bore is genuinely a shell parameter — same family, different finish. A quarry
with a flat ceiling and a grid of left pillars is not; it is construction. Either mines earn a
separate family, or the origin roll's "abandoned mine" result is narrowed to irregular,
collapsed, ceiling-broken workings that the natural family can carry.

**9. Should entrance count be rolled from origin?**
Karst caves have one mouth; burrows have many; mines have an adit plus shafts. That single
number changes everything downstream — approach, bolt-holes, retreat honesty, secrets. Do you
want origin to *determine* entrance count, or should they roll independently?

**10. Is the eyrie still on the roster?**
This study found no usable evidence for it — the geology lanes gave nothing, the animal lane
found no licensable eyrie morphology, and the FFT cohort has no cliff-nest map. Everything
else on the nature roll is now evidenced. Do you want the eyrie kept as a posture and
researched separately, folded into "cliff mouth," or dropped?
