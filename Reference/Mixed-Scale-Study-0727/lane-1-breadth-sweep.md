# Lane 1 — Breadth sweep: what else could Site 11 be?

type: research-lane

status: COMPLETE (declared gaps in `synthesis.md`)

## The question the sweep had to answer

The brief hands Site 11 three examples — "dragon hoard-halls, giant steadings, titan remains
inhabited at human scale." Those are three *instances*. The sweep asks whether they are three
points on one axis or three unrelated things wearing the same number, and whether the real
world knows any others.

The answer is that they are three of **four** modes, and the fourth is the one that actually
recurs most often in the built record.

## The four relational modes

Every real-world case found in this sweep sorts cleanly into one of four relationships
between a space and the bodies involved with it. Nothing found needed a fifth bin.

### Mode A — BUILT BY a larger body, used by a smaller one

The construction's measurements were set by a body that is no longer the one moving through
it. This is the most common real case, because human cultures reliably *inherit* structures
they could not have raised and then read a giant into the gap.

- **Cyclopean masonry (Mycenae, Tiryns, c. 13th c. BCE).** Walls of limestone boulders, some
  over ten tonnes, in places eight metres thick and thirteen metres high. The name is the
  evidence: classical Greeks concluded that only Cyclopes could have moved the stones, and
  Pliny reports the tradition — attributed to Aristotle — that Cyclopes invented masonry
  towers. The players' inference is the historical inference.
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclopean_masonry) ·
  [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/technology/cyclopean-masonry) ·
  [Livius](https://www.livius.org/articles/objects/cyclopean-walls/))
- **The Tiryns casemate galleries.** The load-bearing detail for Genesis: those same
  eight-metre walls are hollow. A corbelled passage runs *inside the wall thickness* at
  human width. One structure, two scales, simultaneously — see `images/MS-01`.
- **Karnak's Great Hypostyle Hall.** 134 columns over ~5,000 m²; the central twelve above
  20 m. Explicitly built to make the human viewer feel insignificant.
  ([Digital Karnak](https://digitalkarnak.ucsc.edu/hypostyle-hall/) ·
  [Memphis Hypostyle project](https://www.memphis.edu/hypostyle/about-architecture/index.php))

**What Mode A teaches:** the tactical signature is *inherited oversize* — a sill you have to
climb, a tread that is a full move, a rail at chest height for the wrong chest. It costs
almost nothing to build (it is the existing kit with the piece-scale dial turned up) and it
is the cheapest legible entry into Site 11.

### Mode B — BUILT FOR a larger body, by a smaller one

A small builder deliberately houses, stables, docks, or services a large occupant. This mode
is rarer in fiction than Mode A and *far* better documented in reality.

- **The Elephant Stables, Hampi (Vijayanagara, 15th–16th c.).** 85 m long, 9 m deep, eleven
  domed chambers, each large enough for two elephants, interconnected by large arched
  openings, with a hook in the central chamber's ceiling for tethering. **And every chamber
  has a small opening at the rear, used by the mahouts to enter and exit.**
  ([hampi.in](https://hampi.in/elephant-stables) ·
  [Incredible India](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/karnataka/hampi/elephant-stables) ·
  [karnataka.com](https://www.karnataka.com/hampi/elephant-stable/))

That last sentence is the most useful fact in the whole sweep. It is a **two-aperture
building**: a big-body opening on the working face and a small-body opening on the back. The
attendant and the animal do not use the same door. That is simultaneously (a) an authentic
construction rule, (b) a legible visual tell, and (c) a complete tactical mechanism — a route
the large body cannot take.

Adjacent cases in the same mode, not separately sourced here: caravanserai camel yards, great
stables, graving docks and dry docks, aircraft hangars, lock chambers.

**What Mode B teaches:** the aperture pair, and with it the whole route-capacity grammar.
This is the mode with the highest gameplay yield per unit of new geometry.

### Mode C — BUILT OUT OF a larger body

The large creature's remains *are* the structure. Documented repeatedly, on two continents,
across ~18,000 years.

- **Mezhyrich, Ukraine (Upper Palaeolithic).** Four huts of ~149 mammoth bones; structures
  12–24 m², roughly circular, 6–10 m across, built from several hundred bones and tusks.
  Recent redating puts the largest at 18,248–17,764 years BP, and small-mammal evidence
  suggests one structure was in use for up to 429 years — a permanent building, not a bivouac.
  ([Sci.News](https://www.sci.news/archaeology/mammoth-bone-structures-ukraine-14448.html) ·
  [Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeologymag.com/2025/11/mammoth-bone-shelters-in-ukraine/) ·
  [PMC radiocarbon paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12639289/))
- **Whale Bone Alley, Yttygran Island, Chukotka (14th c.).** Bowhead skulls and jawbones set
  vertically in two parallel lines running 550 m along the shore, each bone ~300 kg, buried
  deep and packed with stone, grouped in pairs and fours; meat pits behind. Sanctuary or
  butchering station — the debate is live, and both readings are useful to us.
  ([Atlas Obscura](https://www.atlasobscura.com/places/whale-bone-alley) ·
  [ScienceAlert](https://www.sciencealert.com/welcome-to-whale-bone-alley-siberia-s-eerie-answer-to-stonehenge) ·
  [Arctic Russia](https://tourism.arctic-russia.ru/en/sights/whale-bone-alley/))
- **Whale-jaw arches** as standing gateways across the North Atlantic whaling world
  (see `images/MS-07`).

**What Mode C teaches:** anatomy is a construction grammar. A ribcage is a bay system with
slotted cover between the ribs. A jaw is an arch. A skull is a chamber with two eye-socket
openings. The Yttygran evidence is that this gets used as a **repeated module**, which means
it is a kit, not a set-piece.

### Mode D — BUILT AROUND a larger body or presence

A colossal body — living, dead, petrified, or carved — sits in place, and a small-scale
apparatus of routes, cells, scaffolds, and economies grows on and around it.

- **Bamiyan, Afghanistan (3rd–5th c. onward).** Two niches at 38 m and 55 m, with the
  intervening cliff "honeycombed with cells, sanctuaries and passages" — around 1,000
  man-made caves.
  ([UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/208/) ·
  [Sacred Land](https://sacredland.org/bamiyan-valley-afghanistan/))
- **Leshan.** A switchback stair with handrails pinned down the cliff beside the colossal
  seated Buddha, threading past rock-cut niches (`images/MS-05`).
- **Ship-breaking at Chittagong / Alang.** ~200,000 workers dismantling hull-scale masses by
  hand; steel plates of a thousand pounds moved on improvised rollers; 123 deaths in
  thirteen years at Chittagong alone. A living economy inside a carcass.
  ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chittagong_Ship_Breaking_Yard) ·
  [National Geographic](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/magazine/article/The-Ship-Breakers) ·
  [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/features/the-toxic-tide-of-ship-breaking/4015158.article))

**What Mode D teaches:** the colossus is *terrain*, and the human apparatus is *parasitic*
on it — pinned, bolted, cut into. The route never pretends the colossus was designed to be
climbed. That is why Mode D reads as awe rather than as a playground.

## Hoard terrain — the one thing the modes don't cover

"Dragon hoard-hall" implies a floor made of treasure. No architectural precedent covers that,
so the sweep went to granular-mass engineering instead, which covers it exactly.

- **Grain engulfment.** In flowing grain an adult sinks knee-deep in **4 seconds** and can be
  fully engulfed in **20 seconds**. A person buried to the waist needs their own body weight
  plus ~600 lb of force to extract; fully buried can exceed 2,000 lb. Movement on a steep
  surface can itself trigger an avalanche, and the governing property is named explicitly:
  the **angle of repose**.
  ([Great Plains Center for Agricultural Health](https://gpcah.public-health.uiowa.edu/grain-engulfment-and-entrapment/) ·
  [GPCAH factsheet PDF](https://gpcah.public-health.uiowa.edu/resources/pdf/GPCAH_grain_engulfment_2014.pdf) ·
  [U. Arkansas FSA-1010](https://www.uaex.uada.edu/publications/pdf/FSA-1010.pdf) ·
  [UMN Extension](https://extension.umn.edu/farm-safety/grain-bin-safety))

That gives hoard terrain a real mechanical identity: not a decorated floor, but a mass with
a repose angle, a stable band, an unstable band, and an engulfment failure mode on a clock
measured in seconds. See `lane-4-engine-evidence.md` for how that lands on the 5-ft / 2.5-ft
Genesis quantum.

## Game comparanda — documentation only, no images

Deliberately thin, and cited rather than shown, because pulling screenshots into a tracked
repo directory would break the catalog's FFT boundary by construction.

The recurring pattern in the genre — colossus-as-terrain, mount-and-climb traversal, weak-point
objectives on a body — is Mode D with a combat verb attached. It is *not* a fifth mode and it
is not what the census data asks for (see Lane 4). Recorded here so that nobody re-derives
the observation and mistakes it for a new requirement.

## What the sweep rules out

Three things Site 11 is **not**, each argued from evidence rather than taste:

1. **Not "a big map."** Every case above works at ordinary tactical footprint. Tiryns's
   gallery is one cell wide. Bragar's arch is a single bone. The ontology contract already
   says map size decides how much is materialized, not whether a relationship is true, and
   the built record agrees.
2. **Not "a room with a dragon in it."** Remove the dragon from a cave and it is a cave —
   which is Site 7's own boundary note. Site 11 only exists where a *second, differently
   sized* body has a simultaneous consequential relationship to the same space.
3. **Not the anomalous/impossible.** Every case above obeys gravity, euclidean geometry, and
   ordinary material behaviour. Bigness is not weirdness. This is the argument that resolves
   the Megastructure boundary — see `synthesis.md`.
