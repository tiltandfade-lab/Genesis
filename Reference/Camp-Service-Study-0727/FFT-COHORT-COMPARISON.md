# FFT cohort comparison — the service-economy angle

## 0. Starting point: the prior finding stands

The 2026-07-24 Camp Study's lane 4 (`../Camp-Study/lane-4-fft-cohort.md`) read 46 map ids at
view 0 plus 11 additional isometric views — 57 images total, systematic coverage of every
terrain/settlement family in the corpus — and concluded: **the FFT corpus contains no camps
at all.** This pass did not re-run that sweep. It targeted a narrower, service-economy-
specific question the prior pass did not ask: when FFT *does* build a commerce/service
settlement, what relational grammar does it use, and does that grammar resemble Site 2's
camp capsule or contradict it?

## 1. What was read this pass

Two additional map ids, at view 0 (top-down plan), read specifically for their trade/service
program (`fft.h` map list): **map031 "Dorter Trade City"** and its adjacent **map032 "Slums
in Dorter"**, plus **map042 "Warjilis Trade City"** (the corpus's port-trade equivalent).
`fft.h` was also grepped for every trade/market/city name (Dorter, Warjilis, Zarghidas,
Bervenia, Goland, Lionel) to confirm no additional camp-adjacent entry exists — none does,
reconfirming lane 4's finding independently.

## 2. Reading: the trade-city grammar is a rooted-frontage grammar, not a camp grammar

Both Dorter and Warjilis read the same way: **one paved spine road runs the full length of
the map, and buildings flank it symmetrically on both sides, addressing the road directly
with doors, windows, and stall-adjacent open ground.** Warjilis adds a body of water at one
end (the port), with the same road spine running straight down to meet it. Slums in Dorter
keeps the same "road as spine" logic but replaces the flanking stone/tile buildings with
denser, irregular wooden structures pressed closer to the road, with a visible waterline at
the map's far edge.

This is architecture **commanding** its frontage — buildings claim the road, address it, and
sell to it. It is the exact grammar Site 2's capsule explicitly refuses for an ordinary camp
("the camp's transition-relation is 'beside the route,' never 'astride it,' a deliberate,
named inversion of FFT relational rule 3"). The comparison **confirms that inversion is real
and correctly named**: when FFT wants a commerce settlement, it reaches for exactly the
control-the-frontage grammar Site 2 is defined against, not a variant of Site 2's borrowed-
clearing ring. Site 2's rung-2 waystation — which the working spec explicitly describes as
gaining "outward frontage topology addressing the road" — is the point at which the camp
family starts to resemble this grammar. That resemblance is intentional and already named
("the waystation's frontage topology foreshadows the rooftop/frontage-slice dimension, which
banks properly at Site 10" — catalog, "Structural coverage banked").

## 3. Reading: Slums in Dorter as a comparison case for the guest-family urban expression

Slums in Dorter is the closest thing the corpus offers to Site 2's proposed urban-hosted
"shanty quarter" expression, and the comparison is instructive by *contrast*. Slums in Dorter
still obeys the road-spine grammar (dense, irregular, materially poor buildings still address
the same through-road as the wealthier trade-city map beside it) — it is a **poverty
variant of the frontage program**, not a borrowed-ground camp dropped into a city lot. This
supports treating Zaatari-style hosted urban camps and FFT-style urban slums as genuinely
different real-and-fictional cases: a slum is rooted architecture that is poor; a hosted camp
is borrowed ground that has not (yet) rooted into architecture. The distinction the working
spec draws between the guest-family camp and `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md`'s separate
permanent-poor-quarter entry (§3.4) is corroborated by this comparison, not weakened by it —
and it sharpens the FQ-CS-3 boundary question in `MEASURED-REFERENCES.md` §3: the moment a
hosted camp's rooted rung starts reading like Slums in Dorter (fixed frontage, addressed
streets) rather than like Zaatari's early years (tent rows on a grid with no addressed
street frontage) is a plausible, visually testable version of that exit condition.

## 4. No change to the prior finding

Nothing in this pass contradicts lane 4's "no camps in the corpus" conclusion or its
"clearing grammar" lesson. This pass adds a second, independent confirmation from the
commerce/service angle specifically: the corpus's service settlements are frontage
settlements, which is further evidence that Site 2's borrowed-clearing/ring topology has no
FFT precedent to lean on and must be proved on its own terms — consistent with the catalog's
existing acknowledgment that Site 2 is "the first golden site with no FFT composition to
learn from."
