*p. 159 MM 2024*

Griffons are ferocious avian carnivores with the muscular bodies of lions and the head, forelegs, and wings of eagles. When they attack, griffons are as swift and deadly as eagles, even as they strike with a lion's savage might and grace. They hunt in small prides, flying high over plains and forests near their rocky clifftop aeries. Herd animals and horses are the prey they crave above all others — though they will hunt and kill hippogriffs that compete for territory.

Those riding or herding horses dread the griffon's piercing cry, which alerts its pride mates to descend. A griffon ignores a horse's rider when possible; a herder who releases one or two horses can escape while the griffon takes its chosen prey. Riders who protect their horses attract the full fury of the pride.

Griffons are aggressively territorial, engaging in brutal aerial combat to defend their clifftop aeries, tearing the wings off flying intruders to send them spiraling to the ground. A griffon raised from an egg can be trained as a mount, but the process is expensive, time-consuming, and genuinely dangerous. A trained griffon is fiercely loyal to one master, fighting to the death to protect that rider — while retaining its ravening appetite for horseflesh, which a wise master manages carefully.

### Griffon

_Large Monstrosity, Unaligned_

- **Armor Class:** 12
- **Hit Points:** 59 (7d10 + 21)
- **Speed:** 30 ft., fly 80 ft.
- **STR:** 18 (+4) | **DEX:** 15 (+2) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 2 (-4) | **WIS:** 13 (+1) | **CHA:** 8 (-1)
- **Skills:** Perception +5
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft.; Passive Perception 15
- **Languages:** None
- **Challenge:** 2 (450 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The griffon makes two Rend attacks.
- **Rend:** _Melee Attack Roll:_ +6, reach 5 ft. _Hit:_ 8 (1d8 + 4) Piercing damage. If the target is a Medium or smaller creature, it has the Grappled condition (escape DC 14) from both of the griffon's front claws.

### d10 The Aerie Situation (Encounter Context & Terrain)

A griffon encounter is almost always defined by verticality. They live at height, hunt from height, and fight at height. Roll to establish the physical and narrative context before the party arrives.

| **2** | **The Horse Eater** | A merchant caravan has lost two horses from the lead wagon in the last hour — panicked screaming from above, then silence. The griffon is circling at altitude, watching the remaining horses. It has not attacked the humans yet. |

### d10 The Griffon's Behavior (Tone & Variation Table)

Griffons are not simply flying lions. Their eagle instincts can make them unpredictable, occasionally cooperative, and rarely pitiable. Roll for the specific behavioral note that defines this encounter's tone.

| d10 | Behavioral Note | How It Changes the Scene |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **1** | **Focused on the Horse** | The griffon has identified a specific PC's horse and is entirely fixated on it. It will accept significant injury without retaliating against any PC who isn't actively protecting that animal. |
| **2** | **Grieving** | The griffon's pride mate was recently killed. It is hunting erratically, striking at targets far below its usual prey class, driven by something close to grief-rage. DC 15 Insight to recognize the behavior isn't predatory. |
| **3** | **The Pride Call** | At the start of round 2, this griffon screams — a piercing cry audible for miles. 1d4 rounds later, a second griffon arrives from the aerie. The party has a very narrow window to end the fight before reinforcements land. |
| **4** | **Playing with the Kill** | The griffon has already paralyzed a small animal and is performing ritual prey-play behavior, tossing it and catching it mid-air. It is distracted. The party has one round of movement before it notices them. |
| **5** | **Starving** | This griffon's territory has been depleted — probably by overhunting or a magical blight on the local livestock. It is dangerously thin, clearly ill, and attacking something it would normally ignore. DC 13 Nature to recognize this as starvation behavior. |
| **6** | **Bonded to a Child** | A village child made contact with a griffon chick months ago and has been sneaking it food. The adult griffon now tolerates the child's presence — and has transferred some of that tolerance to whoever the child is with when the party meets them. |
| **7** | **The Corrupted** | The griffon's aerie is near a Shadowfell rift or a leyline disturbance. Its feathers have turned patchy and black around the edges. Its behavior is wrong — attacking prey it can't eat, screaming at nothing. DC 14 Nature: this is environmental corruption, not natural aggression. |
| **8** | **The Peaceable Encounter** | The griffon is at rest on a sunny cliff face, clearly fed and unbothered. It watches the party with bright eagle eyes but does not attack unless provoked. A DC 12 Animal Handling check establishes neutral ground. A 16+ result and it permits close approach. |
| **9** | **Hoard Instinct** | This unusual griffon has developed something resembling a hoarding impulse — its nest contains a significant collection of shiny objects stripped from past kills, well beyond what a normal nest would hold. |
| **10** | **The Last of Its Pride** | The other griffons in this territory were killed — by a dragon, a giant, or adventurers. This is the last one. It is not reckless; it is methodical, calculating, and old. It studies the party for a full round before attacking. |
