# Mythic Sandbox Engine – Design Constitution (Early Stage)

> Core Play Promise:
> A procedural mythic sandbox where the world reacts honestly to dice,
> while preserving improvisational authority and 5e mechanical stability.

---

## I. Sacred vs Mutable Rules

### Sacred (Do Not Modify – Load-Bearing 5e Systems)
- Action / Bonus Action / Reaction economy
- Advantage & Disadvantage rules (do not stack)
- Concentration rules
- Spell slot structure
- Short Rest / Long Rest recovery
- Monster stat blocks used as written (reskin allowed only)

These systems keep the engine stable.

---

### Mutable (Safe to Experiment)
- Tarot flavor and triggers
- Procedural tables (weighted, DM veto allowed)
- Loot flavor and weirdness curve
- Critical Magnitude System (2nd roll on 1 or 20)

---

### Forbidden (Until Experienced)
- Custom monster math / CR redesign
- New bookkeeping-heavy subsystems
- New combat rules players must learn
- Hardcore survival meters

---

## II. Critical Magnitude System (Stability Clause)

On a natural 1 or natural 20:
- Roll a second d20 to determine magnitude.

Rules:
- A 1 followed by 1 = Legendary failure (world-marking consequence)
- A 20 followed by 20 = Divine-level intervention (mythic but contained)

Mythic Permission Rule:
Magnitude may rewrite reality only if:
1. It does not invalidate prior player agency.
2. It does not create unsolvable logistics today.
3. It becomes new pressure or opportunity — not a campaign derailment.

---

## III. Combat Design – Beginner Framework

When designing an encounter:

1. Define the Fight Type (one line):
   - Ambush
   - Hold the line
   - Escape
   - Protect
   - Interrupt
   - Capture
   - Survive X rounds

2. Use Monster Manual stat blocks as written (reskin only).

3. Add:
   - 1 Terrain Feature
   - 1 Complication

4. Check Action Economy:
   - Avoid 4 PCs vs 1 enemy without support.
   - Use minions, environment, or positioning to balance turns.

---

## IV. Failure Philosophy

Failure must transform the situation.

Allowed failure outcomes:
- Resource loss
- Position loss
- Time advancement in fiction
- Faction reaction
- Partial truths
- Reputation scars
- Success with consequence

Not allowed:
- “Nothing happens.”
- “Try again.”

---

## V. Pressure Without Timers

No arbitrary countdown mechanics.

Pressure enters through:
- Noise attracting attention
- Faction progression
- Social complications
- Environmental change
- World reacting to player action

Pressure is consequence, not a stopwatch.

---

## VI. Table Discipline

Tables are:

1. Spark Tables – flavor only (always safe)
2. Fork Tables – branching direction
3. Commitment Tables – major consequences (DM-gated)

Rules:
- Tables suggest.
- Improvisation overrides.
- Scripted design always overrides procedural chaos.

---

## VII. Escalation Curve

Mythic escalation frequency target:

- 90% Local
- 9% Regional
- 0.9% Planar
- 0.09% Cosmic

Tier 4 (cosmic breakage) requires DM readiness.

---

## VIII. Scope Control

MVP Includes:
- Dungeon Procedure
- Town Procedure
- Travel Procedure
- Treasure Procedure
- Pressure Procedure

Everything else = Backlog.

No feature expansion until the engine runs cleanly in live sessions.

---

## IX. Design Authority Hierarchy

1. Improvisation (Top)
2. Scripted Design
3. Dice
4. Tables

The world reacts honestly to dice — but the DM curates chaos.

---

## X. Long-Term Vision (Not MVP)

Future scalability goals:
- AI integration
- Procedural map generation
- Multimedia generation
- Modular engine architecture

These remain future-facing and must not interfere with MVP stability.

---
