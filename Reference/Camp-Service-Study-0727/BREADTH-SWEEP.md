# Breadth sweep — what else could "camp / service" be?

Guidelines step 0.5 requires asking, before treating anything as new scope, whether it is a
**host program**, a **cross-host transform**, a **scale/relationship stress case**, a
**substrate case**, or an **ordinary venue fixture** — and the noun/state distinction's hard
tell: *ask what persists when the state ends.*

Six real-world service-economy systems were checked against that test. None of them argues
for a new numbered site. All six argue that Site 2's own internal structure (rungs, rollers,
occupancy roster) is slightly under-specified in named ways.

## 1. Roman `cursus publicus` — mansio and mutatio

The Roman state's road network staffed two station types: **mutationes**, small unstaffed-
beyond-a-groom relay points spaced roughly 8–10 km apart for changing horses only, and
**mansiones**, staffed overnight stations with barracks, stables, and food, spaced roughly
40 km apart ([UNRV Roman History](https://www.unrv.com/government/cursus-publicus.php);
[Birdyslade, "Life Along Roman Roads"](https://www.birdyslade.com/everything-about-roman-roads/life-along-roman-roads)).

**Test:** what persists when the state's road service ends? The buildings — a mansio's ruins
are still a building footprint; a mutatio's are a bare road-relay. **Verdict:** already inside
Site 2's own two-tier ladder (rung 1 ≈ mutatio, rung 2 ≈ mansio — see `MEASURED-REFERENCES.md`
§1). Not a new host. This is evidence *for* the existing ladder's shape, not a breadth
candidate against it.

## 2. Caravanserai network (Silk Road)

Fortified lodging compounds spaced roughly 30–40 km apart — a day's caravan journey —
combining lodging, stabling, secured storage, and often a small internal market
([Algor Cards, "Caravanserais and the Silk Road"](https://cards.algoreducation.com/en/content/5INBPoxv/caravanserais-silk-road-history);
[Silk Road Institute](https://silk-road.com/2025/04/07/the-role-of-caravanserais-in-the-silk-road-economy/)).

**Test:** persists as a building. **Verdict:** this is rung 2 (waystation) at its most
developed and most fortified expression — already named in the spec's "Large — customary or
organized camp" band and licensed-controlled-entrance rule. Not a new host; independent
confirmation that a rooted route-service stop tends to land near "one day's travel" spacing,
which is a route/world-generation fact worth carrying forward (see `synthesis.md` §3), not a
Site 2 structural change.

## 3. Pony Express / stagecoach relay vs. home station

Relay ("swing") stations were minimal — a keeper, a stock tender, three or four horses, no
lodging — spaced roughly 10–25 km apart. Home stations were larger, spaced roughly 100–160 km
apart, and provided lodging, food, and spare stock
([Pony Express Ride, "Relay Stations and Home Stations"](https://ponyexpressride.com/quick-facts-3/relay-stations-and-home-stations/);
[Legends of America](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/we-ponyexpressstations/)).

**Test:** persists as a building; both Hollenberg and Ruby Valley home stations survive today
as protected historic structures (`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-02, CS-03). **Verdict:** a third,
independent confirmation of the same two-tier shape. Not a new host.

## 4. Military camp followers — sutlers and vivandières

Licensed civilian merchants attached to an army camp, selling goods and services the
military did not supply — food, drink, laundry, nursing, sundries
([Wikipedia, "Camp follower"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_follower);
[Grokipedia, "Vivandière"](https://grokipedia.com/page/Vivandi%C3%A8re)).

**Test:** persists as *nothing* — the sutler leaves when the army leaves; no structure, no
service. **Verdict:** correctly a Site 2 **occupant/role**, not a host or even a rung. This is
a genuine, currently-unnamed gap in the occupancy roster (see `synthesis.md` §2) — the working
spec's "Occupancy states and hooks" section names *who controls the camp* (military, bandit,
traveler, empty) but has no entry for *who trades inside it regardless of who controls it*.
Proposed as a small addition, not a new site.

## 5. Fur-trade rendezvous (Rocky Mountain, 1825–1840)

An annual, non-fixed-location trading fair: trappers, merchant caravans from St. Louis, and
several hundred to several thousand Indigenous traders met for weeks of trade, entertainment,
and provisioning, then dispersed completely
([History.com](https://www.history.com/articles/rocky-mountain-rendezvous-fur-trade-wild-west-1800s);
[WyoHistory.org](https://www.wyohistory.org/encyclopedia/road-rendezvous-rocky-mountain-fur-trade-1834)).

**Test:** persists as *nothing* — by design, a "movable trade-fair," chosen specifically
*because* it required no permanent trading post. **Verdict:** this is the single strongest
real-world case for a **named occupancy variant** ("trade rendezvous / fair") sitting inside
the existing "Large — customary or organized camp" expression band, at Rung 0–1 only (a
rendezvous never rooted — that was the entire commercial logic of holding it in the
wilderness). It is not evidence for a new host; it is evidence that the occupancy roster is
missing a genuinely large-scale, temporary, trade-dominant case distinct from "caravan
waystation" (which the catalog already treats as rooted/service-oriented) and distinct from
military/bandit. Proposed addition, not a new site — see `synthesis.md` §2.

## 6. Refugee-camp informal economies (Zaatari, Jordan)

A UNHCR-administered camp on Jordanian state land that grew an estimated $12.7M/month
informal shop economy along an unplanned market street nicknamed "Sham Elysées"
([Refugees International](https://www.refugeesinternational.org/the-difference-a-market-makes-the-case-of-syrian-refugee-camps-in-jordan/);
[Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/global-markets/profit-loss-champs-elysees-syrian-refugee-camp/)).

**Test:** this is the hardest case, and it is genuinely ambiguous over a ten-year horizon. In
its first years it persisted as tents and traces (Site 2's guest-family case). By its tenth
year it reads, by several international accounts, as one of Jordan's largest population
centers — closer to permanent urban fabric than a camp
([UNHCR, "10 facts at 10 years"](https://www.unhcr.org/us/news/stories/jordans-zaatari-refugee-camp-10-facts-10-years)).
**Verdict:** not a new host by itself — but it is the clearest live evidence that the boundary
`SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` §3.4 already names ("a permanent poor-quarter/warren...
distinct from Site 2's *temporary* camp-on-borrowed-ground... this gap is real and unclaimed
by any of the twelve") is not a hypothetical edge case. It is the empirically normal long-run
trajectory for a large, long-duration hosted camp. This is direct evidence bearing on the
guest-family property's caps — see `synthesis.md` §1 and the working spec's founder-packet
FQ-CS-3.

## What this sweep does NOT argue for

No candidate above earns bin (a), a new host program, under the noun/state test. All six
either confirm Site 2's existing two-tier ladder shape (1, 2, 3), belong inside Site 2 as an
occupant or occupancy variant (4, 5), or sharpen — without creating — a boundary already
named as open work in `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` (6). This sweep changes no invariant,
capsule, or ruled decision in the working spec; it proposes two small additions (an occupant
role and an occupancy variant) and evidences the founder packet's three questions.
