| Die | Segment Type | Description | Transition (d4) |
|:---:|:-------------|:------------|:-----------------|
| 1 | The War Council | A faction convenes in secret to plan conflict; the party is invited to observe, advise, or choose a side before the decision hardens. | 1: The party's input tips the decision toward violence or restraint; they're now committed to that path. 2: A council member is revealed to be a spy; chaos erupts and factions realign unexpectedly. 3: The council votes to test the party's loyalty through a dangerous mission before accepting them. 4: The council disbands without consensus; rival members depart in different directions, and the party must choose which faction to follow. |
| 2 | The Merchant Guild Assembly | Guild leaders debate resource allocation, territory disputes, and trade policy; the party's presence matters to at least one faction. | 1: The guild votes to restrict or grant the party trading privileges; this determines their access to safe houses and supplies. 2: A faction uses the party as leverage against rivals, forcing them to choose between competing interests. 3: The assembly dissolves into argument; the party can mediate (gaining favor) or exploit the chaos. 4: A secret faction within the guild is revealed; the party is recruited or marked as a threat. |
| 3 | The Neutral Tavern Meeting | Two rival faction leaders coincidentally drink in the same tavern; the party witnesses the tense negotiation or becomes a forced intermediary. | 1: Neither leader wants violence here; the party can eavesdrop or negotiate freely without fear of bloodshed. 2: A third party arrives, upsetting the balance; tension erupts and sides are demanded. 3: One leader bets or wagers the party's services in a game of chance; the loser pays with the party's debt. 4: The meeting concludes with both leaders eyeing the party as potential allies—whichever they court must decide how to respond. |
| 4 | The Temple Sanctuary Demand | The priesthood shelters the party but demands a favor or service in exchange; refusing sanctuary means losing a crucial safe location. | 1: The favor involves removing a rival from the temple's territory or sabotaging their worship. 2: The priesthood reveals the party is suspected by city authorities; the party must help hide evidence or a person. 3: A faction attempts to raid the temple to reach the party; the priesthood's response determines future relations. 4: The favor is complete, but the priesthood now owes the party a debt they'll collect at a critical moment. |
| 5 | The Dockside Confrontation | Smugglers and city officials face off with goods and contraband in plain sight; the party stands caught between both sides and must choose. | 1: The party is mistaken for officials or criminals; proving their neutrality or choosing a side changes everything. 2: A deal between officials and smugglers is exposed; both sides now have reason to eliminate the party as witnesses. 3: One faction offers the party illicit goods or contraband if they'll help them escape; accepting means burning another faction. 4: The confrontation resolves with the party's next destination claimed by the victors; they'll demand payment for safe passage. |
| 6 | The Noble's Dinner Party | A high-born host entertains faction leaders and rivals at the same table; alliances shift with every course and the party observes or participates. | 1: The host poisons a guest; the party can expose the killer or help them escape, gaining enmity or alliance. 2: An assassination attempt is made at the table; the party's intervention determines who survives. 3: A major secret is revealed during dinner; the party is sworn to silence or marked as knowing too much. 4: The host offers the party a private audience after dinner where the real negotiation begins. |
| 7 | The Fight Pit Test | A faction stages combat spectacles and tests the party's loyalty or worth through forced participation and gambling. | 1: The party is forced to fight in ritual combat; winning earns respect, losing earns debt. 2: A party member is targeted by an assassin disguised as an opponent; the faction either helped or orchestrated it. 3: The party is offered a side bet by a rival faction; winning means turning coat, losing means owing a debt. 4: After the fight, the faction leader privately offers the party a lucrative contract that requires betraying a previous ally. |
| 8 | The Back-Alley Negotiation | Two factions meet in secret to broker an exchange; the party is invited to broker the deal or arbitrate it, with something to gain from both sides. | 1: Both sides offer the party payment; accepting one's money means being hunted by the other. 2: The negotiation is a trap; one faction plans to rob or kill the other, and the party can warn them, help, or profit. 3: Neither side trusts the other; the party's reputation becomes the only reason either side shows up. 4: The negotiation concludes with the party hired as a neutral go-between for future dealings. |
| 9 | The Public Trial | A suspect is tried before a city official, and the party's testimony tips the verdict toward guilt or innocence; both factions watch the result closely. | 1: The party recognizes the accused as an innocent pawn; lying saves a friend but betrays justice. 2: The verdict creates a victim faction and a victor faction; the party is marked as allied with whichever they testified for. 3: The trial is rigged; the party can expose the corruption, attracting dangerous scrutiny. 4: After the verdict, the losing faction blames the party and marks them for retaliation. |
| 10 | The Safehouse Betrayal | The party is sheltered in a faction's secret location and asked to betray someone they've previously helped or allied with. | 1: The party refuses; the faction turns hostile and marks them as untrustworthy. 2: The party agrees but warns their former ally; both factions discover the double-dealing, and war erupts. 3: The party betrays as asked; the faction's trust deepens, but the betrayed party member swears vengeance. 4: The betrayal target is not who the party thought; the party has unknowingly destroyed a faction rival, earning immense favor. |
| 11 | The Sealed Crossroads Market | Rival factions meet in a sealed marketplace at midnight to negotiate trade, exchange prisoners, or settle debts; the party observes the ritual exchange. | 1: A third faction interrupts the meeting; deals collapse and violence spills into the sealed space. 2: The party is asked to serve as neutral witnesses; afterward, both factions know the party can be trusted. 3: One faction attempts to steal from the exchange; the party is blamed as accomplices and must choose who to betray. 4: The exchange reveals something shocking about one faction's activities; the party must decide what to do with the knowledge. |
| 12 | The Secret Summit | Three faction representatives meet in a hidden chamber to broker a massive treaty, alliance, or conspiracy; the party discovers or is invited to witness it. | 1: The summit dissolves as a fourth party interrupts; the three factions turn their attention to eliminating the witnesses. 2: The party learns of a conspiracy that threatens the entire city; they can expose it or profit from it. 3: One faction offers the party a position within their inner circle in exchange for sabotaging the summit. 4: The summit concludes successfully, and all three factions now consider the party as potential allies—or mutual threats that must be monitored. |
| 13 | The Proxy War Skirmish | Two factions deploy hired mercenaries in a contested district; the party is caught in the crossfire and both sides try to recruit them mid-battle as the violence escalates. | 1: The party picks a side and turns the tide; the losing faction marks them permanently. 2: The party brokers a ceasefire, earning temporary trust from both factions but satisfying neither. 3: A third faction enters the fight opportunistically, forcing everyone to reconsider alliances. 4: The skirmish reveals that both factions were manipulated into fighting by a hidden power. |
| 14 | The Debt Auction | A faction publicly auctions the debts, favors, and obligations owed to them; the party discovers their own name on the ledger or finds leverage over someone they need. | 1: The party buys their own debt back at a steep price, but gains freedom from one faction's hold. 2: A rival faction purchases a debt the party owes, gaining new leverage over them. 3: The party buys someone else's debt and gains an unwilling servant or reluctant ally. 4: The auction is disrupted by the debtor faction, and the records are scattered—some recovered, some lost forever. |
| 15 | The Faction Ghost Council | Dead faction leaders manifest through a medium or ritual, issuing commands and settling old scores from beyond the grave; the living obey out of fear or reverence. | 1: A ghost reveals a buried secret that reshapes the faction's current leadership. 2: The spirits demand the party fulfill an oath made by someone long dead—refusal risks a curse. 3: Two dead leaders disagree violently; their spectral conflict threatens to tear the faction apart. 4: The medium is a fraud, but the faction believes; the party can expose the deception or exploit it. |
| 16 | The Blood Oath Ceremony | A faction performs a binding ritual where members swear fealty through blood magic; the party is invited or compelled to participate, and the oath carries genuine supernatural weight. | 1: The party swears and is bound; breaking the oath later carries a curse or geas. 2: One party member's blood reacts strangely to the ritual, revealing hidden lineage or connection. 3: The ritual is interrupted by enemies; the incomplete oath creates unpredictable magical consequences. 4: The party refuses; the faction interprets this as a declaration of war. |
| 17 | The Shapeshifter's Infiltration | A faction discovers that one of their own leaders has been replaced by a shapeshifter, doppelganger, or magical duplicate; paranoia explodes and the party is asked to identify the imposter. | 1: The imposter is someone the party trusted; exposing them destroys a valuable relationship. 2: Multiple members are replaced; the real faction is smaller than anyone realized. 3: The imposter offers the party a better deal than the real leader ever did. 4: The original leader is alive but imprisoned; rescuing them means entering enemy territory. |
| 18 | The Faction Annihilation | One faction is being systematically destroyed—assassinations, arson, sabotage—and the survivors beg the party for protection or vengeance as their world collapses around them. | 1: Protecting the survivors means declaring war on whoever is responsible. 2: The destruction is self-inflicted; a schism within the faction has turned genocidal. 3: The party discovers they were unknowingly responsible for triggering the annihilation. 4: The last survivor offers the party everything the faction owned in exchange for a single act of terrible revenge. |
| 19 | The Convergence Ultimatum | Every major faction in the city simultaneously delivers an ultimatum to the party: choose one faction's protection or be declared an enemy by all; the deadline is dawn. | 1: The party accepts one faction's offer and becomes permanently marked by the others. 2: The party attempts to play factions against each other before the deadline, risking total exposure. 3: A previously unknown faction offers an alternative—but their price is stranger than allegiance. 4: The ultimatum was coordinated; all factions are testing whether the party will fracture under pressure. |
| 20 | The Throne of Ashes | Every faction leader in the city is dead, missing, or deposed in a single night; the power vacuum is absolute, and every surviving lieutenant, heir, and pretender turns to the party as the only neutral force capable of crowning or destroying what comes next. | 1: The party installs a puppet leader and becomes the true power behind the throne. 2: The party discovers the purge was orchestrated by an entity that now demands their service. 3: The party refuses to choose; the city descends into open civil war with the party at its center. 4: The party learns that the purge was a sacrifice—something ancient has been summoned, and faction politics no longer matter. |
