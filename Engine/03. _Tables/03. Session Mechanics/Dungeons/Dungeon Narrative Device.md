| d36 | Device | Core Situation | Player Misread | DM Leverage |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Unwilling Accomplice | This NPC is coerced — their genuine self opposes what they're doing here | Players assume a willing enemy | The coercer is the real target; exposing or removing the coercion turns this NPC into an ally or informant |
| 2 | Misguided Savior | Causing real harm while genuinely trying to help; believes their method is the only option | Players see malice where there is only desperation | Showing them a viable alternative breaks their commitment to this path — they wanted a way out |
| 3 | Hidden Patron | Operating under instructions from someone not yet encountered; loyalty to the patron is the real conflict | Players assume this NPC is the source of the problem | The patron is reachable; this NPC is a thread. What they're willing to say depends on how much they trust the party |
| 4 | Social Leverage | Controls or withholds something the party needs — or the reverse; the party holds something they need | Players assume combat is the only language here | The thing being leveraged defines what they'll trade for and what they will never agree to |
| 5 | Misidentified Villain | The apparent threat is protecting something or someone behind them | Players see hostility and call it aggression | Revealing what they're protecting reframes the entire encounter — and the protected thing is the real issue |
| 6 | Conditional Ally | Will cooperate only if the party resolves a specific prior problem first | Players see obstruction | The condition they name reveals something about their situation — and likely opens a second thread |
| 7 | The Witness | Saw something critical; their testimony is the real prize, not any item or power they hold | Players assume this NPC has leverage, enemies, or weapons | Getting them to talk — or getting them out alive — is the actual objective |
| 8 | The Debt | Owes something to someone in the dungeon, or to a faction the party is connected to; honor and survival pull in opposite directions | Players see a neutral or hostile NPC with unclear motivation | Invoking the right name or faction shifts their stance — the debt is the lever |
| 9 | Last Loyalist | Everyone else abandoned or betrayed the cause; they stayed; their loyalty has become their tragedy | Players see fanaticism | They know they've lost — they're waiting for someone to either finish it or give them permission to stop |
| 10 | Reluctant Exposer | Holds a secret that will hurt someone they care about if revealed; telling the party costs them something real | Players assume self-interest is the motive for silence | The actual cost of the secret is the leverage — and the party must decide whether they'll make someone else pay it |
| 11 | Fallen Guardian | Was once the protector of this place; corruption, grief, or desperation turned them | Players see a monster or a clean villain | Their history with this location is still visible in the room; finding evidence of it changes the fight |
| 12 | The Decoy | This encounter is bait or misdirection; the real threat or objective is elsewhere | Players assume this is the primary obstacle | Recognizing the misdirection mid-encounter opens a decision: finish this or break off and pursue the real target |
| 13 | Infected Will | Dominated, cursed, or possessed; the true self is different from what the party observes; lucid moments are possible | Players assume the behavior is intentional and the entity is fully in control | Lucid windows are the lever — the question is whether the party notices them and what they choose to do |
| 14 | The Architect's Regret | Built or created the thing that became the problem; still here because they feel responsible | Players assume they're complicit in an ongoing scheme | They want it stopped as much as anyone — they just don't believe they deserve to benefit from its ending |
| 15 | Proxy Conflict | Fighting the party because of a prior conflict with someone the party is associated with; misdirected aggression | Players assume this is about them specifically | Once the original source of the conflict is named, the logic of the encounter shifts entirely |
| 16 | The Offer | Will trade something genuinely valuable for something specific; the offer is real, but the motivation behind making it is dangerous | Players assume the offer is a trick or trap | The offer IS real — understanding why they need the thing they're asking for is the actual problem |
| 17 | Inevitable Recursion | Has failed at this exact moment before; this is a loop, a compulsion, a repeating pattern they're aware of | Players see a capable enemy or a competent obstacle | They're not trying to win — they're trying to break the cycle; the party may be the thing that finally does it |
| 18 | The Protector | Guarding something or someone the party hasn't found yet; all hostile behavior is defensive | Players see aggression and assume offensive intent | Finding what they're protecting changes the dynamic immediately — the party can threaten it or secure it |
| 19 | Competing Commission | Hired or tasked to accomplish the exact same goal the party has; they arrived first | Players see an enemy | They're running the same job — negotiate, compete, or absorb; the objective is shared even if the contract isn't |
| 20 | Split Loyalty | Serves two masters whose interests have just come into conflict; the party represents one side, whether they know it or not | Players see a confused or neutral NPC | Naming which master the party is aligned with — or appears to be — determines which way they break |
| 21 | False Identity | Operating under an assumed name, role, or affiliation; their real identity changes what this encounter is actually about | Players assume they know who this is and what they want | The real identity either creates an alliance that wasn't possible a moment ago, or a much larger threat — being recognized forces a choice |
| 22 | The Volunteer | Chose to be here knowing the cost; is not a victim and does not want to be treated as one | Players assume they need rescue, or were placed here against their will | They want someone to understand why they made the choice — that conversation is the scene |
| 23 | The Scapegoat | Blamed or hunted for something they didn't do; their presence in this dungeon is flight, not aggression | Players arrive with information that names this NPC as the villain | The real perpetrator may be the party's own source — or someone still in the dungeon |
| 24 | Doomed Accomplice | Already knows how this ends for them; is using the time left to do something that matters | Players read them as a threat or an obstacle | They know something important and will trade it for help with their final purpose — not their survival |
| 25 | The Examiner | Deliberately presenting a challenge or moral dilemma; their agenda is about how the party behaves, not just what they do | Players assume the obstacle is the objective | Passing or failing the test has consequences — this NPC reports to someone, or their judgment unlocks or bars something |
| 26 | Collateral Architect | Caused the situation through negligence, ignorance, or good intentions gone wrong; no malice, only consequence | Players see guilt and assume intent | They know how to fix it because they know how they broke it — but asking for help means admitting what they did |
| 27 | The Righteous Villain | Their plan is coherent, possibly even correct; the problem is the method, the cost, or who gets hurt | Players see a villain and treat the plan as irrelevant | Engaging with the logic rather than dismissing it is the only route to a non-combat resolution — or to understanding why they can't be stopped |
| 28 | Won't Leave | Lost something here — a person, an object, a purpose — and has decided not to go until they recover it | Players assume this NPC is occupying or guarding the space with intent | Helping them find the thing — or reach acceptance that it's gone — is the fastest path through this encounter |
| 29 | The Turncoat | Has already defected from their original faction; is acting in alignment with the party's interests for reasons they haven't shared | Players assume this is a trap or a manipulation | Their defection is real — but what it cost them, and who is looking for them, is the complication waiting to surface |
| 30 | Hidden Depth | Understands the full situation better than they're letting on; is managing what the party knows and when | Players see a limited or naive NPC | Why they're managing information — protective, manipulative, or oath-bound — determines whether they become an asset or a problem |
| 31 | Moral Mirror | Pursuing the same goal as the party using methods the party has rejected or would reject | Players see an enemy because of the methods | The confrontation is about means, not ends — and this NPC can point out that the party's choices also carry costs |
| 32 | The Echo | A copy, remnant, or echo of someone the party has already encountered — derived, not the original, not an impostor | Players assume they ARE the original, or that this is a deception | They know what the original knew but interpret it differently; their loyalty is to a version of events that may no longer be accurate |
| 33 | The Opening | Wants to be stopped but cannot stop themselves; is leaving deliberate weaknesses or visible clues | Players assume the vulnerabilities are genuine oversights | Recognizing the intentional gaps — and responding to them rather than just exploiting them — changes the encounter from combat to collaboration |
| 34 | Asymmetric Stakes | The party and this NPC want different things, and neither want is wrong; someone has to lose | Players assume conflict means someone is the villain | Acknowledging both sides opens negotiation — but the thing they each need may be zero-sum, which means negotiation still ends in a real choice |
| 35 | Designated Sacrifice | Has accepted, or been told, they will die here as part of a larger plan; at peace with it; annoyed by rescue attempts | Players assume saving them is the right move | The sacrifice's purpose — and whether disrupting it helps or damages something larger — is the actual question |
| 36 | The Archivist | Has been observing and recording events in this dungeon, possibly for a long time; knows things about the party they shouldn't | Players see a threat — how do they know that? | Their records are the prize; whoever they report to is either a resource or a danger depending on who the party is |
