# Synthesis — Layered Control Study

## 1. The three lanes in one paragraph each

**Lane 1 (breadth).** Eleven structurally different real ways for two persistent claims to share one
place — liberty/sanctuary, extraterritorial enclave, enclosed quarter, parallel court, scheduled
court, commercial layer inside custody, company town, monastic exemption, caravanserai, informal
settlement, capped enclave. Every one decomposes into deltas over an existing host: a threshold, a
schedule, a recognition rule, a fixture with two owners, a route one party controls. **None needed
its own geometry generator, which is direct support for the 2026-07-26 classification.** The
dimensions in the ruling cluster into four groups — recognition, exchange, passage, force — and only
*force* requires the map to change shape.

**Lane 2 (FFT).** 78 of 116 named playable maps (67.2%; 83.0% of built/settlement maps) belong to
one of twenty multi-map host clusters, and the naming grammar is explicitly layered — Dorter Trade
City → Slums in Dorter → Hospital in Slums → Cellar of Sand Mouse. **FFT solves layered control by
splitting the map, which Genesis has ruled out.** But four maps carry the split on one board and
give four free devices: the owned diagonal (grain, not topology), the owned approach, the appended
strip, and void-plus-owned-light. FFT also independently confirms the overflow law: the covert room
is a *precommitted adjacent ruin*, not a basement invented when the plot needs one.

**Lane 3 (engine).** The eligibility predicate is satisfied by **12 of 12** rolled worlds (≥2
factions, mean 2.92, each with agenda + method; 60% of methods are covert or parallel-authority).
The PC arrives inside the layering in **10 of 12** starts. Faction clocks and `turnFactionOutcome`
already advance, splinter, merge, take over and collapse claimants with real mutation — **re-gated
against the compiled corpus**, because the module's own comments and the 2026-07-02 table registry
both wrongly report the driving table as uncompiled (lane 3 §2.1). `w.renown.factions` already
tracks per-faction recognition with a hunted threshold. **Everything semantic is live; everything
spatial is absent.**

## 2. The headline: Site 8 is a projection gap, not a content gap

The marathon recorded zero Site 8 scenes — and simultaneously produced a dead-drop, a stakeout
sightline chosen for one specific door, a scored-and-struck-through tally, a courier chit, a decoy
shutter, and a chalk-mark that decides who gets noticed. The fiction is saturated; the map is empty.

This is the single most useful thing the study found, because it moves the first build. Site 8's
first build is **not** "invent layered-control content." It is "give the already-rolled claimants a
spatial vocabulary" — access profiles, thresholds, schedules, tells — and prove it on three unlike
hosts without a host-specific branch.

## 3. Proposed transform contract (all `PROPOSED`)

### 3.1 The four control clusters and their spatial price

| cluster | dimensions from the ruling | minimum spatial price | rung it satisfies |
|---|---|---|---|
| recognition | authority · recognition · ritual · information | **zero geometry**; one decal or one prop | LC-0 / LC-1 |
| exchange | service · property · custody · taxation · labour | **one fixture with two owners** | LC-2 |
| passage | access · time · protection | **one threshold + one schedule** | LC-2 / LC-3 |
| force | violence | fronts, patrols, barriers | LC-5 |

Rationale: lane 1 §2.1. Consequence: the roller's rung weights should favour LC-0/LC-1/LC-2, not
LC-3+. A tactical-map project's natural instinct is the opposite, and it would be wrong.

### 3.2 The grain law (proposed, from lane 2 §3.1)

> When a claimant's zone is materialised inside a host, its **mass grain** shifts relative to the
> host's, and the direction of the shift is a function of the claimant's *formality*, not of depth:
> an informal claimant refines and disorders the grain (more, smaller masses; more doors; irregular
> surface); an institutional claimant coarsens and regularises it (fewer, larger masses; repeated
> bays; dressed surface).

This gives the transform a visual instrument that costs no new topology: LC-3 is generated by
**re-partitioning** an existing composition.

### 3.3 The four free devices (proposed, from lane 2 §2)

1. **Owned diagonal** — the public spine survives; ownership multiplies on both sides of it.
2. **Owned approach** — the claimant is expressed by standing on the host's existing portal.
3. **Appended strip** — the back room is a one-cell corridor with property in reach of both walls.
4. **Void plus owned light** — the covert route is legible because two lamps have an owner and
   nothing else does.

### 3.4 The envelope law (proposed, from lane 1 §1.3/§1.11 and images `lc2`, `lc11`)

> A confined claim expands to the exact limits of its licensed envelope in all three dimensions and
> then stops: it subdivides, stacks, and compresses floor heights rather than crossing its boundary.

This is the constructional face of the ontology's overflow law and it gives the generator a
countable behaviour rather than a prohibition.

### 3.5 The boundary law (proposed, from image `lc10`)

> The boundary between two control regimes is **inherited** — a slope, a water line, a tree line, a
> road, a wall the host already built — far more often than it is constructed by either claimant.
> A fence drawn around a faction zone is a rejection condition unless a rolled fact built it.

## 4. Three unlike hosts — the proposed proof set

The ontology (§11 item 2 and §12 step 6) requires the transform on at least three unlike hosts with
no host-specific branch. Proposed, in build order:

| # | host | the split | why this host teaches the most | evidence |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hospitality venue** (`VENUE-TAVERN-01`) | proprietor owns the public room and official service; a second claimant owns the back room, deliveries and private introductions | smallest honest window; already the required first Golden Venue fixture; forces LC-2 to work with **zero** map growth | `TAVERN-VENUE-ROUTING-BRIEF.md` §5; FFT MAP096 + MAP034 |
| 2 | **Mine / workshop** (Site 5) | the operator runs extraction; a company/enforcer layer owns pay, supply, credit and who leaves | the control fixture is a **store**, not a room — proves the exchange cluster without a second building; the mine's product/people/water circuits already factorise | lane 1 §1.7; images `lc8`, `lc9`; FFT MAP027/28/39 |
| 3 | **Prison / custody** (Site 6) | the state holds; the keeper sells — rents, a tap, chummage, monopoly supply, a master's side and a common side | proves the host's obligations survive **completely intact** under a second claimant, which is the anti-collapse test; also the hardest custody/property case | lane 1 §1.6; images `lc6`, `lc7` |

**Unlike on the axes that matter:** window size (one room / a whole works / an institution), claimant
legitimacy (criminal / corporate-legal / officeholder-legal), and the dominant control cluster
(passage / exchange / custody). If one delta compiler serves all three with no `if host ===` branch,
the transform is proved.

A fourth, **monastery (Site 4) under exemption**, is proposed as the changed-seed companion because
it is the case where *both* claimants are legitimate — the reading that stops the generator treating
"second claimant" as a synonym for "criminal."

## 5. Site boundary corrections proposed

**5.1 Site 8 must not own Underworks.** The census maps 10.47% of urban arrivals to Site 8 via the
Underworks district. Under the 2026-07-26 ruling a transform never owns a district type. Underworks
is a *host that layered control is unusually likely to be applied to*.

**5.2 Underworks + Infrastructure Hub are one unnamed host.** 10.47% of urban plus 13.83% of dungeon
arrivals, currently split across two census tables with different verdicts. Raised as a
settled-life-program bin-(a) candidate; not edited into the catalog by this lane.

**5.3 Site 3 boundary.** Site 3 (dormant/abandoned) owns *absence of control*. Site 8 owns
*simultaneous* control. A place with one absent claimant and one present one is Site 8 over a Site 3
residue, not a new case.

**5.4 Site 9 boundary.** Site 9 (contested fortress) is a scale/relationship stress case with active
fronts. Site 8 at LC-5 and Site 9 overlap deliberately; the difference is that Site 9's *host* is a
large defence with two populations, whereas Site 8 at LC-5 is any host whose split has gone hot.

## 6. Open questions for the founder

**Q1 — The many-claimant rung.** The ruling's ladder is a scale of how much of *one* split is
materialised. Lane 1's caravanserai case is a different shape: N small claims of equal weight around
one shared centre, none dominant. FFT has no equivalent. Is this a sixth rung, a separate transform,
or a composition of several LC-2s? Presented as a build-order question in the spec, not an A-or-B.

**Q2 — Whether the transform may make the host's public route *worse*.** FFT's Slums in Dorter puts
the only public route on the worst ground on the board. That is excellent gameplay and it is a
mechanical change to the host, not a delta on top of it. Does the transform get to re-rank the
host's routes?

**Q3 — Legitimacy weighting.** Five of eleven real cases have two *legal* claimants. Should the
claimant-legitimacy roll be flat, or should the generator lean toward the criminal reading because
it is more immediately playable?

## 7. Declared gaps — what would move `RESEARCHED` from PARTIAL to PASS

1. **No measured plans** of any layered host (Kontor block, khan, ghetto tenement, company store,
   Marshalsea range). Every dimension in the working spec is derived from standee and route logic.
2. **No many-claimant cohort** in either lane 1 (one case) or lane 2 (zero cases).
3. **No non-European parallel-jurisdiction case** — millet, waqf, paréage identified but not read.
4. **No culture study.** The urban pair is the model and Site 8 has no equivalent; the transform's
   claimant-legitimacy and recognition-ritual cards are currently unsupported by evidence.
5. **No changed-seed test of the grain law.** §3.2 is a hypothesis from two FFT map pairs
   (Dorter 31/32 and Orbonne 56/57) plus one confirmation (Goug 40); it needs either a wider FFT
   sample or a clay test.
6. **No proof that one delta compiler serves three hosts.** That is a build result, not a research
   result, and it is the actual gate on the ontology's acceptance item 2.
