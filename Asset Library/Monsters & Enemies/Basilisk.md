*p. 32 MM 2024*

In a gritty, high-pressure campaign, a basilisk is not just a monster—it is an environmental hazard, a terrifying roadblock, and a walking puzzle. Because it turns its prey to stone, its territory is naturally marked by a gallery of perfectly preserved, horrified victims.

A basilisk has eight legs and a sluggish metabolism, moving with a ponderous gait. Its dull brown skin and yellowish underbelly allow it to blend into the shadows of caves or arid wastelands, while its eyes glow with an eerie pale green light.

Here is the 5e rules-as-written stat block, along with campaign-agnostic hooks and modular tables to run a highly tactical, terrifying basilisk encounter.

### Campaign Integration Hooks

- **The Half-Eaten Relic:** Seasoned explorers know that finding remarkably lifelike stone carvings of wildlife or humanoids—especially those with missing parts bitten off—is an immediate warning that a basilisk is near. The party finds a petrified missing person, but their hand has been chewed off.
- **The Alchemist's Desperation:** A prominent NPC has been petrified. Sages know that the fluids inside a basilisk's gullet can be processed into an oil that returns petrified creatures to flesh. The party must hunt a basilisk not just to kill it, but to carefully harvest its internal organs intact.
- **The Smuggler's Egg:** A basilisk born and raised in captivity can be domesticated as a terrifying guardian beast. A local crime syndicate or corrupt noble has purchased a highly prized basilisk egg on the black market, and it has just hatched in the middle of a crowded urban district.

### Basilisk

_Medium Monstrosity, Unaligned_

- **Armor Class:** 15 (natural armor)
- **Hit Points:** 52 (8d8 + 16)
- **Speed:** 20 ft.
- **STR:** 16 (+3) | **DEX:** 8 (-1) | **CON:** 15 (+2) | **INT:** 2 (-4) | **WIS:** 8 (-1) | **CHA:** 7 (-2)
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 9
- **Languages:** —
- **Challenge:** 3 (700 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Actions**

- **Bite:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 10 (2d6 + 3) piercing damage plus 7 (2d6) poison damage.

**Bonus Actions**

- **Petrifying Gaze (Recharge 4–6):** Each creature in a 30-foot cone must succeed on a DC 12 Constitution saving throw. _First Failure:_ the creature is **restrained** and repeats the save at the end of its next turn, ending the effect on a success. _Second Failure:_ the creature is **petrified** instead of restrained (freed only by _greater restoration_ or comparable magic).

### d6 The Basilisk's Stage (Tactical Terrain)

A fight with a basilisk is fundamentally about sight lines and movement. Because characters will frequently avert their eyes (granting the basilisk advantage to hit them and suffering disadvantage to hit it), terrain that forces movement or limits visibility becomes incredibly dangerous. _(Roll or choose one)._

|d6|Lair Terrain & Hazard|2024 Mechanical Integration & Tactics|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1**|**The Macabre Gallery**|The lair is choked with petrified victims. They provide Half Cover to the basilisk. Any missed attack that strikes a statue shatters a piece off; if the victim is ever cured, any body parts lost in stone form remain permanently absent.|
|**2**|**Highly Reflective Pools**|The cave features shallow pools of completely still, highly reflective water or large deposits of smooth quartz. If the basilisk is maneuvered into bright light near these surfaces, it might accidentally petrify itself.|
|**3**|**Cramped Tunnels**|The passageway is narrow (5 feet wide). Averting your eyes means turning around or pressing into the wall, causing characters to naturally bottleneck and block their allies from entering melee range.|
|**4**|**The Blind Drop**|The basilisk waits at the bottom of a steep, 15-foot drop. Characters who jump down land Prone. If they don't declare they are averting their eyes _before_ dropping, they immediately trigger the gaze save upon landing.|
|**5**|**The Alchemical Vent**|A natural fissure vents thick, obscuring steam. The area is Heavily Obscured. While this protects the PCs from the gaze, it also allows the basilisk to ambush them, as its bite delivers potent poison.|
|**6**|**Rubble & Scree**|The basilisk has chewed up several statues, leaving behind a floor covered in porous, organic stone debris. Moving through the rubble counts as Difficult Terrain, punishing PCs who avert their eyes and try to maneuver blindly.|

### d4 Clues, Caches, & Windfalls (The Investigation Layer)

Because basilisks leave perfect, durable statues behind, their lairs are treasure troves of frozen adventurers. However, looting them presents a moral and logistical dilemma.

|d4|Macabre Discovery|Clue / Campaign Hook|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1**|**The Missing Hand**|The party finds a petrified courier clutching a locked scroll tube. However, the basilisk has bitten the courier's hand off, meaning the stone hand and the scroll tube are currently digesting inside the creature's gullet.|
|**2**|**The Alchemical Oil**|The crushed remains of an alchemist's camp. The party finds a single, sealed vial of basilisk gullet oil. It acts as a one-use _Greater Restoration_ potion that can only cure petrification.|
|**3**|**The Frozen Mercenary**|A heavily armored, petrified warrior holding a clearly magical longsword. The PCs can easily break the stone fingers to take the sword—but doing so condemns the warrior to a crippled life if they are ever cured.|
|**4**|**The Nest**|Hidden in the dark is a clutch of 1d4 heavy, leathery basilisk eggs. They are highly prized by beast tamers and black-market merchants, potentially worth hundreds of gold pieces if transported safely.|
