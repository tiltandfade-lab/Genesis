# Lane 3 — The institutional chassis: what actually changes between institutions

**The ruling under test** (GOLDEN-SITES-CATALOG, Site 4, Adam's verbatim intent): "the type
of institutional architecture that can serve a prison, a school, a hospital, potentially a
hotel." The walled-courtyard grammar is the game's institutional chassis — "who runs it
changes room use, dressing, staffing, and rules, **never the architecture**."

This lane tests the "never the architecture" clause. **It survives, but with a real and
useful correction: the architecture stays, the *circulation* changes.**

Evidence: `l3-mobquad.jpg`, `l3-mobquad-corner.jpg` (collegiate quadrangle) ·
`l3-beaune-cour.jpg`, `l3-beaune-salle.jpg` (courtyard hospital) ·
`l3-easternstate-aerial.jpg` (prison precinct) · `l3-almshouse-quad.jpg` (charity housing) ·
`l3-certosa-chiostro.jpg`, `l3-carthusian-cell-plan.png` (the cell-per-person plan) ·
`l4-prejmer-court.jpg` (the refuge-store version). **Courtyard inns are NOT redone here** —
they are cited from `Reference/Camp-Study/lane-3-waystation-typology.md` §2–3 and §6.

## 1. The chassis, stated as the invariant

Across every institution in this lane, six things hold:

1. A **continuous built perimeter** enclosing a single reserved void.
2. **One controlled entrance** through that perimeter, and it is the only place ornament is
   spent on the outside.
3. **Ranges of repeated identical units** forming the perimeter's inner face.
4. **One collective/sacred room** larger than the rest, with a privileged position.
5. **A perimeter circulation path** hugging the inner face of the ranges.
6. **One object in the middle of the void**, or nothing at all.

Everything below is a variation on those six.

## 3. The finding that actually matters: circulation is the tell

The monastery's covered walk is a **collective circulation device** — one shared route that
every claustral room opens off. The community moves as a body.

Merton's Mob Quad (`l3-mobquad.jpg`, `l3-mobquad-corner.jpg`) shows the college doing the
same job with the opposite device. There is **no arcade at all**: the ranges present a plain
two-storey stone face directly onto a lawn, with a rhythm of small windows and, at
intervals, **pointed-arch doorways opening straight onto the quad**. Each doorway is a
staircase serving a handful of private sets. Above, tall traceried library windows and big
dormer oriels break the roof.

That is a *cellular* circulation model, and it is the architectural expression of the
institution's rule: monks share one route because they do everything together; scholars get
separate doors because they do not.

**So the correct form of Adam's ruling is: the chassis holds, and the *walk* is the
adjustable member.** Continuous covered arcade (communal) → open path with periodic doors
(cellular) → tiered open galleries (serviced) → no perimeter walk at all (controlled). That
single axis carries most of the institutional difference and costs one geometry swap.

## 4. Hospital: the ward is the dormitory, and it faces the altar

`l3-beaune-salle.jpg` (Hôtel-Dieu de Beaune, mid-15c) shows the *Salle des Pôvres*: an
enormous hall — documented as roughly 50 × 14 × 16 m — under a painted timber wagon vault
with cross tie-beams, tall lancets in a strict rhythm down both sides, and **two continuous
rows of curtained beds in identical bays** along both walls, with benches and tables down
the middle. **The chapel adjoins the far end of the ward, positioned so bedridden patients
could see Mass from their beds.**

`l3-beaune-cour.jpg` shows the yard: two tiers of open timber galleries on posts, a
polychrome glazed-tile roof in a bold diamond pattern of four colours, and a **stone well on
a stepped plinth under a wrought-iron overthrow** in the middle of the paving.

Two transfers:

- **The "identical bedroom row" and the "hall" are the same room in the hospital
  expression.** The ward *is* the dorter with the altar at its head. That collapses two of
  the seed's ring rooms into one, and it is the most legible institutional space in the
  whole lane — you can read the rule of the house from the floor.
- **The hospital spends its display budget on the roof.** Where the monastery ornaments the
  gate and the fountain, Beaune ornaments the roof plane. A per-institution rule for *where
  trim goes* is nearly free and does a lot of identity work.

## 5. Prison: the same precinct, with the void subdivided and the wall made mute

`l3-easternstate-aerial.jpg` (a 19c engraved bird's-eye of Eastern State Penitentiary)
shows: a high blank perimeter wall with **corner turrets and a single castellated
gatehouse**, and inside, cell blocks radiating from a central hub, each block flanked by
**rows of individual walled exercise yards, one per cell**.

The chassis is intact; three specific mutations do all the work:

1. **The single void becomes many voids.** The yard is chopped into per-inmate cells of open
   air. That is precisely the site-6 "cell partition" invention already banked, applied to
   the *ground* rather than the building.
2. **The outward face goes mute.** No windows, no articulation, one gate. The monastery's
   wall is "for separation, not war" and shows it by having ordinary openings; the prison's
   wall is for control and shows it by having none.
3. **The vertical becomes surveillance.** Corner turrets on the wall, and a hub that watches
   down every block. The monastery's tower rings a bell; the prison's tower looks.

## 6. Almshouse: the same plan at domestic scale, and the void becomes a garden

`l3-almshouse-quad.jpg` shows single-storey cottages around three sides of a court, each
with **its own front door under a small arched porch**, and a larger arched recess at the
centre of the main range (the communal element). The court is a **planted garden with a
lawn**, edged in brick, not a void.

This is the chassis at its warmest, and it supplies the useful bracket: **when the
institution is charitable and small, the yard fills with plants and the range drops to one
storey with individual doors.** It also confirms the cellular-circulation axis at the
opposite end from the monastery.

## 7. The cell-per-person plan, and the serving hatch

`l3-certosa-chiostro.jpg` (Certosa di Pavia's great cloister) and
`l3-carthusian-cell-plan.png` (a charterhouse cell drawn in plan) together give the most
gameplay-legible unit in the whole study:

- Behind a very long arcade runs a line of **identical little gabled houses, each with its
  own chimney**, opening off the walk.
- Each cell in plan is a two-storey house: workshop above; below, a lobby, a *cubiculum*
  with bed, altar, desk and stove, a wet cell, **its own walled private garden**, and — at
  the door onto the walk — a **serving hatch (*Durchreiche*)** through which food is passed
  without contact.

A door, a hatch, and a private walled garden per unit is a complete little tactical object:
a room you can be locked in, a hole a thing can be pushed through, and an outdoor space
nobody overlooks.

## 8. The courtyard inn — cited, not redone

Per the brief, the caravanserai/coaching-inn work is **reused by citation** from
`Reference/Camp-Study/lane-3-waystation-typology.md`. The four relations that lane extracted
transfer directly onto this chassis and are consistent with everything above:

- **one entrance, oversized, ornamented** (Camp lane 3 §2.1 / §6.2);
- **the court is empty on purpose, with a single central object** (§2.2 / §6.3);
- **cells are repeated and identical — one piece × N** (§2.3 / §6.4);
- **stairs up the inside of the wall to a usable roof** — a *rung-2* architectural deck
  rather than a terrain one (§2.4 / §6.6).

Camp lane 3's own headline finding also carries over cleanly and is worth restating here
because it is the chassis's boundary condition: **"the caravanserai's gate does not stop
anyone travelling — it stops people entering the building"** (§1). The institutional wall,
in every expression in both studies, controls its own doorway and never the route. That is
the same fact as the monastery's "renounces the route" capsule, arrived at from a different
direction.

## 9. Where FFT independently agrees (see lane 5 for detail)

- **Military Academy's Auditorium (24)** — the school's collective room is a hall with a
  **raised timber platform** at one end, banners and curtains behind it. Same bones as the
  chapel's raised chancel, different dressing.
- **Hospital in Slums (33)** — the hospital reduced to **rows of timber bunk frames** in a
  dark box. The bed row *is* the institution.
- **Granary of Bethla Garrison (65)** — a high-walled institutional yard filled with **rows
  of identical storage racks** on stepped terraces, one banner on the end wall.
- **Golgorand Execution Site (63)** — a walled grass yard with a **walkable wall top**, one
  **arched gate flanked by hanging banners**, and one raised timber platform in the middle:
  the punitive expression of the same six invariants.

## Findings (plain English)

1. **The chassis holds.** Six invariants — continuous perimeter, one controlled entrance,
   repeated identical units, one privileged collective room, a perimeter path, one object in
   the void — appear in every institution examined.
2. **The correction to "never the architecture" is: the *walk* changes.** Covered arcade
   (communal) → open path with periodic doors (cellular) → tiered timber galleries (serviced)
   → no walk at all (controlled). One member, four settings, most of the institutional
   difference.
3. **The college drops the arcade entirely and replaces it with doors.** Merton's quad has no
   covered walk — plain ranges, a lawn, and pointed doorways at intervals, each a staircase
   to private sets. Circulation encodes the rule of the house.
4. **In the hospital the dormitory row and the great hall are the same room, and it faces the
   altar.** Beaune's ward is ~50 m long with two rows of curtained beds and the chapel at its
   head so patients could see Mass from bed.
5. **Each institution spends its ornament somewhere different.** Monastery: gate and fountain
   house. College: gate tower and hall windows. Hospital: **the roof** (four-colour glazed
   tile). Inn: the carriage arch. Prison: nowhere, on purpose.
6. **The prison keeps the precinct and subdivides the void.** One yard becomes many walled
   per-cell yards; the outer wall goes blank with corner turrets; the vertical becomes
   surveillance rather than a bell.
7. **The almshouse is the warm end of the axis:** single storey, individual front doors,
   and the void becomes a planted garden rather than a reserved emptiness.
8. **The Carthusian cell is the most gameplay-legible unit in the study** — a two-storey
   house with a private walled garden and a **serving hatch** beside its door onto the walk.
9. **Every institution's wall controls its own doorway and never the route.** This is the
   same result the camp study reached for the caravanserai, arrived at independently, and it
   is the chassis's defining limit.
10. **The privileged room's marker is a raised platform, not a shape.** Chapel chancel,
    school stage, execution scaffold, ward altar — the same move: a low dais at the head of
    a long room, reached by steps.
11. **FFT already ships four of these institutions on the same bones** (school hall,
    hospital, granary, execution yard) — evidence that the chassis survives translation to
    tactical scale.

## Declared gaps

- **No historic prison-yard photograph.** The prison case rests on one 19c engraved bird's-eye
  and on the plan logic; I did not obtain interior-yard photography, and the "what a real
  prison yard looks like from inside" question is unanswered.
- **No hospital *cloister*.** Beaune is a galleried court, not an arcaded cloister. The
  Hôtel-Dieu type with a true claustral arcade (e.g. the Spanish/Italian *ospedale* courts)
  was not evidenced.
- **No school other than the collegiate quad.** The grammar-school and madrasa courtyard
  types were out of reach in the time available; the "school" column is one example wide.
- **The circulation-axis claim is inferred from four cases**, not from a survey. It is a
  strong pattern in the evidence I have, not a proven law.
