# Mine / Workshop Study — synthesis

date: 2026-07-25
status: INITIAL DEPTH PASS RETURNED — useful, declared gaps remain
authority note: this is an evidence record, not the live implementation contract;
current rules live in `docs/GOLDEN-SITES-CATALOG.md` and the working specification in

## 1. One-sentence result

The accepted Site 5 direction survives: a mine/workshop is a useful source-to-export
chain whose supports, routes, workers, air, water, power, processing, and waste create
the strategic level; “strained” means part of that chain is under visible pressure
while at least one meaningful production path still works.

## 2. What the evidence changed

The first concept treated the haulage route as the site's deck. That remains correct,
but the deck cannot carry every obligation. Established working mines repeatedly
separate or distinguish:

- product/waste haulage;
- worker travel and escape;
- air movement;
- drainage and pumping;
- structural support;
- maintenance access; and
- processing/inspection after material leaves the face.

The first build therefore gains a narrow manway/air/escape route beside the broad haul
route, a visible pump and discharge path, a last-safe-support threshold, and distinct
ore and waste destinations.

## 3. Relational grammar

### The primary spatial sentence

*A worked opening follows the resource into the ground; a broad route carries useful
material back toward sorting and export, while a smaller service route, supports, air,
and water keep the operation alive.*

### The masses

1. **Host terrain or deposit mass** — owns the broad form and the work face.
2. **Worked void** — deliberate cut, not a natural-cave skin.
3. **Threshold/work yard** — the place where underground flow becomes visible surface
   work.
4. **Waste mass** — spoil, tailings, slag, offcuts, or discharge with a real destination.
5. **Optional processing mass** — sorting shelter, stamp/forge/wash building, or open
   terrace when the material is processed on site.

### The connectors

- broad haulage ramp/drift/road;
- narrow manway/air/escape route;
- drain, pipe, channel, or sump relation;
- shaft/landing/hoist at larger rungs;
- export threshold to road, rail, river, port, pack route, or host settlement.

### The danger boundaries

- last permanent support;
- waterline;
- gas/air threshold;
- active machine or hoist sweep;
- unsupported face;
- unstable spoil/tailings edge;
- heat/smoke zone at fire-setting, roast, forge, or smelt work.

These must be visible or telegraphed. The player may choose risk; the level may not hide
arbitrary instant failure behind decorative geometry.

## 4. Strategic gameplay grammar

The mine is strongest when the same truthful system supports several plans:

- **Direct:** broad haulage is fast, readable, large-creature and cargo capable, but
  exposed and occupied.
- **Indirect:** manway/air path is slower, narrower, and capacity-limited, but supports
  stealth, flank, rescue, inspection, and escape.
- **Conditional:** a flooded or maintenance path becomes usable only when water,
  creature capability, timing, or intervention permits it.

Site levers must change connected facts:

- pump state changes water level and reachable lower work;
- manway state changes escape, air, and alternate access;
- haul blockage changes production, traffic, reinforcement, and retreat;
- support work changes where travel is safe;
- shift state changes witnesses, noise, carts, light, and social access;
- ore/waste movement changes cover and working room;
- processing state changes value, noise, heat, smoke, and objective location.

At least one strong noncombat plan must exist in the Golden Seed: repair, rescue,
negotiation, investigation, theft, labor dispute, rerouting, or production defense.

## 5. Form families

The resource and terrain select the broad form:

| form | source condition | characteristic relationships | Site 5 use |
|---|---|---|---|
| prospect/trench | shallow uncertain source | cut + spoil + tools + temporary shelter | Proof/MVP small family |
| drift/adit | seam/vein meets slope | horizontal portal, broad haul, drainage possible by grade | Golden Seed |
| slope/incline | source descends from surface | ramp/winch, water flows down, haul and travel compete | MVP variant |
| shaft | deep source | collar, hoist, landings, separate ladder/manway, pump burden | Ideal after drift |
| room-and-pillar | broad near-level bed | repeated rooms/pillars, main entries, ventilation loops | Ideal repeated-family |
| stope/vein-following | steep or irregular vein | level + vertical/diagonal extraction volume | Ideal |
| open cut/quarry | exposed broad source | terraces/benches, haul switchbacks, highwall, spoil edge | accepted stretch |
| spring/gradient works | resource carried by water | channels, ponds/troughs, gravity sequence, shared terraces | accepted stretch, Maras model |
| attached workshop | source arrives at work floor | inspect/sort/transform/store/export; waste/heat/power leave | accepted stretch if flow remains honest |

One site roll selects a coherent family. It does not randomly combine a shaft, quarry,
and drift for visual variety.

## 6. Preindustrial mechanism vocabulary

Agricola's 1556 record is especially valuable for Grounded fantasy because it describes
a complete system using wood, stone, rope, leather, water, animal/human power, oil, and
simple metalwork:

- hand, horse, or water-powered hauling;
- ladders separated from bucket/haul space in shafts;
- drains leading to sumps;
- hand pumps, piston pumps, chain/bucket devices, and water-powered pumps;
- natural air routes, windsails, fans, bellows, and wooden air conduits;
- posts/caps/sills/lagging whose density responds to ground;
- oil-rationed shift lamps and carried/spiked light;
- face sorting, surface sorting tables, trays and tubs;
- hammer breaking, roasting, stamps, sifting, washing troughs/strakes, and smelting.

These mechanisms should be assembly families, not one universal technological era.
The Golden Seed uses the smallest set that makes the pump, haulage, support, sorting,
and light flows visible.

## 7. Culture and organization

Culture has strong influence without overriding geology:

- who owns the claim and who may enter;
- household/cooperative/guild/temple/state/company organization;
- whether processing sits at the face, portal, settlement, water source, or export edge;
- how shifts, pay, shares, tally, custody, and maintenance are organized;
- which carrier and power traditions are available;
- support joinery, masonry, carving, marks, warnings, offerings, memorials, and closure;
- whether water is treated as waste, hazard, power, product, or sacred/shared resource;
- how skill, coercion, caste, kinship, reciprocity, and outside capital shape the site.

The evidence explicitly warns against a single “European mine” model. Iwami and Sado
show small labor-intensive Japanese production systems; Maras shows a living Andean
gravity/terrace and reciprocal-ownership system; Santa Bárbara shows a Peruvian
gallery/processing/transport complex under colonial exchange; Rammelsberg shows a
long-lived European hydraulic network; Ombilin shows how colonial industrial systems
can encode coercive hierarchy. These are organizational evidence, not costume packs.

## 8. Lighting verdict

Grounded mine light is local and costly:

- portal daylight creates a hard, directional threshold;
- workers carry candles or oil lamps and may spike/hook them to supports at a task;
- fixed work lamps belong to staffed stations, maintenance, fuel, and shift;
- many small open flames consume air and add smoke; hazardous gas may prohibit them;
- inactive/flooded work loses practical light unless a current fact owns it;
- forge, roast, lava, luminous resource, or magic may provide strong light only where
  the process licenses it.

The renderer may use exposure, restrained environment bounce, AO, contact, wet
reflection, and silhouette separation to keep dark forms readable. Presentation support
does not create a fictional lamp.

## 9. Provisional dimensions and grid translation

The following are proof hypotheses, not universal historical claims:

- Golden haul route: start at **2 cells / 10 ft clear** where large witnesses and carts
  must pass; narrower variants must state their capacity.
- Manway: start at **1 cell / 5 ft clear**, deliberately excluding the largest bases and
  most cargo.
- Repeated timber support: Agricola describes one soft-ground tunnel example at about
  **1.5 fathoms (~9 ft)** between sets, usefully close to two Genesis cells. Actual
  spacing must be ground/material-card driven and clay checked.
- Sorting table: Agricola describes roughly **4 ft square**, a good one-cell assembly
  starting point.
- Vertical change: inherit Genesis `h = 2.5 ft`; broad ramps/terraces are preferred for
  the first build. Ladders and deep hoists remain later because their movement and
  camera proof is not yet mature.
- Supported overhead: start with a normal **4h / 10 ft** work zone where large witness
  fit is required; low seams become explicit crouch/small-creature envelopes rather
  than squeezed visual variants.

Every number may be rejected by standee fit, camera legibility, movement, or tactical
balance. No source measurement outranks fun gameplay.

## 11. Build-order recommendation

1. **Proof:** drift portal, broad ramp, support rhythm, upper face, sorting yard,
   pump/sump/waterline, separate manway, ore/spoil distinction.
2. **MVP promotion:** prospect and slope variants; hand/animal/water mechanism cards;
   culture/organization pair; processing choices; working/strained states.
3. **Ideal promotion:** shafts and landings; room-and-pillar/stope; quarries and gradient
   works; attached workshops; broader cultural/realm/power systems.

This order solves the reusable flow grammar before adding the hardest vertical and
mechanical problems.
