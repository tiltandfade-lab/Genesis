## Findings

**1. There is a single crisp visual discriminator between dug-by-culture and
dug-by-creature/nature: the CEILING.** `L3-04` (Beer Quarry Caves) is unmistakably man-made
and the reason is a **flat ceiling held at one height over regular left pillars**. `L3-03`
(Namur quarry) is also man-made but reads far more cave-like — because its roof is a ragged
pointed arch. Nature and beasts do not produce a level ceiling plane. If Genesis wants the
mine↔cave hybrid boundary to *read* without a caption, ceiling regularity is the axis: flat
+ rhythmic = culture, irregular/absent = nature.

**2. Wall irregularity is a weak discriminator; it fails.** The Sharkham adit (`L3-01`) has
walls of blocky, splintered, wholly irregular rock — hand-cut but indistinguishable from a
natural fissure. Only its **arched crown and its constant section** give it away. So the
"bore vs erosion finish" parameter Adam banked is doing real work, but not at the wall: it
should be expressed as **section constancy** (does the cross-section repeat down the run?)
rather than as surface roughness.

**3. Adit mouths are small, ugly, wet, and easy to miss.** `L3-02`: a sub-metre black slot
under a single lintel slab, half-buried in dead bracken, with a stream running out of it and
a gully leading away. Nothing about it announces "entrance." Compared with the natural cave
mouths in Lane 1 this is *even less* legible. Any lair arrival hook for an adopted void
should assume the mouth is found by its **drainage** or its **spoil**, not by its silhouette.

**4. Abandoned workings drain, and the water is the level datum.** `L3-01` shows the water
surface cutting a perfectly straight line across every irregular wall of the adit, and
`L3-07` shows a level standing behind a deliberately walled-up entrance, flooded to the same
kind of datum. Flooding in an adopted void is not "some water" — it is a **plane at a
height**, and it converts the lower part of the space into a different access class.

**5. The collapse entrance reads as a dish, not a hole.** `L3-06` (a mine run-in) is a
grassy sunken cone in a hillside — from the surface it is nearly indistinguishable from the
karst sinkholes in `L1-01`/`L1-02`. This is a strong convergence result: **collapse-void
origin and karst origin present identically from outside.** Genesis can share one exterior
"depression" arrival presentation across both, and reveal origin only on entry.

**6. Untopping gives the cleanest mishap-entry picture in the study.** `L3-05`: a slate
quarry has been stripped from above, exposing a black arched chamber mouth at the head of a
scree fan, with debris pouring *into* it. That is exactly "the ground gives way and you are
in old workings" — and it shows the geometry that makes it playable: **the debris fan is the
ramp connecting the surface break to the intact void floor**, which is the same relation as
the lava-tube skylight cone in `L1-06`. One noun, two origins.

**7. Big dug voids are bigger than cave chambers.** `L3-03`'s gallery dwarfs two standing
figures; `L3-04`'s pillar hall is a *hall*. Where natural chambers vary wildly (Lane 1
finding 3: 8 ft to 50 ft roof in one system), quarry galleries are **uniformly large and
uniformly proportioned**. If the site-11 dragon-scale boundary ever needs an "ordinary-scale
big room," the quarry gallery is the honest reference, not a cave.

**8. Timbering is a *narrative* layer, not a structural one, in the reference set.** Very
little timber survives in these photographs — what survives is the cut rock. Timber is the
first thing to rot. The FFT colliery maps (Lane 4: maps 28/29/30) show the opposite — heavy
surviving timber frames — because they depict a *working* mine. This gives a clean condition
axis for the adopted void: **working = timber present and legible; abandoned = timber gone,
rock and water remain.**

**9. Pillar rhythm is a free readability gift for a dug interior.** `L3-04`'s left pillars
create regular sightline breaks and cover positions across a big flat floor, with no
authored props. If Genesis ever needs the uninhabited-void case to still be *tactically*
interesting on a flat floor, pillar-and-stall is the answer already proven by real
engineering practice.

**10. The uninhabited case is visually complete.** Several of these images contain nothing
but rock, water, and debris — and they are still legible spaces with a mood. The banked
UNINHABITED result on the inhabitation roll is not a degenerate "empty room"; it is a
first-class presentation with its own vocabulary (level water, spoil, collapse fan, plugged
or walled entrance).

## Where this pressures the banked rulings

- **"Dug origin is still not construction"** — held, with a caveat: at the *mine* end of the
  adopted-void axis, the void genuinely *is* construction (flat ceiling, pillar rhythm, adit
  section). The shell-set parameter approach works for creature bores; it does not cover
  pillar-and-stall. Either the mine case gets a genuinely different room family, or the
  adopted-void origin is scoped to *feral, ceiling-irregular* workings only. This is a
  founder question.
- **"Mishap entry"** — held and strengthened; the debris-fan ramp is the missing geometry
  that makes it playable rather than a cutscene.
- **"Abandoned den → site 3"** — worth re-checking: the abandoned *mine* case here is
  visually much closer to site 7's own uninhabited result than to a ruin site.
