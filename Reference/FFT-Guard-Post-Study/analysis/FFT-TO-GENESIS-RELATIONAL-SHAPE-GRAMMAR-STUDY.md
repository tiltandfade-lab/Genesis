# FFT-to-Genesis Relational Shape-Grammar Study

Status: preliminary visual translation study  
Created: 2026-07-23  
Scope: ten-map Guard Post cohort, five matched views per map  
Production boundary: reference grammar only; no FFT layout, mesh, texture, or art becomes a Genesis
asset

## Conclusion

Adam is not being naive. “FFT-to-Genesis translation study” is understandable and directionally
right. The more precise term is:

> **FFT-to-Genesis relational shape-grammar study**

or:

> **comparative map morphology and grammar induction**

The word **translation** is useful if it means:

```text
observed spatial relationship
→ Genesis table prose
→ typed grammar obligation
→ bounded deterministic solver
→ procedural structure/material realization
→ replay and visual QA
```

It must not mean one-to-one layout conversion, mesh reconstruction for shipping, or copying FFT's
authored maps. Genesis should learn why the maps compose well, then generate new sites from its own
canonical facts.

## Map-level observations

### MAP001 — At Main Gate of Igros Castle

Observed:

- One straight passage is the dominant organizing axis.
- The route crosses water, widens into an apron, and terminates at/continues through a gate.
- The gate sits exactly where the constrained route meets the defensive mass.
- Water and grass form large quiet supporting regions.
- The architectural silhouette is dominant; small dressing is sparse.
- The ground and bridge edges are irregular enough to avoid a sterile rectangular stage.

Genesis lesson:

- A defensive structure should be caused by the route constraint.
- Use nested route beats: approach → narrowing/crossing → working apron → threshold.
- A strong site may obtain most of its interest from one route plus one constraint, not many props.

### MAP006 — At the Gate of Riovanes Castle

Observed:

- A large wall/gate mass occupies one side/corner of the stage.
- Water cuts the ground and reinforces the controlled passage.
- The open approach remains visually broad and comparatively quiet.
- Wall-walk and roof/parapet elevations provide tactical verticality without noisy terrain.
- The building is not centered; the remaining ground mass is intentionally asymmetric.

Genesis lesson:

- Put the dominant defended mass against a boundary when that improves approach readability.
- Terrain constraint, guarded threshold, and high position should reinforce one another.
- Do not require the building to sit in the middle of every generated board.

### MAP012 — At the Gate of Lionel Castle

Observed:

- The road approaches a wall approximately perpendicularly.
- A bridge/causeway crosses water before a broad, legible gate apron.
- The wall forms a simple barrier across the route; the gate is the route's single clear opening.
- One adjacent natural height mass prevents the wall from reading as a floating façade.
- The forecourt has very little clutter.

Genesis lesson:

- Barrier grammar can be simple when approach, opening, and apron are strongly aligned.
- Leave enough quiet space to read the guarded threshold and standees.
- Natural terrain should terminate, support, or continue a wall rather than ignore it.

### MAP025 — Yardow Fort City

Observed:

- The fortified wall/bastion occupies a terrain corner and sits above an exterior approach.
- An interior building/court reads as a second, higher or protected region.
- The road changes direction around/through the defended corner.
- Broad grass shapes separate the architecture and road.
- Rock/cliff treatment and retaining masonry merge the platform with the landform.

Genesis lesson:

- A convex road bend plus an elevated retaining shoulder is a strong Guard Post relation.
- Structure can inherit a landform's edge rather than demanding a rectangular foundation pad.
- A small number of interlocking regions can imply a larger inhabited context.

### MAP035 — Zaland Fort City

Observed:

- Occupied buildings and a defensive wall sit on a raised rocky shelf.
- A natural/rocky exterior approach meets a constructed threshold and enclosed interior.
- The supporting slope is a large mass, not a uniform collection of pebbles.
- Roofs, walls, courtyard, and slope form a compact hierarchy.
- The stage edge follows meaningful built and natural boundaries.

Genesis lesson:

- This is the strongest architectural precedent in the cohort for “old stone, still occupied.”
- Let the road/approach reveal the change from natural ground to maintained working terrace.
- Use recent occupation and repair on top of older landform-bound masonry.

### MAP037 — Ruins Outside Zaland

Observed:

- One tall ruined gateway is the unmistakable dominant landmark.
- The route passes directly through it.
- Grass shoulders, rock slopes, trees, and patchwork ground frame the passage.
- The ruin is interlocked with terrain and road; it is not an isolated prop.
- Large void/edge regions and sparse trees keep the silhouette readable.

Genesis lesson:

- Old structure can carry the primary silhouette while current use appears as repair and clearance.
- For an operating Guard Post, preserve the old frame but keep threshold, footing, and sightline
  functional.
- Do not import the map's ruined state wholesale; translate the chronological layering.

### MAP049 — Fort Zeakden

Observed:

- Several tall stone masses create a compact vertical cluster.
- Narrow timber bridges, platforms, and doors stitch the masses into a traversable network.
- Large quiet snow/ground areas emphasize the towers.
- Vertical architecture, rather than noisy ground relief, supplies most of the elevation.
- Routes are narrow and strongly constrained.

Genesis lesson:

- This is a later tower/fort permutation, not the simplest first Guard Post.
- Separate structural masses can share one semantic site when connectors are explicit.
- Keep high-rise interest in reserve so the hillside Guard Post does not inherit tower complexity.

### MAP081 — Grog Hill

Observed:

- A single through-road is the clearest organizing spine in the cohort.
- Broad terrain shoulders rise on either side while the road remains readable.
- Stone fragments, retaining edges, stairs, rocks, and small built pieces cross or bind the
  shoulders.
- Rocks appear in clusters at height changes and route edges, not as uniform scatter.
- Quiet grass/earth regions separate the clusters.
- The composition is asymmetric while remaining balanced.

Genesis lesson:

- This is the strongest direct precedent for the selected hillside roadside Guard Post.
- Keep the road primary; derive upper shoulder, retaining walls, rocks, and post from its relation.
- One side should normally win the elevation hierarchy instead of mirroring two equal banks.

### MAP085 — Mandalia Plains

Observed:

- Navigable grassy clearings are organized between rock formations and shallow height masses.
- There is no dominant paved road; negative space implies possible routes.
- Rock clusters occupy edges, corners, height transitions, and local landmarks.
- Large grass fields remain visually quiet.
- Walkable elevation is broad/subtle while nonwalkable rocks provide sharp vertical punctuation.

Genesis lesson:

- Separate walkable elevation masses from tall nonwalkable silhouette anchors.
- Rocks should create edges, pockets, and route guidance, not fill every unused cell.
- The ground-material treatment can carry richness while geometry stays comparatively simple.

### MAP098 — Outside Castle Gate in Lesalia

Observed:

- A straight causeway passes through a massive wall/gate.
- Grass/ditch or void bands frame the road.
- Some architecture disappears or is suppressed in particular views, preserving visibility.
- The route and threshold remain legible even when the surrounding wall mass is large.
- The map has an intentionally incomplete diorama edge rather than a sealed rectangular block.

Genesis lesson:

- View-dependent cutaway/occlusion is part of composition, not a late cosmetic fix.
- The renderer may suppress foreground faces, but the committed wall, route, and elevation remain
  canonical.
- A strong composed edge can reveal the diorama while still implying a larger wall beyond it.

## Recurring FFT-like shape grammar

### 1. One primary spatial sentence

Each strong map can be summarized as a short relation:

- a road crosses water and meets a gate;
- a passage climbs to a fortified shelf;
- a route runs between rocky shoulders;
- a road passes through an old frame;
- narrow connectors stitch several high masses.

The scene is not “a collection of medieval things.” It is one dominant spatial sentence with
supporting clauses.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Every site recipe must expose a one-sentence primary spatial relation before detail generation.

### 2. Broad masses before cells

The cohort repeatedly favors a few coherent regions:

- approach/forecourt;
- upper shelf or shoulder;
- building/wall mass;
- water/void/ditch;
- quiet grass/ground;
- rock/cliff boundary.

Texture variation may be cell-rich, but the geometric composition is region-first.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Construct two to four meaningful ground/elevation masses, then tessellate and dress them. Reject
> elevation that reads as independent cell noise.

The exact range remains a hypothesis to calibrate rather than a fixed universal law.

### 3. Architecture occupies a transition

Buildings and walls repeatedly sit:

- across a route;
- above a route;
- at a bridge/causeway;
- on a shelf edge;
- against a water or cliff constraint;
- where natural and constructed ground meet.

Genesis rule candidate:

> A Guard Post anchor must control a committed transition or observation relation. Reject a post
> that merely occupies available flat space.

### 4. Elevation has hierarchy

The maps do not distribute visual importance evenly. They normally establish:

1. a dominant height/mass;
2. a supporting shelf, wall, or shoulder;
3. low approach/negative space;
4. occasional nonwalkable vertical punctuation.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Prefer one dominant elevation mass and one supporting mass; penalize equal-area/equal-height
> terraces that erase hierarchy unless a table-authored site archetype specifically calls for
> symmetry.

### 5. Roads are regions, not centerlines

Roads:

- change width;
- widen into aprons;
- narrow at thresholds;
- cross changes of material;
- use bridges, stairs, retaining edges, or cuts;
- relate to slopes and architecture.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Generate the primary route as a corridor region with width and apron states, not a single-cell
> path painted after terrain.

### 6. Rocks are relational clusters

Rock formations tend to:

- cap or expose a height transition;
- define an outside bend;
- close an unneeded stage edge;
- frame a pocket or route;
- provide one local landmark;
- reinforce a slope or wall endpoint.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Every major rock cluster declares a relation: `tier-edge`, `outside-bend`, `boundary-cap`,
> `route-frame`, `cover-anchor`, or `landmark`. Decorative micro-rocks may follow material breakup,
> but cannot replace cluster grammar.

### 7. Quiet ground is active composition

Grass, snow, water, or stone aprons frequently occupy large areas with little object clutter. These
regions:

- frame sprites;
- reveal routes;
- separate focal masses;
- make elevation readable;
- protect tactical footing.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Reserve quiet-ground regions before incidental dressing. Penalize dressing that destroys the
> silhouette or competes with the primary spatial sentence.

### 8. Composed edges imply a larger world

The stage edge follows walls, cliffs, water, roads, and terrain cuts. It is not always a full
rectangle, and some views deliberately reveal black/empty diorama sides.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Derive the visible envelope from meaningful masses and ingress/egress continuations, then compose
> clipped corners and undercut sides. Do not use arbitrary noisy perimeter erosion.

### 9. Sparse object count, rich surface hierarchy

The cohort is often surprisingly sparse. Visual richness comes from:

- silhouette;
- height;
- material boundaries;
- masonry profiles;
- ground breakup;
- a few trees, torches, rocks, crates, or banners.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Attempt to solve beauty with massing, profile, surface, and light before increasing prop density.

### 10. Camera visibility is grammatical

MAP098 demonstrates view-specific suppression most clearly, and several large gate maps are composed
to keep the approach and threshold visible from useful angles.

Genesis rule candidate:

> Candidate acceptance includes production-camera route, threshold, lookout, and standee visibility.
> Cutaway is a deterministic projection of committed occluders, never a geometry reroll.

## First Genesis Guard Post shape recipe

Working name:

`GP-SHAPE-01 — Shoulder Overlook Through-Road`

### Table-readable spatial sentence

> An old stone Guard Post occupies the uphill shoulder above a pass-through road. The road narrows
> where maintained retaining masonry meets a rock cut, then continues beyond the post.

This is provisional prose for testing, not a new canonical table row.

### Hard obligations

- The road enters and exits through distinct legal continuations.
- The road remains connected and traversable.
- A working approach/apron connects the road to the post threshold.
- The post sits on or embeds into an uphill shoulder relation.
- At least one observation face has a legal sight relation to the approach.
- Every height delta has a typed connector or blocked-riser truth.
- Required workstation, container, supply, and circulation spaces remain legal.
- Maintained overgrowth does not block the road, door, observation station, or working clearances.
- Retreat remains honest.

### Arrangeable obligations

- The road may be straight, bent, gently climbing, switchbacked, or cut through rock as licensed by
  the selected table row.
- The upper shoulder may fall to either side.
- The post may sit on the inside bend, outside shoulder, or near the narrowing if it still controls
  the route.
- A secondary uphill footpath/flank may appear only when the envelope and table license it.
- Rock clusters, short stairs, retaining walls, and parapets are derived support where needed.

### Soft taste goals

- One dominant upper shoulder/post mass.
- One supporting rock/retaining or lower shelf mass.
- A road with at least one meaningful spatial event rather than uniform width and grade.
- Broad quiet grass around the road/post relation.
- Rock clusters concentrated at tier edges, outside bends, wall ends, or composed boundaries.
- An asymmetric but balanced silhouette.
- Clear road, threshold, post, and lookout from the production camera.
- Sparse incidental props.

### Anti-rules

- No flat rectangular pad with a building centered on it.
- No terrain noise applied independently per cell.
- No uniformly scattered rocks or grass tufts.
- No road painted after terrain with no cut, grade, edge, apron, or retaining relation.
- No decorative stair disconnected from the route graph.
- No equal-height/equal-area terraces without a licensed symmetric archetype.
- No overgrowth across operating circulation.
- No foreground wall that permanently hides the threshold or standees.
- No special geometry for the retained golden seed.

## Proposed deterministic construction sequence

1. **Roll meaning:** site form, road relation, shoulder/control archetype, elevation intent,
   observation method, construction/history, and optional secondary opportunity.
2. **Resolve envelope:** choose a bounded stage family and meaningful clipped edges consistent with
   road continuations and negative space.
3. **Resolve road endpoints:** choose distinct entry/exit faces satisfying the table row.
4. **Generate road corridor candidates:** build regions with bends, grades, narrowing, and apron
   events; reject illegal grade or clearance.
5. **Generate broad elevation candidates:** place uphill shoulder, supporting shelf, and low
   approach relative to the road rather than independently.
6. **Score post anchors:** require threshold access and observation relation; prefer causal
   interlock with bend, narrowing, shelf edge, or cut.
7. **Derive support:** emit retaining walls, stairs/ramps, foundation transitions, wall endpoints,
   and rock-boundary slots.
8. **Reserve gameplay:** footing, circulation, deployment, objective, flank, cover, and retreat.
9. **Assemble surfaces:** emit stable frames, roles, masks, sockets, UV scale, and chronology.
10. **Project maintained overgrowth:** combine exposure, drainage, slope, crevice, use, and repair
    fields; never perform an independent renderer roll.
11. **Validate production view:** road, threshold, standees, observation relation, elevation, and
    landmark must read; derive cutaway if needed.
12. **Select a legal candidate:** compare transparent named measures, commit ids/receipts, and save
    the capsule.

## Table authorship candidates exposed by the study

Good prose-table candidates:

- road/site relation;
- shoulder, bend, cut, crossing, terrace, or gate composition archetype;
- signed elevation intent;
- observation method;
- dominant terrain constraint;
- tactically meaningful rock/outcrop or secondary route;
- builder/occupier/repairer chronology;
- maintained-overgrowth condition;
- repair story;
- optional signal or defensive vocabulary when licensed.

Better structured-grammar data:

- legal envelope families;
- parameter ranges and weights;
- road width/grade/bend bands;
- elevation-mass size/adjacency bands;
- compatibility matrices;
- anchor scoring terms;
- rock-cluster profiles;
- quiet-ground reservations;
- cutaway profiles;
- fallback/relaxation order.

Engine-owned:

- exact cells, splines/runs, tier membership, connectors, sockets, transforms, cluster members,
  candidate count, rejection, scoring, and selected plan.

Renderer-owned:

- mesh tessellation, profile extrusion, UV projection, material sampling, deterministic condition
  masks, LOD, cutaway projection, and visual microvariation hashed from stable ids.

## Structure-kit implications

The study elevates these Clayroom families:

1. broad terrain mass and riser/cliff profiles;
2. road corridor with width transitions, bend, grade, apron, and continuation sockets;
3. run-based retaining wall with corners, ends, height changes, and rock termination;
4. stone post foundation that can abut/embed in a shoulder;
5. controlled threshold and deep observation opening;
6. stair/ramp/step connectors consuming the same route ids as mechanics;
7. rock-cluster assembly with boundary-arc and tier-edge sockets;
8. grass/soil surface roles plus quiet-ground reservation;
9. composed-edge and undercut/cap treatment;
10. deterministic camera cutaway/ghost/cap behavior.

## Material-kit implications

The first material family needs:

- old stone for floor, wall, retaining, riser, sill, and cap;
- packed/worn road with stone-to-earth transitions and cleared-use band;
- grass/soil with large quiet fields and restrained microvariation;
- rock/cliff sharing a compatible geological palette without reading as repeated wall stone;
- crevice moss, lichen, damp, and root-pressure masks;
- recent timber and forged-iron repair;
- cutaway/interior cap treatment;
- deterministic exposure, drainage, slope, traffic, and repair inputs.

## Acceptance program

The first grammar is ready to influence the golden site only when:

1. the same seed replays byte-equivalent canonical plans under pinned versions;
2. a changed-seed sheet produces materially different legal landscape, road, and post arrangements;
3. retained rejected seeds remain rejected for named causes;
4. adversarial envelopes fail honestly or follow the declared simplification ladder;
5. road/post/elevation relationships read in clay;
6. material and vegetation do not conceal weak composition;
7. the production camera preserves critical visibility;
8. the hero seed contains no special-case data or code;
9. Adam can identify recurring qualities and failures using named relational language;
10. the resulting rules also remain plausible candidates for later site types.

## Immediate findings worth carrying forward

- The first Guard Post should learn most heavily from MAP081's through-road and asymmetric shoulders,
  MAP035's occupied old masonry on a rocky shelf, MAP037's old-frame/route interlock, and MAP085's
  rock clustering plus quiet grass.
- MAP001/MAP012 teach guarded-route clarity and apron/threshold sequencing.
- MAP025 teaches road bend plus fortified retaining shoulder.
- MAP049 is valuable but belongs to the later tower permutation.
- MAP098 makes deterministic view-dependent cutaway a first-class requirement.
- “Lovely elevation” should mean a few legible masses with hierarchy and connectors, not more height
  noise.
- “FFT-like” should mean a clear spatial sentence, causal interlock, restrained density, and composed
  diorama edges—not pixel imitation.
