# THE LANTERN THRESHOLD


## ROLE / PRESSURE / TAGS
- **Role:** Access • Social Friction • Discovery
- **Pressure:** Moderate — noise, suspicion, and occult instability rise if the PCs linger or fumble.
- **Tags:** urban • upper-district • shared-obstacle • occult • access • stealth-pressure • modular

---

## AT A GLANCE (GM)
- **Best use:** soft occult gate; shared-obstacle node; upper-district infiltration friction
- **Common outputs:** clue • access workaround • NPC connection • pressure escalation • future complication
- **Likely follow-ups:** guarded threshold • hidden bypass • faction contact • rooftop approach

---

## RUN THIS IN 10 SECONDS
A spirit-bound gate blocks entry into an upper-district property while another NPC is also trying to get through. The party can cooperate, compete, inspect the tampering, or try the vertical route—but loud footing, a hazard lane, and neighborhood suspicion make delay risky.

---

## LIKELY FLOW
1. **First impression:** the party spots the gate, the other NPC, the loud cobbles, and the altered masonry.
2. **Test the threshold:** when the PCs first engage the gate, **roll 1d6 on Obstacle Functional Reality** to define what the spirit-bound threshold actually wants or how it resists.
3. **Read the site:** if the PCs inspect the lantern, patchwork, niches, or threshold, **roll 1d6 on Lantern Truth** and **1d10 on Discovery Target** to determine what the scene is really revealing.
4. **Attempt passage or bypass:** if the PCs cross the dangerous strip, rush the route, or force a workaround, **roll 1d6 on Hazard Lane** to define the exact form of the risk.
5. **Resolve or escalate:** if the PCs make too much noise, delay too long, or provoke the gate, the NPC, or the district, **roll 1d6 on Escalation** to transform the scene.

---

## WHAT THE PLAYERS NOTICE IMMEDIATELY
- The gate is the main obstacle, and it is not behaving like a normal lock.
- The cobblestones are loud; rushing the approach will be hard to hide.
- A dangerous strip near the threshold looks unsafe to cross casually.
- The wall has been altered recently: blocked niches and a fresh patch stand out.
- The glow-worm lantern is deliberate and probably useful.
- Another person is already dealing with the exact same obstacle.

---

## GM READ-ALOUD: ON APPROACH
*Use when the PCs first enter the scene.*

The lane is dim and close-walled, lined with fine shops and fortified facades whose locks look made to resist more than thieves. Smooth cobblestones carry every hurried footstep farther than they should. Nearby, a dog barks somewhere behind shuttered windows, and a ladder, hoist, shaft, or drop offers a possible way up or around. Several wall cubbies have been blocked up, and one patch of fresh mortar stands out sharply against older stone. A strange glow-worm lantern gives off a cold, uncanny light near a gate that does not sit still like an ordinary door. Someone else is already there, stalled by the exact same threshold.

---

## GM READ-ALOUD: ON CLOSER INSPECTION
*Use when the PCs inspect the gate, lantern, wall patch, niches, or threshold lane.*

Up close, the masonry has been altered recently and carefully, but not gracefully. The blocked niches feel deliberate, as though something was sealed away or removed in haste. Crushed glass glitters where it should not, waiting to betray a careless step. The glow-worm lantern throws its pale light across the gate and wall in a way that makes some details sharpen while others seem to recede.

---

## GM READ-ALOUD: ON ESCALATION
*Use when the scene turns: noise, delay, magical backlash, local notice, or rising tension.*

The lane stops feeling merely tense and starts feeling watched. A shutter cracks open somewhere above, the dog’s barking sharpens, and the gate’s resistance turns from muttering refusal to active hostility. What was a quiet threshold is now a problem, and everyone in the area seems one step closer to knowing it.

---

## GM READ-ALOUD: ON SUCCESS *(OPTIONAL)*
*Use when the obstacle yields or the party gains a clear edge.*

With a final shift—whether spiritual, structural, or simply well-read—the threshold gives. The resistance slackens, the route ahead opens, and the space beyond suddenly feels closer than it had any right to. Whatever lies past the gate is now a new problem, but at least it is the next one.

## SCENE TRUTH (WHAT’S REALLY GOING ON)
This is actually a socially contested occult threshold, not just a locked upper-district gate.

- The gate has been spiritually disturbed, inhabited, or activated.
- The blocked niches and fresh wall patch suggest recent tampering, sealing, reinforcement, or a failed bypass attempt.
- Someone benefits from the threshold remaining difficult to cross.
- The obvious clue is real, but partial: the gate is only one piece of the problem.
- Use **Obstacle Functional Reality** to define what the threshold truly wants.
- Use **Lantern Truth** and **Discovery Target** to decide what the site reveals if the PCs probe deeper.

### Possible reads
- **Innocent:** panic led to clumsy repairs and the spirit is reactive, not malicious.
- **Criminal:** someone sealed or hid something behind the wall or beyond the gate.
- **Factional:** the threshold protects a covert route, cache, or controlled interior.
- **Decoy:** the gate is the loud problem; the real solution is in the wall or vertical route.

---

## CONTROL DIALS

**Suspicion (0–3):** how much nearby residents, staff, or guards notice the PCs  
• 0 = ignored  
• 1 = someone is listening / watching  
• 2 = windows open, questions begin  
• 3 = alarm, confrontation, or authority response

**Gate Agitation (0–3):** how unstable or hostile the possessed gate becomes  
• 0 = quiet resistance  
• 1 = murmurs / tremors / warning signs  
• 2 = active refusal / magical pushback  
• 3 = loud supernatural reaction / hazard flare / hard denial

**Position (0–2):** who is closer to resolving the threshold first  
• 0 = neutral  
• 1 = one side gains advantage  
• 2 = one side is about to get through

---

## INTERACTABLES (GM CONTROL PANEL)

**Possessed Gate:** the central obstacle  
• Use: confront directly, calm, inspect, negotiate around, or exploit with the right approach  
• Cost: noise, delay, supernatural reaction, or social exposure  
• Twist: its true behavior is determined when the PCs first test it

**Glow-Worm Lantern:** quiet uncanny light source  
• Use: inspect the gate, patchwork, hidden glass, niches, or magical residue  
• Cost: carrying or repositioning it may expose the PCs  
• Twist: its exact function is determined when the PCs read the site

**Blocked Wall Niches:** sealed recesses in the masonry  
• Use: inspect for offerings, ward gaps, hidden mechanisms, or signs of tampering  
• Cost: time and obvious scrutiny  
• Twist: one niche may matter more than the gate itself

**Fresh Wall Patch:** new mortar/plaster against older masonry  
• Use: probe for a hidden route, failed bypass, or concealed object  
• Cost: suspicious snooping; possible backlash  
• Twist: it may be a decoy patch

**Hazard Lane:** a dangerous strip near the threshold  
• Use: avoid, bait others into, or analyze before crossing  
• Cost: damage, exposure, or forced repositioning  
• Twist: its exact form is determined when passage is attempted

## ROLL WHEN TRIGGERED
### 1d6 What is the Gate?
*Roll when the PCs first test, challenge, or negotiate with the gate.*

| Roll | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Bound Witness** | The spirit wants its death, betrayal, or last sight acknowledged before passage. |
| 2 | **False Authority Lock** | The spirit only yields to a title, token, or command phrase, real or convincingly faked. |
| 3 | **Misidentification** | The gate mistakes intruders for an old enemy and must be corrected, calmed, or tricked. |
| 4 | **Ritual Interruption** | The binding is unstable because a ward, offering, or sequence was recently disrupted. |
| 5 | **Threshold Test** | The spirit is intentionally screening entrants, looking for fear, guilt, status, or purpose. |
| 6 | **Decoy Gate** | The spirit is real, but the true access solution lies in the patched wall, blocked niches, or vertical route. |

### 1d6 What Does the Lantern Reveal?
*Roll when the PCs slow down and inspect the lantern, patchwork, gate, or wall.*

| Roll | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Reveals invisible sigils** | Hidden markings appear around the gate and wall. |
| 2 | **Shows fresh spirit residue** | The lantern exposes where someone tampered recently. |
| 3 | **Points toward the true weak point** | It brightens near the correct niche, seam, or hinge. |
| 4 | **Pacifies the gate briefly** | Held in the right place, it calms the spirit for a moment. |
| 5 | **Replays a fragment of the last intrusion** | Shadow, motion, or color echoes what happened here before. |
| 6 | **Marks liars or the uninvited** | The wrong person glows visibly for a few moments. |

*Roll when the PCs cross the threshold lane, rush the approach, or force passage through the danger zone.*

| Roll | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|

|---|---|---|

## PLAYER APPROACHES

### 1) Work the obstacle together
- **Goal:** cooperate with the other NPC to quiet, interpret, or bypass the gate
- **Success:** passage opens or a safer approach becomes obvious
- **Mixed:** progress is made, but Suspicion or Gate Agitation rises
- **Fail:** the alliance frays or the gate rejects the attempt loudly

### 2) Compete, bluff, or outmaneuver
- **Goal:** beat the NPC through, manipulate them, or use them as cover
- **Success:** the party gains threshold advantage
- **Mixed:** they gain position, but create a future rival or social complication
- **Fail:** the NPC, gate, or district turns against them

### 3) Read the site first
- **Goal:** inspect lantern, patchwork, niches, glass, and spirit signs before acting
- **Success:** uncover the true weak point or recent tampering
- **Mixed:** gain a clue, but lose time or initiative
- **Fail:** miss the real answer, make noise, or trigger the wrong feature

### 4) Use the vertical route
- **Goal:** exploit ladder, shaft, hoist, or drop for an alternate approach
- **Success:** gain an alternate line of entry or positional edge
- **Mixed:** bypass part of the obstacle, but remain exposed or split
- **Fail:** the route is unstable, loud, or still gated by the same occult problem

---

## DISCOVERY PROCEDURE
Roll only when the PCs commit time, risk, or focused scrutiny.

To find or decode the threshold’s hidden truth, the PCs must do one of these:
- deliberate search of the patch, niches, and glass-strewn approach
- observe how the lantern changes what can be seen
- probe the other NPC about what they know
- manipulate the environment or test the alternate route

**Success:** reveal the true weak point, recent tampering, or the nature of the spirit-binding.  
**Mixed:** reveal something useful, but raise Suspicion, Gate Agitation, or Position.  
**Fail:** stay outside, misread the site, or worsen the threshold state.
