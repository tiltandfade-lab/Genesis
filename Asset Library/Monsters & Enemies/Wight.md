*p. 321 MM 2024*

A wight is what happens when a warrior of exceptional will and bitter ambition refuses to accept death. Where most undead are animated from outside — raised by a necromancer or haunted by grief — a wight chooses its own transformation. The warrior's hatred, pride, or thirst for vengeance is so consuming that it burns through death itself, leaving behind a cold, intelligent, purposeful undead creature.

Wights retain full memory, personality, and tactical intelligence. They remember who they were. They simply no longer care about the things the living care about — warmth, companionship, rest. What remains is the drive. A wight that died seeking revenge is still seeking it. A wight that died protecting a border will still be patrolling that border.

The Life Drain is not simply physical damage — it leaves the victim feeling hollow, wrong, as if something essential has been taken. A creature killed by a wight's Life Drain rises as a zombie under the wight's command within 24 hours, and this is how wights build armies. A single wight left in a crypt can, over months or years, create a sizeable force of zombie servants from wandering travelers or foolish tomb robbers.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Wights are among the most narratively interesting undead because they have *reasons*.

- **The Frozen Purpose:** A wight will always be found doing something related to its death-obsession. A fallen paladin wight maintains a perimeter. A betrayed noble wight is sitting on a throne in a ruined keep. The activity reveals who they were. A DC 14 History or Religion check on the environment can identify the wight's original identity.
- **The Zombie Army:** Finding a coordinated force of zombies with no apparent necromancer usually means a wight is nearby and commanding them. The zombies move too purposefully — they're being directed.
- **The Warning Signs:** NPCs in the area describe a cold presence at night, strange patrols in the fog, livestock found drained and hollow. A DC 13 Religion check correctly identifies these as wight signs, not ghost or specter activity.

### 1. Wight

An undead warrior of terrible will and cold intelligence, commanding its own growing force of zombie thralls.

_Medium Undead, Neutral Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 14 (studded leather)
- **Hit Points:** 45 (6d8 + 18)
- **Speed:** 30 ft.
- **STR:** 15 (+2) | **DEX:** 14 (+2) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 10 (+0) | **WIS:** 13 (+1) | **CHA:** 15 (+2)
- **Skills:** Perception +3, Stealth +4
- **Damage Resistances:** Necrotic; Bludgeoning, Piercing, and Slashing from nonmagical attacks that aren't silvered
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison
- **Condition Immunities:** Exhaustion, Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 13
- **Languages:** the languages it knew in life
- **Challenge:** 3 (700 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Sunlight Sensitivity:** While in sunlight, the wight has Disadvantage on attack rolls and on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The wight makes two Longsword attacks or two Longbow attacks. It can replace one attack with a Life Drain attack.
- **Life Drain:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one creature. _Hit:_ 5 (1d6 + 2) necrotic damage. The target must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or its hit point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the damage taken. This reduction lasts until the target finishes a long rest. The target dies if this effect reduces its hit point maximum to 0. A humanoid slain by this attack rises 24 hours later as a zombie under the wight's control, unless the humanoid is restored to life or its body is destroyed. The wight can have no more than twelve zombies under its control at one time.
- **Longsword:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 6 (1d8 + 2) slashing damage, or 7 (1d10 + 2) slashing damage if used with two hands.
- **Longbow:** _Ranged Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, range 150/600 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 6 (1d8 + 2) piercing damage.

### 2. Wight Lord

A wight of great age and power — a former commander or king who has spent centuries cultivating its undead army. The Wight Lord retains enough of its former authority to demand acknowledgment even from the living.

_Medium Undead, Neutral Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 16 (chain mail)
- **Hit Points:** 75 (10d8 + 30)
- **Speed:** 30 ft.
- **STR:** 17 (+3) | **DEX:** 14 (+2) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 13 (+1) | **WIS:** 14 (+2) | **CHA:** 18 (+4)
- **Saving Throws:** WIS +5, CHA +7
- **Skills:** History +4, Perception +5, Stealth +5
- **Damage Resistances:** Necrotic; Bludgeoning, Piercing, and Slashing from nonmagical attacks that aren't silvered
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison
- **Condition Immunities:** Exhaustion, Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 120 ft., passive Perception 15
- **Languages:** the languages it knew in life
- **Challenge:** 5 (1,800 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +3

**Traits**

- **Sunlight Sensitivity:** While in sunlight, the wight lord has Disadvantage on attack rolls and on Wisdom (Perception) checks that rely on sight.
- **Legendary Resistance (1/Day):** If the wight lord fails a saving throw, it can choose to succeed instead.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The wight lord makes two Longsword attacks. It can replace one attack with a Life Drain attack or a Command the Dead action.
- **Life Drain:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one creature. _Hit:_ 10 (2d6 + 3) necrotic damage. The target must succeed on a DC 15 Constitution saving throw or its hit point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the damage taken. A humanoid slain by this attack rises 24 hours later as a zombie under the wight lord's control. The wight lord can have up to twenty zombies under its control at one time.
- **Longsword:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 7 (1d8 + 3) slashing damage.
- **Command the Dead (1/Day):** The wight lord issues a command to up to six undead it can see within 60 ft. Those undead may immediately move up to their speed and make one attack.
- **Terrifying Gaze (Recharge 5–6):** The wight lord fixes its terrible gaze on one creature within 30 ft. The target must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw or be **Frightened** of the wight lord for 1 minute. A frightened creature can repeat the save at the end of each of its turns, ending the effect on a success.

### d10 The Hollow Keep (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Commander's Log** | A detailed military log in the wight's hand, maintained across decades. The entries begin in the wight's living years and continue, without interruption, through its transformation. The handwriting never changes. |
| **2** | **The Empty Cradles** | A small chamber off the main hall contains miniature furniture — a child's room, preserved. The wight was a parent. Whatever purpose keeps it here is connected to this. |
| **3** | **The Hollow Men** | Five zombies in this room are dressed in formal court attire, seated at a long table set for dinner. The wight has been maintaining a mockery of its former court. |
| **4** | **The Binding Circle** | A magical circle etched into the floor shows signs of recent reinforcement — someone has been maintaining it. Someone *living* has been containing this wight intentionally, perhaps for leverage. |
| **5** | **The Trophy Wall** | The skulls of adventurers who previously attempted this dungeon are mounted on a wall, each labeled with a date. The wight keeps track. The most recent skull is months old — someone was here before the party. |
| **6** | **The Old Deed** | A land deed written in the wight's living hand claims ownership of the land above this dungeon — land that now belongs to someone else. The wight's undead persistence is technically a legal complication. |
| **7** | **The Drained Merchant** | A recently drained body still carries trade goods. A DC 12 Investigation check reveals the goods are addressed to a specific location — the merchant was delivering something here, which means someone above ground expected this delivery. |
| **8** | **The Witness Zombie** | One of the wight's zombie servants is dressed differently from the others — in clothes that match a description the party was given of a missing person. The wight converted them. Their face is recognizable. |
| **9** | **The Sealed Letter** | A letter, sealed and addressed to a living person, sits on a desk. The wight wrote it recently — the ink isn't fully dry. It's a demand, a threat, or a declaration of continued existence to someone who thought it was safely contained. |
| **10** | **The Restoration Record** | A cleric's journal, found among the bodies, documents several failed attempts to turn or destroy the wight lord. The final entry describes a vulnerability — a specific item, a name, or a divine sequence — that might give the party an edge the clerics never got to use. |
