*p. 144 MM 2024*

The gold dragon is the most powerful, majestic, and aloof of the metallic dragons. Dedicated foes of evil, they are notoriously private, preferring to lair near idyllic lakes or mist-shrouded ruins. Despite their awesome might, they often travel in the guise of a humble peddler, wandering minstrel, or stray animal, observing the world and offering unseen help to the worthy.

A gold dragon’s diet consists almost entirely of pearls and gems, and they are easily won over by gifts of such treasure—provided they are not offered as bribes.

- **Foretelling Fog.** The area within 1 mile of the lair is Lightly Obscured by opalescent fog. Creatures in that area can't be surprised, as the fog swirls into shapes that warn of danger.

### Older Edition Tactical Variants (2024 Escalations)

To add dynamic flair to boss encounters, you can implement these mechanics synthesized from 4e sources:

- **Fiery Wing Riposte (Reaction):** When the dragon is hit by a creature adjacent to it, the dragon can force the attacker to make a Strength saving throw. On a failure, the target is pushed 10 feet and takes ongoing Fire damage.
- **Burning Tomb (Recharge 5-6):** The dragon spits a concentrated burst of fire at a point within 60 feet. It creates a 10-foot radius zone of intense heat. Creatures starting their turn in the zone take Fire damage, and those who fail a Dexterity save upon the initial blast are _Restrained_ (immobilized) by molten slag.

### d10 Gold Dragon Encounters & Motives

Gold dragons rarely engage the party as immediate combatants, often preferring to test them in disguise or enlist them for cosmic errands.

|d10|Encounter Premise|Campaign Tone / Mechanics|
|:--|:--|:--|
|**1**|**The Bumbling Peddler**|**Humorous:** The party meets a seemingly incompetent, elderly merchant whose cart is stuck in the mud. If they help, the "merchant" rewards them with incredibly rare pearls, revealing their true draconic nature as they fly away.|
|**2**|**The Pet Stray**|**Lighthearted:** A stray dog or cat adopts the party's rogue. It is actually a young gold dragon in disguise, tagging along merely to watch the chaotic mortals out of boredom and curiosity.|
|**3**|**The Pearl Ransom**|**Intrigue:** A local lord's treasury of priceless pearls was "robbed." The thief was a gold dragon demanding the pearls as a tax for keeping the region safe from a nearby chromatic threat.|
|**4**|**The Impatient Oracle**|**Mystical:** The party seeks a legendary sage in an idyllic lake ruin. The sage is a massive adult gold dragon who will only answer their questions if they survive its _Weakening Breath_ test of fortitude.|
|**5**|**The Unwitting Shock Troops**|**Tactical:** An adult gold dragon has manipulated a local tribe of minotaurs or trolls to defend its lair, reasoning that evil creatures are best utilized as disposable pawns for a greater good.|
|**6**|**The Vault of Secrets**|**Heist:** The dragon has sealed away an artifact of terrible evil in its impeccably guarded vault. The party needs the item to stop a villain, forcing them to negotiate with or steal from the most paranoid warden in existence.|
|**7**|**The Celestial Emissary**|**High Fantasy:** The dragon arrives flanked by angels or devas. It brings a divine mandate that the party must cease their current quest, setting up a moral conflict between mortal needs and cosmic law.|
|**8**|**The Scorched Earth**|**Dark Fantasy:** An ancient gold dragon has decided a local city is too corrupt to be saved. It takes the "foe of evil" mandate to the extreme, burning the city to the ground in a ruthless display of absolute justice.|
|**9**|**The Lair's Beacon**|**Investigation:** The party is lost in a massive, misty cavern system. They realize their own gems and pearls have begun to magically glow and shed light, indicating they have accidentally wandered within 1 mile of a sleeping Ancient Gold's hoard.|
|**10**|**The Fiendish Sickness**|**Gritty / Horror:** A gold dragon wyrmling has been corrupted by dark magic or swallowed a cursed gemstone. The normally benevolent creature is rampaging through a village, and the party must subdue it without killing it.|
