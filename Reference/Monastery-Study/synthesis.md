## 1. The one-sentence result

Across the ideal plan, the standardised order plan, built survivors on flat and vertical
ground, five other institutions on the same bones, and the FFT institutional cohort, the
monastery converges on: **a continuous built perimeter enclosing one reserved void, entered
at one filtered threshold, faced inside by a repeated unit, with one collective room that
declares its authority by standing on a low raised platform at the head of a long space —
and the only member that really changes between institutions is the walk.**

## 3. The chassis delta sheet (condensed — full table in lane 3 §2)

**The invariant, six clauses:** continuous perimeter · one controlled entrance, and it is
where the outward ornament goes · ranges of repeated identical units · one privileged
collective room · a perimeter circulation path · one object in the void, or none.

**The member that changes is the walk:**

| setting | institution | what it says about the rule of the house |
|---|---|---|
| continuous **covered arcade**, vaulted | monastery | the community moves as one body |
| **open path + periodic doors** to separate staircases | college / school | members are private; the yard is shared, the routes are not |
| **tiered open timber galleries** | hospital, inn, fortified church | the yard is serviced from above; the gallery is the corridor |
| **no perimeter walk at all** | prison | circulation is internal and controlled; the yard is subdivided |
| **path through a planted garden** | almshouse | the void becomes amenity; the institution is domestic |

**Second-order deltas worth their own line:** where ornament is spent (monastery: gate and
fountain house · college: gate tower and hall windows · hospital: **the roof** · inn: the
carriage arch · prison: nowhere, on purpose) · the outward face (ordinary openings → blank) ·
the void (reserved emptiness → subdivided → planted) · the vertical (bell → surveillance →
none).

**FFT independently ships four of these on the same bones** — school hall (24), hospital
(33), granary (65), execution yard (63) — which is evidence the chassis survives translation
to tactical scale, not just to another century.

## 4. THE HILLSIDE VERDICT

> **The stepped courtyard rising to the sanctuary has genuine, ancient, well-documented
> precedent — but not in the cloister tradition. It comes from the terraced sanctuary
> (Palestrina's six terraces with ramps and an axial stair to a summit temple; Kos's three
> terraces with monumental staircases) and from Himalayan monastic practice (courtyard →
> hall → temple, ascending from the gate). The Latin claustral cloister is essentially always
> level, because a covered walk running on all four sides with four doors off it needs one
> datum. The RULED seed is therefore a legitimate HYBRID — a cloister plan on a
> terraced-sanctuary section — and should be adopted as one, with open eyes.**

**What holds, strongly.** "Gate low" is the best-evidenced clause in the entire ruling: every
terraced case in the study arrives at the foot of the climb — Palestrina, Key Gompa,
Tashilhunpo, Sumela, Prejmer, Biertan, and FFT's own Orbonne, which climbs a causeway from a
timber bridge over water to a spired church door. "A fight that climbs" is real, and FFT
already built it.

**What the evidence argues with — one clause.** "the courtyard climbs in three broad steps…
**arcade wrapping all four sides**." A continuous level walk cannot wrap a sloping yard. Every
real precedent solves this a different way, and none of them is "arcade around a slope":

| reconciliation | precedent | what it costs, what it buys |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Arcade on the terrace face** | Palestrina; FFT Orbonne's causeway flank | cheapest; the arcade is seen from below and retains the level above. You lose the wrapped yard and gain a monumental climb. |
| **B. Level yard + one big flight at the head** | Key Gompa, Tashilhunpo, Rila | keeps the wrapped arcade intact; the climb becomes one broad stair into the sanctuary. Least new geometry. |
| **C. Two level courts at different heights** | the dzong (Paro's administrative court ~6 m above the monastic one) | most geometry; buys **two distinct tactical rooms** and a defensible internal stair. |

**Two further honest notes.** (i) Real compounds concentrate their height change into one or
two monumental moves, never an even gradient — so "three broad steps" is more than any
precedent in the study uses. (ii) No case in this set puts a terraced compound's gate at a
*corner*; the evidenced position is the low **centre**, on the approach axis. A low corner
gate is not contradicted, only unevidenced.

## 5. Tower and defence grammar

- **Six tower positions attested**, and they read as different institutions rather than
  different budgets: detached campanile (Meteora) · west front (FFT 36) · crossing spire
  (FFT 50, 56) · perimeter corner keep (Rila) · central *utse* (dzong) · chamber over the
  gate (Whalley).
- **The belfry's universal tell is paired arched openings near the top** — the arcade bay,
  used vertically. The tower can be built from the arcade piece.
- **The occupancy licence is historically real.** The Cistercian General Chapter *prohibited*
  stone bell towers in 1157, permitted only modest wooden bell turrets, and was still
  arguing about tower height fifty years later. Tower presence is a rule-of-the-house fact,
  exactly as banked — **and the licence can be revoked as well as granted**, which makes a
  towerless house *austere* rather than poor, and a house with a tower it should not have a
  story told in one silhouette.
- **A monastery gate is decorated; a fortified gate is equipped.** One line separates site 4's
  threshold from site 1's using pieces both already have.
- **Defence arrives as four separable additions**, never as a different building: blank
  battered curtain · covered wall-walk with loopholes · refuge cells stacked against the
  inner wall face · a retractable threshold.
- **The cheapest defended monastery is Prejmer's:** the ring wall's inner face *is* the
  three-or-four-tier cell row on timber galleries under one long shed roof. Wall and cell row
  are one piece.
- **The working vertical that needs no military licence is a stair up the inside of the wall
  to a roof** — Prejmer, Sumela, and the caravanserai (Camp-Study lane 3 §6.6). That is the
  obvious default deck for an undefended house.

## 7. THE FOUNDER PACKET

Ten questions, plain English. Each is argued by evidence above; none is answered here.

**1. The stepped yard versus the wrapping arcade — which reconciliation?**
The seed asks for a courtyard that climbs in three broad steps *and* an arcade wrapping all
four sides. No real building does both, because a covered walk that runs level on four sides
with four doors off it needs one datum. Real precedent offers three ways out: **(A)** put the
arcade on the *terrace face*, seen from below, and let the climb be monumental (Palestrina —
and FFT's own Orbonne does exactly this under its causeway); **(B)** keep the yard level and
put the whole climb in one broad flight at its head, into the chapel (Key Gompa,
Tashilhunpo, Rila); **(C)** two level courts at different heights joined by a stair, the way
a dzong does it — most geometry, but it buys two distinct tactical rooms. Which one is the
monastery?

**2. Is the gate at the low corner, or the low centre?**
"Gate low" is the best-evidenced clause in your whole ruling — every terraced compound in the
study arrives at the foot of the climb. But none of them offsets the gate to a corner; the
evidenced position is the low centre, on the approach axis. A corner gate gives an oblique,
tactically richer entry; a centre gate gives the processional axis for free. Nothing in the
evidence forbids the corner — it just has no precedent behind it.

**3. Is the bedroom row a shared hall, or a row of cells with a hatch and a private garden?**
The mainstream monastic dormitory is **one long room** over an undercroft — no doors, no
cells. The row of individual cells is one order's invention (the Carthusians), and there the
cell is a small two-storey house with **its own walled garden** and a **serving hatch beside
its door onto the walk**. The cell version is far more legible in play (a room you can be
locked in, a hole things get pushed through, a private outdoor space). Which one is the site's
default — and is the other one a rolled occupancy variant?

**4. One yard, or an outer court and an inner cloister?**
Every real precinct has two: an outer court holding guests, stables, workshops, bakehouse and
almonry, and an inner cloister that outsiders were not admitted to, with a filter between
them. The seed has one yard. A second court gives you a "you may come this far and no
further" beat and a quieter second threshold; it also doubles the site. Is the monastery a
one-yard house, or is the outer court a scale-up variant?

**5. Does the sanctuary command the head of the yard, or stand free in the middle of it?**
The deck ruling puts the chapel at the high end, and FFT agrees five times over. But Rila —
a mountain monastery with a level yard — puts the church **free in the centre of the void**,
on a low stepped plinth, with the ranges' galleries looking down on it from four sides. Those
are two completely different fights: climbing to a commanding platform, versus circling an
object under overlooking galleries. Is one the default and one a variant, or is it rolled?

**6. Is the covered walk the piece that swaps between institutions?**
The study's strongest chassis finding is that the *walk* carries most of the institutional
difference: continuous covered arcade (monastery, communal) → open path with periodic doors
to separate staircases (college, cellular) → tiered open timber galleries (hospital, inn,
fortified church) → no perimeter walk at all (prison). One member, four settings, and it
encodes the rule of the house. Do you want the walk declared as the chassis's swap member —
which is what makes "one kit, five-plus institutions" cheap?

**7. Does the room ring get a book room?**
Your ring is chapel, shared hall, identical-bedroom row, garden, well. Both touchstones make
the library the monastery's real second half — BG3's upper level is the former library, and
FFT gives Orbonne **five floors** of underground book storage that carry an entire act. The
book room is also where FFT's best repeated-bay geometry lives. Should the library join the
ring, and if so is it above ground or the way down?

**8. Is the ceremonial gate the only way in?**
The capsule says one ceremonial gate, and the evidence supports that as the *formal* answer.
But BG3's monastery documents a defended front gate **plus** a broken window into the wine
cellar, and the real vertical houses had bolt-holes of their own (a winch, a ladder that
comes down). A single entrance makes a clean siege and a poor infiltration. Should the site
carry one ceremonial gate plus one honest secondary way — a service door, a broken window, a
drain, a wall stair?

**9. When the house is defended, is the wall the cell row?**
The cheapest fortified monastery in the whole study is the Transylvanian fortified church:
the ring wall's inner face **is** three or four tiers of identical small doors on exposed
timber galleries and steep stairs, all under one long shed roof, with the church free in the
yard in front. That reuses the repeated-cell piece as fortification and gets the wall walk
for nothing. Is that the defended variant's construction, or do you want the wall and the
cells to stay separate objects?

**10. Can the tower licence be revoked as well as granted?**
Your ruling licenses the bell tower to militant/defended orders. The history is better than
that: the Cistercians *legislated stone bell towers out of existence* in 1157, allowed only
modest wooden bell turrets, and kept having to re-enforce it because houses built them
anyway. That gives you a second, free narrative axis — a towerless house can be **austere**
rather than poor, and a house with a tower its rule forbids is a story visible in one
silhouette. Do you want "forbids its own tower" as a rollable occupancy result?

## 8. What this study could not do

- **No measured section of a terraced monastery, and no metric bay width.** Every proportion
  in lanes 1 and 2 is read off photographs and models. The seed's "three broad steps" cannot
  be checked against a real gradient from this evidence, and the arcade bay has no number.
- **Mont-Sainte-Odile and Mont-Saint-Michel are both absent.** Commons returned no usable
  image for either (the Mont-Sainte-Odile file retrieved under that name proved to be an
  unrelated wood carving and was deleted rather than ledgered). The hillside argument rests
  on Palestrina/Kos, the Himalayan set, Meteora, Sumela, Rila and the dzong; the fortified
  abbey rests on Sinai, Prejmer, Biertan and the dzong.
- **No militant-order house.** Tomar and the Templar/Hospitaller commandery type were not
  pulled in time. The "militant order" occupancy is argued from fortified *parish* churches
  and from the dzong — a real substitution, and it should be checked before that variant is
  specified.
- **No FFT Orbonne staging documentation.** Two wiki sources refused automated fetch (HTTP
  403 and 402); the guide that did return says only that the opening battle is a tutorial.
  Nothing about deployment, elevation or chokepoints on map 56 is asserted.
- **The FFT classification is name-primed, and sampling is uneven.** All 121 ids were
  classified from `map.c`; only 25 maps were actually viewed, and only two of those in all
  five views. An institutional interior hiding inside a castle- or city-named id is a low but
  real residual risk, and anything said about an unviewed rotation is unverified.
- **Lane 6 has no imagery, by boundary choice.** Game screenshots may not be committed to this
  repo. The Rosymorn reading is what published documentation reports, not what I observed;
  "triangular terrace," "oval colonnade" and "three levels" are quoted from guides and would
  need confirming in-game if any of them becomes load-bearing.
- **Five ledgered images were retrieved but not read** (marked ✖ in `SOURCE-LEDGER.md`), and
  one file's name is a misnomer for what it actually shows. Nothing in the lane notes rests
  on any of them, and the ledger says so.
- **The prison case has no interior-yard photography**, and the school case is one example
  wide (a collegiate quadrangle). The madrasa and grammar-school courtyard types are
  unresearched.
