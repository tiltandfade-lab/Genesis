# Lane 5 — Touchstones: the occupied-site social-then-violent arc

**The question.** Site 2's social front door — "faction state + player relationship selects battle /
hello / trade / tribute" — needs a shape. The camp is the roster's first site where the player is
likely to *walk in and talk* before anything else happens. Two well-studied designs solve exactly
that, and the brief names both.

---

## 1. The goblin camp — the escalation ladder as physical layers

**Structure (public documentation, cross-checked against play).** The site comes in two bound
pieces: an **outer camp** in the open air, and the **Shattered Sanctum**, an interior beyond it.
The outer camp is entered across a **stone bridge studded with wooden barricades** at a **manned
checkpoint**; a **trader stands near the camp's entrance** and will do business if the player is not
yet hostile; the far end of the outer yard has **heavy doors** into the temple ruin; and inside, a
long entry passage opens into a main hall with side spaces and a separated northern end where the
three leaders are.

Three access routes exist into the outer camp: the front bridge/checkpoint, a **fallen log crossing**
(with trapped approaches), and a **rocky outcropping reachable by a jump** that admits the player
unseen. And above the main interior door there is **a cracked wall that can be broken to reach the
rafters**, giving the high ground inside.

**What that is, structurally.**

1. **Threshold → yard → interior → sanctum.** Four zones, each a decision point, each with its own
   social temperature. The camp's aggression rises as you go deeper; the outer zone is where trade
   happens and the inner zone is where the fight is.
2. **The front door is social, and it is *manned*.** You are stopped and spoken to before you are
   attacked. That is the guard post's "front door is the social loop, combat its failure mode,"
   applied to an occupied site.
3. **There are always at least three ways in, and they differ in what they cost you.** The front way
   costs a conversation. The log costs a risk. The jump costs an ability check and pays back
   surprise.
4. **Verticality is a second, hidden route** — the rafters. The high ground is available to whoever
   thinks to look up.
5. **The population is graded** — rank-and-file in the yard, named leaders in the innermost room.
   The spatial depth *is* the roster hierarchy.

**Transfer to Site 2.** The camp brief already has the pieces to do all five: the apron is the yard,
the crude barricade is the checkpoint, the wagon and the knoll are the high ground, and the
occupancy roll supplies the graded roster. The one thing the goblin camp has that the camp brief
does not is **an interior to escalate into**. A tent is not a room. That is a real structural gap
and it is the sharpest question this lane produces — see synthesis Q7.

## 2. The druid grove — the fortified commune-camp hybrid

**Structure.** The grove is a compound of named sub-places: the outer **Emerald Grove**, **the
Hollow** (where the refugee population and several traders are gathered), the **Sacred Pool** in a
basin, the **Inner Sanctum** behind a stone door, a **Secluded Chamber**, an **underground passage**,
a **hideout**, and a **makeshift prison**. Its entrance is **an overgrown portcullis operated by a
winch above the gate** — a real controlled threshold. At the heart, an idol stands at the centre of
a **stone circle in a basin**, surrounded by druids performing a rite.

**What that is, structurally.**

1. **Two populations sharing one site, with a spatial boundary between them.** Hosts occupy the
   sacred core; refugees occupy the Hollow. The tension of the place is legible as a plan.
2. **It is literally the camp brief's guest-family property.** A refugee encampment is hosted inside
   another site, capped by the host, with its own traders and its own social front door — and the
   friction between guest and host is the story. Genesis's ruling that camps roll *inside* hosts is
   validated by the single best-known example of the pattern in the medium.
3. **The centre is a reserved basin with one object in it.** Idol in a stone circle, in a bowl of
   ground. Lane 3 found the same relation at every service site; lane 4 found it at Public
   Cemetery. **Three independent sources now say: reserve the centre, put one thing in it.**
4. **The threshold is a real gate with a real mechanism** — but this site *is* a fortified commune,
   not a waystation. It is the counter-example that proves the camp's apron-without-gate is a
   deliberate choice rather than an oversight: the moment a camp gains a gate, it stops being a
   camp.
5. **Trade lives in the crowded zone, not at the centre.** The traders are in the Hollow with the
   refugees.

## 4. Findings (plain English)

1. **Both touchstones escalate through physical layers, not through dialogue trees.** Outer yard →
   interior → inner room. The social-then-violent arc is a *plan*, and the plan is what the
   generator has to produce.
2. **Both put trade in the crowded outer zone**, never at the site's sacred or command centre. The
   camp's apron should therefore be a *populated* zone, not just a wear pattern.
3. **Both give the player at least three ways in, priced differently** — a conversation, a risk, and
   a skill check that buys surprise. The camp brief has not yet stated an ingress-variety rule, and
   the evidence from lanes 2–3 (single controlled entrance) actively pushes the other way. This is a
   genuine tension worth a founder ruling.
4. **The grove is the guest-family property, shipped.** A hosted refugee population, capped by its
   host, with its own traders and its own front door, and the guest/host friction as the story.
   Genesis's ruling is validated by the best-known instance of the pattern.
5. **The moment a camp gains a working gate it stops being a camp.** The grove has a winch-operated
   portcullis and reads as a fortified commune. That is exactly the boundary the
   apron-without-gate ruling draws, and the touchstone confirms which side of it Site 2 sits on.
6. **The centre is reserved and holds one object.** Idol in a basin (grove), fountain in a court
   (caravanserai, lane 3), one small structure in an open avenue (Public Cemetery, lane 4), fire in
   a ring (the camp capsule). Four independent sources agree. This is the single most-confirmed
   relation in the whole study.
7. **The camp's structural gap versus both touchstones is that it has no interior.** Tents are
   masses, not rooms; the camp cannot escalate inward. The candidate answers are (a) the deck/knoll
   substitutes vertically for what the touchstones do horizontally, (b) the wagon or a big
   pavilion/officer tent becomes the one enterable volume, or (c) the camp accepts a shallower arc
   than an occupied dungeon and that is fine. This is a founder question, not mine.
8. **Hidden verticality is a cheap tactical gift.** The rafters route in the goblin camp is one
   breakable wall. The camp's equivalents already exist (knoll, wagon top, licensed platform) — what
   is missing is the *concealed* one that rewards looking.
