# Camp / Service evidence-thickening — synthesis

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

Three unrelated historical service systems — Roman roads, Scottish drove roads, and the
American Pony Express/stage network — each independently converged on the same two-tier
minimal-stop/rooted-stop shape the permanence ladder already proposes, which is strong
convergent evidence for that structure. All four proposed tent forms check out against real,
well-documented traditions (bell tent, Roman `papilio`, Bedouin black tent, Romani bender),
closing a citation gap on the 10-Roman-foot claim in particular; the proposed build order's
only real tension is that the cheapest form to build (the dome) is scheduled last, which
mirrors a tension the Site 11 founder packet already resolved the same way, for the same
reason. The guest-family mechanism's best real analog, Zaatari refugee camp, supports its
core claim — a hosted camp really can grow a rooted service economy without becoming
architecture — while also showing, empirically and at scale, exactly the upper-boundary
problem `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` already flagged as open. None of the six breadth-
sweep candidates (mansio/mutatio, caravanserai, Pony Express, sutlers, fur-trade rendezvous,
refugee-camp economies) earns a new numbered host under the noun/state test; two of them
(sutlers, trade rendezvous) do surface small, cheap, currently-missing additions to Site 2's
own occupant/occupancy roster.

## 2. Two small, cheap roster additions surfaced by the breadth sweep

Neither is a new host, capsule change, or ladder change. Both are proposed additions to
already-existing lists in the working spec, offered for the founder's convenience rather than
folded in as if ruled:

1. **A sutler/vivandière-class occupant role**, independent of who controls the camp. Real
   armies licensed civilian merchants who sold what the military didn't supply (food, drink,
   laundry, sundries) regardless of whether the camp's controlling faction was friendly or
   hostile to the player. The working spec's occupancy roster currently names *who runs the
   camp*, not *who trades inside it*. `BREADTH-SWEEP.md` §4.
2. **A "trade rendezvous / fair" occupancy variant**, sitting in the existing "Large —
   customary or organized camp" band, Rung 0–1 only (never rooted — that was the entire
   commercial logic of holding one in the wilderness rather than at a fixed post). The
   Rocky Mountain fur-trade rendezvous is the strongest real case: hundreds to thousands of
   traders, weeks-long duration, then complete dispersal, zero permanent structure by design.
   `BREADTH-SWEEP.md` §5.

## 3. One world-generation-adjacent fact, not a Site 2 change

Three of the six breadth-sweep systems (mansio, caravanserai, Pony Express home station) each
independently land near "about a day's travel" spacing for their rooted/staffed tier
(caravanserai ~30–40 km explicitly stated as "a day's caravan journey"; mansio ~40 km; a
day's ride is the implicit logic behind Pony Express home-station spacing). This is a
route/world-generation density fact — how far apart rung-2 waystations should feel along a
generated road — not a Site 2 structural change. Recorded here so it isn't lost; it belongs
to whichever system eventually paces route generation, not to this working spec.
