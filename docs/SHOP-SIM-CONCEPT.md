# Living Miniatures shop sim — one-page concept

**Working title:** TBD (Adam names things). **Form:** shop-management sim in the
Supermarket Simulator / TCG Card Shop Simulator lineage — the genre spine kept faithful,
one hook nobody else has. **Fiction:** you run a tabletop game shop where the merchandise
is alive. **Price shape:** premium ~$13–15 (the TCG Card Shop price point), no IAP;
Early Access content updates = new realm product lines. **Job in the portfolio:** the
revenue shot; the vertical slice doubles as the publisher pitch.

## The two layers

- **The proven spine (the genre contract — don't innovate here):** order wholesale →
  shelve and price → serve the counter → pay rent → expand the floor → unlock product
  tiers → (late-game) hire help. Genre buyers come for this loop; it stays orthodox.
- **The living layer (the hook — all differentiation budget goes here):** minis on
  display come alive. Customers play at demo tables and their minis actually fight
  (tween grammar, no frame animation — the [[AUTOBATTLER-CONCEPT]] animation law).
  Shop decoration IS diorama-building: display tables are built from terrain pieces and
  figurines, and display quality has mechanical teeth (draw + reputation + price
  tolerance). The player's own shop slowly becomes a Genesis diorama.

## Product architecture (the asset flip, made literal)

- **Realms = product lines.** Each Genesis realm is a merchandise family: starter sets,
  booster packs, single figurines, terrain kits. Unlocking new realm lines is the
  content progression; the loot rarity axis prices singles and seeds pack odds.
- **Sprite→3D as in-game progression:** budget stock = flat cardboard standees (the
  pixel sprite register); premium lines = full 3D minis. Diegetic, mirrors the real
  production pipeline, and lets the game ship gracefully mid-art-transition.
- **The counter pack-open moment** (TCG Card Shop's dopamine centerpiece, kept):
  customers open boosters at the counter and react; rare pulls animate — a mini comes
  alive in their hands. The clip-generator moment; polish it beyond its mechanical weight.
- **The in-shop game:** customers at demo tables play *the autobattler* — v1 simulates
  it abstractly (minis brawl ambiently); the real embed (playable at the table) is the
  later flip once the autobattler exists. Cross-promotion made diegetic.
- **Night mode (taste call, small):** after close, the shop stirs — minis rearrange,
  patrol shelves, tiny vignettes. Pure ambience, zero systems, capped scope. It exists
  because it is the single most streamable/clippable thing in the game.

## Customers & demand

Archetypes with wants (the kid with allowance · the collector · the competitive player ·
the parent buying a gift · the lore nerd), pulling from the NPC skin/role corpus. Weekly
demand trends per realm line (a realm "gets hot" — the real TCG market, simulated
shallowly: one trend table, not an economy engine). Satisfaction → reputation → traffic.

still builds ONE winner only; no dual-camera production.

## The diorama editor (Adam, 2026-07-26)

The decorating layer is a diorama EDITOR by necessity, so it is built as one — under a
scope law: **constrained kit placement, not a freeform gizmo tool.** Curated parts per
realm line, grid/anchor snapping, place-and-rotate simplicity, undo + save — nothing
more. (Editors are scope sinks; the constraint is the savior.) UI rides the shared kit;
parts come from the structure-kit/place-parts/prop registers. Dioramas serialize as
data (parts + positions + skin refs — the usual manifest seams), which opens two later
flips, both explicitly speculative and NOT v1:

1. **Player dioramas import into the flagship** — UGC; sits behind the flagship's own
   freezes and moderation questions. A promise to explore, never a v1 commitment.
2. **Adam-authored iconic scenes enter the procedural pool** — hand-built set-piece
   dioramas become templates the engine can roll and reskin per realm. This is the
   golden-site methodology (authored family + procedural variation) given a GUI: the
   editor doubles as internal authoring tooling for the flagship regardless of whether
   player import ever ships.

## Tech & art

Walk/theater engine as the lens; game logic standalone and headless-testable (same
doctrine as everything). The shop interior is a *site* — the structure-kit/place-parts
lane literally feeds shop fixtures and expansion floors. UI reuses the autobattler's UI
kit (ordering, pricing, inventory = the same component grammar; the kit was built to flip).
Icons/packaging art ride the Codex icon pipeline. Fiction continuity: display tables may
depict actual Genesis worlds (the flagship's universe, glimpsed from the shop floor).

## Vertical slice = the publisher pitch (definition)

One shop room · one realm product line end-to-end (order → shelve → sell) · five customer
archetypes · the counter pack-open moment fully polished · ONE living-table moment ·
chosen camera only. Nothing else. This slice is what sim-genre publishers see at Next Fest.

## v1 cut lines (hard)

No multiplayer · no real autobattler embed (simulated tables only) · no market/economy
engine beyond the weekly trend table · no employee management beyond late-game minimal
hiring · SKU count capped per realm line · no real-money anything beyond the premium
price · night mode ships only if it stays ambient (the moment it grows systems, cut it).
