# Lane 1 — Tent forms across cultures (the culture channel)

**The question this lane answers.** Adam ruled that tent FORMS are culture-expressive — "what
different forms would different cultures use for tents" — and that the silhouette must carry the
cultural read at gameplay scale without signage or hue shifts. This lane extracts, per form: what
survives the production camera, the footprint and pole logic, the §5 geometry/paint split applied
per form, and how each tradition expresses status (troop vs officer vs chief).

**Companion sheet:** `tent-form-silhouettes.svg` — the nine forms drawn side-by-side at one shared
scale with the 2.5 ft vertical quantum ruled across them. Read it beside this file.

---

## 0. The three findings that came out of the images before any single form did

These emerged from looking at all nine families together and matter more than any one form.

**F1 — There are only THREE silhouette genera, not nine.** At gameplay scale every tent tradition
collapses into one of three mass archetypes, and the nine named forms are parameter variations
inside them:

| genus | mass | forms that live in it | what the eye reads first |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CONE** | single apex over a round base | tipi · lavvu · bell/Sibley · round pavilion | the apex, and what the apex does at the top |
| **DRUM** | vertical cylinder wall + shallow cap | ger/yurt | the horizontal wall/roof break |
| **SPAN** | low horizontal cloth stretched between poles | Bedouin black tent · wedge/A-frame · Roman papilio · marquee | the ridge line and the ground-to-ridge slope |
| *(deformed span)* | span pulled down to the ground on all sides | wickiup/bender (dome — a span with no ridge) | the absence of any straight line |

The cultural read is NOT carried by the genus alone (two cultures share CONE). It is carried by
**apex treatment, wall/roof break, and plan aspect ratio** — three cheap geometric facts. That is
the practical version of Adam's ruling: culture selects a mass tier, and the mass tier is three
numbers, not an art order.

**F2 — The cheapest cultural signal in the whole set is what happens ABOVE the apex.** The tipi's
projecting pole crown, the ger's stove pipe, the pavilion's finial-and-pennant, the lavvu's shorter
pole splay, the bell tent's bare point: five distinct reads, all in the top 1h (2.5 ft) of the
model, all silhouette-only. Evidence: `l1-tipi-great-camp-02.jpg` (dozens of cones at distance —
only the crossed pole crowns distinguish them from anything else at that range),
`l2-tipi-village-hill.jpg` (a ridge line of cones read against sky; the crown is the entire read),
`l1-ger-camp-01.jpg` (the stove pipe is the tallest and darkest thing in the frame and reads before
the ger does), `l1-pavilion-caerlaverock-02.jpg` (ball finial), `l1-bell-single-02.jpg` (bare
point). This is the single highest-value geometry pennyworth in the lane.

**F3 — Decoration really is paint, and the reference proves it hard.** The Caerlaverock pavilion
(`l1-pavilion-caerlaverock-02.jpg`) is a plain cone with a short vertical skirt; everything that
makes it read as "a lord's tent" is a two-colour gore pattern and a scalloped valance — pure UV
work. The ger (`l1-ger-camp-01.jpg`) is a plain drum; the blue meander band at the hem, the rope
bands and the door textile are all flat pattern. The Bedouin sheikh's tent
(`l1-bedouin-sheikh-03.jpg`) is a plain low span whose entire wealth statement is a woven
geometric-patterned back curtain. **In all three cases the status read is 60–80% paint on an
unchanged mass.** Site 2's LOCKED tent rule therefore survives contact with reality — with one
exception, F5 below.

## 1. Per-form extraction

Grid conventions used throughout: horizontal cell = 5 ft; vertical quantum **h = 2.5 ft**; storey =
4h = 10 ft (STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG §2). Footprints are given in cells so the roller can reserve
ground; heights in h so the deck/cover math stays native.

### 1.1 Ger / yurt — the DRUM

*Evidence: `l1-ger-camp-01.jpg`, `l1-ger-frame-02.jpg`, `l1-yurt-kazakh-03.jpg`,
`l2-mongol-ring-miniature.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette at gameplay scale.** A short vertical cylinder capped by a very shallow cone. The
  *break* between wall and roof is the read — it is the only tent in the set with a true vertical
  wall, and at any distance it is the only tent that reads as a **building**, not a shelter. In
  `l1-ger-camp-01.jpg` the wall is roughly half the total height and the roof slope is shallow
  enough that from a raked camera the drum reads almost as a cylinder with a lid.
- **Real numbers.** A standard five-*khana* ger: 5.8 m diameter, wall height ~1.5 m, apex ~2.3 m —
  i.e. **~4 cells across, 2h wall, 3h total**. Four to eight lattice panels (six typical); up to ~108
  roof poles into a compression ring (*toono*).
- **Footprint + pole logic.** Round plan, no guy lines needed at gameplay scale (compression ring
  + tension band does the work — visible as horizontal rope bands in the photo). The frame photo
  (`l1-ger-frame-02.jpg`) shows the assembly is *panelised*: expanding lattice sections stood in a
  circle, then a door frame, then radial poles. That maps almost one-to-one onto a socketed piece
  set if it is ever wanted — but see the §5 split.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the drum mass; the wall/roof break; **the door frame** (an orthogonal rectangular
    notch in a round mass — gameplay-legible threshold, and the single most characterful piece of
    ger geometry); the **stove pipe** (a 1h vertical stick; cheap, and it is the F2 signal).
  - *Paint:* hem band, meander motif, rope bands, felt patching, door textile, the crown ring's
    radial pattern.
  - *Explicitly not modelled:* lattice, roof poles, crown ring as real geometry — they are an
    interior/crown decal at most.
- **Status/hierarchy.** Expressed by **diameter and count, not by form** — a khan's ger is a bigger
  drum, sometimes with a second ring of gers around it. This is the one form where status is
  legitimately a *scale* variant rather than a *shape* variant, which makes it the cheapest
  hierarchy in the set (one mesh, two scales).
- **Camp-brief fit.** The ger is the strongest candidate for a **rooted/tribal occupancy** and the
  most "permanent-looking" tent — which is a hazard: it reads as rung-2 material even when the camp
  is rung 0. Worth a founder flag (see synthesis Q3).

### 1.2 Tipi — the tall CONE with a crown

*Evidence: `l1-tipi-arapaho-01.jpg`, `l1-tipi-great-camp-02.jpg`, `l2-tipi-village-hill.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** The tallest and steepest cone in the set, and the only one whose **poles project
  past the apex** into a visible splayed crown. Two ear-like smoke flaps sit either side of the
  crown. In `l2-tipi-village-hill.jpg` (a row of tipis on a ridge, shot against sky) the crown is
  legible when the cover itself is barely a value block — this is the gameplay-scale proof.
- **Real numbers.** An 18 ft cover takes ~17 poles ~25 ft long — i.e. **~4 cells across, ~6h to the
  cover apex, poles reaching 1–2h beyond**. The cone is *tilted*, not symmetrical: steeper at the
  back, slacker at the front, so the crown sits off-centre toward the door.
- **Footprint + pole logic.** Three- or four-pole foundation tripod, then the rest laid in; the door
  faces a consistent direction (east, in most Plains practice) — a free, culturally-true rule for
  the roller: **all tipis in one camp face the same way.** That single constraint is what makes the
  Grabill photograph read as one people's camp rather than scattered tents.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the tilted cone; the **projecting pole crown** (a handful of tapered sticks — the
    signature, and non-negotiable if the form is to read); the door as a shallow oval notch.
  - *Paint:* smoke flaps (paint them onto the cone as darker shaped panels rather than modelling
    hinged flaps), painted band/medicine designs, hide seams, lacing pin line, the sooted upper
    cone (a free condition layer — every real tipi is dark at the top).
  - *Ruling risk:* the smoke-flap poles are two extra sticks leaning against the outside. They are
    the difference between "cone with sticks on top" and "tipi." Recommend modelling; flag it.
- **Status/hierarchy.** Plains practice expresses rank by **painted design and position in the
  circle**, not size — a chief's lodge is a normally-sized cone with paint, standing at a named
  place in the ring. That is a *paint + placement* hierarchy, and it is the cleanest possible fit
  for the geometry/paint law. It also means the camp's LAYOUT carries the status information (lane
  2, ring doctrine).

### 1.3 Lavvu — the squat CONE

*Evidence: `l1-lavvu-sami-01.jpg`, `l1-lavvu-modern-02.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** The same genus as the tipi but a **markedly lower, wider, sagging** cone —
  half-angle noticeably fatter, cover slack between poles so the profile is faintly concave rather
  than dead straight, poles projecting in a shorter and messier splay. In the 1890 photochrom
  (`l1-lavvu-sami-01.jpg`) two lavvut sit side by side and the profile sag is the dominant read.
- **Real numbers.** Small — roughly **2.5–3 cells across, 3h high**; the aspect is close to a
  half-height tipi at the same diameter.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* squat cone (a different cone piece, or the same cone with a licensed
    height-scale — that is a real engineering choice, see synthesis Q2); short pole splay.
  - *Paint:* the slack/sag as a normal-map/silhouette-notch cheat, hide seams, soot.
- **Status/hierarchy.** Essentially flat — the lavvu is a subsistence form; a "chief's lavvu" is a
  bigger circle of the same thing. **Useful precisely because it has no hierarchy**: it is the right
  form for a culture the design wants to read as egalitarian/poor.
- **The important negative finding.** Tipi and lavvu are the **least distinguishable pair in the
  set at gameplay scale.** If both enter the kit, they must differ by more than a slider —
  recommend the tipi keeps a straight profile + tall crown and the lavvu gets a bowed profile +
  stub crown, or one of them is cut.

### 1.4 Bedouin black tent — the low SPAN

*Evidence: `l1-bedouin-1912-02.jpg` (the key image), `l1-bedouin-sheikh-03.jpg`,
`l1-bedouin-wadirum-01.jpg`, `l1-bedouin-bakhtiari-04.jpg`, `l1-lowtension-kandahar-05.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** The most distinct mass in the entire lane, and the one that most resembles
  nothing else in the structure kit: a **wide, low, horizontally-scalloped plane** — the roof dips
  between poles and peaks over them, so the ridge is a series of shallow catenary sags, not a
  straight line. Rear and side walls hang to the ground; the front is **open**. In
  `l1-bedouin-1912-02.jpg` the tent is perhaps three times as wide as it is deep and barely taller
  than the people beside it.
- **Real numbers.** Averages around **15 m × 3.5 m, 2 m high** — call it **10 cells × 2 cells × 2–3h**
  for a large one; three to nine ridge poles (*rukaz*) in a single line; small versions are three
  poles and ~4 cells long. Woven in long strips on a ground loom and sewn edge to edge.
- **Footprint + pole logic.** A **strip, not a blob** — a linear footprint that the roller can lay
  along a contour or a wind line. Guy lines run far out from the eaves in the photographs; at
  gameplay scale they should be paint/decal or omitted entirely, but note that they claim a lot of
  ground in reality (a real reason to keep a clear apron in front of the tent).
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the scalloped span plane; the open front; the solid back wall (**a real occluder —
    cover truth**); the pole line as 3–5 thin verticals if budget allows, since without them the
    scallop looks unexplained.
  - *Paint:* the woven stripe (black/white/brown warp banding, seen clearly in
    `l1-bedouin-wadirum-01.jpg`), the patterned rear divider curtain, the goat-hair fibre.
  - *Free gameplay dividend:* this is the only tent form in the set that is **naturally open on one
    side**, giving the tactical map a piece that is simultaneously cover, concealment, and a room
    you can shoot into. It is the best combat-value tent in the lane.
- **Status/hierarchy.** Expressed by **length** (pole count) and by the **quality of the interior
  dividing curtain** (*ma'nad*/*sahah*) separating the guest half (*shigg*) from the domestic half.
  The sheikh's tent in `l1-bedouin-sheikh-03.jpg` is not taller — it is *longer*, and its back
  curtain is the wealth display. So: **status = pole count + one paint swap.** That is very cheap,
  and it also hands the camp a diegetic reason for a "receiving" tent, which is exactly the social
  front door the brief wants.
- **Adjacent evidence.** `l1-bedouin-wadirum-01.jpg` shows the same woven cloth used as a
  **free-standing screen wall** forming a corridor — direct evidence for a screen/windbreak run
  piece, which the bandit "screened from the route" composition profile would use.

### 1.5 Bell tent (and Sibley) — the disciplined CONE

*Evidence: `l1-bell-single-02.jpg`, `l1-bell-fenton-rows-01.jpg`, `l1-bell-fenton-light-03.jpg`,
`l2-cavalry-rows.jpg`, `l2-fenton-naval-brigade.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** A cone with **a short vertical skirt at the base** and a **bare point** — no
  projecting poles, no crown. The skirt is the tell: `l1-bell-single-02.jpg` shows the profile
  clearly breaking from cone to vertical about 1h off the ground. From a distance
  (`l1-bell-fenton-light-03.jpg`, a whole division's camp receding to the horizon) a bell tent
  field reads as a dense field of identical pale triangles — **the most repeatable, most
  ordered-looking tent in the set.**
- **Real numbers.** The Sibley: **18 ft diameter, 12 ft high** — ~4 cells across, ~5h tall, one
  telescoping centre pole on a tripod, 24 pegs, **no guy ropes**, ~a dozen men. British bell tents
  of the same era are smaller (~14–16 ft, ~4h).
- **Footprint + pole logic.** Round, single centre pole, base pegged. The Sibley's tripod sat over a
  fire pit — a diegetic reason for the tent to be the warm place.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* cone + skirt break; the door as a slit notch (the two flaps rolled back read as a
    V-notch — see `l1-bell-single-02.jpg`); optional 1h pole finial.
  - *Paint:* seams, mildew/weather staining, unit markings, the rolled-back door flaps, the
    brailed-up skirt.
- **Status/hierarchy.** In the Crimean lithographs the officers' quarters are NOT bell tents — they
  are marquees (§1.7) or dug-in earth-banked structures. So the bell tent is the **troop unit** of
  its tradition, and its hierarchy is expressed by *what stands next to it*, not by a bigger
  version of it.
- **Why this form matters most to Site 2.** It is the cheapest possible "military encampment reads
  as ordered rows of uniform troop canvas" — one mesh, one material, an array. The camp brief names
  exactly that read. If only one troop-tent form ships, the evidence says this is it.

### 1.6 Wedge / A-frame, and the Roman contubernium tent — the SPAN as troop unit

*Evidence: `l2-roman-fortress-plans.jpg` (the *TENTORIA* vignette, bottom right of the Cybulski
plate), `l1-wedge-featherston-01.jpg` (the ladder's top rung, see below).*

- **Silhouette.** A straight ridge with two flat triangular ends — the simplest tent geometry that
  exists, and at gameplay scale essentially **a low prism**. Read comes from the ridge line and the
  vertical gable ends.
- **Real numbers (Roman *papilio*).** **10 Roman feet square (~2.96 m) and about 5 Roman feet
  (~1.48 m) tall**, goat leather, ~40 kg, one per *contubernium* of eight men, carried by the
  section's mule. In Genesis units: **2 cells × 2 cells, 2h high.** That is a startlingly small,
  startlingly regular module — a 2×2 footprint at half a storey.
- **Footprint + pole logic.** Ridge pole between two uprights, guyed at the corners; the Cybulski
  vignette shows the leather panelled with visible seams and a scalloped "butterfly" front (the
  name *papilio* comes from the flaps).
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the prism; the gable-end opening (a legible threshold); guy pegs omitted.
  - *Paint:* the leather panel seams and stitch lines (this form is nearly all seam pattern), unit
    marks, mud splash at the hem.
- **Status/hierarchy.** Roman camps are the clearest hierarchy in the whole lane because it is a
  *plan* hierarchy: identical 2×2 troop tents in blocks, a larger tent for the centurion at the end
  of each block, and the *praetorium* (the commander's pavilion) at the camp's centre on the main
  cross-street. **Status = size + position + adjacency to the void of the central square.**
- **Value to Site 2.** The papilio is the best possible **grid module**: exactly 2×2 cells, which
  means a military row can be laid out on the combat grid with no fractional geometry at all. If
  the kit wants "ordered rows" to be truly ordered, this is the piece that makes the arithmetic
  free.
- **Note on `l1-wedge-featherston-01.jpg`.** This image is *not* a wedge-tent camp — it is a WWI
  training camp of gable-roofed timber huts in rows, with white bell tents visible on the horizon.
  It is kept deliberately as evidence of the **ladder's far end**: when a camp stops being canvas
  it becomes rows of the shed/gable roof the structure kit already owns (§7 roof build order). The
  camp and the barrack are the same layout with a different roof family.

### 1.7 Pavilion / marquee — the CONE and SPAN as status objects

*Evidence: `l1-pavilion-caerlaverock-02.jpg`, `l1-pavilion-wisby-01.jpg`,
`l2-fenton-naval-brigade.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** Two sub-forms, both present in `l1-pavilion-wisby-01.jpg` in one frame:
  1. **Round pavilion** — a cone on a short vertical wall with a **ball/spike finial** and often a
     pennant on a separate flagstaff. Taller and steeper than a bell tent, and the wall is taller
     (you can stand at the edge).
  2. **Ridge marquee** — a long span with a *flat-ish* wide roof, vertical walls, and multiple
     poles; the Wisby image shows one as a broad pale plane floating above head height. This is the
     officer's/commander's form and the ancestor the brief names.
- **Real numbers.** Highly variable; a serviceable band is **4–6 cells across, 4–5h high** for the
  round pavilion and **4×8 cells, 4h** for the marquee. Guy lines are numerous and long — in the
  Wisby photo they are the dominant linear element in the frame and they *claim ground*.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the taller vertical wall (this is what separates a pavilion from a bell tent — it
    must be geometry); the finial spike; the marquee's flat roof plane and its **awning
    extension**, which is a real overhead-cover piece with tactical meaning.
  - *Paint:* the gore stripes (`l1-pavilion-caerlaverock-02.jpg` — purple/cream and red/black gores
    are the entire cultural statement), the scalloped valance, heraldic panels, the pennant device.
  - *Open question:* guy lines. They read strongly in every pavilion photograph and they are the
    reason a pavilion *feels* pitched rather than placed. They are also exactly the kind of thin
    geometry §5 and the low-poly economy forbid. Candidate compromise: a **ground decal** of
    radiating stake shadows plus 2–4 short stake stubs — no rope geometry. Flagged for the founder.
- **Status/hierarchy.** This form IS the hierarchy. In every image the pavilion is the tallest, the
  most saturated, the one with the flag, and the one with the most cleared ground around it. Cheap
  rule the roller can enforce: **the officer tent gets a reserved quiet apron; the troop tents do
  not.**

### 1.8 Wickiup / bender — the DOME

*Evidence: `l1-wickiup-curtis-01.jpg`, `l1-wickiup-kan-02.jpg`.*

- **Silhouette.** A low, shaggy, roughly hemispherical dome with **no straight line anywhere** and
  an arched door hole punched in one side. In the Curtis photograph the mass reads almost as a
  haystack; the *only* legible geometric feature is the black arch of the doorway.
- **Real numbers.** Roughly **2 cells across, 2–3h high**.
- **Footprint + pole logic.** Bent saplings lashed into a dome, thatched with grass/brush; the "Kan"
  photograph (`l1-wickiup-kan-02.jpg`) shows the frame with cloth and salvaged planks laid over —
  i.e. **the form naturally accepts scavenged material**, which is precisely the makeshift set in
  STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG §8.
- **§5 split.**
  - *Geometry:* the dome; the door arch (the only legible opening — and the read).
  - *Paint:* thatch direction, the shag, mixed-material patching (canvas panel, plank, hide), soot
    at the crown.
- **Status/hierarchy.** None. This is a **poverty/expedient form** — the correct shelter for
  hunters, refugees, prisoners, monsters, and anyone the design wants to read as improvised. It is
  also the closest tent form to the lair's vernacular and the shanty quarter's, which makes it the
  best cross-site earner in the lane.
- **Warning.** A shaggy dome is the hardest form in the set to make read at low poly — it has no
  edges. If it enters the kit it will lean hardest on paint and on the door arch. Recommend it be
  proved in clay before it is committed.

## 2. The typology sheet (the deliverable table)

Footprint in 5-ft cells, height in h = 2.5 ft. "Signal" = the one geometric fact that must survive
the production camera or the form does not read.

| form | genus | footprint | height | **the signal** | geometry | paint | status expression | culture-channel candidate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ger / yurt | DRUM | ~4×4 round | 3h (2h wall) | wall/roof break + stove pipe | drum, break, door frame, pipe | hem band, meander, rope bands, felt patch | **scale** (bigger drum) + ring position | steppe/horse-nomad; rooted tribal |
| Tipi | CONE (tall) | ~4×4 round | 6h + crown | **projecting pole crown** | tilted cone, crown, flap poles | painted band, smoke flaps, soot, lacing | **paint + ring position** (not size) | plains/hunter; migratory |

## 3. Findings (plain English)

1. **Nine forms are really three shapes.** Cone, drum, span (plus a dome that is a span pulled to
   the ground). Culture is carried by what happens at the apex, whether there is a vertical wall,
   and whether the plan is round or a strip. That is three cheap numbers, not nine models.
2. **The top 2.5 ft of the model does most of the cultural work.** Projecting poles (tipi), stove
   pipe (ger), finial and pennant (pavilion), bare point (bell). This is the highest-yield geometry
   spend in the lane, and it survives distance better than anything else.
3. **The Bedouin black tent is the only genuinely new mass in the set** — wide, low, scalloped, open
   on one side. It is also the best tactical piece (cover + concealment + a room you can shoot
   into), and its status ladder is free: a longer tent with a better curtain.
4. **The Roman troop tent is exactly 2×2 cells at 2h.** A 10-Roman-foot square is 2.96 m ≈ two
   5-ft cells. If ordered military rows are wanted, this module makes the layout arithmetic exact
   with no fractional geometry.
5. **Status is expressed three different ways across cultures, and only one of them is "make it
   bigger."** (a) *Scale* — ger. (b) *Paint + position in the ring* — tipi. (c) *A different form
   entirely, standing in its own cleared apron* — Roman/European (troop wedge vs commander's
   pavilion). (c) is the read most legible at gameplay scale, and it is the one the camp brief's
   "troop tent / officer tent" split already assumes.
6. **The bell tent is the cheapest possible ordered-military read** — one mesh, arrayed. Crimean
   lithographs show a whole division's camp as a field of identical pale triangles receding to the
   horizon, and it still reads as an army.
7. **Tipi and lavvu are too close to ship as a pair** without deliberate divergence (straight
   profile + tall crown vs bowed profile + stub crown). Otherwise one of them is wasted budget.
8. **The wickiup/bender naturally absorbs scavenged material** — the 1900 "Kan" photograph shows
   canvas and salvaged planks laid over a brush dome. That makes it the same object as the shanty
   quarter's lean-to and the makeshift vernacular's shelter, so it earns across three sites.
9. **Guy lines are the lane's one honest conflict with the §5 economy.** They are load-bearing to
   the read of pavilions and wedges, and they are thin geometry. A stake-stub-plus-ground-decal
   compromise is the obvious candidate but it is a founder call, not mine.
10. **The ger reads as architecture, not shelter** — vertical wall, door frame, chimney. That makes
    it a permanence-ladder hazard: a rung-0 camp full of gers will not read as "borrowed a
    clearing." Either the ger is reserved for rooted/tribal occupancies, or the ladder gains an
    exception.

## 4. What this lane did NOT settle

- Whether cone-family forms share one parameterised mesh or ship as distinct meshes (a real
  geometry-budget question, and an §3 fine-tier/assembly-tier question).
- The guy-line ruling.
- Whether the smoke-flap poles are modelled.
- Whether tent interiors are ever enterable, or whether all tents are solid cover masses. Nothing in
  the reference forces the answer; the Bedouin open-front tent is the form that makes the question
  urgent, because it is *inherently* half-interior.
