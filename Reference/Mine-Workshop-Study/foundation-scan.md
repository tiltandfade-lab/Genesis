# Mine / Workshop Study — foundation evidence scan

date: 2026-07-25
status: INITIAL SCAN — sufficient to pressure the concept, not a completed research gate
live concept: `docs/SITE-5-MINE-WORKSHOP-CONCEPT.md`

## Question tested

Does the accepted Site 5 concept—useful production under visible pressure, first proved
by an active upper mine over a flooding lower branch—match real mine organization well
enough to justify deeper research?

Yes. The sources strongly support the production-chain premise and add one important
correction: haulage, worker travel/escape, ventilation, drainage, support, and waste
must be modeled as connected systems rather than collapsed into one decorated tunnel.

Modern mine-safety sources are not being imposed as fantasy building codes. They are
used to identify spatial truths and useful gameplay relationships: supported versus
unsupported ground, separate escape, air continuity, clear travel, and the consequences
of a partially blocked network.

## Findings that survive the scan

### 1. The accepted promise is sound

Real mines are legible as linked operations, not isolated chambers. Extraction,
processing, transport, utilities, waste, settlement, and export remain spatially
connected even when technology and culture differ radically.

### 2. Geology selects the large form

Drift, slope, shaft, room-and-pillar, stope, placer, open-cut, and terraced forms answer
the deposit and terrain. Culture and technology strongly affect how that form is worked,
supported, scheduled, processed, and marked, but cannot arbitrarily replace the
resource geometry.

### 3. The haulage spine is necessary but insufficient

The main cart/ramp/lift route is the site's deck, yet a credible producing mine also
needs worker travel and escape, airflow, drainage, and maintenance access. These may
share portions of the main route, but the first build should include a distinct
secondary manway/air path so they can become tactical levers.

### 4. Support is a boundary, not wallpaper

Repeated frames or pillars should mark where travel and work are considered supported.
The last support before an active or failed face is a readable danger threshold.
Spacing and type respond to geology and method rather than becoming evenly scattered
decoration.

### 5. Ore and waste must separate

Ore stock, low-grade material, spoil, tailings, slag, offcuts, and water have different
destinations. A generic pile cannot communicate whether the site is producing,
blocked, waste-choked, stripped, or rich.

### 6. Partial flooding is an excellent first strain

It changes access, pump labor, water level, lighting, air routes, recovery, and output
without requiring the entire mine to fail. It also creates a visible clock and a
player-addressable problem.

### 7. Culture should change organization, not only skin

Iwami and Sado support small-scale labor-intensive production chains in which mining,
processing, settlements, routes, and administration remain closely linked. Kennecott
and Sewell show different large-scale, terrain-driven industrial organizations.
Culture therefore has room to change work groups, interfaces, processing proximity,
load carriers, claims, ritual/administrative markers, and maintenance practice while
geology still owns the excavation.

## Immediate design consequences

- Retain the accepted active-upper/flooded-lower build.
- Give it a broad haul route and a physically separate narrow manway/air/escape route.
- Show the last safe support before the live face.
- Place the pump where crews can reach and maintain it; connect it to a visible
  discharge path.
- Separate useful ore, processing stock, and waste/spoil.
- Let terrain own surface processing placement and export direction.
- Show an active shift and an unstaffed/offline state with different motivated lights.
- Preserve a hard distinction between Site 5 strain and Site 3 abandonment.
