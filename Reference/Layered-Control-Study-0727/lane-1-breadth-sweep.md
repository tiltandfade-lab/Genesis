# Lane 1 — The breadth sweep: what else could layered control be?

## 0. Why this lane exists and what would have made it fail

Site 8's binding ruling (`docs/DESIGN.md`, 2026-07-26) defines the transform semantically:
eligible from **persistent simultaneous claims with a consequential control difference**, never
from map size. The obvious failure mode for a docs-only transform is that "layered control"
collapses into one imagined case — a criminal gang in the back of a tavern — and the whole
generator quietly becomes a gang generator.

So this lane asked the opposite question first: across real institutions, how many *structurally
different* ways are there for two persistent claims to sit on one place? The answer is at least
**eleven**, and they differ from each other on axes the tavern case never exercises: some are
recognised by both claimants, some are secret; some are permanent, some exist only for a few
days a year; some overlap on the same square metre, some partition it; some are the *host's own
law* and some are its negation.

**What would have falsified the transform:** if every real case turned out to need its own
geometry — its own rooms, its own portals, its own circulation — then Site 8 would be a host
program, not a transform, and the 2026-07-26 classification would be wrong. That did not happen.
Every case below decomposes into deltas over an existing host: a threshold, a schedule, a
recognition rule, a fixture with two owners, a route one party controls. That is the lane's single
most load-bearing result and it *supports* the existing ruling rather than amending it.

## 1. The eleven cases

### 1.1 The liberty / sanctuary — a hole in the host's jurisdiction

The English **liberties** were "areas where the residents had special privileges and exemptions …
anomalous jurisdictions within the broader urban landscape." Whitefriars in London — later
nicknamed **Alsatia** — was Carmelite property and therefore outside City authority; after the
dissolution its status became genuinely *unclear*, and the ambiguity itself hardened into a
recognised debtors' sanctuary that survived until the Escape from Prison Act of 1697, with the
sheriffs and the sanctuary's occupants fighting pitched battles at the boundary in 1691.
([pasttense.co.uk](https://pasttense.co.uk/2019/07/04/today-in-london-riotous-history-1691-the-occupants-of-the-alsatia-sanctuary-battle-the-sheriffs/) ·
[Whitefriars, London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitefriars,_London))

At the single-building scale the same idea is a **door fitting**: Durham's sanctuary knocker
admitted a fugitive to thirty-seven days of protection, and those "who had committed a great
offence, such as murder in self-defence or breaking out of prison" could use it.
([Durham World Heritage Site](https://www.durhamworldheritagesite.com/learn/architecture/cathedral/intro/sanctuary-knocker) ·
[Old Church Lore, Andrews](https://elfinspell.com/AndrewsSanctuary.html))

**Design content:** control differences of *authority* and *protection*, with a hard threshold and
no shared fixtures. The boundary is a line the host's own enforcers may not cross. Its tell can be
a single prop. **This case is invisible until someone tries to cross it** — which makes it the
purest LC-1 (social/recognition) rung and the cheapest to generate.

### 1.2 The extraterritorial enclave — a foreign law inside the host

Hanseatic **Kontors** — the Steelyard in London, Bryggen in Bergen, the Peterhof in Novgorod —
were "legally privileged merchant communities with their own rules, warehouses, chapels, and
officers," inside which "Hanseatic law applied rather than local law … and local rulers had
limited jurisdiction over what happened inside the Kontor boundaries." The Steelyard was a walled
community on the Thames controlling England's wool and cloth exports for three centuries; at
Bryggen the German merchants lived under their own administration, forbidden to marry locally or
own land outside the quarter. ([Kontor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontor) ·
[Hanseatic League overview](https://socialstudieshelp.com/world-history/the-hanseatic-league-baltic-trade-and-northern-european-cities/))

The modern-scale version is **Shanghai**: "one city, but divided among (at least) three
jurisdictions: the Chinese city, International Settlement, and French Concession," to the point of
needing three drivers' licences to cross it, with extraterritoriality making foreign residents
"free of Chinese law even while residing in China."
([Shanghai International Settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_International_Settlement) ·
[This Week in China's History](https://www.sinicapodcast.com/p/this-week-in-chinas-history-the-creation))

**Design content:** control differences of *authority, custody, taxation and information*, expressed
as a **bounded quarter with its own independent access to the resource** (the Steelyard's own river
stairs and crane — see `images/lc4-steelyard-london-1600s.jpg`). The enclave is *recognised* by the
host, which is what separates it from a criminal layer.

### 1.3 The enclosed quarter — a claim confined against its own growth

The Venetian Ghetto inverts the enclave: the host recognises the community but confines it. Gates
were closed at night, locked by Christian guards **whose salaries the Jewish community itself
paid**, with the surrounding canals patrolled by boat to enforce the curfew. Because the area could
not expand horizontally, the buildings reached seven to eight storeys — "quite unusual for Venice."
([Jewish Venice](https://www.jewishvenice.org/history-culture/) ·
[Aish](https://www.aish.org.uk/content-article/the_history_of_jewish_venice) ·
[Marriott Traveler](https://traveler.marriott.com/venice/venice-jewish-ghetto/))

**Design content:** the first case where the control difference has a *visible constructional
consequence*. A claim with a fixed footprint and a growing population subdivides and stacks. This
is the real-world shape of the ontology's **overflow law** — and Kowloon Walled City
(`images/lc11-kowloon-walled-city-1989.jpg`) is the same law under a three-dimensional cap.

### 1.4 The parallel court — a second law over the same ground

Derbyshire's **Barmote courts**, established 1288 and still sitting, determine the customs of lead
mining and settle its disputes; crucially their "jurisdiction extend[s] to both the crown lands of
the Duchy of Lancaster and those under individual ownership" — a landowner does not control the
minerals beneath his own field, and any free miner could work anywhere except churchyards, gardens,
orchards and highways.
([Barmote court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barmote_court) ·
[Duchy of Lancaster](https://www.duchyoflancaster.co.uk/about-the-duchy/duties-of-the-duchy/further-key-appointments/barmote-courts/) ·
[Derbyshire Heritage](https://derbyshireheritage.co.uk/buildings/others/moot-hall/))

**Design content:** two claims over the *same cells*, partitioned not by space but by **subject
matter** — surface versus subsurface, mineral versus soil. No boundary exists on the ground at all;
the tell is a building (see `images/lc5-wirksworth-moot-hall.jpg`), a set of measuring vessels, and
a body of custom. This is the case a purely spatial transform would miss entirely, and it is the
strongest argument for LC-0/LC-1 rungs that add *no geometry*.

### 1.5 The scheduled court — a claim that exists only for a while

The **court of piepowders** was "a temporary court set up for the duration of a fair or market to
deal with travellers who were not resident in the town," with unlimited jurisdiction over personal
actions arising in the market, and it existed in many hundreds of instances.
([Court of piepowders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_piepowders) ·
[Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/piepoudre-court) ·
[Old Market, Bristol](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Market,_Bristol))

**Design content:** the pure **schedule** axis. Same ground, same fixtures, same people — and a
different authority in force between two dates. It maps exactly onto the DESIGN.md ruling's phrase
*"shared-room recognition/schedule differences"* and it costs the generator nothing but a state
field and a dressing change.

### 1.6 The commercial layer inside a custody institution

The Marshalsea was a state debtors' prison run as a private business. It had a **master's side** of
fifty rentable rooms and a **common side** of nine unfurnished rooms holding around three hundred
people thirty-plus to a room; the jailer held a monopoly on food, clothing, furnishings, candles,
straw and fuel with no price controls, and a keeper paid £140 a year for the wardenship plus £260
for the right to collect rents and sell food and drink. A detached **Tap** sold beer and wine, and a
wealthier prisoner could pay his roommates to leave — "buy out the chum" — for half a crown a week.
([Marshalsea overview](https://www.historic-uk.com/HistoryUK/HistoryofBritain/Marshalsea-Debtors-Prison-Dickens/) ·
[theprison.org.uk](https://www.theprison.org.uk/SouthwarkMarshalsea/))

**Design content:** the *host is not subverted; it is monetised.* Custody obligations remain fully
intact while a second claimant sells everything inside them. This is the cleanest proof that Site 8
can ride Site 6 without becoming a prison-break scenario, and it supplies concrete mechanical
verbs — rent, tab, chummage, monopoly supply, a bar inside a gaol.

### 1.7 The company town — the employer as the second government

Pennsylvania's **Coal and Iron Police** (1865–1931) were "established by the Pennsylvania General
Assembly but employed and paid for by the various coal companies" — a statutory private force. Coal
companies paid miners in **scrip**, "specialized coins only redeemable at stores owned by the
company that issued them," in towns where "the only store in town was usually owned or run on
behalf of the coal company," producing debt bondage.
([Coal and Iron Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_and_Iron_Police) ·
[Appalachian History](https://www.appalachianhistory.net/2018/09/company-store-scrip.html) ·
[WV Mine Wars Museum](https://wvminewars.org/16tons))

**Design content:** control differences of *commerce, labour, protection, taxation and violence* at
once — and the whole thing hangs on **one fixture**, the company store (see
`images/lc8-koppers-company-store.jpg` and `lc9-caples-company-store.jpg`). Site 5's mine host plus
this delta is a complete, unforced second proof of the transform.

### 1.8 The monastic exemption — an institution outside its own hierarchy

From the reign of Urban II, papal *libertas Romana* "came to be regarded as exemption from the
authority of the bishop," and from the twelfth century exemption of orders and monasteries "became
the rule." The Liberty of Durham, and jurisdictional immunities like the Liberty of Whitby Strand,
gave ecclesiastical bodies their own courts and franchises over defined territory.
([Catholic Encyclopedia: Exemption](https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05706a.htm) ·
[Liberty of Whitby Strand, EHR](https://academic.oup.com/ehr/article/140/603/283/7750531))

**Design content:** Site 4's monastery host with a control difference of *authority and recognition*
between the local and the distant claimant. Notably the two claimants here are **both legitimate** —
which forces the generator to stop treating "second claimant" as a synonym for "criminal."

### 1.9 The caravanserai / khan — N private claims around one shared centre

A khan's court is a single vaulted or open space with a water source, ringed by individually doored
cells at ground level and individually shuttered rooms on a galleried upper floor
(`images/lc12-khan-asad-pasha-court.jpg`). Each merchant party owns its own goods and its own cell;
the building owns the water, the gate, and the night.

**Design content:** the *many-claimant* case, distinct from the two-claimant default. Shared
circulation, shared light, shared water, one controlled portal — and N separately attributed
property envelopes. This is the case the transform must not flatten into "two sides."

### 1.10 The informal settlement — parallel governance with no drawn line

Rocinha against São Conrado (`images/lc10-rocinha-sao-conrado.jpg`) shows the boundary as slope,
tree line and elevated road rather than wall — orderly formal towers on both flanks and a
fine-grained accreted mass between.

**Design content:** the boundary between control regimes is frequently **inherited terrain or
infrastructure**, not built by either claimant. Directly contradicts the lazy generator instinct to
fence the faction zone.

### 1.11 The capped enclave — control expanding to exactly its licensed envelope

Kowloon Walled City, an ambiguous-sovereignty enclave inside British Hong Kong, grew to a hard
rectangular footprint and a flat uniform roofline at the airport height cap, with an interior grain
roughly an order of magnitude finer than the host city around it and no legible public route in.

**Design content:** this is the ontology §4.2 overflow law rendered as a photograph. **The claim
expands to the exact limits of its precommitted envelope in all three dimensions, and then stops.**

## 2. What the sweep changes about Site 8

**2.1 The control-difference dimensions in the ruling are not a flat list — they cluster.** Across
the eleven cases, four clusters recur, and each has a different spatial cost:

| cluster | dimensions | typical spatial cost |
|---|---|---|
| **recognition** | authority · recognition · ritual · information | zero to one prop (a knocker, a carved panel, a sign) |
| **exchange** | service · property · taxation · labour · custody | one fixture with two owners (a store, a counter, a locked cabinet, a Tap) |
| **passage** | access · time · protection | one threshold plus a schedule (a gate, a curfew, a stair, a wicket) |
| **force** | violence | fronts, patrols, barriers — the only cluster that needs LC-5 |

Only the fourth cluster requires the map to change shape. The first three are the reason the
map-size ruling is correct.

**2.2 "Second claimant" ≠ "criminal."** Five of eleven cases (liberty, Kontor, Barmote, piepowder,
monastic exemption) are *legally recognised* second authorities, and two more (ghetto, company town)
are the host's own instrument turned on part of its population. A generator that always rolls a
gang has misread its own law.

**2.3 Both claimants keep operating.** In every case the host program continues: the mine mines,
the prison holds, the fair trades, the abbey prays. This confirms the ruling's requirement that the
host retains its identity — and it gives the rejection ladder a countable test (§3 of the synthesis).

**2.4 The transform's *cheapest true* expression is a rule, not a room.** Barmote and piepowder both
govern real ground with no boundary on that ground at all. This is the evidence that the LC-0 and
LC-1 rungs are the *common* case and LC-3+ the rare one — which is the opposite of the intuition a
tactical-map project naturally has, and it should shape the roller weights.

**2.5 There is a case the ruling's ladder does not obviously cover: the many-claimant khan.** The
ladder runs latent → social → localized → distributed → site-wide → open conflict, which reads as a
scale of *how much* of one split is materialised. The caravanserai is a different shape: N small
claims of equal weight around one shared centre, none of them dominant. This is recorded as an open
question for the founder rather than silently patched into the ladder (§6 of the synthesis).

## 4. Declared gaps

- **No measured plans.** This sweep is textual and photographic; it contains no surveyed floor plan
  of a Kontor, khan, ghetto block or company store. Dimensional hypotheses in the working spec are
  therefore *derived from standee and route logic*, never from a measured source, and are labelled
  as such.
- **No non-European many-claimant governance case beyond the khan.** The millet system, waqf
  administration, and Andorran-style co-lordship (paréage) were identified as candidates but not
  researched; they are the obvious next lane if the founder wants the many-claimant rung.
- **No fantasy/genre cohort.** This lane deliberately read institutions, not games. The genre
  comparison lives in lane 3 (FFT) and is explicitly narrow.
