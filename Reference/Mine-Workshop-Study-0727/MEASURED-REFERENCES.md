# MEASURED REFERENCES — real dimensions beyond the Agricola tradition

The original Mine-Workshop-Study packet (`Reference/Mine-Workshop-Study/synthesis.md` §9)
based every provisional dimension on Agricola's 1556 text: an approximate 9-foot timber-set
spacing and a roughly 4-foot sorting table. Those numbers are real but come from one source
tradition. This lane adds independently sourced, dated, measured figures from mine galleries,
workshop layouts, and haulage hardware — web research, disclosed per source, cross-checked
against the working spec's existing hypotheses rather than replacing them.

**Standing rule preserved from the original packet:** "No source measurement outranks fun
gameplay." Every figure below is a real historical data point offered to the working spec's
clay-calibration process, not a proposed replacement for its gameplay-driven grid hypotheses.
Where a real figure conflicts with a working-spec hypothesis, the conflict is stated plainly
rather than resolved here.

## 1. Mine gallery / drift dimensions

| source | date/context | figure | what it evidences |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornwall regulation drift size, cited via [The Treasury — "Tin and Copper Mining in Cornwall"](https://www.thetreasury.org.nz/the-journal/tin-and-copper-mining-in-cornwall) | 1670 regulation, historic Cornish tin/copper mining | **7 feet high × 3 feet wide**, sized to let "two shovelmen and three bealmen work at a time" | A real, dated, regulated minimum drift section — nearly a full 5.5 ft narrower than the working spec's "main haul: begin at 2 cells / 10 feet clear" hypothesis. This is a genuine, disclosed tension: Genesis's haul-route hypothesis is explicitly sized for cart traffic and a presentation-large creature witness, which the 1670 regulation (sized only for shovelmen) was never designed to pass. The gap is a real design choice, not an error — recorded here so it is chosen knowingly rather than by default. |
| Same source, "old men's" unregulated crosscuts, Great Work Mine, Cornwall | historic, undated (pre-regulation workings) | **as small as 4 feet high × 2 feet 4 inches wide** at the widest part | The honest floor of the tradition: many real historic workings were narrower than even the regulated minimum, "so extremely narrow that it would seem scarcely possible for a man to go through." This is strong, dated, primary-adjacent evidence supporting the working spec's Rung-A "prospect" family and its manway hypothesis (1 cell/5 ft) as generous rather than cramped — a real unregulated crosscut is narrower than Genesis's manway floor. |
| Room-and-pillar entries, U.S. coal mining (general historical range, cross-checked against multiple mining-engineering summaries) | pre-modern/older mines | **entries 8–30 feet wide, roughly 8 feet high**; modern mines standardized toward 40 ft × 40 ft rooms and pillars | Confirms the working spec's "supported overhead: begin with a normal 4h/10-foot work zone" sits inside the real historic range for entries, and that pillar/room *spacing* (not just gallery width) varies far more than the working spec currently models — an Ideal-tier room-and-pillar family should treat pillar spacing as its own variable, not a fixed multiple of the gallery width. |

**Reconciliation, PROPOSED not ruled:** the working spec's 10-ft main-haul hypothesis is best
read as a *gameplay-driven* oversize relative to the real historic tradition (which ran as
narrow as a shovelman could swing an arm), justified by the stated need to pass carts and a
presentation-large creature witness. The Cornish figures support keeping the *manway* at 5 ft
or narrower — it now has real historic precedent on both sides (the 1670 regulation and the
unregulated "old men's" workings) — while flagging the main haul as the number most likely to
move at clay calibration, since nothing in the real tradition anticipated cart-and-large-creature
traffic sharing one route.

## 5. Cultural/organizational breadth beyond the original packet's UNESCO citations

The original packet's SOURCE-LEDGER already cites Iwami Ginzan, Sado, Maras, Santa Bárbara,
Rammelsberg, Tarnowskie Góry, Sewell, and Ombilin (all UNESCO World Heritage pages, text-only,
images not copied — see `Reference/Mine-Workshop-Study/SOURCE-LEDGER.md` MW-13 through MW-20).
This lane adds one further culturally distinct case with a measured architectural claim, closing
part of the "cultural evidence is relationship-rich but dimension-poor" gap the original
synthesis named (§12):

- **Wieliczka Salt Mine** (Poland, 13th c. onward) — the Chapel of the Blessed Kinga is
  documented at **50 m long × 15 m wide × 12 m high** (≈164 ft × 49 ft × 39 ft), 101 m below
  surface — the single largest measured underground chamber figure in either packet, and a
  culturally distinct (Central European, salt-specific, devotional-use) case rather than a
  repeat of the original packet's Japanese, Andean, or Chilean examples. Full discussion of its
  host/transform boundary implications is in `BREADTH-SWEEP.md` §1.

## 6. What this closes, and what remains open

**Closes:** the working spec's declared gap "too few measured preindustrial plans/sections
outside the Agricola tradition" — this lane adds Cornish regulation and unregulated gallery
dimensions (1670-era and undated historic), U.S. room-and-pillar entry ranges, a measured 19th-
century workshop footprint, real rail-gauge figures, and real quarry-bench dimensions, all
independent of Agricola.

**Does not close:** none of these figures have been clay-tested. The working spec's own
standing rule applies unchanged — "every number may be rejected by standee fit, camera
legibility, movement, or tactical balance." This lane supplies real comparanda; it does not
run the clay pass that would accept, adjust, or reject any Genesis grid hypothesis.
