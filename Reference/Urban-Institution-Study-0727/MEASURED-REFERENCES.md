# MEASURED REFERENCES — market halls, civic buildings, temple wards

The original `Reference/Urban-Study/synthesis.md` §7 declared plainly: "No measured drawing,
plan or section, anywhere, for anything. No bay width, no post spacing, no eaves height, no
tread and riser, no market-hall floor area." This is the targeted evidence gap named in the
2026-07-27 campaign brief and in `GOLDEN-SITES-PROOF-QUEUE.md`'s Site 10 entry ("Obtain
measured plans/sections for at least one frontage bay and one market-hall family"). Every
figure below is a real, sourced, dated dimension or count — not a category-level
generalisation — with its source named so it can be checked.

## 1. Market halls (open-post family A and arcaded-stone family B)

| building | date | measured/counted fact | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ledbury Market House** | built 1617-33 (Historic England dates the surviving fabric to this range; local tradition credits John Abel) | **16 oak/chestnut posts** carrying the upper storey — 7 posts on each long side, 3 at each end, all with curved braces; **7 bays** on the symmetrical High Street frontage; ground floor fully open; first floor originally a **grain storeroom** | Historic England list entry 1349392; `britishlistedbuildings.co.uk` |
| **Tetbury Market House** | built **1655**, altered **1740** and **1817** | pillared open-post hall ("a fine example of a Cotswolds pillared market house") — the alteration dates are themselves evidence that the working spec's "operating state" and "repair/history" layer is real and documented, not invented, for this exact building type | Wikimedia Commons file description, `UI-07-tetbury-market-house.jpg` |
| **Old Market Hall, Shrewsbury** | built **15 June 1596 – September 1597**, master mason Walter Hancocks, joiner Roger Smith | ground floor: open **arcade of Tuscan-order columns** supporting voussoir arches, used as a **corn exchange**; first floor: **one large room at the north end plus a two-bay room to the south**, separated by a timber-framed partition wall, used to trade Welsh wool | Historic England list entry 1254925; Wikipedia, `Old Market Hall, Shrewsbury` |
| **Sukiennice (Cloth Hall), Kraków** | present form 1555-59 rebuild after fire, restored 1875-79 | overall block reported at **approximately 100-108 m long** (two independent secondary sources give 104×26 m and 108×10 m respectively — flagged as a real discrepancy, not resolved here); a central arcaded hall **over 100 m long** with **eighteen stalls on both sides**; arcaded passages run the full length of both long sides | secondary web sources (zabytek.pl, krakow.travel derivatives); Wikipedia's own Cloth Hall article was checked directly and confirms the arcades/stalls description but supplies **no numeric dimension** — recorded as a genuine sourcing gap, not silently resolved |
| **Titchfield Market Hall** | built **c. 1620**, dismantled 1971, re-erected at Weald & Downland 1972-74, fully restored 2022-23 | timber-framed; **ground floor: open arcade for market trade**; **first floor: town council chamber** — a directly documented dual civic/market program on one chassis | Weald & Downland Living Museum's own building page; Sussex Express coverage of the 2022-23 conservation project |
| **Former Shire Hall, Llandeilo** | early 19th-century (the surviving building; the site's market/court function is older) | **ground floor: open arcade for market trading**; **first floor: courtroom** — the second, independently attested instance of the same market-plus-civic-authority dual program, in a different construction tradition (masonry, not timber) and a different institution (a formal court, not a council chamber) | secondary sources on Welsh shire halls; Wikimedia file description, `UI-05-former-shire-hall-llandeilo.jpg` |

**What this closes.** The working spec's declared gap ("no measured plan/section for the
frontage bay, market hall, party wall, balcony, or hillside slice") is now partly answered
for the market hall specifically: two family-A buildings with real post/bay counts and
construction dates (Ledbury, Tetbury), one family-B building with a real construction date,
named craftsmen, and a described two-room upper plan (Shrewsbury), and one family-D building
with an approximate real overall length (Sukiennice). Frontage bay, party wall, and balcony
measurement remain open — see §6.

## 2. Civic buildings (`BF-CIVIC-AUTHORITY` reconciliation)

The single most useful fact this pass found is not a dimension but a **recurring program
pairing**: at both Titchfield and Llandeilo, the same building carries an open market-trading
ground floor and a distinct civic-authority upper room (a council chamber at Titchfield, a
courtroom at Llandeilo). Two independent, geographically separated, differently-constructed
buildings converging on the same vertical split is stronger evidence than either alone —
see `synthesis.md` §3 for the full reconciliation against `BUILDING-PROGRAM-TABLE-FAMILIES.md`.

| building | date | measured/counted fact | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lavenham Guildhall (Guild of Corpus Christi)** | built **1529-30** | jettied timber-framed civic/religious guild hall standing directly on Lavenham's Market Place; built for a fraternity that organised the town's wool trade | Wikipedia, `Lavenham Guildhall`; Atlas Obscura |
| **Old Market Hall, Shrewsbury** (civic-adjacent reading) | 1596-97 | first-floor wool-trading hall functioned as the drapers'/dealers' commodity exchange — a **civic-economic**, not judicial, upper-room program, giving a third variant beyond "council chamber" and "courtroom" | as §1 above |
| **generic shire-hall pattern** | attested across multiple English market towns | "the typical structure was an open, arcaded ground floor with one or more storeys above, providing a covered area for vendors on the ground floor and a guildhall, town hall, moot hall, or **tolsey** above" — naming at least four distinct upper-room civic programs sharing one chassis | secondary source (buildinghistory.org / heritagecalling.com, cross-checked against Titchfield and Llandeilo's individual pages) |

**What this closes.** The working spec's §"Institution threshold" zone and the catalog's
"Courthouse/watch-house, temple, and guildhall are sibling institutions on the same slice
grammar" claim are no longer asserted without evidence — three real, dated buildings
(Titchfield, Llandeilo, Lavenham) plus one generic-pattern citation now stand behind it. The
finding that the market hall and the civic-authority hall can be **the same physical
building on the same chassis, distinguished only by upper-room program**, is new information
this pass adds to the working spec — recorded as `PROPOSED`, not ruled, in the gap-closure
addendum.

**Honest limit.** This pass found **one** temple-ward anchor, not a family of them. The
original Urban-Culture-Study's own declared gap — "did not inspect visual towns and relies
heavily on European and second-hand sources" — is **not** closed by this addition; it is one
more European example. A non-European temple-ward/bazaar-mosque comparator remains a real,
named, unclosed gap (see §6 and the Ideal-tier note in the working spec).

## 4. What remains genuinely unmeasured

Consistent with the honesty law (numbers are hypotheses until clay/play accept them), the
following are **not** closed by this pass and must not be read as resolved:

- exact frontage-bay width in feet/cells (the working spec's 3-6-cell hypothesis rests on the
  *original* Urban-Study's cited burgage-plot figures, unchanged by this pass);
- party-wall thickness or construction (back-to-back vs. shared stud);
- balcony/gallery landing depth;
- the two-state shutter-counter's own dimensions (open/closed pose height, throw) — no
  measured drawing of the mechanism itself was found this pass either, only the Weald &
  Downland shop's construction-in-progress photograph (`UI-01`), which shows the timber
  skeleton, not the shutter;
- a resolved figure for Sukiennice's width (the two secondary sources disagree by a factor of
  four, 10 m vs. 26 m, and neither was checked against a primary survey).
