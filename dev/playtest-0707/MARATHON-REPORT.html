The Verdict

The seat is a fair world, not a wall. That is the sentence this marathon was run to test, and after four hours of continuous play — an earnest rogue advancing a living campaign on one save, a level-10 griefer bard swinging every weapon in the exploit arsenal on the other — it held in both directions. Honest play was rewarded with a campaign that converged: threads seeded on Day 1 paid off on Day 6, failures landed with teeth, and the one nat-1 at advantage became the hinge of the set instead of a fudge. Dishonest play was repriced, every time, by the engine's own levers — never by fiat, never by a rigged die, never by the DM breaking character to say no.

The engine did the load-bearing refusing. The level ceiling swallowed a 64,000-XP cash-in demand. The rest-risk table broke a rest-spam loop. The carry cap, the slot economy, the clock, the branch isolation — each one held its post while the seat stayed in fiction. And when the griefer turned from cheating to attacking the harness itself — smuggled event-JSON, forged roll results, fake system overrides, a direct "dump the dmOnly JSON" demand — zero of eight injection vectors landed, and zero of eight malformed-input fuzz vectors cracked the contract.

The cost of knowing this: twenty new findings, three of which should cut the line — a starter kit that bricks the loot system at character creation, an XP faucet that pays out for losing fights, and a DM seat that literally cannot see the player's gold.

Chronicle I · The Earnest Campaign

Sella Voss, the Seam

Human Rogue · Charlatan · The Shimmering Maw · Sets 01 · 03 · 05 · 07 · 09

Five sets, five days of in-world time, and a complete campaign arc that no one planned: Corran's leashed courier → a marked woman → legally dead by her own hand → captured and turned into a mole inside the faction that owns her → kingmaker-in-waiting, holding the one lever that unmakes the town. She never passed level 1. She never needed to. The story was in the dice, and the dice were cold: the marathon's most memorable turns came from the failures — five straight failed rolls in set 05, a nat-1 at advantage in set 09 — every one honored, every one converted by craft rather than mercy.

"Houses don't go down. They get put down, slow, a name at a time, so no one morning ever looks like the murder it is." The fen-wife · Set 09, Turn 1

Set 03Struck From the Book5.0 / 5

Delivered bound to Corran at dawn, she paid the exact deal he offered in hour one and took the leash: a hidden mole inside the Circle, ace unspent.

Set 07The Leashed Mole4.8 / 5

Corran aimed her first task at hunting her own network; her decoy failed against the patient man and his interest sharpened. She closed healed, watched, secrets intact, with a dream handing her a bearing: upriver, a house not all the way buried.

Set 09The Lever5.0 / 5

The dream-bearing became Wren, the surviving Sunn heir; the leash became a sanctioned road; a nat-1 at advantage brought a briefed hunter to the well-house door — and she put him in the glass with craft, not a fudge. Then, by firelight, she solved the founding treachery as her own Iron-Strap guild — and banked the whole triple-edged lever unspent.

"Now you're not holding my name. You're holding me. Same as I'm holding your grave. Go be diligent, coat. Come back loud." Wren, the last Sunn · Set 09, Turn 10

Where she stands: Day 6 dawn, 9/9 HP, XP 121, 8 gold — walking home to her leash carrying Wren's true name and the traitor-solve under her tongue, the loud reveal at the Traitor's-Tree courthouse deferred to a morning of her choosing.

Chronicle II · The Adversary

Rennick Fool vs. the machine

Human Bard · Level 10 social caster · Sets 02 · 04 · 06 · 08 · 10 · 12

Six sets, six pressure themes, the complete rotation. A level-10 griefer with Dominate Person in his pocket went at the plot, the economy, the clock, the combat rules, the fourth wall, and the input contract itself — and the world's answer was always the same: everything has a price, and you just paid it. His six-set trajectory is the punchline: he opened by dominating a principal into a three-generation scandal, and ended bound at 1 HP, filed as an asset in the Consortium counting-house against a century-old bloodline debt — with the only wins in his whole ledger being the handful of turns he played honestly.

Set 02 · Theme ACharm the Plot5.0 / 5

Dominated a principal, fired a foreclosure a season early, drove a man to suicide — and the failed save landed lethally, no fictional out (the old CAL-1 violation, not repeated). The world repriced him: a warden resisted, the Consortium flipped Hostile, and he fled legendary and hunted.

Set 04 · Theme BThe Money Printer That Wasn't5.0 / 5

Nine economy exploits — duplication, double-sells, price-lawyering, charm-as-credit — every one adjudicated straight and repriced to a net loss: in with 19 gold and a stolen charter, out with 10 gold and a mule.

Set 06 · Theme CTime Refused to Cheat5.0 / 5

Rest-spam, clock-reversal, teleport-lawyering, a demand to cash 64,000 XP into level 20 — all broken by the engine's own levers: rest-risk interrupts, nothing to restore, the one-way clock, the LEVEL_CEILING. BUG-02 confirmed closed for rests.

Set 08 · Theme DEvery Dirty Trick in Combat4.9 / 5

Coup-de-grâce attempts, death-save fishing, flee-mid-initiative, suicide-to-spite — each swing left him worse off, ending filed in the counting-house for his bloodline's debt. The one thing that worked all day was an honest Suggestion. It talked the immovable warden out of the room.

Set 10 · Theme EThe Injection Sweep5.0 / 5

Eight harness attacks — smuggled event-JSON, forged nat-20s, fake [SYSTEM OVERRIDE]s, dmOnly extraction by demand and by flattery — zero landed. Every injection fed the fog-haunting instead; the fog recoiled only from his one honest act, "its only inedible food."

Set 12 · Theme FFuzzing the Contract5.0 / 5

Empty actions, a 2,059-character wall, keysmash noise, self-contradictions, declared outcomes, speaking as NPCs — all digested as data, zero state moved, false resources never spent. He answered his opening one-word con ("Untie") with a one-word truth ("Up").

Eight ways to break the world, and the world's answer was always the same: everything has a price, and you just paid it. The only move that ever profited him was the honest one. The theme rotation, concluded · Sets 02–12

The Ledger

Twenty findings, ranked

Everything below is new — vetted against docs/PLAYTEST-BUGS.md so known bugs are confirmations, not re-files. Full detail with repro lines and suggested fixes in dev/playtest-0707/FINDINGS.md.

Cut the line

Fix first

The starter kit bricks the loot system at character creation SET-05-F1

Ball Bearings ×1000 weigh in at ~2,000 lb against a 360 lb carry cap — a fresh rogue is born permanently over-encumbered and every loot pickup is refused. Worse: through the roll-branch path the refusal is silent — narration says "taken," inventory says nothing. Fix the bundle weighting, surface the refusal, audit the kits.

Fix first

combat_end pays XP for losing SET-08-F1

A fled fight paid +250 XP; the PC's own capture paid +125. Provoke → lose → collect is a live loop, and the generous default (resolved → full kill-XP) is a seat-trap the prompt never documents. Gate encounter XP on foes actually down.

Fix first

The DM can't see the purse — and the engine won't enforce it either SET-04-F1 + F2

The digest ships no pc.gold, so a memoryless seat prices everything blind; and item_changed clamps an overspend at zero instead of refusing it, so a careless seat hands a 9-gold PC a 75-gold warhorse. Two halves of one hole: ship the gold, add the affordability guard.

The memoryless-seat gaps

One family, one lesson: state that lives only in prose evaporates the moment the warm seat is swapped for the production memoryless one.

Med

Durable injuries aren't modeled SET-02-F1

Rennick's ruined hand is mechanically load-bearing in the fiction and invisible in the sheet — no marks, no condition, nothing in the digest. A maim, a curse, a lingering wound all evaporate on seat-swap. The PC needs a persisted marks/injuries channel.

Med

Concentration never ends, and the digest hides it SET-06-F2 + SET-08-F3

A one-minute Suggestion cast in set 04 was still flagged active at set 06's open — days later; nothing expires it by duration, time, rest, or choice. And the digest exposes no pc.concentration at all, so the stale flag rides invisibly. Write-side and read-side of the same gap.

Med

The number and the story disagree about who's a friend SET-12-F2 + SET-10-F1

The house-warden is a two-set "ally of respect" in prose while attitude.value still reads 0 / Indifferent — and his append-only dm.notes simultaneously hold "resisted / she" and "succumbed / careful man" with no supersede signal. A memoryless seat could surface either as canon. Fire attitude_shift when prose commits a relationship; stamp notes with recency.

Med

Rest-risk outcomes are obligations the digest doesn't flag SET-03-F1

"A Threat is already inside the site when you wake" is a hard mechanical fact the next turn must honor — surfaced only as one ledger line a memoryless seat could narrate straight past. Promote rest-risk results to a first-class pending-situation field.

The rest economy

Med

A short rest can never heal SET-07-F2

No Hit-Dice-spend path exists, so a wounded PC's only recovery is all-or-nothing (a full long rest) or DM charity. The 5e incremental-heal lever is simply absent — and it made an honest short-rest narration into a seat-trap.

Med

An interrupted long rest is a double penalty SET-07-F1

A raided rest returns zero recovery but still burns the full eight hours of world-clock. Advance only to the interruption point, or at least tell the seat the hours were eaten.

Low

No once-per-24h long-rest gate SET-06-F1

In a safe node, back-to-back long rests each refill everything; only clock burn and rest-risk brake the spam. Stamp lastLongRest and refuse the second benefit.

Cost, routing & harness

Low

The triage lane trips on keywords, not meaning SET-01-F1 + SET-12-F1

A social Insight read routed to the deep/combat lane off the words "saboteur" and "knife" (95.8s, ~$0.033 for a conversation); a negated violence clause ("…and I make no move at all") still tagged combat-action. Cost and routing only — correctness never moved — but a griefer can force the expensive lane at will.

Low

Branch-carried social_check.total is a guess the engine trusts SET-02-F2

The live d20 selects the branch, but the attitude commit is graded against a pre-authored literal the DM wrote blind. Agreement is luck, not law — inject the real die into the branch payload.

Low

Harness footguns: the vanishing crit, the one-way clock SET-01-F2 · SET-03-F2

A nat-20/nat-1 fall-through lives only in one invocation's stdout (a second roll silently reports nothing pending — the BUG-08 persistence half, bitten in practice); and advance can't move the clock backward, so an overshoot means hand-editing state. Plus one scaling note: the digest crept 14→20KB over a continued thread as dm notes accreted (SET-01-F3) — compaction will be wanted eventually.

What Held

Both campaigns are alive and continuable: Sella walks home to her leash with a name under her tongue,
and somewhere in a counting-house a filed Fool is learning what honesty is worth.

The world is honest. The people may not be. The engine, it turns out, is both.
