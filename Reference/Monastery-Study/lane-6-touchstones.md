# Lane 6 — Touchstones at gameplay scale

Two named touchstones: Baldur's Gate 3's **Rosymorn Monastery** (a terraced mountain
monastery in a modern 3D CRPG) and **Final Fantasy Tactics' Orbonne Monastery** staging (the
grid-tactics case). Sources are listed in `SOURCE-LEDGER.md` under "documentary sources."

---

## 1. Rosymorn Monastery (Baldur's Gate 3) — what the documentation says it is

A ruined cloister consecrated to Lathander, high in the mountains, reached by the **Rosymorn
Monastery Trail**. Documented structure:

- **Three levels**, plus a roof, plus a further descent. Traversal between them uses
  **jumping, flying, climbing vines and knotted roots, rope routes, wall breaches and
  windows**.
- **The approach.** The front gates carry a **statue of Lathander** and are held by githyanki.
  Guides describe reaching a **triangular terrace before the gate** by leaving the bridge on
  the approach and descending a rocky path beside it.
- **A second way in.** A **broken window to the left of the main gate** drops into the **wine
  cellar**, from which the whole monastery can be explored. So the front gate is a *contested*
  entrance with a bypass, not a funnel.
- **Ground level:** firewine storage with stained glass; an antechamber with an enchanted
  bust ("Magic Mouth"); a **central cloister hall with a large statue of Lathander at its
  centre**; a spiral staircase descending to the githyanki crèche below.
- **Upper level:** the former library (now infested), an area with an **oval colonnade at its
  centre**, eastern chambers with graves, and the **Dawnmasters' Memorial** with stained-glass
  windows depicting four religious leaders.
- **The roof:** reached by knotted roots and covered in twisting vines; holds a **giant
  eagles' nest**, a mural, an **ancient device described as a powerful weapon**, and loot.
- Named sub-areas documented: Dawnmasters' Memorial, Gremishkas' den, Firewine storage,
  Eagle's Nest, Guardian chamber, and multiple **balconies**.

### The structural reading

1. **The monastery is a vertical dungeon, and the roof is a room.** The site is stacked —
   cellar / ground / upper / roof / crèche below — and the top surface is not scenery but
   *content*, holding a nest, a mural and a plot object. This is the strongest possible
   support for the walkable-pitch law's payoff clause: **rooftop fighting becomes real** only
   if the roof has a reason to be visited, and BG3 gives it three.
2. **The courtyard's centre holds one object, and it is a statue of the god.** Same grammar
   as the well, the fountain house, the scaffold and the caravanserai fountain — one central
   object in a reserved void — with the object naming the house's owner.
3. **There are at least three ways in, and the front gate is the hard one.** Terrace-before-
   the-gate (the ceremonial route, defended), broken window to the cellar (the sneak), and
   the vertical routes. A single ceremonial gate as the *only* entrance would make this site
   much poorer; BG3's version keeps the ceremonial gate as the *fought-over* one and lets the
   cellar window and the vines carry the alternatives.
4. **The chapel's identity is carried by stained glass and by named memorial figures**, not
   by architecture. The Dawnmasters' Memorial is a *room of portraits*; the "who runs this
   place" fact is on the glass. That is a cheap, rollable per-occupant dressing slot on a
   fixed room.
5. **Ruin plus overgrowth is the state, not the exception.** Vines and roots are the
   climbing routes. The site's decay is its traversal system — a much better use of
   overgrowth than surface dressing.
6. **The approach involves leaving the obvious path.** The documented route to the terrace is
   "go back to the bridge and descend the rocky path beside it." The spur-off-the-road
   approach in the banked seed matches this instinct: the monastery is found by turning aside.

## 2. FFT Orbonne staging — what is documented, and what is not

**What is documented:** Orbonne is the game's opening. Ovelia is at prayer in the monastery;
the house is attacked; Agrias and the mercenaries fight outside. The first battle is a
**tutorial** in which the player controls only Ramza and the guests act on their own, so
published guides say explicitly that little strategy is required. A later chapter returns to
Orbonne and descends through the **Monastery Vaults / Underground Book Storage**, five floors
deep.

**What is not documented, at least in the sources reachable here:** the opening battle's map
dimensions, deployment tiles, elevation profile, or any tactical account of the causeway and
the church door as a fight. Two of the wiki targets refused automated fetch (one 403, one
402); the guide page that did return said only that the battle is a tutorial. **I am
recording this as a gap rather than inferring staging from the map images.**

**What I can say, from lane 5's own image reading rather than from documentation:** the
opening map (56) is a single climbing route from a timber bridge over water to a church door
at the top of a raised causeway, with large reserved quiet ground either side. That is a
composition in which the defender starts high and the attacker starts low — which is
consistent with an opening scene in which the player's side is arriving. But I did not verify
deployment, and I should not pretend otherwise.

The five-floor descent (57–61) is worth one structural note: **FFT gave the monastery a
vertical dungeon too, and it went down rather than up.** BG3's monastery stacks toward the
roof; FFT's stacks toward the vault. Both treat the monastery as a site with a hidden second
half beneath the sacred room.

## 3. What both touchstones agree on

1. **The monastery is not one room; it is a stack.** Both give it a hidden vertical
   dimension — BG3 upward to the roof and downward to the crèche, FFT downward through five
   library floors. Neither treats it as a single courtyard encounter.
2. **The sacred room is the hinge, and the interesting space is behind or beneath it.** BG3's
   cloister hall leads to the spiral stair; FFT's chapel sits above the vaults.
3. **One central object in the middle of the void**, and it identifies the owner (a statue of
   Lathander; a scaffold; a fountain).
4. **The library is a real room type.** Both give the monastery a book-space, and in FFT it is
   five floors of one — enough to carry an entire act. The banked room ring (chapel, hall,
   bedroom row, garden) has no library in it.
5. **Verticality is traversal, not just cover.** Vines, roots, ropes, balconies, spiral
   stairs, five descending floors. Height in a monastery is a route system, not a firing
   position.

## Findings (plain English)

1. **BG3's monastery is a three-level stack plus a roof plus a descent** — and the roof is a
   real room with a nest, a mural and a plot device on it. If Genesis wants walkable roofs to
   matter, this is how they are made to matter: put content up there.
2. **The front gate is the contested route, not the only route.** BG3 documents a defended
   ceremonial approach *and* a broken window into the cellar. A single-gate monastery loses a
   lot; a single *ceremonial* gate plus one broken way in loses nothing.
3. **The courtyard's centre object names the owner** — a statue of the god the house serves.
   That is the cheapest possible expression of the "who gets rolled here" cascade, on a piece
   the site already has.
4. **Who runs the house is written on the glass and the portraits**, not in the architecture.
   The Dawnmasters' Memorial is a rollable dressing slot on a fixed room.
5. **Overgrowth is the traversal system**, not surface decoration — vines and roots are the
   climbing routes. That is a better job for overgrowth than weathering.
6. **The monastery is found by turning aside from the obvious path** — the documented route
   to BG3's gate terrace leaves the bridge and descends a rocky path. Consistent with the
   banked spur approach.
7. **Both touchstones give the monastery a hidden second half.** BG3 goes up to the roof and
   down to the crèche; FFT goes down five library floors. Neither is a one-room site.
8. **The library is a missing room in the banked ring.** Both touchstones make it central; the
   banked room ring (chapel, shared hall, bedroom row, garden, well) has no book room.
9. **FFT's Orbonne opening is a tutorial**, and no published account of its deployment or
   elevation was reachable. The tactical staging claim the brief hoped for is **not
   evidenced**.
