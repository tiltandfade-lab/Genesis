# TTRPG Terrain System Census — Piece Taxonomies, Set Compositions, Interconnect Standards

## PER-SYSTEM REPORTS

### 1. Dwarven Forge (Dwarvenite cast, pre-painted, premium)
**(a) Taxonomy (their terms):** Floors · Walls · Corners · Doors (operable) · Elevation (risers, ledges, stairs, platforms, catwalks — sci-fi adds "biscuit peg" catwalk/pipeline system) · Scatter (stalagmites, rocks, rubble, plants) · Accessories/Dressings (furniture, treasure, LED-lit pieces) · Terrain Trays / Battleboards (magnetic base surfaces — a distinct product category) · Trees (peg-based, with separate stumps as sockets — Wildlands). Catalog navigation: Dungeons (Classic / Sinister / Vaulted / Forsaken Temple skins), Caverns (Stone / Ice / Lava / Underdoom skins), Cities (Buildings / Scaffolding / Infrastructure / Ruins), Castles (Towers / Walls), Wilderness (Forest / Mountain / Swamp / Ruins / Burrows), Sci-Fi (Catwalks / Pipelines / Scatter), Table Coverage & Accessories.
**(b) Starter/core:** 19-pc Starter (floors, doors, walls, corners only) and 45-pc Core Set (same four categories, larger counts). A basic Cavern Set: 4 curved wall sections, 3 straight walls, 2 complex corners, 2 dead-ends, 3 floors, 2 conversion pieces (corner/twisted — style-transition pieces), 2 stalagmites, 3 flat rocks, 2 water tubs. Note ~half of a cavern box is edge/wall geometry; scatter is included but sparse.
**(c) Connection:** Freeform gravity placement plus **anchor magnets** embedded in piece bottoms that grip steel Terrain Trays/Battleboards (lets a pre-built room be "flown in" whole); newer dungeon lines have magnet-socketed separate walls that seat on floor edges; sci-fi vertical uses biscuit pegs. **1" grid sculpted into every floor**; floor modules at 2×2", 4×4", 8×8". No clips — alignment is by footprint + magnets.
**(d) Archetypes:** dungeon, cavern (stone/ice/lava), city street+building interiors, castle, forest/mountain/swamp wilderness, sci-fi station. The widest catalog of any system.
**(e) Showpiece pattern — STRONG:** Dungeon of Doom and Caverns Deep are sold as **15 "Encounter" packs each** — every encounter bundle exists to stage one signature multi-stage trap/puzzle/feature (official per-encounter build guides at dwarvenforge.com/pages/build-guides-dungeon-of-doom-encounter-areas-1-15). Wilderness sold as "compositions" (a hill, a river run). The encounter pack = generic floors/walls + one bespoke feature piece is their core commercial unit.

### 2. WizKids WarLock Tiles (pre-painted plastic, clip system)
**(a) Taxonomy:** Tiles (floors, dual-sided) · Walls (exterior vs **interior** — a distinction most systems lack; straight/inside-corner/outside-corner) · Wall Doors (1" and 2" widths) · Corner Pillars (the joint piece where wall runs meet) · **Edge Caps** (finishers for exposed wall ends) · Accessories/"Dressings" (props) · Encounter boxes. Product tiers: Base Sets (Dungeon I–III, Town & Village I–III, Caverns, Forgotten Sewers), Expansion Packs, Accessory packs (Dungeon Dressings, Doors & Archways, Stairs & Ladders, Marketplace, Merchants, Town Watch, Summoning Circles…).
**(b) Core set (Dungeon Tiles I, 106 pieces):** 26× 2"×2" floor tiles + 2× 4"×4" + 2× 2"×8" (=30 floors) · 18 straight stone walls (2") + 16 interior walls (2") · 4+4 exterior wall doors (1"/2") + 2 interior wall doors · 8 corner pillars · 5+5 inside/outside corner walls · 20 edge caps · plus 90 WarLock clips, 8 DRAGONLOCK adapter clips, 8 OpenLOCK adapter clips. Caverns Base Set is instructive for organic ground: 8 floor **shape classes** (1-space, 2-space A/B, 3-space, 3-space curve, 5, 6, 8-space blobs) + 6 wall shape classes (single/straight-small/straight-large/corner/curve/S-corner) + rocks + a plank-bridge kit. Dungeon Dressings accessory (~35 pcs): chairs, tables, crates, bed, throne, chests, boulder, stools, cheval-de-frise, benches, rubble, kegs, treasure pile, statue, altar, barrels, long table, 2 pit traps.
**(c) Connection:** proprietary **WarLock clip** into sockets on tile undersides/edges; **1" square is the game unit, 2"×2" the standard tile**; walls clip to tile *edges* sitting flush ON the grid line (their pitch: walls don't consume floor squares); ships adapter clips for DRAGONLOCK and OpenLOCK — explicit cross-ecosystem bridging.
**(d) Archetypes:** dungeon, town/village (plaster+half-timber, boardwalks, town square, upper storeys), cavern, sewers, marketplace, ship accessories. Interior-biased.
**(e) Showpiece:** sold as separate small "encounter"/accessory boxes (throne, altar, summoning circles) rather than one-per-core-set.

### 3. Archon Studio Dungeons & Lasers (hard plastic sprue, unpainted)
**(a) Taxonomy:** Floors (square + "long") · Walls (standard + long) · Doorways · Clips (floor clips, wall clips, vertical clips, pins — four distinct connector types) · **Customization bits** (snap-on wall decor: banners, skulls, pipes, vents — the theming layer) · Props packs · Rooms (themed expansions) · Showpiece monsters (dragons).
**(b) Fantasy Starter (286 pcs):** 9 floors + 6 long floors · 15 walls + 8 long walls · 4 doorways · 40 floor clips + 44 wall clips + 26 vertical clips + 64 pins · 70 customization bits. So the *structural* vocabulary is just 5 piece types (~42 pieces); over 60% of the count is connectors + decor bits. Sci-Fi Starter identical skeleton (280 pcs, 64 bits) — same geometry, different skin, fully cross-compatible.
**(c) Connection:** proprietary plastic clips joining floor-to-floor edges and walls standing in clip seats; embossed 1" grid; walls are tall (dollhouse-style full height); multi-storey via vertical clips. Everything mixes across themes.
**(d) Archetypes:** fantasy dungeon, sci-fi facility, sewers (with clear-plastic water), caves (D&L VI: curved walls), castle (Grand Stronghold, 317 parts incl. balustrades/roof/stairs), themed rooms: Cursed Cathedral, Dwarven Mine, Torture Chamber, Hall of Heroes, Warlock Altar, AI Center, Engine Room (63-pc room-size boxes).
**(e) Showpiece — STRONG:** each expansion = one themed room; Kickstarters heavily marketed **dragons/giant monsters as the centerpiece figure** (free dragon minis with core sets); "one room + one monster" is their scene formula.

### 4. Mantic TerrainCrate (pre-colored PVC, props-only line)
**(a) Taxonomy:** essentially **all props/dressing** — furniture, containers, treasure, traps, doors (free-standing), scatter, small structures. No floors, no walls (Battlefield line adds fences/barricades/hedges/walls as free-standing scatter). Sold as themed "crates" in small/medium/large tiers.
**(b) Contents:** *Dungeon Depths* (73 pcs): cabinet, candlesticks, book piles, parchment, potions, ladder, desk, comfy chair, mirrors, orrery, owl-on-perch, scrying pool, round/rectangular tables, chairs, stools, benches, debris barricades, debris piles, skulls, braziers, tankards, bowls, traps… *Tavern* (26 pcs, 13 types ×2): round table, rect tables, stools, chairs, benches, sconces, plates, flagons, fireplace+hearth, barrel stacks, kegs, wine racks, bar tables. Catalog spread: Dungeon Essentials, Crates & Barrels, Dungeon Doors, Dungeon Treasures, Tavern, Blacksmith Forge & Stable, Church/Cathedral, Guard Barracks, Library, Wizard's Study, Gothic Mansion, Dark Lord's Tower, Medieval Village, Town Centre, Abandoned Town, Gothic Graveyard, Haunted Woods, Forest Trees, Military Campsite, Mine & Quarry, Torture Chamber, Temple Relics, Horse & Cart, Tables & Chairs, Battlefield walls/fences.
**(c) Connection:** none — pure freeform placement at 28–32mm scale; designed to sit on anyone else's floors/mats.
**(d) Archetypes (via props alone):** dungeon, tavern, study/library, forge, church, village/town, graveyard, woods, camp, mine, torture chamber, court.
**(e) Showpiece:** every crate anchors on 1–3 signature pieces (scrying pool + orrery in Dungeon Depths; fireplace + bar in Tavern; the Dark Lord's Tower IS a showpiece crate).

### 5. Printable Scenery OpenLOCK (open interconnect standard, 3D-print STL)
**The spec (printablescenery.com/2017/04/26/openlock-developer-information/):**
- **Base unit = the 1" dungeon square** (25.4mm), sized so a 25mm mini base fits one square. Footprint tessellation on 1"/2" increments (2"=50.8mm, ½"=12.7mm). Square + triangle tessellation templates published (triangles give diagonals/angled walls).
- **Heights:** full = 50.8mm (2"), half = 25.4mm; but wall height is explicitly *not* mandated — any height allowed.
- **Ports:** standardized rectangular clip sockets in piece sides at fixed locations — "the location of the OpenLOCK ports must never alter"; "the footprint tessellation must never change." These two rules are the entire hard standard.
- **Clips:** separate printed double-ended clips inserted through adjacent ports: basic clip, two springy variants, magnetic clip, grip clip; v5.x adds top-locking pins for vertical stacking. Clips are flexible PLA consumables (resin too brittle) — replaceable/printable independently of pieces.
- **Free to the designer:** surface aesthetics, height, whether a piece is wall or floor, interior geometry. License: CC BY-NC (commercial via free BSD-style license on request).
- **Ranges built on it:** Rampage dungeons, sewers, castles, caverns, Cities of Ramshackle & Ruin, Throne & Ash, sci-fi, ships; third parties (Saucermen Studios etc.) — plus WarLock ships OpenLOCK adapters. It's the de facto lingua franca of printed terrain.

### 6. Fat Dragon Games DRAGONLOCK (3D-print STL, 28mm)
**(a) Taxonomy:** floor tiles · walls · corners · pillars · doorways (with operable/removable doors) · stairs · clips (Dragonbite™) — plus the DRAGON TILES sub-line: same footprint with **low walls** (sightline-friendly) and modular pillars for multi-level/LED.
**(b) Dungeon Starter Set (2nd ed):** 6× 2"×2" floor tiles, 6 straight walls, 2 corners, 1 narrow pillar, 1 doorway w/ working door, 1 stairs (+mini adapter), Dragonbite clips, instructions. **~17 pieces / 6 shape types is pitched as a complete playable dungeon kit** (infinite reprint being the point; ~$0.40/tile in filament).
**(c) Connection:** Dragonbite triangular flex-clips into side sockets; 2"×2" tile on 1" grid; multi-level stacking native. (WarLock ships DRAGONLOCK adapter clips — same-family geometry.)
**(d) Archetypes:** dungeon, caverns, Lost City (dwarven), village, forest (Shadowgrove), Vallis Mortis (gothic outdoor), sci-fi, ships/city via expansions.
**(e) Showpiece:** expansion sets each themed around a feature (bridges, cathedral pieces); starter itself has none — stairs is the closest.

### 7. Monster Fight Club — Monster Scenery (pre-painted wilderness)
**(a) Taxonomy (their tags):** **Area Terrain** (footprint-template + contents: forests) · **Scatter Terrain** (rocks, bushes, crystals) · Rocks & Hills (multi-level stackable) · Forests & Trees (trunk + removable canopy, so minis stand under trees) · Templates (neoprene ground patches: forest floors, rivers, roads) · Wildlife (deer, penguins, bears — scene-dressing creatures) · Obstacles/Barricades · Game Mats.
**(b) Typical set:** *Verdant Forest*: 3 trees (removable canopies) + stump + double-sided neoprene forest-floor template. *Rock Hills*: 1 large multi-level hill + 1 medium + 1 small rock (consistent L/M/S triad across all hill SKUs). *Broken Ground*: 5 rocks + 2 fallen trunks + stump + 2 templates. *Rivers & Roads*: 5 double-sided neoprene strips. Sets are tiny — 3 to 10 pieces.
**(c) Connection:** fully freeform; the neoprene template defines the area footprint ("the forest") while pieces on it stay movable; hills stack in removable layers so minis stand on each tier.
**(d) Archetypes:** temperate wilderness (verdant/autumn/blossom/pine forests), ice wilds (snowy/glacial hills, ice fields, ice crystals), rivers/roads, plus modern/urban lines.
**(e) Showpiece:** the large hill / large tree in each set; the template+contents pattern means every SKU is one self-contained vignette.

### 8. Loke Battle Mats (2D printed mat books, 1" grid)
**(a/b) Format:** books of wipe-clean laminated map pages; Big Book = A4/12×9" ~58–60 maps, Giant = A3/12×16–17×12", Little = 6×6"; 360° lay-flat spine so two facing pages form one double-size map; two-volume slipcase sets pair to a 2'×2' play area. Also "The Terrain Set" (reusable scenery decals/overlays = 2D props layer) and scenery decal packs.
**(c) Standard:** universal 1" = 5ft grid; no interconnect — adjacency by opening pages side by side; modular sets are designed so page edges tile together (roads/walls continue across pages).
**(d) Ground archetypes (the census of what 2D "ground" needs):** dungeons; rooms/vaults/chambers (generic interiors); cells & shrines; bastions & strongholds; castles/crypts/catacombs; towns & taverns (alleys, markets, plazas, docks, tavern interiors); streets/sewers/cemeteries; wilderness (forest, marsh tracks, rivers, lairs, camps, roads); wilds/wrecks/ruins; dragon lairs; throne rooms; sci-fi ships/stations. Note their bundling: interiors sell by *function* (cells, vaults, shrines, throne rooms), exteriors by *biome*.
**(e) Showpiece:** each map page IS a set-piece composition (a throne room, a lair) — identity painted into the ground itself.

## CROSS-SYSTEM SYNTHESIS

**(1) Common-denominator taxonomy — every serious 3D system converges on exactly this stack (bottom-up):**
1. **Base surface** (magnetic tray / neoprene template / mat page) — optional but present in 5/8 systems; defines the region and lets built scenes move as one object.
2. **Ground/floor modules** — square tiles on a 1" game grid, standard module 2"×2" (DF, WarLock, DRAGONLOCK, OpenLOCK all agree), with a few large slabs (4×4, 8×8, 2×8) for fast area fill; organic lines (caverns) swap squares for a small family of blob shapes (WarLock's 8 floor classes).
3. **Verticals** — straight wall, inside corner, outside corner, door-wall, plus finishers (pillar at junctions, edge cap at exposed ends). Interior-vs-exterior wall is the mature distinction (WarLock).
4. **Circulation** — operable doors, stairs/ladders, bridges (always their own SKU; the door is a *mechanism*, not a wall texture, in every 3D system).
5. **Elevation** — risers, multi-level hills, platforms, catwalks (the premium-tier differentiator; DF and DRAGONLOCK do it natively, Monster Scenery via stackable hill layers).
6. **Props/dressings** — furniture, containers, treasure, traps (universally sold as separate packs; never in the structural core box).
7. **Scatter** — rocks, rubble, vegetation, barricades (small, freeform, ownerless).
8. **Showpiece/feature** — one large signature object per themed set (altar, throne, scrying pool, big hill, dragon, multi-stage trap).
9. **Connectors** — clips/pins/magnets as separate consumable parts, never molded into pieces in clip systems.

**(2) Ground : vertical : prop ratios in structural starters** (excluding connectors):
- WarLock Dungeon I: 30 ground : 76 vertical : 0 props ≈ **1 : 2.5 : 0**
- DRAGONLOCK starter: 6 : 10 : 0 (+1 stairs)
- D&L Fantasy: 15 : 27 : 0 structural (+70 decor bits ≈ 1 : 1.8 : 4.6 counting bits)
- DF core: 4 categories, floors ≈ ⅓, walls+corners ≈ ½, doors rest.
→ **Interior builders run ~1 ground : 2–2.5 vertical, zero props in core.** Props arrive in accessory packs at ~25–75 pieces per themed room (TerrainCrate: a *whole tavern* is 26 props; a *whole dungeon's* dressing is 73). Wilderness inverts completely: ~all ground/scatter, near-zero vertical (walls→cliff/hill edges). Connectors, where they exist, outnumber pieces (90–174 per ~40–106 pieces).
- Figures: none in any terrain core (D&L's bundled dragon is the marketing exception) — figure : terrain is a separate axis in every catalog.

**(3) Minimum viable vocabulary:** startlingly small. DRAGONLOCK proves **6 shape types** (floor, wall, corner, pillar, doorway, stairs) = playable dungeons; WarLock's 106-piece flagship uses ~12 shape classes; D&L's 286-piece box has **5 structural molds**; WarLock Caverns covers all organic ground with 8 floor shapes + 6 edge shapes. Variety is manufactured through: (i) texture/theme skins over identical geometry (D&L fantasy vs sci-fi; DF's 4 dungeon skins × 4 cavern skins), (ii) dual-sided pieces (WarLock, Loke, Monster templates), (iii) props. **Rule of thumb from advertised layouts: ~10–15 shape classes per biome cover ~90% of builds; props carry the rest.**

**(4) What carries scene identity:** *props, then wall skin, then ground texture — in that order for interiors; reversed for exteriors.* TerrainCrate is the existence proof: a tavern is 13 prop types (tables, bar, kegs, fireplace, flagons) with NO architecture at all — the room shape is generic, the furniture is the tavern. WarLock makes dungeon→town by swapping stone walls for plaster/half-timber over the same tiles + swapping altar/chains props for lampposts/market stalls. Wilderness identity lives entirely in ground+scatter (Loke sells only painted ground; Monster Scenery sells canopy color as the *only* difference between four forest SKUs). Function-specific interiors (shrine, cell, throne room) are prop-defined; biome exteriors are texture-defined. A game taxonomy should therefore treat **prop-set = room archetype** and **ground/edge skin = biome**, orthogonally.

**(5) Interface-standard lessons (OpenLOCK, plus the field):**
- **Standardize only two things:** the footprint tessellation (1" unit, square+triangle) and the connector port location/geometry. Explicitly free: height, surface art, and even the piece's *role* (any footprint may be wall or floor).
- **Connectors are separate parts**, not piece features — so wear, upgrades (magnetic/springy/grip variants, v5 top-pins for stacking), and cross-brand adapters evolve without re-cutting the piece library. WarLock shipping DRAGONLOCK+OpenLOCK adapter clips *inside its core box* shows interop is a selling point, not a threat.
- **The 1" grid is the true universal** — every system 2D or 3D, clipped or freeform, agrees a mini occupies one 1" square (25mm base fits inside); 2"×2" is the universal working module (big enough to place fast, small enough to shape rooms).
- Two philosophies coexist and both work: **locked** (clips: WarLock/D&L/print systems — rigid, portable, slower) vs **anchored-freeform** (DF magnets-on-tray, Monster's template+loose-pieces — fast, re-poseable mid-scene, the template defining "the area" while contents stay movable). The template/tray idea — *a region object that owns its loose contents* — is the most directly game-translatable convention in the whole survey.
- Commercial packaging converges on: **structural core box (geometry only) + themed dressing packs (identity) + encounter/showpiece packs (one big feature + its supporting cast)** — Dwarven Forge's 15-encounter module structure being the purest form: generic architecture reused everywhere, one signature feature piece per scene.
