## Mission — and why VERBOSE is in the name

Produce a long-form cultural sourcebook of pre-industrial urban life that Genesis can
mine for YEARS — hooks, occupants, rumors, meanings, textures. This is a READING study,
not a contact-sheet study: its output is prose, deliberately verbose. The usual
compression instinct is inverted here — when in doubt, write more, cite where it came
from, and keep the flavorful specifics (the exact street cry, the exact fine for a
false measure) rather than abstracting them away. Images optional and secondary.

## Boundaries

Public-domain and open sources (older urban histories on Project Gutenberg and the
like, encyclopedic sources, museum essays); cite every claim's source inline in the
lane notes (author/title/link) — a SOURCE-LEDGER.md of texts, not images. No game
screenshots. Outputs in-repo at `Reference/Urban-Culture-Study/`. Period band: broadly
medieval-to-early-modern European urban life as the base lane, with a closing lane on
non-European counterpoints so the culture axis doesn't calcify into one continent.

## Lanes (long-form notes per lane; all one pass for a background run)

1. **The day and the year.** How time structured a town: bells and calls, market days
   and market rights, curfew, the gates closing at night, the night watch's rounds,
   fairs, festivals, seasonal rhythms of trade. → Feeds the in-world clock, time-of-day
   arrival hooks, and the freshness of "when you arrive" as a rolled fact.
2. **Work and the street.** Guilds, masters/apprentices, street sellers and their
   cries, porters, water carriers; WHICH trades cluster WHERE and why (tanners and
   smiths downwind and by water, luxury trades at the center); the shop-house pattern
   (work below, live above); how a market hall actually operated hour by hour.
   → Feeds occupancy rosters (who is IN the market hall at dawn vs noon vs night),
   district logic, and dressing-with-meaning.
3. **Power and order.** Who ran a town: councils, guild masters, charters and market
   rights, tolls at the gate (the guard post's urban cousin), courts, the watch,
   punishments IN THE PLAZA — stocks, pillory, gallows as center objects loaded with
   meaning; fines, measures, and the policing of trade (false weights, bad bread).
   → Feeds faction texture, the plaza center-object choice, law-flavored hooks.
4. **The social fabric.** Class geography (who lives at the center, who against the
   wall); strangers, inns, and how arrival actually worked; the poor quarter (the
   shanty ruling's cultural backbone); outsider and minority quarters; servants and
   women's work; hospitality codes; the tavern as the rumor engine. → Feeds the social
   front door, NPC webs, and the DM's information economy.
5. **The senses and the squalor.** Smells, waste, water supply, animals in the street,
   noise, fire as THE urban catastrophe, plague and quarantine, death and burial in
   town. → Feeds honest narration texture and the condition/consequence systems.
6. **Belief and festival.** Temples/churches in daily life, processions claiming the
   streets, saints' days and their economics, charms over doorways (a decal that MEANS
   something), superstition and omen. → Feeds hooks across the spice bands — most of
   the Strange band's urban wrongness cues live here.
7. **Counterpoints.** A shorter lane of non-European pre-industrial urban life (souk
   and bazaar economies, caravanserai districts, East Asian ward systems, Mesoamerican
   market cities) — enough to keep the culture axis plural and feed
   CultureVisualConstitution with more than one urban tradition.

## Synthesis deliverables

- **The sourcebook itself** (the verbose lane notes — the primary deliverable).
- **Routed extract sheets**, each thin and pointed:
  - hook fodder: 30+ arrival-hook candidates sorted by spice band, each traceable to a
    sourced cultural fact (Grounded "the gate toll doubled this week" → Strange "the
    bells rang thirteen at noon");
  - occupancy fodder: who is actually present in a market hall / plaza / frontage at
    each time of day, as roster candidates;
  - meaning-bearing dressing: every prop/decal from the sibling brief's census that
    carries social meaning, annotated with what it says (routed back into
    URBAN-STUDY-BRIEF lane 2's roster);
  - DM lore fodder: the specifics worth quoting (cries, fines, customs) routed toward
    the DM's voice lanes.
- SOURCE-LEDGER (texts) · the founder packet (≤10, plain English).

## Report format

Per lane: file path · a LONG summary is welcome here (verbose is the contract) · the
5–10 most game-relevant findings flagged · declared gaps stated plainly. Founder
questions batched at synthesis. Adam rules.
