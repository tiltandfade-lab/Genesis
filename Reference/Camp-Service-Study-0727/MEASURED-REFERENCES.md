# Measured references — evidence for/against the three named build orders

## 1. The permanence-ladder implementation order

**What's proposed** (working spec, "Growth ladder and implementation order"): Exterior Rung 0
Golden Seed → Rung 1 traces → militarized Rung 0–1 → Rung 2 waystation → hosted expressions.
The ladder's underlying rung structure itself (0 bare / 1 customary / 2 waystation, two
rollers one technology) is recorded in the catalog as "(THE RULING)"; what remains open is
the *order in which the family gets built and proved*, and whether real evidence changes it.

### Evidence FOR the rung 1 / rung 2 distinction

Three independent, historically and geographically unrelated service-and-lodging systems
converge on the exact same two-tier shape the ladder proposes:

| system | minimal/unstaffed tier | staffed/lodging tier | spacing | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Roman `cursus publicus` | **mutatio** — horse-change only | **mansio** — barracks, stables, food, lodging | mutatio ~8–10 km; mansio ~40 km | [UNRV](https://www.unrv.com/government/cursus-publicus.php) |
| Scottish drove roads | **stance** — unbuilt resting ground, cattle fed/watered | **drovers' inn** — built, staffed, stabling, food | inns roughly every 4 miles on busy routes | [Local Drove Roads, "Drovers' Inns"](https://www.localdroveroads.co.uk/drovers-inns/); [Country Life](https://www.countrylife.co.uk/articles/drovers-routes-the-ancient-trails-from-farm-to-market-and-how-theyve-shaped-our-roads-to-this-day-252116) |
| Pony Express / stage route | **relay ("swing") station** — keeper + stock tender, horse-change only, no lodging | **home station** — riders housed, meals, spare stock, larger staff | relay ~10–25 km; home ~100–160 km | [Pony Express Ride](https://ponyexpressride.com/quick-facts-3/relay-stations-and-home-stations/) |

Two of the three surviving home-station buildings were photographed and downloaded this pass
(`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-02 Hollenberg, CS-03 Ruby Valley) — both explicitly documented as the
*lodging-and-spare-stock* tier, not the horse-change-only tier. This is real, physical, still-
standing confirmation that rung 2's "family takes root... standing shed... apron-without-gate"
description matches how real rooted road-service actually presents.

**Reading:** three unrelated cultures, three unrelated eras (antiquity, early-modern Scotland,
19th-century America), zero shared lineage, and all three independently produced the same
minimal/rooted split. This is strong convergent evidence that the rung 1 → rung 2 distinction
is a real structural fact about route service, not an invented design convenience.

### Evidence complicating (not against) the proposed build order

The real systems above are **linear network properties** — spacing decisions made by a state
or company laying out a whole route at once — while the ladder as specced is written as a
**per-site growth-over-time property** (one site climbing from rung 0 to rung 2 through
repeated use). A mutatio was built as a mutatio; it did not start as a bare camp and
accumulate into one. A drovers' inn is the one case in this evidence set that *is* documented
as organic growth ("stances... including inns and friendly farms" growing up around
long-used resting places — [RuralHistoria](https://ruralhistoria.com/2023/12/07/drovers-were-the-nomads-of-rural-britain/)).

This does not argue against the proposed build order — it argues that the working spec's
already-ruled "two rollers, one technology" split (camp and waystation are *separate* roller
entry points; a waystation is not required to escalate from a bare camp inside one roll) is
the correct design for handling **both** real patterns: an institution-built rung 2 (rolled
directly, as a waystation) and an organically-grown rung 2 (reached by repeated camp use over
game-time). The proposed build order tests the organic path first (0 → 1 → 2), which is the
harder, more informative case per the guidelines' learning-value test (a directly-rolled
waystation is comparatively free once the kit exists). **Recommendation: keep the proposed
order**, but add one cheap card fact this pass did not find named anywhere in the working
spec — an **origin tag on rung 2** (`organic-grown` vs. `institution-built`) — since real
waystations visibly differ (a state-built mansio reads uniform and planned; a grown drovers'
inn reads accreted and asymmetric) and the fact is free once rolled. Full reasoning in
`synthesis.md` §3; this is FQ-CS-1 in the working spec's founder packet.

### What real evidence does NOT cover

None of the three systems documents anything resembling rung 0 (bare campsite) as a named
institutional tier — by definition, a one-night, un-repeated campsite leaves no record for
historians to find. Rung 0 is evidenced by the *absence* of a documented lower tier below
the two real ones, and by the "beside, not astride" capsule logic itself, not by a named
historical institution. This is an honest gap, not a contradiction — flagged so the working
spec's confidence in rung 0 is not overstated on this evidence's strength.

## 2. The tent-kit build order

**What's proposed** (catalog, "The tent kit + camp composition"): bell cone → Roman troop
tent → the low open span → the dome/bender.

### Evidence FOR each named form, in order

1. **Bell/cone.** The British Army bell tent entered regular service by the Crimean War
   (1853–56) and remained standard through WWI — a single mass-produced mesh, arrayed in
   ordered rows, sleeping up to ~15 soldiers radiating from a central pole
   ([Bell Tent UK, "The Origin of Bell Tents"](https://belltent.co.uk/pages/the-origin-of-bell-tents);
   [Life inTents](https://www.lifeintents.com/blogs/the-lit-list/the-history-of-bell-tents)).
   Two 1900 photographs of an actual ordered bell-tent cavalry camp (Fort Lawton, Washington)
   were downloaded this pass (`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-05, CS-06) and show exactly the
   "standardized-unit read" the spec claims, plus a mule shed and picketed horses at the
   carrier/store edge the spec's zoning also requires.
2. **Roman troop tent (`papilio`).** Confirmed 10 Roman feet square (≈2.96 m), housing one
   8-man `contubernium`, carried by a dedicated pack mule
   ([Karwansaray Publishers, "The Roman Camp"](https://www.karwansaraypublishers.com/en-us/blogs/gary-brueggemans-the-roman-army-ancient-warfare/the-roman-camp);
   [x-legio.com](https://x-legio.com/en/wiki/tabernaculo)). This directly sources a number the
   working spec already asserted without citation ("the ruled Roman troop tent begins near
   2 × 2 cells") — the historical 10 Roman ft square genuinely does sit almost exactly at
   2 × 2 five-foot cells, closing a citation gap rather than surfacing a conflict.
3. **The low open span.** The Bedouin `bayt al-sha'ar` ("house of hair") is low, flat-topped,
   woven from goat/camel hair, and **open at the front** by design, with side panels raised
   or lowered to adjust ventilation and privacy
   ([Wadi Rum Nomads](https://www.wadirumnomads.com/bedouin-house-of-hair/);
   [Moroccan Styled](https://www.moroccanstyled.com/blog/bedouin-tents-all-you-need-to-know-E0ZUP)).
   A period photograph (`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-07) shows the low, open-fronted profile. This
   directly supports the spec's characterization of this form as "the one genuinely new mass;
   cover + concealment + a room you can shoot into" — the open front is a real, load-bearing
   architectural feature of the tradition (a reception/majlis space), not a game abstraction
   invented to create a sightline.
4. **The dome/bender.** Romani/Traveller bender tents are a real, documented form: bent
   hazel or willow withies lodged in the ground and woven into a dome, covered with canvas or
   tarpaulin, fast to erect and strike, historically used for seasonal work such as hop-
   picking and fairs ([Preindustrial Craftsmanship, "Tents and the Vardo Life"](https://paleotool.com/2018/11/23/tents-and-the-vardo-life-what-is-a-bender/);
   [Wikipedia, "Bender tent"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bender_tent)). This directly
   supports the spec's "expedient, locally framed, or repair-constrained" characterization.
   **No Wikimedia Commons image of a bender tent was located this pass** despite three search
   attempts — recorded honestly as `DOWNLOAD-PENDING` in `LICENSE-LEDGER.md` rather than
   substituted with an unrelated image.

### Evidence complicating the proposed order

The evidence above supports every individual form claim. It also surfaces a real tension in
the *order*: by construction cost and erection speed, the dome/bender is arguably the
**simplest and cheapest** of the four (a single bent-frame family, fastest historical
erect/strike time on record), which is the opposite end of the ladder from where it's
proposed. The counter-case — and the reason the proposed order likely still holds — is the
same one the SITE-11 founder packet used for its own build-order tension (`FQ-11-1`): the
*cheapest to build first* and the *most informative to build first* are different questions.
A single dome teaches nothing about ordered-row placement, grid-exact tent footprints, or
open-front sightline mechanics; the bell/cone does, immediately, and at zero fractional-cell
cost once the Roman tent follows it. **Recommendation: keep the proposed order** on learning-
value grounds, consistent with the guidelines' step 4 test — but this is a real, arguable
tension, not a settled question research alone can close. This is FQ-CS-2 in the working
spec's founder packet.

## 3. The guest-family property (hosted-camp mechanism)

**What's proposed** (catalog, "The guest-family property," working specifics marked
`PROPOSED — Fable's mechanism, awaiting redline"): camps may be summoned inside a host
(dungeon, urban lot, natural interior) under host-governed population/development caps;
dungeon caps stay at rung ≤1, urban caps loosen toward an "impoverished/shanty quarter"
expression.

### Evidence FOR the mechanism's core claim

Jordan's Zaatari refugee camp is the clearest available real-world analog: a bounded camp on
land it does not own (Jordanian state land, UNHCR-administered — structurally the same
relationship as "the camp borrows someone else's floor" in the ruling's own language), which
in fact grew a real, substantial service economy — an unplanned market street nicknamed
"Sham Elysées," an estimated $12.7M/month informal economy, shops, a rental-dress business, a
pizza restaurant
([Refugees International](https://www.refugeesinternational.org/the-difference-a-market-makes-the-case-of-syrian-refugee-camps-in-jordan/);
[Business of Fashion](https://www.businessoffashion.com/articles/global-markets/profit-loss-champs-elysees-syrian-refugee-camp/)) —
while remaining, in law and administration, a temporary camp rather than a municipality. Two
images were downloaded this pass: an aerial/satellite view showing the camp's real bounded
grid footprint (`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-08) and a ground-level shop row
(`LICENSE-LEDGER.md` CS-09). This is direct, photographed evidence that a hosted/guest camp
really can root a service economy — "same kit, same capsule, same occupancy/social
machinery," exactly the ruling's claim — at a scale far beyond a single waystation.

### Evidence complicating the proposed caps

The same case also shows the proposed caps may be too soft on the *upper* end. Zaatari is
not a small camp reaching an "impoverished/shanty quarter" rung — after roughly a decade it
functions, in several published accounts, as one of Jordan's largest population centers, with
concrete-block replacement structures increasingly common
([UNHCR, "10 facts at 10 years"](https://www.unhcr.org/us/news/stories/jordans-zaatari-refugee-camp-10-facts-10-years)).
This is the live, empirical version of a boundary `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` §3.4
already names as open and unclaimed: *"a permanent poor-quarter/warren... distinct from Site
2's temporary camp-on-borrowed-ground... this gap is real and unclaimed by any of the
twelve."* The real-world case argues that a hosted camp's rooted rung needs an explicit
**upper exit condition** — a named point at which continued growth stops being "Site 2's
urban-hosted shanty-quarter expression" and starts requiring the settled-life slum entry
instead — rather than leaving the urban cap as an open-ended "loosens." This is FQ-CS-3 in
the working spec's founder packet.

### A cautious, non-literal analogy for the dungeon cap

No real-world case involves monster-inhabited dungeon rooms, so the dungeon-host rung-≤1 cap
cannot be evidenced directly. The closest functional analog is well documented, though not
literal: informal occupation of derelict or abandoned structures (squatted factories, unused
tunnels, ruins) overwhelmingly stays at "bare/customary" intensity — bedding, fire, minimal
gear — specifically *because* occupants do not own or control the structure and cannot
modify it. This is consistent with, and mildly supportive of, the dungeon cap's logic (use
without construction), but it is offered as a reasoning analogy, not a sourced historical
case, and should be weighted accordingly.
