*p. 151 MM 2024*

The harpy is an ambush predator that hunts with sound. Its luring song is not a supernatural effect imposed from a distance — it requires the creature to actually sing, and the song works on any creature that can hear it and fails its save. This makes harpies most dangerous in echoing caves, cliff faces, rocky coastlines, and dungeon spaces with good acoustics. A harpy singing from the ceiling of a large cavern can affect an entire party simultaneously.

What sets harpies apart from simple fliers is the Luring Song's enduring effect. A charmed creature doesn't fight back — it moves toward the harpy. In a dungeon, "move toward the harpy" often means "move toward a pit, a ledge, a group of other harpies, or an area full of other hazards." The harpy isn't trying to fight the charmed creature; the harpy is trying to get the charmed creature to walk off a cliff, and *then* feed on it at leisure.

Harpy packs are territorial and loud. They screech constantly when threatened and will call in nearby flock members when combat starts. Unlike most monsters that benefit from silence, harpies become more dangerous with sound — more harpies singing creates a cacophony of overlapping saves.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Harpies are audible before they are visible.

- **The Singing:** A distant, haunting melody in the dungeon or cliffside that doesn't match any known instrument. DC 12 Perception to hear. DC 14 Arcana or Nature to identify as a potential harpy song before making the saving throw.
- **The Dive Marks:** Harpy talons leave distinctive parallel gouges where they land — on ledges, cliff faces, dungeon floors. Fresh claw marks from above indicate harpy roosting above.
- **The Charmed Victim:** Finding a humanoid that has walked into a dangerous location (over a ledge, into a flooded corridor, into a trap) with no apparent reason may indicate harpy luring. A DC 13 Medicine or Insight check can identify the dazed, post-charmed state.

### 1. Harpy

A vicious aerial predator that charms prey with its luring song before feeding on it.

_Medium Monstrosity, Chaotic Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 11
- **Hit Points:** 38 (7d8 + 7)
- **Speed:** 20 ft., Fly 40 ft.
- **STR:** 12 (+1) | **DEX:** 13 (+1) | **CON:** 12 (+1) | **INT:** 7 (−2) | **WIS:** 10 (+0) | **CHA:** 13 (+1)
- **Senses:** passive Perception 10
- **Languages:** Common
- **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The harpy makes two attacks: one with its Claws and one with its Club.
- **Claws:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +3 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 6 (2d4 + 1) slashing damage.
- **Club:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +3 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 3 (1d4 + 1) bludgeoning damage.
- **Luring Song:** The harpy sings a magical melody. Every humanoid and giant within 300 ft. that can hear the song must succeed on a DC 11 Wisdom saving throw or be **Charmed** until the song ends. The harpy must take a Bonus Action on each subsequent turn to continue the song. It can stop singing at any time. The song ends if the harpy is incapacitated. While charmed, a target is Incapacitated and ignores the songs of other harpies. If the charmed target is more than 5 ft. away from the harpy, it must move on its turn toward the harpy by the most direct route. It doesn't avoid opportunity attacks, but before moving into damaging terrain (such as a pit or fire), the target can repeat the saving throw. A target that successfully saves is immune to this harpy's song for the next 24 hours.

### 2. Harpy Matriarch

An elder harpy of exceptional size and social dominance. The matriarch's song is more powerful and can sustain itself even while she attacks.

_Medium Monstrosity, Chaotic Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 12 (natural armor)
- **Hit Points:** 65 (10d8 + 20)
- **Speed:** 20 ft., Fly 40 ft.
- **STR:** 14 (+2) | **DEX:** 14 (+2) | **CON:** 14 (+2) | **INT:** 9 (−1) | **WIS:** 12 (+1) | **CHA:** 16 (+3)
- **Skills:** Perception +3
- **Senses:** passive Perception 13
- **Languages:** Common
- **Challenge:** 3 (700 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Flock Leader:** While the matriarch is alive and conscious, harpies within 30 ft. have Advantage on attack rolls against creatures that are Charmed by any harpy.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The matriarch makes two Claw attacks.
- **Claws:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 9 (2d6 + 2) slashing damage.
- **Luring Song:** As per the standard harpy, but the DC is 14 and the matriarch can maintain the song as a free action (not Bonus Action), allowing her to attack while singing.
- **Screech (Recharge 5–6):** The matriarch emits a discordant screech. All creatures within 30 ft. that can hear it must succeed on a DC 13 Constitution saving throw or be **Deafened** and **Frightened** of the matriarch until the end of their next turn.

### d10 The Luring Scene (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Walking Dead** | A humanoid body is found at the base of a cliff or pit — no wounds except those from the fall. The victim walked off the edge willingly. Harpy feathers are lodged in the rocks above. |
| **2** | **The Talon Marks** | Deep, parallel claw gouges in the rock at a ledge or window — where harpies land regularly. The freshness of the marks indicates recent, repeated use. |
| **3** | **The Dazed Survivor** | A survivor of a harpy encounter wanders in a confused state — still partially affected by the song, responding slowly to questions, staring at nothing. They can be snapped out of it with a successful DC 12 Medicine check. They have crucial information about what's ahead. |
| **4** | **The Feather Collection** | A nest visible above contains shiny objects — coins, buttons, a small mirror — gathered by the harpies. Among the collection is one item that doesn't fit: a scholar's lens, a military identification token, a piece of correspondence. |
| **5** | **The Wax Plugs** | A dead adventurer in the room has crudely torn strips of cloth wrapped around their head and stuffed into their ears. They were trying to block the song. It didn't work well enough. |
| **6** | **The Echoing Corridor** | A specific architectural feature — a curved wall, a vaulted ceiling — amplifies sound unusually. Standing in the center of this room, the party hears a distant harpy song as if it were in the room with them. The harpies aren't here. Yet. |
| **7** | **The Roost Calendar** | Accumulated guano and bone deposits at specific ledges indicate harpy roosting patterns. A DC 13 Nature check reveals the harpies roost here at dawn and dusk only — and hunt during the night. |
| **8** | **The Failed Ward** | A magical circle on the floor, drawn in salt and ash — someone attempted to ward an area against the luring song. The circle is broken. The ward failed. The caster's notes are still nearby, describing how to make a functional ward with 10 gp worth of components. |
| **9** | **The Music** | Harpy feathers are arranged on the floor in a pattern — roughly musical in structure, resembling sheet music. Whether this is deliberate communication, territorial marking, or something else entirely requires a DC 15 Arcana check to determine. |
| **10** | **The Sympathetic Nest** | A harpy nest contains evidence that is disturbing in a different way: the nest includes fabric, soft material, and the arrangement suggests maternal nesting. Eggs. Several of them. The harpies in this dungeon aren't a raiding party — they moved here to breed. How the party handles that information is up to the table. |
