# Synthesis — FFT × Triangle Strategy world study

**The verdict in one paragraph.** The hypothesis survived pressure with one refinement:
FFT supplies the *composition economy* (measured: median 120-cell boards, never larger
than 225; 2–4 masses; one spatial sentence; quiet ground; composed edges) and Triangle
Strategy supplies the *presentation stack* (documented and observed: hand-dotted surfaces
on 3D forms, full-range value with saturated accents, lit citizens, motivated practicals
against scene grades, context bands that continue the world, tilt-shift framing) — but
the deepest finding is that both games are *the same discipline at two budgets*, and the
discipline is ordered: pixel-native base art first, effects built up, never photoreal
reduced down (Asano's "accurate HD-2D," which independently confirms Adam's sprite-first
ruling). Genesis already owns the effects tier and the sprite tier; what stands between
the current engine and Triangle-Strategy-grade world beauty is **three productions and a
proof** — materials routed to production scenes (with the measured value-ceiling/chroma
claim), context Bands 1+3, mutation channels 1–3, and the C1H composed-battlefield proof
— all inside the vignette scale the corpus itself validates. Genesis's wedge (persistent,
roll-authored, per-instance visible consequence) exists nowhere in the corpus and is
cheaper than what the corpus spent on rotation and animation.

## 11.4 The compact-world recipe (the §11.4 question, answered plainly)

**Spatial:** stay in the vignette band (the whole corpus lives there); spend cells on 2–3
stacked elevation bands, one drop, and one scenery-only vertical rather than floor; land
the arrival ON the first decision; clip every true continuation honestly.
**Visual:** pixel-DNA materials at the approved texel scale in 3–5 families; claim the
top value decile on one sun-keyed face (or practical, at night); one saturated accent
family; practicals against the grade; context Band 1 (portal/support apron) + Band 3
(far premise) around a sharp floor; composed edges, never void.
**Transition:** large sites are linked windows (the shipped Wolffort model): 2–3
precommitted frontiers per window; crossing one materializes the next; no rendered travel
without decisions (the movement doctrine is corpus-normal).
**Persistence:** the wedge — decal/attachment/override channels bind consequences to
object instances with receipts, so revisited windows *remember*; coalesced residue keeps
history legible without clutter. Neither reference game does this; it is Genesis's
distinct contribution and costs less than what they spent on rotation.

## 11.5 Transfer and anti-transfer

**Ten rules to take from FFT** (all measured/observed in ≥3 cohort maps):
1. One primary spatial sentence per board. 2. 2–4 broad masses with ranked hierarchy.
3. Roads are regions with width/apron/narrowing events. 4. Architecture anchors a
transition (across/above/beside the route). 5. Quiet ground is reserved composition.
6. Rock/vegetation in relational clusters, never scatter. 7. Real geometry for every
affordance; paint for identity. 8. Composed board edges that follow masses and
continuations. 9. Sparse props; silhouette and surface carry richness. 10. Median-120
board sizes with hard first-decision adjacency.

**Ten rules to take from Triangle Strategy:**
1. Pixel base art first; effects build up (the "accurate HD-2D" order). 2. Hand-dotted
material DNA shared between figures and world (Genesis: via sprite-first MM parents).
3. Full value range claimed in every lighting state; accents saturated above terrain.
4. Warm practicals against cool grades (and the reverse) as the mood engine.
5. Context bands: thin blurred FG, sharp board, hazed far premise. 6. The roof/upper
lane as contested space (two-lane streets). 7. Weather/state variants of the same board
(day/rain pairs) instead of new boards. 8. Sibling windows of one persistent place.
9. Tile-property readouts surfacing terrain truth (Height/material HUD — kin to
Genesis's inspector). 10. Edge treatment as a *designed* question with a budget (Arai).

**Ten things not to copy:**
1. Free camera rotation (the documented cost driver). 2. Multi-facing sprite sets.
3. Cast-wide frame animation. 4. Bespoke per-map hand-texturing. 5. One-off scripted
set-pieces as the consequence model. 6. FFT's black void edge. 7. FFT's blob shadows.
8. IC's paper-grain full-frame filter (fights the crisp-texel law; noted, not adopted).
9. Photoreal-reduced environments (rejected by the register's own authors). 10. Any
layout/mesh/texture/palette lift from either game (grammar transfers; content never).

**Five ways Genesis remains visibly and mechanically different:**
1. Boards are *rolled and compiled*, never authored — infinite sites inside the corpus's
   beauty envelope. 2. Persistent per-instance visible consequence (the wedge). 3. One
   fixed composed camera with omission grammar (a *stronger* diorama statement than
   rotation). 4. The DM-seat narrative layer binding every visible change to fiction.
5. The tabletop-miniature register (standee bases, plinth presentation, warp motion) as
   identity rather than imitation of HD-2D's illustration register.

## 11.8 Founder packet (4 questions — everything else proceeds regardless)

**F-1 · Medium-site windows vs linked windows.** *Evidence:* every shipped corpus board
≤ ~500 est cells; Genesis medium intents 528–832; the vignette band is corpus-native;
Wolffort ships "large" as three windows (lane 1.10, lane 6.5). *Options:* (A) battle-band
cap 12×16 + always-link; (B) status quo (16×18 ceiling; medium = site windows with
enforced decision density); (C) two-band law — site cap ~18×22, everything larger links
(R6). *Recommendation:* **C** — it preserves every documented program while making the
movement doctrine structural. *Tradeoff:* window-transition polish becomes early
infrastructure; fewer single-frame vistas. *Proceeds regardless:* E-1…E-5 all run inside
the vignette band under any option.

**F-2 · Edge register: plinth, continuation, or hybrid.** *Evidence:* targets ship the
tabletop plinth; TS ships world continuation; FFT's void is rejected; both remaining
registers are defensible and read differently (lane 6.1). *Options:* (A) continuation
everywhere (TS-like immersion); (B) plinth everywhere (tabletop identity, cheapest); (C)
hybrid — plinth as the presentation chrome with Band-3 premise behind it, continuation
aprons only at true portals. *Recommendation:* **C** — keeps the miniature identity and
the worldfulness. *Tradeoff:* two edge grammars to maintain. *Proceeds regardless:* E-3
tests both reads from one fixture.

**F-3 · The value ceiling and accent chroma.** *Evidence:* measured p98 gap (corpus
daylight ≥85, Genesis ≤75; env chroma ~half the corpus); the grim register is Adam's law
(SUBTLE-TEXTURE, muted realms) (lane 3.7–3.8). *Options:* (A) keep the muted envelope as
identity; (B) claim the ceiling only on sun-keyed faces + practicals + one accent family
per scene (corpus-normal highlights, register otherwise intact); (C) full corpus range.
*Recommendation:* **B**. *Tradeoff:* A keeps maximum grimness but leaves the measured
"pop" on the table; C risks the WoW/candy failure the canon bans. *Proceeds regardless:*
E-1 captures both A and B variants for the ruling.

**F-4 · The snow gap.** *Evidence:* p05 is the only packet with no Genesis column; snow
is the corpus's cheapest value-control lesson (Zeakden satMean 26 + full value range).
*Options:* (A) commission one snow/ice target + one material family now; (B) defer until
a rolled world demands it. *Recommendation:* **A** (small, closes an environmental
class). *Tradeoff:* one more material family in the first production pass. *Proceeds
regardless:* everything.
