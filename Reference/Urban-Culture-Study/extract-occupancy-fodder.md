# Extract — occupancy fodder: who is actually here, and when

**Nothing here is locked. Adam rules; this file evidences.**

**What this sheet is.** Roster candidates for the three primary urban surfaces — **the market
hall**, **the plaza**, and **the street frontage** — resolved by time of day, with day-type and
occupancy-state modifiers. Every entry is traceable to a lane finding; entries marked *(inferred)*
are reasonable extensions of a sourced fact rather than sourced facts themselves, and are marked so
they can be stripped.

**The governing lane-1 result:** time of day in an urban site is not a lighting parameter, it is a
**rules parameter**. The same plaza at the market signal and at curfew is a different legal regime
with a different population.

**Cascade note.** Per WORLD-TURN §5a this sheet is *fresh-roll fodder only*: the engine remembers
first (written ledger facts LOCK), the region leans the odds, and only then does it roll on
something like the tables below. The freshness valve guarantees a fresh roll every X segments —
these rows are what that valve should be drawing from.

## 1. THE MARKET HALL

| slot | who is here | trace |
|---|---|---|
| **pre-dawn** (before cockcrow) | forestallers buying privately, illegally, in the dark; one or two sellers willing to be bought from | L1 §1.2 — "out at cockcrow to buy privately when the citizens were at Mass, so that by six o'clock, there was nothing left in the market" |
| **dawn (4 a.m., summer)** | **the mayor in person, walking the stalls**; the hall's keeper; the beam's farmer opening up; porters arriving | L2 §2.3 — Roger Achley, 1512; the beam "farmed" to a rent-paying keeper |
| **morning, market day** | traders in their assigned sections; **the beam-crew — a master, four master porters, porters under them, a strong cart and four great horses**; foreign merchants compelled to sell here and nowhere else, paying the higher storage rate; a clerk taking dues | L2 §2.3 — the Leadenhall dossier; the Weyhouse establishment |
| **morning, NON-market day** | the keeper; grain in store (the hall doubles as the city granary); a few permanent tenants; the beam idle under lock | L2 §2.3 — "the show of the said wares to be made three days in the week"; the hall as granary |
| **noon** | peak crowding; disputes reaching the market's own court; cook-sellers working the doors *(inferred from the Westminster Gate cook-stalls at high prime)* | L1 §1.3 |
| **afternoon** | thinning; carts loading; the porters' cart shuttling to the beam and back; sweepings accumulating | L2 §2.3 |
| **evening / curfew** | shutting up; the keeper locking; goods left overnight are the keeper's liability *(inferred)* | L1 §1.4 |
| **night** | empty and locked, with a watch obligation if goods are stored — compare the fair's booths "closed in with walls, and gates locked every night, and watched" | L1 §1.7 |
| **scarcity state** | a crowd pressing at the doors for grain: "such press about them, that one man was ready to destroy another, in striving to be served for their money" | L2 §2.3 — 1512 |

**Non-European variants worth keeping on the same fixture:**
- **Three official market days a week** appears independently in London (Mon–Wed) and Hangzhou —
  so "the hall is cold four days in seven" is a cross-cultural default. (L2, L7)
- **Opening at noon on 300 drum strokes, closing on 300 gong beats an hour and three-quarters
  before dusk** (Chang'an) is the sharpest available alternative schedule. (L7 §7.6)
- **A magistrate stationed in the market itself** — the *muhtasib* on his raised *dikka*;
  Tlatelolco's three judges and constables; Kinsay's two facing court-palaces per square. (L7 §7.2)

## 4. DAY-TYPE OVERRIDES (these replace the table above, not decorate it)

| day type | what changes | trace |
|---|---|---|
| **Sunday / holy day** | shops shut — "because apprentices could not be trusted to carry on work in the absence of their masters at church"; the parish church occupied in strict rank order, servants standing in the aisles | L1 §1.6, L4 §4.5 |
| **Saturday** | half-holiday from noon, "seems to have been universal" | L1 §1.6 |
| **FAIR** | civic government suspended; every shop in and around town closed by the fair-holder's order; the town's weights carried off for assay; the fair's own court (piepowder) sitting daily; booths inside a walled churchyard with gates locked and watched nightly; residents required to bring bloodshed cases to the fair's tribunal | L1 §1.7 |
| **CHURCH ALE** | taverns closed by order of the council; each ward has built a booth in the cemetery; the whole town in the churchyard; you may bring food but must buy the drink | L6 §6.2 |
| **PROCESSION DAY** | the route dressed with arches of green boughs, altars at named stations, lights in windows, fresh rushes strewn ahead; the countryside in town for days either side; **and at night the sacred quarter locked and empty while the drinking quarter is crowded to the doors** | L6 §6.4 |
| **MIDSUMMER** | bonfires in the streets; the wealthy setting tables of bread and drink before their doors for neighbours *and passers-by*; doors dressed with green birch, fennel, St John's wort, orpin and white lilies; glass oil-lamps burning all night; the marching watch on a 3,200-yard route with 700 cressets, each cresset with two men | L6 §6.5 |

## 5. SEASONAL AND STANDING MODIFIERS

- **Entertainers are resident in town from All Saints (1 Nov) to Candlemas (2 Feb)** and travelling
  the rest of the year. A plaza in November has minstrels, harpers, pipers and players living in it;
  the same plaza in June does not. (L1 §1.7)
- **Statutory work hours:** March–September, 5 a.m. to 7–8 p.m., with half an hour for breakfast
  and an hour and a half for dinner; September–March, daybreak to nightfall; **a permitted
  after-dinner sleep only from May to August**. (L1 §1.6)
- **The market's illegal hour** is cockcrow-to-six, and it exists all year. (L1 §1.2)

## 7. HOW OCCUPANCY STATE CHANGES ALL OF THE ABOVE

Site 10's declared occupancy states are *functioning · corrupt · faction-seized · abandoned →
site 3 · a front → site 8*. The lanes supply what each does to the roster:

| state | roster consequence | trace |
|---|---|---|
| **functioning** | as the tables above | — |
| **corrupt** | the fines still happen and are still paid — they are a licence fee; the beam's farmer shades the weight; the forestalling laws are enforced only against people too poor to forestall | L3 §3.5 |
| **faction-seized** | someone else's officers hold the weights and the court; the bell may belong to them; the mace is lowered at a line in the street | L1 §1.7, L3 §3.1–3.2 |
| **abandoned** | the punishment furniture stands empty; the well under the plaza is planked over and forgotten; the fair booths have become permanent tenements; the grid has gone back to fields | L3 §3.4, L1 §1.7, L7 §7.6 |
| **a front** | the second-hand market where stolen goods surface; the fripperers and upholders beside the pillory | L2 §2.5 |
