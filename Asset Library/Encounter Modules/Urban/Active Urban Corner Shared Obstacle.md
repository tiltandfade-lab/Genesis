## 1) 30-Second Run

### Table-Fast Read
There’s **movement somewhere nearby** in a **fish-and-brine-smelling sharp corner**, and the whole place feels like a working edge of town that’s gone slightly wrong. A **scorched brazier** and a **small oil flask with a swollen cork** suggest recent heat or hurried use, while signs of labor cluster around a **worktable** in the dim light. The corner itself limits sightlines, so whatever is moving stays just out of view. Three interactable details stand out immediately: a **cracked clay jar sealed with pitch**, a **Fountain of Clear Water** that offers rare healing respite, and a **Rotted Mooring Post** that can be kicked over to drop a pursuer. The live pressure here is social and procedural, not combat-first: **someone else is stuck at the exact same obstacle as the party**, creating a moment to cooperate, bargain, or compete to get through.

### What Is Happening
This is a **social bottleneck wrapped around a blocked route or access problem**. The party arrives at a constricted corner where somebody else is already delayed, and the immediate question is not “who attacks first?” but “who gets through, and how?” The environment suggests a rough dockside or labor district—brine smell, mooring timber, worktable, sealed containers—but with a specific obstacle ahead that both parties must deal with. The **fountain of clear water** creates an unusual pocket of value in an otherwise shabby space, which makes the location feel claimed, watched, or quietly important. The NPC at the obstacle can become a helper, rival, tollkeeper, or source of exposition depending on tone.
