> hold the original single-line seeds until their band batches are crafted. Band-first rolling
> owns play frequency; row layout is coverage only.

| 01 | Grounded | The NPC needs a body between them and the door until sunrise — they won't say who's coming, but they've already nailed the back window shut. | Whoever's coming knows the NPC sleeps alone after the taproom closes; the window is midnight to dawn. | At dawn the NPC is gone — door forced, no blood, no note — and half the street saw the party turn them down. |
| 02 | Grounded | A bounty notice with the NPC's face is nailed at the crossroads — the sum is a year's honest work, and the NPC just offered the party double to "lose" it. | Two freelancers rode in this morning asking the right questions; they drink fast and haven't unsaddled their horses. | The freelancers take the NPC alive on market-day, in front of everyone — and whoever posted the bounty now knows this town won't lift a finger. |
| 03 | Grounded | Someone has followed the NPC for three days — always two stalls back, always buying nothing. The NPC has started taking wrong turns to be sure. | The follower is mapping routine, and the routine runs out of variations tomorrow: the weekly ledger-walk to the counting-house, alone, at dusk. | The ledger-walk happens; so does the ambush — and the follower turns out to have been only the hired scout. |
| 04 | Grounded | Their home was opened last night with a key that shouldn't exist — nothing taken, but every drawer left a finger short of shut. | Whoever it was searched for something small and didn't find it; the NPC knows exactly what, and moved it a week ago on instinct. | The second visit comes while the NPC is home. The thing changes hands, and the NPC stops answering their door at all. |
| 05 | Grounded | A rival has paid the town crier to read something at noon — the NPC knows roughly what, and it's true. | Noon. The square fills for market regardless; the crier's been paid and won't be bought off for less than double. | It's read aloud. The apprentices quit by evening, and the rival offers to buy the shop "out of sympathy" before week's end. |
| 06 | Grounded | The NPC's bodyguard of nine years didn't come to work — room paid up, sword over the mantle, boots gone. | The NPC has stood exposed for two days and someone noticed: a stranger asked the stable boy when the NPC next travels. | The guard turns up in the next town (roll d6 — 1–3: his body; 4–6: him, flush with new coin) — and either way, whatever he was guarding against arrives on schedule. |
| 07 | Grounded | The NPC moves like cracked ribs and calls it a fall from a ladder. There is no ladder in their trade. | The attacker promised a second visit if the first got reported — and the watch sergeant keeps asking friendly questions. | The second visit happens anyway. This time the NPC pays what was demanded, and keeps paying — the ladder story becomes a monthly expense. |
| 08 | Grounded | The NPC set a trap inside their own household: three slightly different secrets, told to three trusted people, to see which surfaces. | One already surfaced — the NPC just can't accept which mouth it came from, and is stalling instead of acting. | The traitor notices the trap and moves the timeline up: the real betrayal lands this week, at full force. |
| 09 | Grounded | A collector with two hired knives is drinking across the street, watching the door; the NPC owes 40 gold by the midnight bell. | Midnight bell. After it rings: a broken hand, and the debt doubles. | The NPC pays from a strongbox that wasn't theirs to open — by tomorrow, someone they answer to knows. |
| 10 | Grounded | The NPC saw two respectable men load something person-shaped onto a grain barge at third bell of night — and one of them saw the NPC seeing. | Since then: a too-generous purchase order from a firm nobody's heard of, and a man idling outside the shop in the rain. Carrot first; stick ready. | The NPC takes the carrot and becomes owned. The barge runs monthly — and the NPC now helps schedule the third bell. |
| 11 | Grounded | The neighbor has been feeding the NPC's hound behind the fence for months — it's fat, glossy, and last week it followed the neighbor home and wouldn't come back. The NPC wants the dog's heart back, not just the dog. | The neighbor has started calling it by a new name in public, and it answers. The NPC has begun leaving scraps out for the neighbor's prize goats. | The street becomes an arms race of stolen affections — dogs, goats, then somebody's apprentice — until it ends the old way: a fence line, an axe, and the watch. |
| 12 | Grounded | A crate of "farm tools" the NPC was paid to warehouse was seized on the road — and the manifest bears the NPC's mark beside a name they can't afford to know. | The seizing officer is either honest (bad: an investigation) or crooked (worse: an auction) — and the NPC must learn which before answering any summons. | The NPC gets named. The name above theirs walks free; the NPC hangs on market-day as the entire conspiracy. |
| 13 | Grounded | The NPC's daughter came home with a carved wooden toy nobody gave her — the third gift this week, each left closer to the house. | The gifts are a countdown; the NPC recognizes the carving style and won't say from where. | A fourth gift appears inside the house. The NPC packs the family and abandons the shop overnight. |
| 14 | Grounded | The NPC's kiln was sabotaged with real skill — nothing broken outright, everything set to fail slowly under load. Their guild rival apprenticed in exactly this craft. | The commission that would clear the NPC's debts is due in six days, on equipment that will now fail at full load. | It fails on day six, in public. The commission passes to the rival at a rush premium, and the guild opens a negligence inquiry — against the NPC. |
| 15 | Grounded | The NPC is boarding windows in broad daylight and selling stock at a loss — they think tomorrow's grain announcement starts a riot, and their shop sits between granary and square. | The announcement comes at morning bell. The militia posting sheet shows no one assigned to their street. | Riot or no riot, the NPC's early panic is remembered — as prophecy or as the spark. One of those gets them arrested. |
| 16 | Grounded | The neighbor painted their fence a shrieking festival orange, visible from every window the NPC owns. Ward complaints went nowhere — the painter's brother sits on the ward committee. | The NPC has already bought lamp-black and a brush. They are, by their own count, about two ales from using them tonight. | Vandalism by midnight, retaliation by dawn — and within a month the ward committee is genuinely split over a fence while older debts get called in on both sides. |
| 17 | Grounded | The wagon the NPC's whole season rides on is nine days late. The pass reported clear, other traffic got through, no wreck was found. | The buyer holds the contract price only to week's end — and a rival has mysteriously just come into identical stock. | The buyer buys from the rival — the NPC's own goods, everyone quietly understands — and the carter's family starts asking the NPC where their father is. |
| 18 | Grounded | The saddler's wife and the ferryman have gotten careless. Half the market pretends not to know. The saddler truly doesn't — and he's the NPC's oldest friend. | The ferryman has noticed the NPC noticing: suddenly there are free crossings, small gifts, favors uninvited. The silence is being purchased, and accepting even one more makes it a sale. | The saddler learns last and learns publicly — and the NPC's long silence reads as betrayal. The friendship, and the partnership riding on it, dissolve in front of the whole market. |
| 19 | Grounded | The quiet loan that got the NPC through last winter is called in full, years early — legal, per a clause they never read, invoked the same week the lender took on a new silent partner. | Fourteen days to produce principal already spent — and the new partner has been buying up debts all down the street. | The NPC signs over the deed "temporarily." Two more shops follow within the month. Somebody is assembling the street. |
| 20 | Grounded | The NPC found the second ledger. Their partner of twelve years has skimmed enough to buy the building across the way — the one that just sold to "a foreign buyer." | The partner boards the morning coach for a "buying trip" in three days, carrying the season's takings. | The coach leaves with the takings, the partner — and, the NPC discovers, the deed to their own premises. |
| 21 | Grounded | The NPC needs a strongbox moved across town without the watch, the guild, or their own family knowing. | 22 | Grounded | An auditor is in the warehouse right now, and the NPC is stalling with wine — because bay nine holds goods the NPC agreed not to ask about, for a fee. | The auditor works alphabetically. | 23 | Grounded | The NPC's reputation stands on the winter they killed the grey wolf — the story is bought drinks, the good table, their children's standing. | 24 | Grounded | The NPC's signature — perfect, down to their favored ink — sits on a purchase agreement they never saw, for the old tannery by the marsh. | First payment on "their" agreement is due at month's end; disputing it means calling a notary a liar who swears he watched the signing. | The NPC pays rather than fight — until they learn what the tannery is used for. | 25 | Grounded | The NPC is converting everything to small portable valuables and drilling the family on a rally point outside town. | 26 | Grounded | The NPC is paid a town stipend to keep the flood-drain clear, and hasn't touched it since spring — it's silted to the grate. | 27 | Grounded | A local leader holds the NPC's letters — genuine, from a worse time — and the price of silence just changed from money to services. | The first service is small and due Sunday: leave the side gate unbarred. | 28 | Grounded | The NPC keeps copies: ten years of manifests that don't match the council's published tariffs. | 29 | Grounded | Two greater houses are fighting through the NPC's small trade: one buys everything at double, the other blocks their cartage. The NPC hasn't picked a side. | 30 | Grounded | A noble's steward left a sealed schedule of "requisitions" — goods and labor, uncompensated, framed as loyalty owed. None of it is legal. | 31 | Grounded | A rumor about the NPC arrived in town wearing details only two living people could know. | 32 | Grounded | An old scandal the NPC outlived is back in every mouth, told fresh — including the part everyone had mercifully forgotten, plus one part that never happened. | The NPC's daughter is being courted by a family that prizes reputation. | 33 | Grounded | The marriage tying the NPC's family to their supplier's family is failing loudly — last night's shouting carried three doors — and both trades are held together by that bed. | Next season is contracted on family rates that die with the marriage. | 34 | Grounded | The heir's horse came back saddled at dawn, blood on one stirrup; the NPC is saying "away on business" through their teeth. | Every hour of daylight the trail cools — and the NPC won't involve the watch because of where the heir was going. | A ransom note arrives tonight naming a price the NPC can only raise by selling out someone else. |
