# SITE 8 — LAYERED CONTROL (cross-host transform)

---

## 1. Authority, honest gates, ruled decisions, and unresolved proposals

### 1.1 What is ruled (and may not be re-opened here)

Adam's 2026-07-26 ruling, recorded in `DESIGN.md`, is binding and this spec implements it without
amendment:

> Site 8 is a cross-host `LayeredControl` transform, eligible from persistent simultaneous claims
> with a consequential control difference — **not from map size**. Its spatial expression ranges
> from shared-room recognition/schedule differences through localized and distributed control to
> open conflict. **Map growth requires semantic overflow plus prelicensed unobserved capacity or a
> real external provider; it may never invent a useful room or exit after observation.**

Three further ruled facts constrain this document:

- **Site 8 does not own a map grammar** (`GOLDEN-SITES-CATALOG.md`, "Portfolio roles"). It applies
  to a tavern, prison, mine, monastery, fortress, camp or other host when the predicate is met.
- **The transform must be proved on at least three unlike hosts with no host-specific branch**
  (ontology §11 item 2). A tavern alone does not earn the portfolio gate (§11 closing paragraph).
- **A transform never earns its own geometry generator, and a host never earns a second number for
  being in an unusual state** (`SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` §2.2, proposed language; §2.1's
  noun/state rule is the operating principle this spec follows).

## 2. The plain-English promise, and the classified invariants

### 2.1 The promise

**You are standing in a place where two different sets of rules are in force at once, and you can
tell.** Somebody here can do things you cannot. Somebody here is owed something. A door that is
merely a door for the people who work here is a decision for you. The place is still doing its job —
the drinks still get poured, the ore still comes up, the prisoners are still held — and the second
set of rules runs *through* that job rather than replacing it.

What playing here should feel like: **reading a room correctly is the primary skill.** Before
anything else, the player learns who actually decides, what the tells are, and what changes at what
hour. The promise is not a fight over a stronghold. It is the specific pleasure of *finding the
seam* and then choosing what to do with it — use it, buy into it, expose it, break it, or walk past
it having simply understood it.

### 2.2 What this promises that no other site does

Every other Golden Site promises a **place**. Site 8 promises a **relationship** that the place is
carrying. It is the only member of the portfolio whose subject is *permission*.

Concretely: it is the only case where the same square of floor has two correct answers about what
you may do on it, and the answer depends on who you are, what hour it is, and what you have been
told. Nothing else in the twelve produces that.

### 2.3 The functional capsule (congruent-brief §1)

> **A host that is still doing its job while a second persistent claimant takes a consequential cut
> of its authority, access, service, custody, property, information, ritual, labour, protection,
> taxation, time or violence — expressed at the lowest truthful rung, never by growing the map.**

### 2.4 Invariants, defaults and licensed cases

Classified per the catalog's recommendation classes.

**REQUIRED — the host program remains the thing under control.** At LC-0 through LC-4, a
layered-control tavern still gives hospitality, a mine remains causally related to extraction,
and a prison preserves custody and property obligations. LC-5 may temporarily impair or suspend
operation, but the conflict must still be over those named circuits and retain their recovery or
failure obligations. A host whose program has become irrelevant is a different composition, not
Layered Control. (Ontology §5.3.)

**REQUIRED — two persistent, simultaneous claims.** Both must be *persistent*: temporary
co-presence is not layered control. Two crews drinking in one room is not the transform; a
proprietor who runs the public room while a second party owns the back room and the deliveries is.
(DESIGN.md; ontology §5.1.)

**REQUIRED — a consequential difference in at least one control dimension.** "Consequential" means
the player can act differently because of it. A difference nobody can act on is flavour, and flavour
does not license a transform.

**REQUIRED — the lowest truthful rung.** The transform materialises at the lowest rung its canon
supports. Promotion follows canonical events and semantic overflow. Level size and map area are
never promotion inputs.

**REQUIRED — no invention after observation.** No helpful cellar, faction wing, evidence annex or
secret exit may appear once the player needs it. Expansion needs prelicensed unobserved capacity or
a real external provider, and the frontier must have been committed before it became useful.

**REQUIRED — deltas, not a replacement plan.** The transform contributes claimant profiles, claim
targets, access/schedule/service/custody/information deltas, occupation zones, tells, triggers and
outcomes. It never emits a substitute site plan.

**DEFAULT — the split is narrow.** One threshold, one fixture, one route, one hour. Breadth is the
exception and needs a canonical reason. (Evidence: lane 1 §2.4 — real parallel jurisdictions
routinely govern large ground from one small room.)

**DEFAULT — the second claimant is present, not hiding.** Reputation, recognition and service
differences are visible to anyone who reads the room; what is *secret* is usually the evidence, not
the fact.

**LICENSED — a criminal claimant.** Requires actual rolled facts. Five of the eleven real cases in
lane 1 have two *legal* claimants; a generator that always rolls a gang has misread its own law.

**LICENSED — open conflict (LC-5).** Requires canonical events that made it hot. Faction clocks,
`turnFactionOutcome` results and the opening tension are the legitimate triggers.

**LICENSED — map growth.** Only under the full overflow test (§10.3).

**VARIANT — the many-claimant shape.** N small equal claims around one shared centre (the
caravanserai case). Not yet placed on the ladder — see Q1.

**PROPOSED — everything in §1.3.**

### 2.5 Identity, culture and circumstance

Guidelines step 2 requires the three layers to be separated. For a transform they read as:

- **Transform identity** — the *relation*: who claims what, on which dimension, with what
  recognition, at what hours, over which of the host's semantic roles. This is what stays true in
  every valid version.
- **Cultural expression** — how a claim is *asserted and acknowledged*: the greeting or password,
  the mark, the seat, the order of service, the coin accepted, the oath, the courtesy that says "I
  know whose room this is." Culture owns the vocabulary of recognition, and it must change a
  construction, workflow, ownership or interaction fact — not just a palette.
- **Local circumstance** — the host program, the window size, the terrain and inherited boundaries,
  the current hour, the rolled faction agenda and method, the PC's own standing and renown, and what
  the player has already observed.

A rule shaped like "Culture X always gets a hidden door" is rejected. The contributing facts must be
named.

## 3. Scene ladder, implementation order, learning rationale, and the Golden Seed

### 3.1 The five-expression family (guidelines step 3)

Because Site 8 is a transform, its "family" is five expressions of **the same split across unlike
hosts and rungs**, not five buildings.

1. **Smallest believable — the recognised threshold.** One room, one door, one rule. A back door
   that some people may use and others may not; a sanctuary line the host's own enforcers will not
   cross. Zero added geometry; the whole transform is one prop and one permission.
2. **Ordinary — the fixture with two owners.** One counter, store, cabinet, tally board or scale
   that both claimants use for different purposes, with different books. The recommended first
   structural family.
3. **Large / advanced — the distributed claim.** Several zones and routes with different access
   profiles inside one coherent host: a works where the pay office, the store and the gate answer to
   a different party than the face and the haulage.
4. **Degraded / repurposed — the residue.** The conflict resolved; what survives is changed access,
   a scar, a schedule nobody explains, and a memory. (Image `lc7`: the Marshalsea's alley outlives
   the Marshalsea.)
5. **Unusual but supported — the shared centre.** N small equal claims ringing one court, water
   source and gate, none dominant (image `lc12`). Licensed by a rolled multi-party fact; not yet
   on the ladder (Q1).

### 3.2 The rung ladder (from the ruling) and its proposed weighting

| rung | spatial expression | proposed weight |
|---|---|---|
| `LC-0 latent` | canon and schedules only; no added active geometry | common |
| `LC-1 social` | same room and fixtures; recognition, permissions, service, testimony or schedule differs | common |
| `LC-2 localized` | one threshold, fixture, cabinet, table, workface, shrine or back room carries the split | common |
| `LC-3 distributed` | several zones or routes with different access/claim profiles; host still one coherent site | uncommon |
| `LC-4 site-wide` | control shapes circulation, services, staffing, signals and objectives across the window | rare |
| `LC-5 open conflict` | fronts active; barriers, patrols, hazards, objectives and tactical state expose it | rare |

**P7 (PROPOSED).** The weighting above biases hard toward LC-0/LC-1/LC-2. Rationale from lane 1
§2.4: real parallel jurisdictions — the Barmote court over 115 square miles, the piepowder court
over a whole fair — govern large ground from one small room or from no room at all. A tactical-map
project's natural instinct is to weight the visible rungs; that instinct is wrong here and would
produce a generator that turns every faction into a fortification.

Demotion is legal and symmetrical: a resolved conflict may leave changed access, scars, schedules
or memory without preserving a combat layout.

### 3.3 The Golden Seed

**A small hospitality venue at `LC-2`: the proprietor owns the public room and official service; a
second claimant owns the back room, the deliveries, and who receives private introductions.**

Chosen because it is the smallest composition that contains every load-bearing relationship: two
recognition profiles, shared fixtures with different permissions, one threshold carrying the split,
a schedule or signal that changes access, visible tells with hidden evidence, and **no invented
basement**. It is also already the required first Golden Venue fixture
(`TAVERN-VENUE-ROUTING-BRIEF.md` §5, §6), so the transform rides a proof the project owes anyway.

### 3.4 P1 (PROPOSED) — the three-host proof set and its build order

Build order: **hospitality venue → mine/workshop → prison/custody.**

| # | host | the split | learning rationale — what this teaches toward the next problem | ladder links |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Hospitality venue (`VENUE-TAVERN-01`) | public room vs. back room, deliveries, introductions | Proves LC-2 in the smallest honest window with **zero map growth**. Forces the delta compiler to work against a plan that has almost no spare space, which is the discipline every later host inherits. | promotes to #3's institutional case; degrades to LC-1 (same room, different service) |
| 2 | Mine / workshop (Site 5) | operator runs extraction; a second layer owns pay, supply, credit, and who may leave | Proves the **exchange** cluster on a host with real circuits. The control fixture is a *store*, not a room (images `lc8`, `lc9`) — so it tests whether one fixture with two owners can carry a site-wide claim without a second building. Site 5's six circuits already factorise, so it also tests delta-over-factorised-state. | promotes to LC-4 when the claim reaches the gate and the haulage; degrades to LC-1 (scrip accepted, nothing else changed) |
| 3 | Prison / custody (Site 6) | the state holds; the keeper sells — rents, a tap, chummage, monopoly supply, a master's side and a common side | The **anti-collapse test**: proves the host's obligations survive completely intact under a second claimant. Custody and property are the hardest obligations to keep honest, so if the compiler cannot break them here it will not break them anywhere. | promotes to LC-5 (a wing lost); degrades to LC-2 (the tap only) |

**Unlike on the three axes that matter:** window size (one room / a whole works / an institution),
claimant legitimacy (criminal / corporate-legal / officeholder-legal), and dominant control cluster
(passage / exchange / custody). If one delta compiler serves all three **with no `if host ===`
branch**, ontology acceptance item 2 is met.

**Fourth, as the changed-seed companion:** a monastery (Site 4) under exemption — the case where
*both* claimants are legitimate. It exists to stop the generator equating "second claimant" with
"criminal."

### 3.5 What remains unknown after the proof passes

Whether the transform composes with *another* transform on the same host (layered control over a
dormant host, or under flooding); whether the many-claimant shape needs its own rung; and whether
the grain profile (§4.5) survives changed seeds.

## 4. Required delta obligations, spatial zones, and provisional grid hypotheses

### 4.1 Why "zones" reads differently for a transform

A host program owns zones. A transform owns **obligations that must find a home inside the host's
existing zones**, and may only request a new one under the overflow test. So this section states
what must be *homed*, not what must be *built*.

### 4.2 The seven delta obligations

Every valid `LayeredControl` instance must home all seven. Sharing is preferred; several may land on
the same physical object.

1. **A recognition surface** — the place where "who are you" gets answered. A door, a counter, a
   gate, a stair head, a seat. May be a fixture the host already owns.
2. **A claimed target** — the specific semantic role the second claimant takes a cut of: a room, a
   fixture, a route, a service, a property store, a schedule slot, or a person.
3. **A permission delta with a stated exception** — at least one thing the second party may do here
   that the first may not, or vice versa, expressible as a sentence a player could hear.
4. **A tell** — something *visible* that a player who reads the room can find: a mark, a wear
   pattern, a seat left empty, a lamp with an owner, a sign that states a rule (image `lc9`).
5. **An information state** — either the claim is openly legible, or evidence sits behind a
   knowledge gate distinct from its visible tell. Hidden evidence is committed before observation
   and consumes `place-secret` where one is rolled; an overt legal or customary claim need not
   invent a secret.
6. **A schedule or signal that changes access** — an hour, a shift, a bell, a closed gate, a
   fair's duration. May be the only spatial expression at LC-1.
7. **One truthful egress under each regime** — the player must be able to leave, and the two
   claimants must not both have a veto on the same single door unless that is the point and it is
   stated.

**Rejection:** if any of the seven cannot be homed without contradiction, the transform demotes a
rung before it considers expansion (§10.3).

### 4.3 Provisional grid and standee hypotheses

**These are starting points for clay, not architecture.** Genesis's quantum is `h = 2.5 feet`; one
cell is 5 feet.

- **Recognition threshold:** begin at **1 cell / 5 feet clear** of doorway with **1 cell of standing
  room on each side**. Real construction: a doorway in an existing wall, its head at the host's
  existing lintel height; the standing room is *not* built, it is reserved out of the host's
  circulation. A recognition surface with no room to stand on both sides cannot stage a refusal.
- **Appended strip (the back room / store):** begin at **1 cell / 5 feet clear width**, **3–5 cells
  long**, **head height 2h–3h (5–7.5 feet)**. Real construction: this is a lean-to or a partition
  bay, not a second masonry room — you build it by running a light stud-or-plank partition parallel
  to an existing load-bearing wall at 5 feet, and roofing the gap either by extending the host's
  rafter tails or by a shed roof pitched off that wall. Nobody builds two masonry walls five feet
  apart to store goods, so the far side is always the light element and the near side is always the
  host's real wall. That asymmetry is the read.
- **Claimed fixture footprint:** begin as a **1-cell object with 1 cell of working clearance on its
  service side and at least 2 approach directions** for the public side. A counter both parties use
  must be reachable from both.
- **Sparse occupation zone (LC-3):** begin at **2×2 to 3×3 cells**, placed on **inherited boundaries**
  (§10.2), never fenced.
- **Overhead:** the transform never changes the host's overhead boundary. If a claim needs a
  different ceiling it needs a different room, and that needs the overflow test.
- **Standees:** the proof must show small, six-foot-human, presentation-large and largest-legal
  envelopes at the recognition threshold and along the strip. **A capacity limit is part of the
  strip's value** — a large creature does not get the back corridor for free, and the tactical
  overlay must say so.

No number becomes binding until standee fit, collision, fixed-camera legibility and actual play
choices agree.

### 4.4 The deck (congruent-brief §5)

The guard post's roof ruling generalises to "every site has an elevation-as-objective." For a
transform the honest answer is different and should be stated plainly:

> **Site 8 does not own a deck. It owns the question of *who owns the host's deck*.**

The transform's contribution is that the host's high ground — the gallery, the roof walk, the stair
head, the loft, the pay-office window — acquires an owner, a permission, and an hour. Where the
second claimant holds the deck, the player's read of the room changes before anything is said.

Real construction note: the galleried upper ring in image `lc12` is the canonical form — a first
floor of individually shuttered rooms opening onto a continuous balcony carried on timber joists
cantilevered or corbelled off the court wall, with a wooden balustrade. That is a real, buildable,
repeatable piece and it puts every claimant's door in sight of every other. It is proposed as the
transform's preferred inherited deck where the host licenses one.

### 4.5 P3 (PROPOSED DEFAULT) — the grain profile

> Where a claimant has actually altered a legally repartitionable part of the host, its
> **mass grain may shift** relative to the host's. Claimant formality biases the direction:
> informal occupation tends toward smaller irregular masses and opportunistic access;
> institutional occupation tends toward repeated bays, coarser masses and regular surfaces.

Evidence: FFT MAP031 → MAP032 (trade city → slum: two large regular masses become twenty small
irregular ones, dressed stone becomes dirt) versus MAP056 → MAP057 (monastery → underground archive:
irregular organic ground becomes a rigidly symmetric masonry hall with repeated shelf bays);
confirmed by MAP038 → MAP040. Lane 2 §3.1.

Consequence for the generator: an LC-3 candidate may re-partition an existing composition only
when construction, tenure and observation facts license it. Many LC-3 cases should remain purely
operational overlays. Grain is never evidence that a claimant exists and never permission to
rewrite host structure.

## 5. Distinct route and plan promises

### 5.1 The route table

The transform does not add routes; it **assigns owners, permissions and hours to routes the host
already has**, and may append at most one strip under §4.3.

| route | gives the player | asks the player to accept | capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the host's public route** | speed, legitimacy, witnesses who can vouch, normal service | you are seen, recognised, and priced; the second claimant knows you came | everything the host normally carries |
| **the claimed route or threshold** | access to the claimed target, the private introduction, the real price | recognition risk, a debt, a favour owed, or an exposure if you are read wrong | ordinary and small bodies by default; large bodies need an explicit capacity fact |
| **the schedule window** | the same ground with a different authority in force | waiting, and whatever changes while you wait | unchanged geometry; changed permission |
| **the appended strip (LC-2+ only)** | the property, the evidence, the back way | single file, no cover, one exit, and it is obviously someone's | ordinary and small only unless stated |

**Changing a seed may change which route is strongest. It may not erase the choice, and no route may
be best on speed, safety, access and information at once.**

### 5.2 The three-plan requirement

Every valid instance must support at least three viable, non-identical plans, **at least one
noncombat**, and at least one that resolves by *understanding* rather than by acquiring anything.

### 5.3 P4 (PROPOSED) — the four free devices

From lane 2 §2. All four are occupancy, lighting or partition facts over existing geometry; none
enlarges the window.

1. **Owned diagonal.** The host's public spine survives unchanged; ownership multiplies on both
   sides of it. The public route becomes the most exposed ground on the board without moving a cell.
2. **Owned approach.** The claimant is expressed by standing on the host's existing portal. Zero new
   geometry — an occupancy fact and a permission.
3. **Appended strip.** The back room is a single-file corridor with property in reach of both walls
   (construction in §4.3). It reads as "someone else's" because a host's public circulation is never
   one cell wide.
4. **Void plus owned light.** The covert route is legible because two lamps have an owner and
   nothing else does. Satisfies readability without inventing an unexplained lamp — the CAUSAL LIGHT
   LAW arriving from the board-grammar side.

## 6. Operating circuits and factorized mutable state

### 6.1 The five circuits

The transform tracks five connected systems. Each can change without repainting the site as
generically "controlled."

1. **Recognition** — who is known to whom, on whose word, with what proof, and what happens when the
   answer is wrong.
2. **Permission** — what each party may do, where, and when; including the host's own staff.
3. **Exchange** — what is paid, to whom, in what medium, on whose books; credit, debt, tab, scrip,
   cut, rent, tithe, toll.
4. **Information** — what is known, by whom, what is a tell and what is evidence, and what a
   knowledge gate opens.
5. **Custody** — who holds which property, person, record, key or route, and under whose obligation.

### 6.2 Factorized state — no `layeredControl = true`

Friendly labels ("the gang runs the back," "the company owns the pay") are *derived*. The underlying
facts are:

- **claimStrength:** latent / acknowledged / operative / dominant;
- **openness:** secret / signalled / open / declared;
- **legitimacy:** criminal / customary / chartered / official (both claimants carry one);
- **recognition:** stranger / known / vouched / member / marked / hunted;
- **scheduleState:** which authority is in force in this window;
- **frictionState:** accommodated / strained / breached / hot;
- **cause and reversal:** what set it there, and what could set it back.

A single boolean would make every consequence simultaneous and rob the player and the DM of levers.
This mirrors Site 5's rejection of `mineState = failing` and is the same rule.

### 6.3 What the engine already supplies (and must not be re-rolled)

Per lane 3, the following are **LIVE** and the transform consumes them rather than minting parallels:

- claimant identity, `dominant` flag, `agenda`, `method` (`rollFaction`, `SS.fAgenda`, `SS.fMethod`);
- the PC's tie (`rollFactionProximity`) and arrival asymmetry (`SS.eStanding`);
- per-faction recognition score, hunted threshold at `-6`, decay and epithets (`w.renown.factions`);
- transition outcomes — advance, setback, splinter, merge, takeover, collapse (`turnFactionOutcome`);
- the 65-value urban claimant roster (`urban-threat-identity-t1/t2`);
- `place-secret` for the hidden fact.

**`recognition` in §6.2 is not a new field.** It is the reading of `w.renown.factions[slug].score`
against the existing thresholds, plus the entry `standing`. Minting a second reputation number would
be the bug shape this project already names.

### 6.4 Strategic levers

- **Being read correctly or incorrectly** changes price, service, access and who is told you came.
- **Buying in** — accepting the debt, the scrip, the favour — opens the claimed route and closes the
  legitimate one.
- **Exposing the tell to the right third party** changes the claim's openness without a fight.
- **Waiting for the schedule** costs time and changes who is in force.
- **Taking the claimed fixture out of service** (the store, the counter, the cabinet, the scale)
  attacks the exchange circuit without attacking a person.
- **Owning the light** — extinguishing or lighting the claimant's lamps changes what is legible.
- **Standing where the refusal has to happen in public** converts a private permission into a social
  event.

### 6.5 Three demonstrable plans (Golden Seed, hospitality venue at LC-2)

- **Social / recognition (noncombat).** Get vouched. Use the host's public room, the proprietor's
  service, an existing tie (`rollFactionProximity`) or a rolled favour to be introduced across the
  threshold as a known quantity. Cost: you now owe someone, and the claimant knows your face. No die
  needs to be rolled in anger.
- **Access / timing (noncombat).** Use the schedule. Arrive when the deliveries do; the threshold is
  open because it has to be, and the recognition surface is busy. Cost: witnesses to the delivery,
  a hard time box, and the strip's single-file capacity limit.
- **Exposure (noncombat, changes the world).** Find the tell, get the evidence through the knowledge
  gate, and put it in front of the party who can act on it — the host's own authority, a rival
  claimant, or the room. Cost: the claim's openness changes permanently and the transform may
  promote or demote as a result; nothing you wanted is in your hands at the end.
- **Forceful (licensed).** Only if the fiction produced it. Uses the same threshold, the same strip
  and the same lights; it does not summon a new arena.

Reject or revise the instance if fewer than three of these are viable, if all three cost the same,
or if a changed seed erases the choice.

### 6.6 Founder questions

Presented per THE GENERATOR PRINCIPLE as proposed build orders, not A-or-B picks.

**Q1 — the many-claimant shape (the caravanserai).**
Plain English: the ruling's ladder measures how much of *one* split is materialised. Lane 1 found a
different shape — N small claims of equal weight around one shared court, water source and gate,
with nobody dominant (image `lc12`). FFT has no equivalent in 121 maps.
Alternatives: (a) a sixth rung on the existing ladder; (b) a separate `SharedCentre` composition that
several LC-2s mount onto; (c) not modelled in v1.
Recommendation and reason: **(b), built third** — it is a *composition* rule, not a degree of one
split, and folding it into the ladder would make the ladder mean two different things.
What it changes later: whether markets, khans, guildhalls, boarding houses and shared works get an
honest reading, or get flattened into "two sides."
Easy to revise? Yes — it is additive.
Proof needed to judge: a plan diagram of one court with four separately doored cells and a shared
water source, against the same court read as a two-claimant LC-3.

**Q2 — may the transform change route desirability without changing route truth? (potentially blocking)**
Plain English: FFT's Slums in Dorter puts the only public route on the *worst* ground on the board.
That is excellent gameplay, and it is a change to the host, not a delta on top of it.
Alternatives: (a) the transform may derive claimant- and PC-relative desirability from
access, risk, schedule and service deltas while preserving topology, capacity and host route
truth; (b) strictly no derived ranking; (c) derived ranking only at LC-3+.
Recommendation and reason: **(a), built into the first proof.** A public route may become slower,
more exposed or unavailable to one actor without ceasing to be the host's public route. The
transform records those costs; it may not mutate topology or replace a canonical ranking with one
global answer.
What it changes later: the delta compiler's contract, and whether Site 8 can make an existing site
meaningfully harder without touching its geometry.
Easy to revise? **No.** This is the one answer that shapes the compiler's boundary, which is why the
founder-ruled gate stays `PARTIAL` until it is answered.
Proof needed to judge: the same tavern composition, tactical overlay only, with and without route
re-ranking.

**Q3 — legitimacy weighting.**
Plain English: five of eleven real cases have two *legal* claimants, and the engine's own method
roll is 60% covert-or-parallel but includes "law, charters, and bureaucracy" and "faith and
persuasion." Should the claimant-legitimacy roll be flat, or lean criminal because it is more
immediately playable?
Alternatives: (a) flat across criminal / customary / chartered / official; (b) lean criminal;
(c) derive legitimacy from the claimant's committed authority, ownership, law, custom and
recognition facts, while `method` describes how the claim is pursued.
Recommendation and reason: **(c), built first.** Tactic is not standing: coercion can enforce a
legal office and persuasion can serve an outlaw. The world must either supply legitimacy facts or
record the claimant as disputed/unknown; the transform may not infer legitimacy from `method`
alone.
What it changes later: the tone of the whole transform, and whether the monastery-exemption case is
reachable at all.
Easy to revise? Yes — it is a mapping table.
Proof needed to judge: a 20-roll cohort crossing `method` with authority/ownership/recognition
facts, including deliberate mismatches, read side by side.

## 7. Strategic levers and the strategic-gameplay test

Restating the catalog's governing law against this site specifically. The instance passes only if:

- the player can **read** the split from the room before being told it;
- there are at least **two viable approaches with different advantages, risks and consequences**,
  and at least one is noncombat;
- the player can **change** the situation by using the site — a permission, a schedule, a fixture, a
  light, a witness;
- position, routes, timing, occupants, light and objectives create tradeoffs rather than decoration;
- the player can **recognise what changed** after a choice — a door that was closed is open, a price
  that was one thing is another, a face that did not know them now does;
- **approach, commitment, failure, retreat and recovery** are all possible; and
- the reading survives changed seeds.

A believable, procedurally valid layered-control instance that produces one obvious plan **fails**,
and the generator must reject or revise it.

## 9. Generator contract — inputs, semantic blueprint, ordered generation

### 9.1 Inputs the transform consumes (it rolls none of these itself)

- host program and operating model, with its zones, fixtures, routes, schedules and obligations;
- the committed `SemanticSitePlan` and the current `MaterializationWindow` with its licensed
  frontiers;
- claimants: name, `dominant`, `agenda`, `method`, clock state, and committed
  authority/ownership/recognition facts from which standing may be resolved;
- the PC's `standing`, `rollFactionProximity` tie, and `w.renown.factions` scores;
- the opening tension / current pressure;
- `place-secret` and any committed rumour, job, debt, property or promise;
- the walk family that brought play here, and the approach direction;
- current hour, shift and weather;
- what the player has already observed (the observation-hardness fact);
- encounter objective, threat band and creature envelopes.

### 9.2 The semantic blueprint the transform emits

A delta set, never a plan:

- claimant profiles with recognition rules;
- claim targets bound to **named semantic role ids in the host's plan**;
- access, schedule, service, custody and information deltas per role;
- sparse occupation zones and, at LC-5 only, fronts;
- tells (visible) and evidence (gated), each bound to a fixture or surface id;
- transition triggers and their legal outcomes, consuming `turnFactionOutcome`'s vocabulary;
- capacity facts for any appended strip or restricted threshold;
- three candidate plans with their tradeoffs;
- any newly required spatial obligation, each tagged with the overflow clause that licensed it.

### 9.3 Ordered generation pass

1. Test the eligibility predicate against canon. If it fails, emit nothing — this is not a fallback,
   it is the correct answer.
2. Resolve claimant standing from authority, ownership, law/custom and recognition facts; use
   `method` only for tactic. Resolve PC-relative recognition from `renown` + `standing`.
3. Choose the **lowest rung** whose obligations the current window can home.
4. Bind claim targets to existing semantic role ids. Prefer sharing; a shared physical realization
   does not collapse two semantic identities.
5. Home the seven delta obligations (§4.2). Run the capacity test.
6. Place tells on surfaces the fixed camera can actually see; where a hidden fact exists, place its
   evidence behind a gate.
7. Assign owners and hours to the host's existing routes and deck. Derive actor-relative route
   desirability if Q2 resolves to (a), without mutating topology, capacity or host truth.
8. Apply the grain profile (§4.5) only where committed construction and tenure facts license
   re-partitioning.
9. Reserve approach, retreat, quiet ground and interaction space; do not fill quiet ground to prove
   the claim exists.
10. Assign light owners; leave unowned ground unlit.
11. Validate: fixed camera, standee fit at the threshold and in the strip, collision, egress under
    both regimes, and the three-plan test.
12. Dress the retained ids. Never move gameplay geometry to improve a beauty shot.

### 9.4 Honest empty space

Ontology §4.3 applies with force here. A large host under layered control may keep broad quiet
ground; the claim concentrates into one or two truthful knots. Image `lc2` is the reference: a broad
quiet paved square with the single activity knot in the ground-floor colonnade on one side. **The
renderer may not scatter claimant props to prove the transform fired.**

### 9.5 P5 (PROPOSED PROFILE) — confined-envelope compression

> A claimant that is canonically confined, permitted to build, and under demonstrated capacity
> pressure may fill its licensed envelope before seeking overflow. It may subdivide, stack,
> compress floor heights and multiply doors only where support, access, construction and
> observation facts permit.

Evidence: the Venetian Ghetto's seven-to-eight-storey blocks in a city that does not build that way
(image `lc2`); Kowloon Walled City's hard footprint and flat roofline at the aviation cap (image
`lc11`). Real construction: stacking under a height cap forces reduced storey heights, internal
stairs replacing external ones, light wells instead of yards, and party walls carrying loads they
were not sized for — which is *why* such fabric reads as fine-grained and improvised from outside.

This is one countable compression profile, not a universal behavior of confined claims. A legal
enclave may retain gardens; a sparse occupation may never build; an institutional claimant may
prefer demolition and regularisation.

## 10. Rejection rules and the deterministic fallback ladder

### 10.1 Countable rejection checks

Reject the candidate before presentation when:

1. the predicate is not met — fewer than two persistent claims, or no consequential difference;
2. at LC-0–LC-4 the host's program is no longer performable, or at LC-5 its impairment/
   suspension lacks a committed cause, recovery/disposition path, and continuing host
   obligations;
3. any of the seven delta obligations (§4.2) has no home;
4. the permission delta cannot be stated as a sentence a player could hear;
5. a hidden claim has a tell but no committed evidence, or evidence but no observable route to
   learning it;
6. the hidden fact was committed **after** the player observed the space;
7. a room, exit, cellar or wing was added without a satisfied overflow clause;
8. all viable plans cost the same, or one plan is best on speed, safety, access and information;
9. no noncombat plan exists and the premise permits one;
10. the only egress is controlled by both claimants without that being the stated point;
11. a claimant zone is fenced by geometry neither claimant built (§10.2);
12. claimant props are scattered across quiet ground to prove the transform fired;
13. a tell is placed where the fixed camera cannot see it;
14. a light has no owner, fuel or shift;
15. the rung is higher than the canon supports;
16. a large creature blocks a required route with no explicit capacity fact;
17. the transform emitted a replacement site plan rather than deltas;
18. a changed seed preserves the picture by destroying the reading.

### 10.2 P6 (PROPOSED DEFAULT) — inherited-boundary bias

> Prefer an **inherited** boundary — slope, water line, tree line, road, schedule, legal line or a
> wall the host already built — when it truthfully carries the split. A claimant-built fence,
> checkpoint, partition or barrier is legal when a committed construction or enforcement fact
> caused it.

Evidence: image `lc10` (Rocinha/São Conrado: the boundary is where the slope starts); FFT MAP091
(water and one pier); the Alsatia liberty, whose boundary was a *legal* line the sheriffs fought at
rather than a wall.

### 10.3 The overflow test (the only route to map growth)

All five conditions from ontology §4.2 must hold, and the receipt must record which clause licensed
each new area:

1. required semantic obligations cannot fit without contradiction or unusable circulation;
2. the canonical site has licensed unmaterialized capacity **or** an established external provider;
3. the new area contradicts no observed extent, adjacency, portal or promise;
4. the frontier or external route was **committed before it became useful**;
5. the expansion receives stable ids, lineage and a replayable receipt.

FFT's own precedent supports clause 4 precisely: the Cellar of Sand Mouse is a *precommitted
adjacent ruin*, visibly old and obviously not part of the pub (lane 2 §3.2).

### 10.4 Deterministic fallback ladder

If the transform cannot be honestly materialised, try in this order and **record the relaxation**:

1. share a fixture between claimants rather than duplicating it;
2. move the split to a schedule instead of a space;
3. demote one rung (LC-3 → LC-2 → LC-1 → LC-0);
4. externalise through a real provider named in canon;
5. reduce the claim's breadth — one target instead of several — keeping the difference consequential;
6. emit `LC-0 latent`: the canon stays true, nothing is materialised, and the DM still has it;
7. reject the transform for this materialization and record why.

**The generator may never** invent a room, exit or provider; delete the host's obligations to make
room; collapse two semantic identities because their realization is shared; or promote a rung to
make a scene more exciting.

## 11. Claimant, legitimacy, and recognition cards

Culture must change a construction, workflow, ownership or interaction fact — palette and props
cannot carry it alone. The first pair of cards should differ on at least four of these:

- **how a claim is asserted** — a mark, a seat, an oath, a coin, a badge, a courtesy, a silence;
- **how it is acknowledged** — who greets whom first, who is served first, who is not asked to pay;
- **what medium the exchange uses** — coin, scrip, credit, tab, favour, tithe, share, labour;
- **where the second claimant sits or stands** — the deck, the door, the fire, the back, the stair;
- **what the tell is** — wear, a mark, a sign stating a rule, an empty seat, a lamp, a ledger;
- **what the evidence is, and who may see it** — a book, a token, a key, a body, a name;
- **what hour belongs to whom**;
- **what happens to someone read wrong** — priced, refused, followed, warned, marked, taken;
- **how a claim is transferred or lost** — inheritance, purchase, oath, defeat, collapse;
- **what the host's own staff are permitted to notice.**

**Coercion is not an automatic flavour of layered control.** It requires explicit history,
institutions and encounter consequences — the same rule Site 5 applies to forced labour.

**Declared gap:** Site 8 has **no culture study**. These cards are a structure awaiting evidence, and
that is the principal reason `RESEARCHED` is proposed at `PARTIAL`.

## 12. Occupancy, hooks, lighting, world context, and site boundaries

### 12.1 Occupancy states

The same valid instance may currently be: accommodated (both parties have settled into a working
arrangement) · newly imposed (one claim is recent and the host has not adapted) · strained (an
agreement is failing) · concealed (one claim denies itself) · declared (both claims are public) ·
in transition (a clock just fired — advance, splinter, merge, takeover, collapse) · residual (the
conflict is over; the access changes remain).

### 12.2 Arrival hooks

Each must touch a real circuit **and** at least one map lever: a debt called in · a delivery that
must not be seen · a person who may not be served · a room that is closed on the wrong day · a
ledger with two sets of numbers · a body found on the wrong side of a threshold · a licence,
charter or writ that one party will not recognise · an inspector expected · a price that changed ·
a favour owed to the wrong party · a rival claimant's approach · a key that used to work · someone
asking the player to *carry* a recognition, not an object.

### 12.3 Lighting and cutaway

- The transform never adds an unmotivated light. It may **assign an owner** to a light the host has.
- Owned light is the primary covert-route device (§5.3 device 4); unowned ground stays unlit and is
  read through exposure, restrained ambient bounce, AO, contact and honest cast shadow.
- A claimant may extinguish, shutter, or add a light — each is an interaction with a cost and a tell.
- Cutaway omits the camera-facing overhead exactly as the host defines it. **The transform never
  changes the host's overhead boundary**, and a cutaway may not be used to reveal a claimed space
  the player has not reached.

### 12.4 Rolled world-context projection

Context must explain the *host* and the claim's reach: the district or region the claimant also
holds, the route the deliveries come by, the other frontage that answers to the same party, the
authority whose absence makes the claim possible. Only direct visual nouns, physical properties and
visible processes may project automatically, and visible processes still require current local
state. **A painted second banner, checkpoint or crowd cannot replace a canonical claimant, route or
provider**, and context must never add traversable cells or false affordances.

Context-off and context-on captures must retain identical roles, permissions, thresholds, tells,
ids, knowledge state and mechanics.

### 12.5 Boundaries with other sites

- **Site 3 — Dormant/Abandoned** owns the *absence* of control. Site 8 owns *simultaneous* control.
  A place with one absent claimant and one present one is Site 8 over a Site 3 residue.
- **Site 9 — Contested Fortress** is a scale/relationship stress case whose host is a large defence
  with two populations and active fronts. Site 8 at LC-5 overlaps it deliberately; the difference is
  that Site 8 is host-agnostic and Site 9 is a specific host at a specific scale.
- **Site 6 — Prison/Custody** owns custody as a program. Site 8 rides it (proof host #3) without
  becoming a prison-break scenario.
- **Site 5 — Mine/Workshop** owns the production chain. Site 8 rides it (proof host #2) and must not
  reach for the mine's own strain vocabulary.
- **Ordinary venue programs** (tavern, shop, shrine, home, clinic) are hosts, never Site 8's content.
  `VENUE-TAVERN-01` is shared evidence; passing it does not make Site 8 `CLAY-PROVED`.

### 12.6 P9 (PROPOSED) — the Underworks correction

The walk census maps **Underworks — 27 of 258 urban arrivals, 10.47%** — to Site 8, with the honest
note that "sewers read as the concealed-layer condition, not a standalone noun." Under the
2026-07-26 ruling **a transform never owns a district type**, so this mapping should be retired:
Underworks is a *host that layered control is unusually likely to be applied to*, not a Site 8 noun.

Read beside the census's unmapped **Infrastructure Hub — 35 of 253 dungeon arrivals, 13.83%** —
described as "a city-utility space, not extraction/craft" — the two are plausibly **one unnamed host
program (urban utility substrate) split across two walk tables**. Combined that is a larger demand
signal than several numbered hosts, and it is a bin-(a) candidate the settled-life program has not
named. Raised here as a proposal for the settled-life lane; **this spec edits neither the census nor
the catalog.**

## 13. Structure, mechanism, material, decal and prop demand

### 13.1 Structural coverage banked (congruent-brief §6 / BATTLEMAP-TOWNTRAY-COMPOSITION §10.3)

Site 8 banks **almost no new structural dimensions**, which is the point. It exercises:

- **thresholds and portals** — doorway, wicket, gate, hatch, stair head, counter opening, service
  hatch — at a higher rate and with more states than any host site;
- **partition** — light stud/plank partitions inside an existing envelope (the appended strip);
- **galleried upper ring** — a first-floor balcony on cantilevered or corbelled joists with a
  balustrade, serving the shared-centre and deck cases;
- **sparse occupation over inherited boundaries** — no new geometry, placement rules only.

### 13.2 Inherit / invent split (congruent-brief §7)

**Inherited (shared kit, no new purchase):** every wall, floor, roof, stair, ramp, door frame,
window, counter, cabinet, table, bench, hearth, lamp socket, yard and route in the host's kit;
terrain slabs and inherited boundaries; the host's cutaway and camera.

**Site-owned (new, and deliberately tiny):**
- a **threshold state set** — open / shut / barred / watched / wicket-only / by-recognition-only, as
  a state on an existing door rather than a new door;
- a **partition run** with door and hatch variants (the appended strip's light side);
- a **claim-marker socket** — a mount point on any surface where a tell can legally sit;
- a **two-owner fixture wrapper** — a state layer over an existing counter, store, cabinet, scale,
  tally board or ledger stand that gives it a second book and a second permission;
- a **lamp-ownership state** on existing light sockets.

That is five pieces, and four of them are states rather than meshes. **If the transform needs more
than this, it has stopped being a transform.**

### 13.3 Mechanism and interaction ownership

Lock/bar/latch/wicket states · a signal device (bell, shutter, lamp, chalk mark, knotted cord) ·
a two-book ledger interaction · a tally or scrip exchange · a key/token custody chain · a
watch-post occupancy anchor.

The Proof subset is: threshold states, one signal device, one two-owner fixture, one claim marker,
and lamp ownership.

### 13.4 Material, decal and prop demand (congruent-brief §8)

- **Material:** nothing new. The transform re-uses the host's materials and changes their
  *distribution*. Where a partition is added it takes the host's cheapest interior material.
- **Decal / paint — the transform's real demand, and it is almost all here:** claim marks and
  counter-marks · rules painted on walls (image `lc9`) · tallies scored and scored through ·
  chalk marks · wear patterns at a threshold used by one party more than another · a boundary line
  painted, cut or inlaid · seal wax and stamped tokens · a carved emblem panel naming a jurisdiction
  (image `lc5`) · soot or hand-grime concentrated on one door.
- **Props:** ledgers and tally sticks · scrip tokens, chits and tabs · keys and key boards ·
  seals and wax · a lamp with a named owner · a locked or double-locked cabinet · a delivery crate
  with two marks · a seat nobody else takes.

Anything that changes collision, cover, route capacity, sight, support, light position or
interaction reach is geometry or a mechanism — not a decorative prop.

### 13.5 Stretch analysis (congruent-brief §10)

The transform's reach is unusually good precisely because it owns so little:

- **Across hosts:** every retained host proof plus ordinary venue programs can take it. The
  proof set deliberately spans three unlike hosts.
- **Across walks:** urban (a district or frontage), wilderness (a route service or camp under a
  second claim), dungeon (an occupied level with a resident authority). The same delta set serves
  all three because it never touches traversal.
- **Across scales:** one room to a whole district, with the *same* five owned pieces.
- **Pure reskin value:** the five site-owned pieces are host-agnostic by construction. A threshold
  state set and a two-owner fixture wrapper built for a tavern serve a mine's pay office and a
  prison's property store with no change.
- **Cross-site rhymes:** Site 1's threshold and observation vocabulary; Site 6's custody and property
  chains; Site 5's tally and shift points; Site 4's court and hierarchy; Site 2's guest-family
  hosting relationship (a camp inside a host is a *property* relationship that layered control can
  transform without new geometry).
- **What a reskin buys:** the decal bin is the transform's whole visual identity, and decals are the
  cheapest thing in the project to author per culture. One decal set per culture reskins the entire
  transform.

## 16. Architecture integration ruling — 2026-07-30

Layered control inherits the host's battle-space mode, institutional/civilization
scale, body profiles, support graph, and committed geometry. It may change recognition,
permission, attribution, schedule, service, property, information, barriers, and route
desirability. It may not manufacture a new room, exterior, ruin, cutaway, or hybrid to
make the conflict legible.

Any physical control delta must use an admitted placement slot and rerun the host's
collision, support, stair, headroom, and camera gates. Failure demotes the physical
expression; it does not expand the materialization window.
