#hazard-severity
> **What an improvised danger costs — an anti-drift damage reference.** Today the DM eyeballs the damage for a fall, a fire, a crushing trap, a lungful of poison gas — and the same drop deals 2d6 in one session and 5d6 in the next. This pins improvised environmental harm to a **severity band** (how bad the situation is) crossed with **Tier** (how tough the party is), so the same kind of danger costs the same kind of damage every time. This is a **reference, not a roll table** (no dice rolled off the first column — it is named, so the compiler skips it; the DM rolls the listed expression for the *result*). Numbers are anchored to SRD norms (fall ≈ 1d6 per 10 ft; the standard 20d6 fall cap) but the bands and the Genesis-native tuning are original (authored 2026-06-28). Tier bands follow `[[DIFFICULTY]]` / `docs/TIER-SCOPE.md`: **T1 = levels 1–4, T2 = levels 5–10** (this version caps at T2 — no T3/T4). Pairs with the difficulty rungs in `[[DC Ladder#^dc-ladder]]`: the DC is the *check to avoid*, this is the *cost of failing*.

**The three severity bands.**
- **Setback** — a hard knock, not a real threat to a healthy character: a short fall, a glancing flame, a thin wash of bad air. Stings, slows, warns.
- **Dangerous** — could genuinely hurt or drop a careless or already-wounded PC: a long fall, a wall of fire, a closing crusher, a gas-filled room.
- **Deadly** — can kill outright, and is owed a loud, honest tell first (DM Charter `§5` — lethal danger is **always telegraphed**): a killing drop, an inferno, a press that crushes, water that drowns. Never spring a Deadly hazard with no warning.

**How to use it.** Find the band, read the Tier column for *this* party, roll the listed expression. A **successful save or check usually halves** the damage (or avoids it entirely if the fiction allows a clean dodge) — fail-forward per the Charter. Damage **type follows the source** (fall = bludgeoning, fire = fire, gas = poison, etc.). Treat the numbers as the *center of gravity*, not a cage: nudge for scale (a fall is 1d6 per 10 ft up to the noted caps; a bigger fire hits harder), but stay on the same rung the next time the same thing happens.

| Severity | T1 dmg (lvl 1–4) | T2 dmg (lvl 5–10) | Typical save | Looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Setback | 1d6–2d6 | 2d6–3d6 | DC 10–12 | A 10–20 ft fall · brushing through flame · a single shallow breath of fouled air · a slamming door or a thrown stone · scalding steam from a burst pipe. |
| Dangerous | 3d6–4d6 | 5d6–6d6 | DC 13–15 | A 30–40 ft fall · a sheet of fire or a flaring brazier-trap · a room slowly filling with poison gas · the first grind of a crushing wall · a half-minute held under churning water. |
| Deadly | 5d6–8d6 (telegraphed) | 8d6–12d6 (telegraphed) | DC 16–18 | A killing drop (50+ ft, toward the 20d6 / ~70 ft cap) · an inferno or a wall of flame · a press that crushes between closing stone · drowning in a flooding chamber · a thick cloud of killing vapor. |
