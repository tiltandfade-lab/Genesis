# SITE 1 — GUARD POST — WORKING SPEC

## Authority and honest status

This document consolidates the Guard Post rules currently spread across the Golden Site
and Structure Kit catalogs into the Mine-standard generator and proof contract. It is a
consumer and index of those rulings, not a replacement authority.

The Guard Post has passed research, founder, and brief-congruence gates. It has not
passed the Clayroom Reset gate or produced a retained clay fixture.

Still open at card/proof time:

- exact recent slope-pressure/drainage repair expression;
- overgrowth intensity;
- lighting/weather/time selection;
- final value/palette script; and
- exact Upland defensive-edge construction, while preserving ruled cover equality.

Exact dimensions, grades, thicknesses, budgets, scoring, camera framing, and triangle
bands remain clay-calibrated.

## Plain-English promise

A Guard Post makes a journey pass through somebody else's decision.

The player should understand where the road narrows, who watches it, how the barrier and
guardroom control passage, which high position gives the defenders their advantage, and
what alternatives exist besides simply attacking the front.

It is small, operating, and externally supplied. Maintained age and overgrowth show
history without turning it into a ruin.

## What makes it a site family

Site 1 owns an **operated threshold**:

`approach → narrowing → controlled crossing → world continuation`

Observation, barrier control, shelter, readiness, supply, and an elevated position make
that threshold enforceable. Terrain and construction may change radically, but the
player must still be able to read who can see the approach, who can stop passage, how
the post sustains a watch, and what it costs to bypass or take control.

A watch hut beside an irrelevant path is not a Guard Post. A fortress that owns a whole
district belongs to Site 9. A dead checkpoint whose operating relationship has vanished
belongs to Site 3.

## Required invariants

- **REQUIRED — genuine pass-through road.** Distinct entry and exit continuations remain
  connected and traversable.
- **REQUIRED — architecture controls a transition.** The post, barrier, observation,
  and terrain relationship create a real threshold—not a building beside empty flat
  ground.
- **REQUIRED — shoulder relation.** `GP-SHAPE-01` embeds or abuts the uphill shoulder
  above a road narrowing.
- **REQUIRED — social front door.** Relationship and occupancy support battle, greeting,
  trade, tribute, papers, warning, or another ruled checkpoint interaction.
- **REQUIRED — observation is real.** A shuttered observation opening and deck have
  legal sight, access, cover, and camera visibility.
- **REQUIRED — defensive-edge equality.** Institutional and Upland variants grant the
  same cover class at the flat deck edge even when construction differs.
- **REQUIRED — approach and counterplay.** Front, flank/timing, and deck-control plans
  carry distinct tradeoffs.
- **REQUIRED — maintained operation.** Overgrowth, repair, stores, light, and occupants
  do not block working clearances.
- **DEFAULT — operating supply state.** Strained/dormant production is not Site 1's
  first proof.
- **LICENSED — occupancy and aftermath.** Military, bandit, trader, dungeon guard,
  haunted/empty aftermath, and later host expressions preserve the functional topology.

## Identity, culture, and circumstance

### Site identity

The identity is the live relationship among route, threshold, watcher, barrier, shelter,
and continuation. The player can change the site by gaining permission, changing the
watch or signal, opening or defeating the barrier, taking the observation position, or
accepting the cost of a secondary approach.

### Cultural expression

Culture affects how authority is displayed, how the deck edge provides cover, how rock,
earth, timber, masonry, and vegetation meet, how strangers are hailed, how records and
signals work, and what “maintained” looks like. The Institutional and Upland profiles
must preserve the same tactical promises while producing visibly different assemblies
and habits—not the same post with different paint.

### Local circumstance

Route class, slope, available shoulder, climate, local materials, traffic, supply,
faction, threat, age, repair history, weather, current occupants, and current alert
state determine the actual posture.

## The site's deck

The Guard Post's deck is its **reachable observation and control position** above the
crossing. It should improve sight, signaling, cover, and local command without becoming
an unbeatable roof.

Its construction may be a flat parapet, earth-and-rock breastwork, timber fighting
platform, cavern shelf, bridgehead landing, or another licensed cultural/environmental
solution. Its access, landing capacity, defensive edge, counterplay, and fixed-camera
visibility remain mechanically honest in every expression.

## Five-expression family

1. **Small — single-room gate watch.** The open-top guardroom component controls a
   narrow lane, alley, dungeon throat, or light road barrier. The room alone is a
   component test; it becomes a legal site only when the pass-through threshold exists.
2. **Ordinary — shoulder overlook post.** `GP-SHAPE-01`: one guardroom, one road
   narrowing, one barrier, and one flat lookout deck—the recommended first complete
   site.
3. **Large — tower checkpoint or bridgehead.** Stronger vertical circulation, longer
   sight, more readiness space, and a broader controlled crossing extend the same
   grammar without becoming a fortress.
4. **Degraded or repurposed — improvised operating checkpoint.** Damage, bandit use,
   trader control, haunted watch, or hasty repair changes access and presentation while
   someone or something still controls the threshold. If control and upkeep are wholly
   gone, the scene crosses into Site 3.
5. **Unusual — realm- or host-owned threshold.** A cavern mouth, giant causeway,
   woodland pass, magical customs line, or dungeon entrance may host the family when
   route, barrier, observation, shelter, and continuation remain real.

## Growth ladder and implementation order

### Scene ladder

1. **Rung A — open-top guardroom component:** rectangular room, controlled door,
   shuttered observation opening, workstation/storage, circulation.
2. **Rung B — wall/gate checkpoint:** exterior threshold and barrier controlling a
   committed road.
3. **Rung C — roadside shoulder post:** complete weather shell, hill/road/support
   relationship, observation deck—`GP-SHAPE-01`, the Golden Seed.
4. **Rung D — small tower stage:** vertical circulation, stronger sightlines, tower
   deck, cutaway and camera stress.

### Implementation order

**A → B → C → D.**

The component first proves openings, room, collision, light, and furnishing boundaries.
The checkpoint adds the controlled route. The shoulder post then proves the complete
Golden Site composition. Tower work waits until the lower structure, access, and
cutaway are stable.

Culture construction is phased after neutral structural proof:

**neutral clay relation → structure → materials/cutaway/gameplay → paired Institutional
and Upland construction → Ideal spatial mutation.**

## Recommended first build

The implementation lane still begins with Rungs A and B because they cheaply isolate
room and threshold failures. The first **complete retained site** should be Rung C:
`GP-SHAPE-01`. It is the smallest composition that proves the road, shoulder, barrier,
watch, deck, counterplay, culture pair, cutaway, and operating history together.

### Golden Seed — `GP-SHAPE-01`

`GP-SHAPE-01 — Shoulder Overlook Through-Road`:

- an old operating post occupies the uphill shoulder;
- the road enters, narrows where retaining masonry meets rock cut, and exits;
- barrier/gate controls the pass-through threshold;
- working apron joins road to guardroom;
- shuttered observation opening watches the approach;
- flat deck and defensive edge create the elevation objective;
- workstation, storage, supply, readiness, and circulation remain legal;
- a licensed secondary flank or timing/social alternative counters the front;
- maintained overgrowth and repair reveal chronology without blocking use; and
- retreat and world continuation remain honest.

## Working spatial specification

### Required zones

1. **Entry continuation:** road visibly arrives from the larger world.
2. **Approach/quiet ground:** enough room to read the checkpoint and choose a plan.
3. **Road corridor and narrowing:** geometric route region with edge, grade, and
   retaining/rock relation.
4. **Controlled threshold:** barrier/gate and its operating/interaction apron.
5. **Guardroom:** occupied room beside the threshold.
6. **Work/readiness zone:** workstation, records/tally where licensed, storage, arms/
   supply, and clear circulation.
7. **Observation face:** deep shuttered opening with real sight relation.
8. **Flat deck:** reachable standable high ground.
9. **Defensive edge:** culture-expressive construction granting equal cover class.
10. **Secondary flank/timing relation:** terrain footpath, blind side, schedule,
    negotiation, signal, or another licensed counterplay.
11. **Exit continuation and retreat:** road proceeds and encounter withdrawal remains
    possible.

### Provisional grid hypotheses

- Horizontal movement uses the 5-foot grid; vertical change uses `h = 2.5 feet`;
  storey and deck relations begin from the Structure Kit's shared 4h / 10-foot rule.
- Road width, grade, apron, barrier clearance, shoulder depth, retaining height, room
  depth, stair width, deck size, wall thickness, and observation reveal remain clay
  questions.
- The road and gate reserve presentation-large and expected carrier capacity before
  wall, vegetation, barrier, and furnishing placement.
- The guardroom reserves a six-foot witness, doorway sweep, workstation interaction,
  storage proxy, and circulation without invisible tactical furniture.
- The deck reserves natural standee bases and equal cover test positions for both
  cultures.
- A secondary flank declares body, slope, access, visibility, and cargo limits.
- Fixed camera must show both road continuations, threshold, observation relation, and
  deck objective without orbit.

### Route promises

| approach | gives the player | asks the player to accept | capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| controlled road | fastest and broadest passage; lawful/social resolution | observed, stopped, barrier/authority, defender deck | party and route traffic |
| terrain flank | bypass, observation, surprise, alternate retreat | slope/climb/time/exposure, limited bodies/cargo | terrain-card dependent |
| timing/signal | changed guard, barrier, witnesses, light, or alert | time/world cost and uncertain faction response | non-spatial but map-visible |
| deck assault/control | strongest local position, observation, signal/cover | access commitment, exposure, defenders' advantage | landing/access dependent |

At least three plans—including the checkpoint interaction—must be viable. The flank
cannot be a free road duplicate, and the front cannot simultaneously be fastest, safest,
quietest, and best-informed.

### Tactical furnishing

- barrier, shutter, door, deck edge, stairs/ramp, rocks, and retaining wall carry real
  collision, cover, sight, and access;
- workstation, container, supply, signal, drain, and repair receive minimal physical
  proxies only when their mechanics need them;
- threshold, barrier mechanism, records/toll, signal, deck, garrison, and exit are
  candidate objectives;
- opening/closing the barrier, shutter, or route; taking the deck; changing signal;
  exploiting repair/drainage; or altering alert produces local consequences;
- quiet ground and road apron remain free of beauty clutter; and
- foreground cutaway never hides the key standees or threshold.

## Seven operating circuits

1. **Road and traffic:** entry, narrowing, controlled crossing, exit, backlog.
2. **Barrier and permission:** open/closed, who may pass, papers/toll/tribute, breach.
3. **Observation and signal:** approach sight, shutter, deck, warning, reinforcement.
4. **Garrison and routine:** roster, watch, rest, interaction posture, change of guard.
5. **Supply and readiness:** externally supplied stores, records, equipment, maintenance.
6. **Structure, repair, and drainage:** shoulder pressure, retaining, water, recent
   repair, maintained overgrowth, damage reservation.
7. **Light and visibility:** daylight hero, owned night practical, shutter/opening,
   exterior darkness and alert.

Friendly states derive from:

- **traffic:** clear / waiting / blocked / diverted;
- **barrier:** open / controlled / closed / breached;
- **watch:** routine / suspicious / alert / disabled;
- **occupancy:** staffed / reduced / changed / empty-after;
- **condition:** maintained / repaired / strained / damaged;
- **permission:** welcome / inspect / trade / tribute / deny / hostile;
- **cause and recovery:** actor, event, faction response, and available world action.

Do not implement `guardPostState = enemy`. Faction relationship, occupancy, watch,
barrier, and aftermath are independent.

## Strategic gameplay contract

The Golden Seed must demonstrate:

- **Checkpoint plan:** resolve passage through faction relationship, papers, trade,
  tribute, deception, intimidation, warning, or another scripted social interaction.
- **Flank plan:** spend movement, slope risk, exposure, or body/cargo capacity to
  observe or bypass the controlled road.
- **Deck/control plan:** contest the defenders' advantage, signal, barrier, or
  observation position.
- **Timing/condition plan:** use guard change, traffic, night, repair/drainage, or alert
  state to alter witnesses and access.

At least three must be viable and one noncombat. The same site levers must matter in
combat, social play, stealth, investigation, and aftermath.

### Generation order

1. Commit route, terrain, culture, occupancy, relationship, hook, objective, and
   witnesses.
2. Resolve composed envelope and meaningful entry/exit edges.
3. Generate road-corridor candidates with narrowing, grade, apron, and quiet ground.
4. Generate broad terrain/shoulder candidates relative to the road.
5. Score post anchors that genuinely control the transition.
6. Place guardroom, barrier, observation, flat deck, and access.
7. Derive retaining, foundation transition, stairs/ramps, rock clusters, drainage,
   repair, and wall endpoints.
8. Reserve routes, deployment, objectives, cover, interaction, flank, and retreat.
9. Assemble surface frames/material roles and apply chronology/overgrowth.
10. Validate mechanics, culture envelope, standees, collision, camera, light, and
    changed/adversarial seeds.
11. Select, receipt, version, commit, persist, and project cutaway/dressing.

### Required rejection checks

Reject when:

- road entry/exit is false or disconnected;
- the post occupies flat leftover space rather than a transition/observation relation;
- road is paint without corridor, edge, grade, apron, or support truth;
- the threshold does not control passage or blocks the required route capacity;
- guardroom, workstation, storage, or circulation relies on invisible blockers;
- observation has no legal sight or is hidden by fixed camera;
- deck is unreachable, unsupported, too small for its witness, or lacks counterplay;
- culture variants change route graph, tactical reservation, or cover class in MVP;
- culture reads only through palette, flag, sign, or unique prop;
- overgrowth blocks operation or repair lacks chronology/surface truth;
- flank is absent without another meaningful counterplan;
- practical night light has no owner/task/fuel/power;
- a furnishing changes mechanics without geometry; or
- the retained hero requires a seed-specific patch.

### Deterministic fallback ladder

1. remove noncanonical furniture and vegetation;
2. shift decorative rock/repair detail within its causal support slot;
3. simplify wall endpoints, trim, or secondary support;
4. simplify the licensed flank while preserving counterplay;
5. reduce room/furnishing complexity within the same threshold relation;
6. degrade one rung while retaining the road-control capsule;
7. reject and reroll.

Never move a road endpoint, erase the threshold, change culture mechanics, flatten the
shoulder, overlap standees, or add an invisible route/cover object to save a seed.

## Culture construction profiles

### Institutional Frontier Works

Low, rectilinear, standardized planning; dressed corners; coursed/role-disciplined
stone; measured replacement; crenellated or ruled institutional defensive edge.

### Upland Vernacular Station

Compact terrain-fitting posture; fitted irregular rubble; dressed stone reserved for
load/wear; deep eave where the weather profile uses it; buttress and timber cribbing;
culture-native defensive edge with the same cover class.

The paired MVP holds road, threshold, room roles, route graph, access, observation,
repair event, condition, collision, cover, sight, interaction, camera, weather, time,
and seed byte-equivalent. The profile changes silhouette/construction and material role
inside prelicensed envelopes.

Each future culture profile binds planning, wall, roof/weather, opening, threshold,
lookout, repair, material and furnishing roles, compatibility, fallback, and version.
It selects families, never authored coordinates.

## Occupancy states and hooks

Military checkpoint, dungeon entrance guard, bandit toll post, makeshift trading post,
changed guard, empty-after conflict, and haunted/inhumanly operated post may roll while
preserving threshold, observation, and occupied-room topology where “occupied” may be
supernatural or evidenced rather than living.

Every arrival requires an active hook: papers, toll, missing guard, suspicious uniform,
broken barrier, contradictory orders, changed shift, drainage repair, hidden captive,
false trade, wanted traveler, or a post whose signal is active but room is empty.
Aftermath propagates: discovered bodies or absence place the owning faction on alert and
start a search.

## Lighting, roof, and cutaway

- Canonical hero is daylight; one motivated night practical is required.
- Practical light belongs to garrison, trader, task, signal, or supernatural fact.
- The flat walkable deck exists in both MVP cultures.
- Defensive edges differ in construction while preserving cover class.
- Ambient, AO, contact, and honest silhouette shadows keep road grade, threshold,
  observation, and standee contact readable.
- Cutaway is a deterministic projection of committed occluders; it may hide/ghost
  licensed pieces and cap sections but never redesign geometry.
- Selected-base material emission remains presentation state, not a world light source.
