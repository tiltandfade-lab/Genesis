*p. 226 MM 2024*

Ogres are dumb, hungry, and mean. That's not a dismissal — those three qualities, in a creature that stands ten feet tall and hits like a battering ram, make for a genuinely dangerous encounter. The ogre's lack of intelligence is its defining characteristic both mechanically and narratively. It doesn't plan. It doesn't retreat. It doesn't understand why hitting things isn't solving the problem it's currently having. When an ogre encounters something it can't immediately smash, it hits it again, harder.

What makes ogres more interesting than their CR 2 suggests is their social mobility. Ogres are frequent hireling muscle for goblinoid warbands, bandits, cults, and anyone else who can offer them regular food and something to hit. They don't ask questions about their employers' motives. They are a versatile encounter component that can legitimately show up in almost any faction-based dungeon.

The Half-Ogre (ogrillon) represents a creature of mixed heritage — generally the result of orc-ogre parentage. These creatures are Medium-sized but possess unusual strength, and occupy an interesting tactical space as heavy melee combatants in factions that can't accommodate or control a full-grown ogre.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Ogres are visible in every environment they occupy.

- **The Scale of Damage:** Everything in an ogre's vicinity is damaged in a specific way — furniture is crushed rather than broken, doors are smashed inward rather than forced, food stores are emptied in a single sitting. The scale of the damage is the tell.
- **The Employer's Problem:** Ogres work for food. If the food stops coming or isn't enough, the ogre turns on its employer. Finding an ogre in a dungeon means its employers are either very organized (regular food delivery, a dedicated keeper) or very recently eaten.
- **The Simple Lair:** Ogres don't decorate. Their space contains the things they actively wanted (food, a sleeping spot, a weapon) and the wreckage of everything else. The simplicity of the lair is a reliable indicator.

### 2. Half-Ogre (Ogrillon)

A creature of mixed orc and ogre blood. Smaller than a true ogre, but with a combination of raw strength and goblinoid cunning that makes it more versatile in a warband role.

_Medium Humanoid (Orc, Giant), Chaotic Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 12 (hide armor)
- **Hit Points:** 30 (4d8 + 12)
- **Speed:** 30 ft.
- **STR:** 17 (+3) | **DEX:** 10 (+0) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 7 (−2) | **WIS:** 9 (−1) | **CHA:** 7 (−2)
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 9
- **Languages:** Common, Giant, Orc
- **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Aggressive:** As a Bonus Action, the half-ogre can move up to its speed toward a hostile creature it can see.

**Actions**

- **Battleaxe:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 7 (1d8 + 3) slashing damage, or 8 (1d10 + 3) if wielded with two hands.
- **Javelin:** _Ranged Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, range 30/120 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 6 (1d6 + 3) piercing damage.

### 3. Ogre Howdah

Not an ogre variant exactly — this is an ogre carrying a platform on its back, with up to four small creatures (goblins, kobolds, halflings) riding it and attacking from the elevated position. The ogre is the vehicle; the riders are the crew.

_Large Giant, Chaotic Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 13 (hide armor + modified rig)
- **Hit Points:** 68 (8d10 + 24)
- **Speed:** 40 ft.
- **STR:** 19 (+4) | **DEX:** 8 (−1) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 5 (−3) | **WIS:** 7 (−2) | **CHA:** 7 (−2)
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 8
- **Languages:** Common, Giant
- **Challenge:** 3 (700 XP, does not include howdah riders) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Howdah:** Up to four Small creatures can ride the howdah platform simultaneously. Riders have three-quarters cover. If the ogre moves more than 15 ft. on its turn, each rider must succeed on a DC 10 Athletics or Acrobatics check or fall off (5 ft. below, 3 (1d6) bludgeoning damage, Prone).

**Actions**

- **Greatclub:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +6 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 13 (2d8 + 4) bludgeoning damage.
- **Fling Rider (1/Turn):** The ogre grabs one willing goblin rider and hurls them at a target within 30 ft. The goblin makes a melee attack with Advantage against the target. The goblin lands in a space adjacent to the target (if possible), taking no damage.

| **1** | **The Hired Muscle** | **Faction Layer:** The ogre is working for the dungeon's main faction — hired to guard a specific room in exchange for regular meals. It isn't loyal; it's contracted. If the party kills its employers first, the ogre might simply walk away. |
| **2** | **The Negotiation** | **Darkly Comic:** The ogre understands Common well enough to negotiate. It wants food. The party has food. The ogre is very large and very good at making its wants known. |
| **3** | **The Ogre's Pet** | **Character Texture:** The ogre has a pet — a captured wolf, a goblin it inexplicably likes, a bird in a cage too small for it. The ogre will not endanger the pet. This is a surprisingly effective lever. |
| **4** | **The Howdah Squad** | **Circus Fight:** Three goblins are riding the ogre in a platform lashed to its back with rope and poorly hammered nails. They have shortbows. The ogre has a greatclub. The goblins are extremely enthusiastic about this arrangement. |
| **5** | **The Bottleneck** | **Environmental Tactical:** The ogre fills a narrow corridor almost perfectly. Getting past it without taking its full attack sequence is the challenge. Spells and flanking require getting around a creature that *is the hallway*. |
| **6** | **The Hungry Problem** | **Urgent:** The ogre hasn't been fed in three days. It is attacking everything. It already ate two of the dungeon faction's members. The rest of the faction is as frightened of the ogre as of the party, and will briefly cooperate to deal with the ogre first. |
| **7** | **The Double Threat** | **Escalation:** Two ogres. They don't cooperate intentionally, but they attack the nearest target and there are two nearest targets. |
| **8** | **The Tamed Thing** | **Horror Undertone:** The ogre is wearing a magical collar and responds to spoken commands from a figure in the back of the room. The figure is small, robed, and absolutely confident that the ogre won't attack them. The party needs to reckon with both. |
| **9** | **The Wreckage** | **Discovery:** The party arrives after the ogre has already destroyed the room. Every occupant is dead, every container is smashed, and the ogre is still here, confused about why the smashing didn't make it feel better. |
| **10** | **The Simple Request** | **Pathos:** The ogre approaches, not attacking, making a sound that is almost a question. It doesn't understand what happened to its keepers. It's hungry. It's lonely. It is going to attack in about four rounds if nobody does anything. |

### d10 The Smashed Hall (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Food Contract** | A written agreement (simple, large lettering, clearly drafted for the ogre's benefit) describing a food-for-service arrangement. The contract names the employer. The employer may still be in the dungeon. |
| **2** | **The Crushed Cage** | A large iron cage in the corner has been crumpled inward from the outside. The ogre was being kept here. Someone released it — recently. The cage door's lock is on the outside. |
| **3** | **The Scale of Violence** | Marks on the wall at ten feet indicate the ogre stood here and struck repeatedly. The force of the blows cracked the stone. Whatever the ogre was hitting was in the adjacent room. |
| **4** | **The Keeper's Remains** | The remains of the ogre's keeper — the person responsible for managing it — are present. Their death explains why the ogre is now uncontrolled. A DC 12 Investigation check determines cause of death: not the ogre (likely). Something else killed the keeper first. |
| **5** | **The Crude Map** | A large piece of hide with simple pictograms drawn on it — the ogre's personal map of the dungeon, from its perspective. It marks "food room," "sleep place," and one location with a symbol that might mean "danger" or "do not go." |
| **6** | **The Stacked Treasure** | A pile of objects the ogre valued — mostly shiny things, a broken sword, a specific type of rock, a cloth of an attractive color. Among the items is one that shouldn't be here: a piece of evidence from a previous scene, a key, or a stolen personal item. |
| **7** | **The Goblin Account** | A surviving goblin (hiding behind a barrel) can explain the ogre's entire history in this dungeon: who hired it, what it was supposed to do, and, crucially, what it did instead. The goblin is traumatized and very willing to talk in exchange for not being hit. |
| **8** | **The Bite Radius** | The pattern of damage in the room is educational: the ogre hit something large and immovable (the wall) multiple times before turning on the smaller, softer things. It was frustrated before it started killing. Something made it angry before the party arrived. |
| **9** | **The Howdah Wreckage** | The remains of a goblin riding platform — straps, a wooden board, two tiny crossbows — are scattered near the ogre's sleeping spot. The goblins who rode it either fled, died, or were relocated. The ogre misses them. |
| **10** | **The Other Ogre's Mark** | Huge knuckle-drag marks on the floor lead out of a side passage and into the ogre's territory. A second ogre passed through here — recently, given the freshness of the marks — and didn't stay. It was scouting. |
