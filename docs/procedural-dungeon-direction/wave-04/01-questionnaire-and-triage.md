### 13 Wave 4 opening — preserve the complete bank before deep dives

#### Inherited laws Wave 4 may deepen but not silently contradict

- Canonical graph nodes/edges, committed geometry, portal/connector ids, provenance, scale domains, structural
  support, and material state remain engine-owned truth; render products and provider prose cannot invent them.
- A connection is a causal object with typed endpoints, state, direction, capacity, conditions, knowledge, and
  lifecycle—not a label painted between rooms.
- Exact tactical placement and coarser site/travel relations remain precision tiers over one owner. Traversal and
  remount may not teleport, duplicate, omit, or reroll actors, objects, vehicles, pursuit, or consequences.
- Unknown topology, known evidence, belief, rumor, partial map knowledge, and revealed connection truth remain
  distinct. Presentation may not leak an endpoint because it exists in canon.
- Builder/original-use scale and explicit transition architecture constrain ordinary access. Squeezing, breach,
  guise, magic, vehicles, crowds, and unusual bodies require their actual capability and causal owners.
- Hard topology and progression invariants never silently relax. An impossible dependency emits a diagnostic or an
  explicitly owned failing/catastrophe state rather than hiding the required solution behind itself.
- One fixed production camera family with governed focus/cutaway must show known connection state honestly; lack of
  player rotation cannot be repaired through false geometry or provider narration.
- Provider-neutral DM narration receives viewpoint-safe facts, legal actions, typed receipts, and truthful fallback.
  It may dramatize discovery or transition but cannot decide access, topology, movement, or mutation by prose.
- The no-cash Mac proof comes first. Broader portal families, split-party traversal, mutable planar networks, paid
  device evidence, and mature presentation remain evidence-triggered feature breadth.

### 13.2 Read-only current-engine and secret-frequency audit

#### What Genesis does with ordinary connections today

Genesis already owns a persistent undirected room graph. `dungeon-walk.js` chooses one of twelve topology builders,
stores adjacency, breadth-first depth, and stable room numbers, then projects every adjacent node into that room's
`exits[]`. The spatializer visits each unique graph edge, carves one corridor, and places an aperture on both room
sides. This is a useful foundation, but it is not yet the Wave 4 endpoint contract:

- each room independently calls `dwalkDoorRoll()` for its own half of an adjacency;
- the two halves may therefore disagree about door type or state;
- rich table nouns currently mix open arches, ordinary leaves, grilles, fissures, trapdoors, false walls, magical
  barriers, portcullises, and compound entrances without a normalized mechanical ontology;
- `this side` and `far side` state prose is ambiguous when the same edge is rolled twice;
- the two spatial apertures share a segment pair but no canonical connection id, version, lifecycle, or state owner;
- static reachability treats door cells as passable, so a lock, bar, seal, collapse, or permission gate does not yet
  participate in progression validation; and
- the historical connection-point and room-slot sketches identify useful needs, but no present shared record owns
  endpoint geometry, orientation, span, clearance, motion envelope, direction, capacity, or failure behavior.

The target should preserve the existing graph and apertures while making **one canonical connection record** the
owner. Per-room exits, tactical apertures, map marks, interaction cards, and DM-seat facts become viewpoint-safe
projections of that record rather than independently rolled truths.

#### The recovered secret-frequency lineage

Adam's earlier frequency has three historical layers, and the newest accepted design—not the oldest procedure—must
govern:

1. The old pen-and-paper procedure rolled a d6 once per room; only a 1 created a secret (**one in six**).
2. `Dungeon Procedure Update for v 0.2` explicitly replaced that presence check with one Secret Type, one Secret
   Delivery, and one Secret Payoff Size **per segment**, while warning that big results should usually downgrade to
   micro outside a finale.
3. The current walker does create `segment.secret` in **every room**, including the finale, but currently rolls only
   Secret Tier and Reveal Type. It does not currently consume the Secret Type or Payoff Size tables in that call.

Closed Wave 1 then supplied the controlling design interpretation: **every realized room receives a local discovery
opportunity, but not an independent heavyweight secret**. Quiet rooms can answer broad inspection with a small true
observation. Major/Mythic truths, hidden routes, subordinate complexes, and campaign revelations are budgeted and
coordinated at site/zone scale. Progression-critical truth needs fair independent evidence, and no discovery packet
may lead nowhere.

`WALK-CARD-DEALING.md` sketches a compatible `walk.secretNetwork`, including hidden -> noticed -> revealed states
and clue/payload references. The player-safe projection can already read such a network in tests. The production
walker does not currently build one, so it is a promising seam—not a finished or canonical implementation.

The live design question is therefore not whether to return to one-in-six. It is how a room-level **opportunity
cadence** feeds a smaller, richer set of graph-bound secret networks without turning every empty cell, prison cell,
or repeated child into a bespoke payload.

### 13.4 First material deep-dive batch — ten easy-numbered decisions

These ten response numbers preserve P4.1-P4.10 and merge only the additive partners Adam requested. P4.11's
technical default and P4.12's required acceptance audit are carried outside the batch. **Option B is recommended in
all ten**, but each recommendation is a phased architecture rather than a claim that the ideal breadth belongs in
the first prototype.

#### 1. P4.1 — should connection nouns be separate mechanics, one universal edge, or composed capabilities?

**Option A — a separate schema and resolver for every noun.** A door, ladder, chute, lift, window, bridge, and planar
portal each get their own data model and bespoke rules. A secret rotating wall that is also barred and one-way needs
special cross-type code. The DM seat can describe each well, but combinations multiply. **Cost: high schema,
resolver, migration, and combinatorial QA cost.**

**Option B — one canonical Connection base plus composed capability/mechanism facets (recommended).** Every
connection owns endpoints, direction, topology, traversal, visibility, sound, capacity, state, knowledge, physical
binding, and provenance. Facets then say what it is: `path`, `aperture`, `closure/barrier`, `climb`, `fall-space`,
`conveyance`, `support-bearing crossing`, `concealment`, `nonlocal transit`, or `hazard`. Ordinary defaults remain
recognizable: an arch is an open aperture; a crawl changes posture/body eligibility and throughput; stairs/ramp/
ladder connect elevations differently; a shaft permits sight, sound, hanging, falling, and perhaps climbing; a lift
moves a platform between landings; a window begins as sight/sound and may become constrained traversal; a Breach is
an action-created aperture; teleport/planar portals join nonlocal endpoints; secret and one-way are orthogonal state
or direction facets rather than mutually exclusive nouns. A barred secret lift can therefore be one object instead
of three systems. The DM seat receives `visible seam; cold air; no legal traversal yet`, not permission to infer the
hidden shaft. **Cost: medium-high ontology/resolver work, then lower expansion cost.**

**Option C — keep a generic graph edge plus free tags/prose.** `type:"ladder"` or a sentence tells the provider what
probably happens, while checks and movement are improvised. Cheap initially, but capacity, fall rescue, pursuit,
save/load, and mutation drift. **Cost: low prototype cost and very high correctness debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove B with open arch, hinged/barred door, crawl, stair, shaft/ladder, and one secret
connection; extend MVP to bridges, windows/breaches, one-way transitions, and simple lifts; promote moving,
multi-landing, planar, vehicle-bearing, and compound connections after corpus evidence. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 2. P4.2 — what is the canonical endpoint contract, given today's duplicated half-doors?

**Option A — retain per-room exits and reconcile disagreements.** Each room keeps its own roll; a rule chooses the
stricter side or first observed side when they conflict. In a crypt, the chapel might say `open arch` while the
ossuary says `locked iron door`; the resolver repairs this at use time. **Cost: low migration, permanent ambiguity
and debugging debt.**

**Option B — one connection record with endpoint projections (recommended).** One stable id owns two or more typed
endpoints, endpoint frames/orientation, directionality, travel time, shared and endpoint-local state, capacity,
owners, access predicates, knowledge by viewpoint, failure policy, version, and provenance. Room exits contain only
the connection id plus their local endpoint projection. One barred portcullis may legitimately be operable only from
the guardroom, but both rooms agree that it is the same portcullis and whether it is raised. Tactical apertures,
maps, interaction cards, and the provider-neutral DM seat all read the same version. **Cost: medium migration and
validator work; high leverage.**

**Option C — let the active room/DM seat establish connection truth on first use.** This hides present conflicts
until play, then canonizes prose. Cheap for demos but makes topology provider-dependent. **Cost: low now, very high
persistence/replay risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** adapt current undirected edges into one id/shared state and emit loud diagnostics for
legacy half-door conflicts; MVP adds typed endpoint geometry, direction, capacity, access, mutation versioning, and
save migration; the ideal supports moving endpoints, multi-landing lifts, vehicles, planar networks, and exact
SceneLineage handoffs. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 3. P4.3 — how should locks, permissions, restrictions, hazards, and bypasses work?

**Option A — mostly binary authored gates.** The brass key opens the archive; without it the edge is closed. This is
legible but makes keys brittle and underuses spells, tools, factions, bodies, time, noise, and destruction. **Cost:
low implementation, high content burden and softlock risk.**

**Option B — typed access predicates plus certified approaches and consequences (recommended).** A gate states what
currently blocks traversal and which systems can lawfully change or satisfy it: key/custody, mechanism knowledge,
password/ritual, faction permission, size/posture, tool/capability, power, hazard tolerance, resource payment, time,
noise, or structural destruction. The engine offers approaches from actual possessions, abilities, relationships,
and geometry; a roll resolves uncertainty inside an eligible approach rather than granting universal permission.
For a sealed archive the DM seat might offer `use canon key`, `ask the archivist`, `pick the mundane secondary
lock`, `break hinges: loud + evidence + collapse risk`, or `search for power control`; it may not invent a DC and let
any axe bypass an arcane seal. **Cost: medium-high integration and content-normalization cost.**

**Option C — broad improvised DCs with provider discretion.** Nearly any plausible pitch receives a check. Flexible,
but physics, permissions, resource custody, and progression become inconsistent between models and sessions.
**Cost: low schema cost, high fairness/provider QA cost.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prototype key/open, pick, force, and alternate-route predicates with the existing
puzzle failsafe; MVP adds permissions, size, hazard, resource, spell/tool, time/noise, and dependency validation;
the ideal grows a broad certified-action ecology without turning every interaction into physics simulation.
**Recommendation: B.**

#### 4. P4.4 — how much vertical space becomes real play?

**Option A — flatten verticality into adjacency and narration.** Stairs cost movement; a shaft is a special exit;
height is mostly visual. Cheap and readable, but weakens falling, rescue, large bodies, high ground, cross-level
sight/sound, and hanging actions. **Cost: low, with a low tactical ceiling.**

**Option B — semantic 3D with bounded tactical 2.5D realization (recommended).** Canon stores elevation intervals,
headroom, landings, fall spaces, supports, climb surfaces, endpoint heights, cross-level sight/sound, and body/
load limits. The active tactical area realizes only the exact local tiers and connector needed. A broken bell-tower
stair can support a visible upper landing, a hanging PC, a companion bracing rope below, debris falling through the
shaft, and an ogre unable to use the narrow remnant. The DM seat receives positions, legal rescue actions, fall
distance, support condition, and uncertainty; it does not decide who is magically adjacent. **Cost: medium-high
mechanics, camera, pathing, and QA cost.**

**Option C — full continuous 3D/rigid-body traversal.** Exact ropes, bodies, lifts, debris, and collision determine
the result. Powerful, but far beyond the no-budget proof and brittle with standees/turn-based rules. **Cost: very
high.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** one two-tier room with stair, ledge, ladder/shaft, fall, and rescue; MVP adds multiple
landings, hanging, large-body limits, sight/sound, pursuit, and persistent damage; the ideal adds moving lifts,
complex collapses, flying, deep shafts, and richer support simulation. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 5. P4.5 — should every site satisfy the same loop/escape formula?

**Option A — universal circulation quotas.** Require a loop, shortcut, alternate escape, and bounded dead ends in
every site. Easy to validate, but a tomb, burrow, prison, palace, mine, and extradimensional maze begin to feel
authored by the same planner. **Cost: medium solver work and high sameness risk.**

**Option B — purpose/history-specific circulation profiles with universal safety invariants (recommended).** A
prison may have public intake, guarded staff circulation, cellblock control chokepoints, maintenance routes, and a
rare escape route; a mine may branch around extraction and haulage, ventilation, drainage, and emergency egress; a
tomb may deliberately funnel intruders while preserving builder/ritual access that history later sealed. Profiles
budget loops, bottlenecks, service access, defensible thresholds, shortcuts, dead ends, evacuation, and escape
differently. Universal invariants only protect required solvability, builder/operator plausibility, and supported
failure. The DM seat explains the resulting evidence; it does not add a convenient tunnel because the graph feels
linear. **Cost: medium-high profile/solver/test work, with strong variety payoff.**

**Option C — generate topology freely and repair circulation in narration.** Maximum variety, but service routes,
escape, and defenses become fake when needed. **Cost: low generator cost, high play and continuity debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** label one main route, one service/alternate opportunity, and intentional dead ends in
three contrasting site profiles; MVP couples the twelve-site corpus to circulation obligations and history damage;
the ideal evaluates evacuation, pursuit, operating logistics, occupation changes, and catastrophe without making
all sites equally connected. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 6. P4.6 — which secret cadence should become canon?

**Option A — restore the old one-in-six independent secret check.** Most rooms have no discovery promise; a hit
creates its own cache, passage, lore, or device. Clean and sparse, but it discards the accepted Wave 1 attention law
and produces disconnected slot-machine secrets. **Cost: low, but a design regression.**

**Option B — one lightweight room opportunity feeding a bounded site-scale Secret Network (recommended).** Every
realized room/child can reward deliberate attention, often through a quiet tell, useful negative evidence, safe
position, trace, clue fragment, observation point, or confirmation. The site budgets a smaller set of actual secret
truths, routes, compartments, encounters, and Major/Mythic revelations. Each has graph-bound homes, clue refs,
hidden/noticed/revealed state, delivery actions/costs, payoff budget, evidence redundancy when progression-critical,
and a fulfilled reference or promissory card. In a six-room keep, four room opportunities might all point toward one
smugglers' route; only the concealed pantry panel is the connection. The DM seat can narrate flour disturbed near
the wall before discovery but cannot reveal the destination. **Cost: medium-high dealer/network/attention work, but
far lower content bloat than a full secret per room.**

**Option C — make every current `segment.secret` a complete independent secret payload.** This follows the literal
v0.2 checklist but turns repeated spaces into busywork and inflates treasure/revelations. **Cost: high content,
search, UI, and save debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** retain every-room opportunities but group a small site into one route secret and one
non-route truth with hidden/noticed/revealed projections; MVP adds budgeted network kinds, broad-search aggregation,
critical-evidence validation, map-safe state, and save/load; the ideal supports historical false maps, faction-held
knowledge, cross-site chains, and generated promissory fulfillment. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 7. P4.7 — when may a site or route become inaccessible?

**Option A — never permanently; the game quietly restores a route.** Collapsed bridges respawn, timed portals
reopen, and lost keys reappear. This avoids lockouts but cheapens consequences and can contradict visible history.
**Cost: low validation, high fiction/persistence damage.**

**Option B — permit legible local/optional irreversibility while protecting required progression (recommended).** A
route or even an entire optional site may become inaccessible through player-caused collapse or sealing, flood/
fire/cave-in, a destroyed or departed lift/raft, a one-way drop, timed realm alignment, revoked faction permission,
quarantine, loss of required body/capability, depleted power, lost/destroyed access custody, or a deliberate ritual
choice. Before an action closes a known route, the engine owns preconditions, travelers/custody, consequences, and
an irreversible-choice signal when the PC could understand it. A required campaign dependency must instead have
validated redundancy/recovery, a recoverable owner-driven route, or an explicit supported failing/catastrophe branch
that the campaign can continue through. Optional content may truly be missed. **Cost: medium-high dependency,
mutation, warning, recovery, and save/replay QA.**

**Option C — allow unrestricted harsh irreversibility.** The simulation may permanently eliminate the only route to
the main objective because the key burned or the bridge failed. Dramatic but inappropriate for a solo AI-DM game
without enormous recovery authorship. **Cost: low protective logic, very high campaign-loss/support risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** validate keys/controls and one destructive bypass so required content never sits
behind its own gate; MVP adds route-loss receipts, optional-site missability, recovery dependencies, explicit
failure branches, and save/load; the ideal lets factions, disasters, seasons, planar cycles, and player engineering
reshape access across the world. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 8. P4.8 — how should an existing connection move a group and handle forced separation?

**Option A — all-or-nothing party teleport.** A transition succeeds only for the whole party, which remounts
together on the far side. Safe for pre-alpha but cannot represent crowds, squeezing, pursuit, vehicle capacity,
closing gates, or someone stranded on the threshold. **Cost: low, with visible fiction limits.**

**Option B — a canonical traversal transaction, phased to the accepted one-active-party law (recommended).** The
transaction names the connection version, ordered participants, carried/custodied objects, formation, load,
vehicle/follower relations, capacity, required actions, time, pursuit distance, closing condition, and outcome per
traveler. A portcullis can descend after the PC and before two companions; nobody teleports or disappears. In
pre-alpha, one party scene remains active. Same-scene `inside`/`at portal`/`adjacent` participation is preferred;
if a persistent forced split occurs, the foreground side continues and the other becomes the already accepted exact,
self-inert cold record until rescue/remount. The DM seat narrates the committed order and visible consequence but
cannot secretly grant the cold side turns. **Cost: medium-high transaction, ordering, pursuit, custody, UI, and QA;
bounded below full split simulation.**

**Option C — immediately support independently advancing party branches.** Each subgroup acts, consumes time, meets
encounters, and mutates the site concurrently. This is the ideal breadth but contradicts accepted pre-alpha scope.
**Cost: very high concurrency, chronology, arbitration, camera, save, and DM-context cost.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** atomic whole-party crossing plus exact threshold roles and one forced-cold-separation
fixture; MVP adds ordered/capacity-limited crossings, enemies/followers/vehicles, closing gates, rescue goals, and
deterministic persistence; independently advancing parent/child SceneLineages promote only after same-scene
companion autonomy and measured need. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 9. P4.9 + G4.1 — how should a dangerous improvised crossing create or mutate a connection?

**Option A — resolve it as a one-off check and prose result.** The PC jams an axe into masonry, ties a rope, rolls
Athletics, and the DM says everyone crosses. Fast, but there is no persistent axe custody, anchor condition, load,
return route, or explanation when the rope later fails. **Cost: low now, high continuity debt.**

**Option B — create/change a canonical connection through a typed receipt (recommended).** The proposed action
identifies endpoints and uses real objects, geometry, materials, capabilities, time, helpers, and current hazards.
The engine derives eligible approaches and uncertainty; DC/physics is only one input. An axe-held rope crossing
therefore records the axe and rope identities/custody, anchor/support choice, length, knots or fastening capability,
load and throughput, condition, allowed actions, time/noise, failure profile, expiry/monitor, evidence, and return
path. A roll may decide whether an uncertain fastening holds, not whether an imaginary axe or impossible span
exists. Its receipt can create, damage, stabilize, invert, close, repair, or destroy the connection and reconcile
travelers/pursuit atomically. Failure during an ordered crossing may trigger Question 8's cold forced separation.
**Cost: medium-high action-certification, object/custody, mutation, and recovery work.**

**Option C — full engineering/rigid-body simulation.** Exact stress, friction, collision, rope dynamics, and material
fracture own every outcome. It minimizes hand-authored abstractions but is unaffordable and will not cover magic or
social permissions gracefully. **Cost: extreme.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** one rope/anchor crossing and one breach/collapse receipt over the shared Connection
record; MVP adds repair, destruction, blocking, simple bridges, portal stabilization, traveler reconciliation, and
persistent degradation; the ideal expands certified construction, magical route creation, moving endpoints, and
large-scale engineering without requiring a separate bespoke simulator per noun. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 10. P4.10 + G4.2 — what should maps, memory, rumors, and cross-scale discovery own?

**Option A — the map mirrors canonical topology once generated.** Easy orientation, but secret endpoints and
unvisited geometry leak because engine knowledge becomes player knowledge. **Cost: low UI cost, fatal mystery leak.**

**Option B — canonical topology plus separate evidence, belief, and viewpoint knowledge (recommended).** A
connection/secret has one truth owner; each actor and map has references with states such as unknown, rumored,
evidenced/partially located, noticed, revealed, traversed, disproven, or stale. A scratch in Room 2 may reference a
site-secret id without exposing Room 7; a captured smuggler's map may place a believed exit approximately; opening
the pantry panel promotes the exact endpoint; later discovering the route serves a campaign clue chain by stable id
rather than provider memory. Tactical view shows only viewpoint-safe local evidence, the site map shows earned

### 13.7 Generated material follow-up batch — ten easy-numbered questions

#### 1. F4.1a — when is a corridor merely a connection path, and when is it a playable place?

**Option A — every corridor is its own mounted scene.** Even a safe ten-foot connector receives separate arrival,
movement, search, and departure beats. This maximizes spatial literalism but makes ordinary circulation slow and
inflates scene/card/save work. **Cost: high pacing and content cost.**

**Option B — every corridor is canonical space, but promote it to a playable segment only when materially relevant
(recommended).** Length, geometry, doors, visibility, sound, capacity, and traversal cost always exist. A short safe
hall may resolve as one transition; a long gallery with murder holes, intersecting doors, a patrol, a secret, a
chasm, pursuit, or a meaningful choice mounts as a scene without being retroactively invented. The DM seat may say
`you cross twelve quiet feet to the chapel` for the first and must expose the real positions/actions for the second.
**Cost: medium promotion-rule, geometry, and continuity work.**

**Option C — corridors are never playable; all action snaps to rooms.** Cheap, but corridor fights, pursuits,
ambushes, and hazards become prose detached from position. **Cost: low now and high tactical-fiction debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove one compact safe connector and one promoted hazard/pursuit gallery; MVP adds
deterministic promotion triggers and remount; the ideal supports long multi-stage circulation and cross-level
pursuits. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 2. F4.3a — what bypass guarantee applies to a gate?

**Option A — every gate must expose several simultaneous solutions.** A lock always has a key, pick, force, spell,
and alternate route. Very safe, but gates lose identity and character capability matters less. **Cost: high content
and solver burden.**

**Option B — guarantees depend on progression role (recommended).** A mandatory route needs at least one validated
solution plus a recovery/failsafe path if that solution can become unavailable; it does not need to advertise three
equivalent buttons. An optional vault may remain closed until the party earns the particular permission, tool,
spell, key, or destructive capability, provided retreat remains legal and no required campaign truth is trapped
there alone. A puzzle keeps the existing solo-player failsafe. **Cost: medium dependency classification and
validation work.**

**Option C — no guaranteed bypass or recovery.** Any gate may permanently block the campaign. **Cost: low validator
work and very high frustration/support risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** classify critical versus optional gates and validate one solution plus recovery on a
small graph; MVP adds custody loss, faction help, destructive bypass, and puzzle integration; the ideal supports
dynamic world-owned recoveries without making every gate interchangeable. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 3. F4.4a — how harsh should falling and failed vertical traversal be?

**Option A — falls are normally soft setbacks.** The character takes modest damage or returns to the last landing.
Readable, but deep shafts and precarious rescues lose threat. **Cost: low mechanics and low dramatic range.**

**Option B — use a legible consequence ladder (recommended).** Height, landing surface, current position, gear,
support, helpers, body, and action determine eligible outcomes: lost time/noise, prone or dropped custody, damage,
injury, hanging, lower-level displacement, forced separation, or death at genuinely extreme and perceptible stakes.
A causal rescue window exists when someone could actually catch, brace, feather, fly, or reach the fall; it is not a
universal mercy interrupt. The DM seat describes known danger before commitment without revealing an unseen weak
stone. **Cost: medium-high resolution, participation, and QA work.**

**Option C — approximate real-world lethality whenever possible.** Harsh and coherent in one register, but poorly
matched to fantasy abilities and the abstract turn system. **Cost: very high tuning and player-trust risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prototype short fall, hanging state, rescue, and severe fall; MVP adds custody,
helpers, magic, large bodies, and separation; richer flight/collapse interactions are the ideal. **Recommendation:
B.**

#### 4. F4.6a — may Genesis generate red herrings and false secret beliefs?

**Option A — every clue is literally reliable.** Players never waste time on false information, but deception,
stale maps, mistaken inhabitants, and ambiguous history become mechanically thin. **Cost: low knowledge work and
reduced mystery range.**

**Option B — permit bounded, causally authored false beliefs with fair closure (recommended).** A liar, old map,
camouflage, damaged sign, sincere misconception, planted evidence, or changed route may support a false belief. The
record distinguishes the claim from truth, preserves who believes it and why, and provides finite evidence that can
confirm, refine, or disprove it. Random empty busywork is forbidden; mandatory progression never depends on
guessing which unsupported clue is fake. **Cost: medium-high knowledge/provenance/content and DM-seat QA.**

**Option C — freely roll deceptive tells and dead-end clues for old-school uncertainty.** Mysterious, but especially
dangerous with an AI DM and a solo player. **Cost: low generation and high fairness/context debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove one stale map contradicted by current physical evidence; MVP adds lies,
mistaken witnesses, belief updates, and fair closure; the ideal supports faction disinformation and long-running
cross-site misconceptions. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 5. F4.2a — may a character knowingly enter a portal whose destination remains unknown?

**Option A — every traversable portal reveals its destination first.** Safe and easy to map, but removes blind-
threshold wonder and danger. **Cost: low, with reduced mystery.**

**Option B — permit blind transit when the connection is physically usable, while revealing only perceptible or
earned facts (recommended).** Frost, salt wind, screams, a known sigil, a rope pulled taut through the surface, or a
returning scout may provide evidence. Unknown destination, one-way state, instability, or expiry stays unknown
unless it can be perceived or is already known. A foreseeable irreversible commitment receives the established
preflight; uncertainty itself is not converted into a spoiler. **Cost: medium knowledge, transaction, and recovery
work.**

**Option C — unknown destinations are mechanically blocked until mapped or identified.** Safe but restrictive,
especially for strange/Breach play. **Cost: medium gating content and lower emergent range.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove one known local teleporter and one legal unknown destination; MVP adds one-way,
timed, unstable, scout/rope evidence, and return-path handling; mutable planar networks remain the ideal.
**Recommendation: B.**

#### 6. F4.8a — who chooses the order when crossing order matters?

**Option A — the engine always uses formation order.** Fast, but a closing gate may strand the wizard merely because
of a hidden default. **Cost: low UI and high agency risk.**

**Option B — routine transitions stay automatic; materially risky transitions expose a recommended order the
player may revise before commitment (recommended).** A rope that bears one traveler, a failing bridge, a capacity-
limited lift, or a closing portcullis shows the known stakes and proposed party/load order. Helpers, rope custody,
rear guard, passengers, followers, and enemies then resolve against that committed order. The DM seat does not ask
ten questions for a safe doorway. **Cost: medium transaction-preview and accessibility work.**

**Option C — prompt for every traveler at every transition.** Maximum control and intolerable traversal friction.
**Cost: medium UI implementation and very high pacing cost.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prototype one automatic doorway and one editable rope-crossing order; MVP adds
vehicles, followers, pursuit, and partial completion; formation policies and richer tactical presets are the ideal.
**Recommendation: B.**

#### 7. F4.9a — how explicitly should dangerous traversal expose risk?

**Option A — always show exact DCs, probabilities, load limits, and failure tables.** Transparent, but makes every
rope or weak floor read like an engineering dashboard and may expose facts the party cannot know. **Cost: medium UI
and high fiction/knowledge tension.**

**Option B — show qualitative danger and concrete known stakes, with expandable mechanics when lawfully known or
needed for accessibility (recommended).** `The cracked anchor may hold one armored traveler at a time; failure could
drop the crosser and strand whoever remains` is actionable. A known rules DC or exact measured capacity may be shown
when the character/tool/system has established it. Hidden defects remain hidden, and the player-rolled die stays
open. **Cost: medium risk-language, knowledge, and accessible-detail work.**

**Option C — keep odds and failure shape behind the DM screen.** Dramatic, but important losses can feel arbitrary.
**Cost: low UI and high trust risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prototype qualitative states plus one expandable mechanical detail view; MVP adds
consistent risk vocabulary, hidden/known defects, assistance, and voice/screen-reader parity; richer tactical
forecasting promotes only if playtests need it. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 8. F4.10a — how much dungeon mapping should be automatic?

**Option A — automatically draw the exact canonical site graph after any room is entered.** Convenient, but leaks
unknown connections, spatial precision, and changed routes. **Cost: low map logic and fatal mystery debt.**

**Option B — automatically remember what the party actually observed at honest precision, with optional player
annotations (recommended).** Entered rooms, traversed connections, perceived relative relations, and known changes
appear without requiring graph paper. Unexplored endpoints, secret edges, uncertain distances, rumors, and copied
maps remain separate claims and may be approximate, stale, or false. Player notes never become world truth merely
because they were written. **Cost: medium-high epistemic map, annotation, save, and accessibility work.**

**Option C — require mapping tools, a cartographer character, or manual notes for reliable memory.** Strong old-
school texture but burdens a solo chat-driven game and accessibility. **Cost: medium content/UI and high player
labor.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prototype visited rooms/traversed edges and no secret leakage; MVP adds honest
precision, changed-route marks, rumors/artifact maps, notes, and accessible text equivalence; shared/private/stale
multi-map ecosystems are the ideal. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 9. F4.9b — may players and NPCs deliberately control routes with the same connection mechanics?

**Option A — only authored/scripted events change route state.** Players can use gates but not meaningfully bar,
spike, cut, jam, dismantle, or reroute them unless a bespoke event exists. **Cost: low action breadth and high
affordance frustration.**

**Option B — certified connection actions are symmetric across capable actors (recommended).** A PC may bar a door,
spike it open, cut a pursuit rope, disable a lift, repair a bridge, or stabilize a portal when real position,
knowledge, tools, time, ownership, and mechanics permit it. Guards, factions, monsters, disasters, and operating
systems use the same truth and may later counter, repair, or exploit the result through their own receipts. Symmetry
means shared laws, not identical intelligence or available actions. **Cost: medium-high action certification,
offscreen-owner, and adversarial QA.**

**Option C — let provider narration accept any plausible route manipulation.** Broad, but state, fairness, and
counterplay drift between providers. **Cost: low schema and very high continuity debt.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove bar/spike/cut on one door/rope with one enemy response; MVP adds repair,
faction control, lift/bridge/portal actions, and persistence; siege engineering and broad operating-system behavior
remain the ideal. **Recommendation: B.**

#### 10. F4.6b — how much does discovering a secret connection reveal immediately?

**Option A — discovery reveals and maps the full edge and destination.** Clear, but a draft under a panel can expose
an unseen chamber and its route before the panel opens. **Cost: low state work and high mystery leakage.**

**Option B — reveal progressively according to evidence and action (recommended).** `noticed` exposes only the
licensed tell; `located` may identify a likely seam or mechanism; `opened` creates a viewpoint-true aperture;
threshold sight/sound/smell reveals only what that position permits; `traversed` or mapped evidence establishes the
far endpoint and route at earned precision. A known architectural plan can lawfully skip stages. The DM seat never
equates `there is a draft` with `Room 7 contains four cultists`. **Cost: medium reveal-state, projection, and map
integration work.**

**Option C — keep destination knowledge vague even after opening/traversal.** Preserves mystery at the cost of false
spatial ignorance. **Cost: medium narration and high player-comprehension risk.**

**Prototype -> MVP -> ideal:** prove tell -> located -> opened -> traversed on one panel; MVP adds multiple reveal
vectors, plans/maps, cross-level senses, occupants, and save/load; richer magical/false-perception routes remain the
ideal. **Recommendation: B.**

Wave 4 remains **OPEN**. These ten generated follow-ups must be answered and audited before the record advances to
closure/phasing. No implementation is authorized.
