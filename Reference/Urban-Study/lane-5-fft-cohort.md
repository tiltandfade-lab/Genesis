## 2. The street-slice composition: how much street, how much building

Read across the cohort, FFT builds a town slice one of three ways, and the split is not
subtle.

**(a) One frontage, one big quiet street (99 Main Street of Lesalia).** In plan the map is
roughly half roofs and half paving, and the paved half carries almost nothing: one tree in
a pit, one well, one bench, a few steps. The entire identity budget is spent on a single
continuous run of building fronts along one edge. **This is the strongest single argument
in the corpus for site 10's "quiet ground is active composition" inheritance surviving
urbanisation.** A town map does not have to be full.

**(b) Two facing frontages with a lane between (31 Dorter).** Two rows of jettied
timber-frame houses face each other across an irregular paved path with grass verges on
both sides. The lane bends. The buildings step in height (2–3 storeys) and their roof
ridges run in different directions. There is exactly one big tree standing in the open. The
"street" is not a formal paved corridor — it is a worn path through a leftover space.

**(c) The stepped street with a level above and a lane below (47 Zarghidas, 44 Bervenia,
27 Goland).** A masonry terrace carries one street; a stair drops to a lower one. In
Zarghidas the lower lane is bare dirt while the upper terrace is dressed pale flagstone —
**the material change does the level-reading work before the geometry does.**

**The proportion, eyeballed, not measured:** in every case the built mass occupies well
under half the plate, and the largest single continuous surface in the map is ground. FFT
never fills a town map edge to edge with buildings.

## 3. Frontage rhythm at gameplay scale

- **Party walls are visible and constant.** In 31, 47, 48 and 99 buildings share walls;
  roofs run into each other; there is no gap between units. The frontage is one continuous
  object with a wobbling top edge, not a row of separated houses.
- **Height varies unit to unit, always.** No two adjacent units in the corpus have the same
  eaves line. The variation is roughly one storey, not three.
- **Roof direction varies too.** Gable ridges run both parallel and perpendicular to the
  street inside the same run (99, 31, 48). That single alternation does more for the
  silhouette than any prop.
- **The bay is legible.** In 99 and 47 each unit reads as one wide opening at ground level
  (arched carriage entry, shop opening, or a plank double door) plus one or two windows
  above. That is the bay module: **one wide ground opening, one or two upper windows.**
- **Jetties are real in the northern skins.** 31 and 48 show upper storeys oversailing the
  ground floor. In the southern/stone skins (42, 47) there is no jetty at all — the wall is
  flat and the shade comes from a lean-to instead.

## 4. Where FFT spends dressing, and where it spends paint

This is the lane's most transferable result, and it maps cleanly onto the geometry/paint
law already in STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG §5.

**Built as geometry (things you can stand on, shoot through, or be stopped by):**
stairs and stair runs · retaining faces and terrace edges · low parapets · deep door
openings · window reveals · gate arches · lean-to and shed masses attached to a main mass ·
projecting galleries/balconies (27) · bridges and culverts (22, 98, 38) · walkable wall
tops (25) · the market's timber produce trays (42) · barrels, crates, tables and benches
(96) · the windmill sails (38, 103) · trees in the paving (99, 22, 31).

**Carried by paint/decal (things that read but are not massed):**
timber framing patterns · roof tile and shingle pattern · plaster cracks, patches and
missing render · brick coursing · the wall sign board at Warjilis · the heraldic banner at
Lesalia's gate (a flat hanging plane, essentially a decal with one poly) · moss and
vegetation at wall bases and in paving joints (99) · paving joint patterns and the dirt/
flagstone boundary · water surface.

**The count is lopsided on purpose.** Across all thirteen ids I can identify fewer than
about a dozen distinct free-standing prop objects, but every single building surface
carries three or four paint layers (base material, coursing, damage, growth). **FFT's town
looks dressed because its surfaces are dressed, not because its streets are full.**

## 5. Rooftops and balconies — what the corpus actually supports

- **A connected roof chain exists** in 42 (view 3), 47 (view 2) and 48: adjacent roofs
  touch and overlap so that the roofscape reads as one continuous stepped surface.
  Whether FFT lets units *walk* on them is not something these images can prove — the
  five-view renders show no unit tokens and I did not run the game.
- **Flat roof decks with parapets exist** — 22 has a flat-roofed block with a low parapet at
  the rear, and 91 Thieves Fort (Camp-Study lane 4 §3.3) is a whole map of stacked flat
  blocks. So the flat-deck roof is native to FFT's town vocabulary.
- **Projecting galleries/balconies exist** at 27 Goland — a covered timber gallery
  oversailing the street on brackets. This is the only clear balcony in the ids I read.
- **The walkable wall top is proven** at 25 Yardow (rampart with stairs from inside) and,
  per Monastery-Study lane 5 §5, at 63 Golgorand.

**Honest limit:** the corpus supports the *existence* of a roof chain, a flat deck, and a
gallery in FFT's town vocabulary. It does not, from these images, prove FFT stages combat
on them.

## 6. The market fixture, as FFT builds it

Warjilis (42) is the only unambiguous market in the ids I read, and it is worth stating
precisely because the banked seed is a market hall.

- The stalls are **open timber trays** — a boxed frame, waist-height, with produce visible
  as coloured mass (green vegetables, red fruit) filling the top face. In plan view the
  tray reads as a small square of pure colour framed in brown. That is a very cheap, very
  legible object.
- The trays **stand against building walls**, in the pockets and aprons between masses.
  They do not stand free in the middle of an open space.
- There is **no market hall** in the corpus — no open-sided civic structure over a trading
  floor. FFT's markets are stalls in leftover space.
- Zarghidas (47) adds a **lean-to stall box** projecting from a wall (a small shed-roofed
  timber cabinet) and a large **cloth canopy** over part of the upper terrace.
- **A well exists** at 99 Main Street of Lesalia — a round stone kerb in a paved forecourt.
  **No fountain appears anywhere in the ids I read.**

## 7. Anti-rules — what FFT never does in a town

1. **Never a full grid of streets.** No map shows an intersection of two formal streets.
   FFT shows one route, occasionally forking, never a network.
2. **Never a uniform frontage.** No run of identical units; height and roof direction always
   change.
3. **Never a filled street.** No map buries its ground plane in props.
4. **Never a free-standing object in the middle of an open urban space** in this cohort —
   the well at 99 sits in a corner forecourt, the trays at 42 hug walls, the ship at 43 is
   the site. (Compare 63 Golgorand and 100 Public Cemetery, where a central object *is* the
   grammar — those are institutional/civic yards, not streets.)
5. **Never a slope without a wall or a stair.** Every level change in 27, 44, 47 and 35 is
   held by a masonry face and crossed by a built stair. There is no graded urban ramp.
6. **Never an unowned light.** I saw no free-standing street lamp anywhere in the cohort;
   the only lights I could identify were the hearth fire and a counter light inside 96 Pub.

## Findings (plain English)

1. **FFT's town map is mostly ground.** Main Street of Lesalia is roughly half building
   roofs and half open paving, and the paving carries a tree, a well, a bench and nothing
   else. The urban tray does not have to be full to look like a town.
2. **The identity is all in one continuous frontage.** FFT buys "this is a city" with a
   single unbroken run of shared-wall buildings whose heights and roof directions change
   every unit. That one alternation does most of the work.
3. **The frontage bay is simple**: one wide opening at ground level and one or two windows
   above. Repeat it, vary the height, and the street wall exists.
4. **The terrain-street deck is the deck FFT actually builds.** Four separate trade cities
   put a level paved street above a lower lane, held by a masonry wall and joined by a
   stair. Adam's "San Francisco" instinct has more FFT support than the roofline or the
   balcony does.
5. **Material change reads the level before geometry does.** At Zarghidas the upper terrace
   is dressed pale flagstone and the lower lane is bare dirt. You know which is the good
   street before you register the height.
6. **FFT has no market hall and no fountain.** Its markets are open timber produce trays
   standing against walls, and its one water object is a plain well. The banked seed is
   inventing rather than inheriting here, and should be argued from history, not from FFT.
7. **The stall is legible because of what is IN it.** The produce tray reads at plan scale
   as a square of pure colour in a brown frame. Goods, not carpentry, make the stall.
8. **FFT spends its dressing budget on surfaces, not objects.** Fewer than a dozen distinct
   props across thirteen town maps — but every wall carries base material, coursing,
   damage and growth. The town looks rich because the walls do.
9. **Every level change is walled and stair-bound.** There is no soft urban slope anywhere.
   If the ground drops, masonry holds it and a built stair crosses it.
10. **The town gate is plain masonry plus hanging cloth.** Lesalia's great gate spends its
    ornament on one red heraldic banner over a completely plain ashlar wall — the same
    finding the Monastery-Study reached at Golgorand, arriving from the town side.
11. **A single repeated prop can name a town.** Goug is ordinary timber-frame buildings plus
    three windmills, and it reads as "the machine city" instantly. That is the cheapest
    occupancy-swappable identity lever in the corpus.
12. **The interior prop census is generous where the exterior is spare.** Map 96's pub
    interior alone carries counter, shelving, barrels, crates of produce, a big ceramic
    jar, tables, benches, stools, hanging cloth, a hearth fire, a floor mat and a mezzanine.
    FFT saves its object budget for rooms you enter.

## Declared gaps

- **The classification is name-primed for the maps I did not open.** 108 of 121 ids were
  classified from `map.c` alone. An urban street hiding inside a castle-named id (the
  "Office of…" and "Inside of…" sets) is a real residual risk.
- **I read 21 images across 13 ids.** Only four ids (22, 47, 99, 27) were read in three or
  more views. Anything said about a map's unseen side is unverified.
- **No measurement of any kind.** Every "storey", "half the plate", "waist-height" and
  "2–3 storeys" here is an eyeball read off 280 × 420 px renders upscaled to 800 px. No
  tile counts, no elevations. If a number becomes load-bearing it must come from a map
  inspection tool, not from these images.
- **I could not determine what is walkable.** The five-view renders show terrain and
  architecture with no unit tokens and no height data. Every statement about rooftops,
  galleries and wall walks in this lane is about what is *built*, not about what FFT lets
  you stand on.
- **Snow defeats the plan view at Goland (27).** View 0 is close to a whiteout; the hillside
  reading rests on views 1 and 2.
- **I did not re-open slums 32 and 40**, and I did not re-derive any camp-study or
  monastery-study classification. If either of those is wrong, this lane inherits the error.
- **No cross-check against the guard-post study's own cohort overview** in the study
  archive, which may already contain urban readings. This lane worked from the images.
