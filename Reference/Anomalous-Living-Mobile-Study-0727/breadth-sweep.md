# BREADTH SWEEP — "what else could Site 12 be?"

type: research-lane

lane: 1 of 3 (breadth sweep · image lane · FFT cohort)

## The question this lane answers

The campaign began by describing Site 12 as the **substrate/ownership stress case**. The
2026-07-28 adversarial review narrows that label: Site 12 proves `SubstratePlan` plus
terminal transformation; ownership/tenure is only one optional axis. The catalog's original
shopping note was one line:
"exotic · smallest structural buy · organic/impossible connectors; biggest skin buy"
(`STRUCTURE-KIT-CATALOG.md` vernacular matrix).

That line is a *palette* answer. It quietly assumes the site is a skinning problem — that
Site 12 is Site 7 with a weirder texture. This lane exists to test that assumption before a
spec inherits it.

The sweep asks: **if you set aside "a hive" and "a sky-whale," what other real things have
a ground that is alive, moving, or wrong — and what do they have in common?**

## The headline finding

Six substrate families exist, not one. **Four of the six are entirely mundane.** Living,
moving, and unreliable ground is ordinary human building practice — reed islands, stilt
towns, boat mills, cart camps, tidal causeways, bog paths, grown bridges. The exotic
register is one family out of six, and it sits on top of the same relational grammar as
the other five.

That reframes the whole site. Site 12 is not "the weird one." It is **the axis that runs
from a plank over a bog to a market on a living god**, and the reason it deserves a number
is that the axis has to hold together across that whole range without switching generators.

## The six substrate families

Each family is stated as: what the ground actually is · the real cases · what it forces the
generator to own · which Genesis rolls already point at it.

---

### A. Vegetal / floating mat — the ground is a raft of plants

**Real cases.** Uros reed islands on Lake Titicaca; Nordic and British quaking bogs
(*schwingmoor*, *gungfly*); Tonlé Sap and Ha Long floating villages; Iraqi marsh *tuhul*
platforms; log-drive rafts.

**Measured facts worth keeping.**
- Uros islands are built from stacked totora root blocks **1–2 m thick**, anchored to the
  lakebed with eucalyptus stakes and rope, each island roughly **50 ft × 50 ft**; fresh
  reed is added on top on a **two-week to eight-month** cadence because the bottom rots,
  and after **~25–30 years** the whole island is abandoned to sink and a new one is built.
  ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/travel/people-peru-live-manmade-islands-constructed-plants-180956218/),
  [Earthly Mission](https://earthlymission.com/uros-islands-lake-titicaca-uru-people-floating-reeds-totora/))
- A quaking-bog floating mat runs **0.5 m to several metres** thick over open water or
  semi-liquid peat and visibly undulates under a walker; nearby trees sway.
  ([Floating mat, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_mat),
  [Meres and Mosses](https://themeresandmosses.co.uk/2021/06/23/what-makes-a-bog-bounce/))
- Tonlé Sap's water level swings **up to 10 m** annually and the lake's area quintuples;
  pontoon houses on barrels or bamboo are tethered to poles driven into the bed so they
  rise and fall *in place*.
  ([Aqua Expeditions](https://www.aquaexpeditions.com/blog/tonle-sap-lake))

**What it forces the generator to own.** A substrate with a **maintenance clock** and a
**consumption cost**: the floor is a resource that decays and is renewed. A thickness
value that is also a load limit. An anchor system distinct from the floor itself. A
"trusted path" affordance (plank walkway) laid over untrusted ground.

**Where Genesis already points here.** `place-master-setting` d100 = 4 *Mire-End* (stilt
village, seasonal flood), 15 *The Drowned Port* (half the streets reachable only at low
tide — the **single most-rolled** TIYL world-origin, 3 of 12), 67 *The Sunken Belfry*.

### B. Vehicle / vessel deck — the ground is a made thing that travels

**Real cases.** Danube and Tisza boat mills; Mongol *ger tereg* cart-dwellings; canal and
river barge families; Aberdeen/Tanka boat communities; timber raft crews; caravan and
circus trains; drifting Arctic ice stations (a hybrid with family E).

**Measured facts worth keeping.**
- **4,301** boat mills were working on the Tisza and Danube in **1863**; the platform is
  moored at the strongest point of the current or to bridge piers, and *floats precisely so
  that output stays constant as the river's level changes*. Hungary's last one sank in the
  hard winter of 1968 when ice broke the hull.
  ([Low-tech Magazine](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/2010/11/boat-mills-water-powered-floating-factories/),
  [Ship mill, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_mill))
- William of Rubruck (1253–55) recorded cart-dwellings **up to 30 ft across** on carts
  **20 ft between wheel tracks**, drawn by **22 oxen** in two ranks of eleven, the axle "as
  large as a ship's mast."
  ([Yurt wagon, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yurt_wagon))
- Soviet drift station **NP-1** (1937–38): four men on a floe **3 × 5 km** and **>3 m**
  thick, which carried them **2,850 km** in nine months before break-up forced an
  icebreaker evacuation.
  ([North Pole-1, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole-1))

**What it forces the generator to own.** A **portal that is a prop** — gangway, ramp,
mooring line — which can be removed, cut, or drawn up, turning the site into an island. A
**deck datum** set by the vehicle, not the terrain (wheel radius, freeboard, hull depth), so
every edge has a step. A **motive system** with its own failure modes (the mooring, the
team, the current, the ice). And the fact that the site's *relationship to the world*
changes while the site itself does not.

**Where Genesis already points here.** `place-master-setting` d100 = 90 *The Dorsal Market*
(Strange band — a market on a flying whale's spine, one of the twelve TIYL starts);
64 *The Ice-Road Town* (Grounded — a settlement that exists only while the lake is frozen).

### C. Living body — the ground is an organism

**Real cases.** Whale-back epibiont communities; coral reefs as inhabited structure;
termite mounds and social-insect nests; Meghalaya living root bridges; grafted/pleached
living architecture; whale falls (a body as a succession of habitats after death).

**Measured facts worth keeping.**
- A single humpback can carry up to **450 kg (~1,000 lb)** of barnacles. Whale barnacles
  are **host-specific** species (*Coronula diadema* on humpbacks, *Cryptolepas
  rhachianecti* on grays), and their wall plates contain chambers **into which prongs of
  whale skin grow upward** — the anchor is a joint made *with* the living host. Whale lice
  then shelter in the lee of the barnacle clusters.
  ([Hakai](https://hakaimagazine.com/features/what-whale-barnacles-know/),
  [Scienceline](https://scienceline.org/2010/03/how-do-barnacles-attach-to-whales/))
- Khasi living root bridges are grown over **15–30 years**, span **2 – 52.7 m**, carry
  **~50 people** at once, **gain** load capacity with age through adaptive secondary growth
  and inosculation, and are estimated to last **~500 years**.
  ([Nature Sci. Rep.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-019-48652-w),
  [Meghalaya Biodiversity Board](https://megbiodiversity.nic.in/living-root-bridges))

**What it forces the generator to own.** **Anchor-into-host** as a construction type: you
do not found on a living substrate, you *join* to it, and the host grows around the joint.
A **host state** the site cannot control (the whale sounds, the tree drops a limb, the
colony swarms). An **occupancy history written as scars** — the living substrate carries the
marks of everyone who was anchored there before, and there are far more scars than
occupants. And, crucially, the root-bridge case proves the register can be **maintained,
mundane infrastructure** rather than horror.

**Where Genesis already points here.** `dungeon-type` d100 = 100 *The Living Hive*
("Biological growth / Nest — pulsing walls breathe in warm, claustrophobic rhythm");
`place-master-setting` 8 *Old Oak Wharf* (Grounded — a trading post built entirely from one
gargantuan fallen tree: a *dead* body as substrate) and 98 *The Living Tapestry* (Volatile —
buildings woven from sentient self-repairing silk).

### D. Wrong / reactive mineral ground — the ground is stone behaving badly

**Real cases.** Vitrified hillforts; Centralia and other underground coal-seam fires;
karst sinkhole subsidence and mine run-ins; Lake Peigneur's drain-out; lava crusts and
lava lakes; salt-pan crusts; permafrost thaw slumps.

**Measured facts worth keeping.**
- **~60** vitrified forts are known in Scotland (**~3%** of its hillforts). The mechanism is
  mundane and buildable: timber-laced rubble ramparts, set alight, burn in a kiln-like
  enclosure and reach **1000–1250 °C**, partially melting the stone into a glassy phase.
  ([Vitrified fort, Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitrified_fort),
  [EGU GeoLog](https://blogs.egu.eu/geolog/2016/05/25/great-walls-of-fire-vitrification-and-thermal-engineering-in-the-british-iron-age/))
- Centralia, PA: burning since **May 1962**; Route 61 dropped **eight feet**; steam vented
  from pavement cracks; vent pipes installed 1980; surface soil temperatures recorded
  **beyond 80 °C** against a normal 16–20 °C; the borough was condemned by eminent domain
  in **1992** and its ZIP code discontinued in **2002**.
  ([Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/fire-in-the-hole-77895126/),
  [Lions Talk Science, Penn State](https://lions-talk-science.org/2023/11/29/i-fell-into-a-burning-ring-of-fire-the-story-of-centralia-a-town-on-fire/))

**What it forces the generator to own.** A **crust/subgrade split** — the wrong material is
usually a *layer*, with ordinary ground beneath it, which makes breaching it a lever. A
**perimeter** — real anomalous ground is a patch inside normal ground with a legible halo,
not a whole biome. A **slow clock measured in decades**, not a boss timer. And a **cause**
that a player can find, because Centralia's cause is a fire and the vitrified fort's cause
is a bonfire.

**Where Genesis already points here.** `place-master-setting` 87 *The Shimmering Maw*
(Strange — a village suspended by chains over a crater of fused multicolour glass) and 99
*The Fracture-Market* (Volatile — a rift widening a hand-span per year, the oldest families
owning the bridging platforms); `dungeon-type` 98–99 *The Megastructure*
("Anomaly / Ancient technology — non-euclidean walls shift in faint mechanical rhythm").

### E. Cyclic ground — the ground exists only part of the time

**Real cases.** Tidal causeways (Lindisfarne, Mont-Saint-Michel, Passage du Gois); tidal
flats and wadden crossings; seasonal ice roads and frozen-lake settlements; monsoon
floodplains; ephemeral salt-lake crusts; reservoir drawdown zones.

**What it forces the generator to own.** A **schedule that is public**, so plans can be made
against it. A **marker system** that encodes the clock in geometry (the causeway's refuge
posts). Two committed floors — *high state* and *low state* — with the same ids and the
same portals, so the site is one place in two conditions rather than two maps.

**Where Genesis already points here.** `place-master-setting` 15 *The Drowned Port*
(Grounded, most-rolled TIYL origin), 64 *The Ice-Road Town* (Grounded), 93
*The Tide-That-Stopped* (Strange — a wave arrested mid-break, its frozen water forming the
walls, bridges, and cathedral ceiling). The marathon's whole Sella arc — drowning rooms,
failing spans, a flooding row read "like a cheat's-eye view of a card table" — is family E
with no site to live in (`intel/marathon-spatial-mining.md` §4 gap 2).

### F. Self-willed ground — the ground has an intention

**Real cases.** *None.* This family has no real-world referent and must not pretend to
one. Its honest anchors are (i) the *phenomenology* of families A–E — ground that betrays,
consumes, remembers, or refuses — and (ii) the folk explanations real communities attach to
D and E: the bog that "takes," the mountain that "is owed."

**What it forces the generator to own.** A **will expressed only through the other five
families' mechanics** — the site's intention is legible as a change in the schedule, the
anchors, the crust, or the host state, never as a floating cursor of malice. A **bargain
surface**: something the ground wants and something it will trade. A **memory**: it treats a
returning player differently from a first-time one.

**Where Genesis already points here.** This is the `Making It Real` chain in
`Engine/03. _Tables/01. World Building/Starting State/Starting State - Pressures.md` —
**The Becoming** (d8), **The Intrusion** (d6 + d4 manifestation), and **The Buried
Power** rows 1/2/8. See `engine-evidence.md`: this door is **LIVE**, fires at world
genesis, and is far and away the largest real source of Site-12 demand in the engine.

## What the sweep changed

1. **Site 12 is an axis, not a mood.** Six families, four mundane. The catalog's "exotic
   vernacular · biggest skin buy" line is true only of family F and half of D. Families
   A, B, C, and E are *timber, rope, plank, piling, and stone* — the cheapest structural
   buy in the portfolio, reusing Camp (2), Mine (5), and Urban (10) kit almost wholesale.

2. **Every family shares one relational grammar** — see `synthesis.md`. Substrate ·
   anchor · trusted path · datum · clock · perimeter · host-state. That shared grammar is
   what makes one generator legitimate across the whole axis.

3. **The mundane end is where the demand is.** The most-rolled TIYL world-origin in the
   whole batch (Drowned Port ×3) is family E. The census's "Living Hive 1.2%" is family C
   only, and it is the *smallest* of the six doors.

4. **"Living" and "mobile" are not the same problem and should not share a rung.** A
   living substrate has a *host state* the site cannot control. A mobile substrate has a
   *removable portal* and a *deck datum*. They compose (the Dorsal Market is both) but
   they are separate mechanisms, and a spec that treats them as one word will build
   neither.

## Declared gaps in this lane

- **No measured drawings.** Every dimension quoted above comes from prose sources, not
  from a survey drawing I read. A later pass should obtain at least one measured section
  (a boat-mill hull, a stilt-house piling grid, a reed-island core) before any grid
  hypothesis in the spec is promoted past PROPOSED.
- **Family F has no evidence and cannot get any.** Its treatment in the spec is derived
  from the engine's own live tables and from families A–E, and is labelled as such.
- **No non-European mobile-settlement source beyond the Mongol cart and Tonlé Sap.**
  Sea-nomad (Bajau/Moken/Orang Laut) and Arctic sea-ice settlement practice are named but
  not sourced here; both are likely to change the family-B and family-E cards.
- **No acoustic/haptic evidence.** Every family in this sweep is a *felt* register (the
  bog quakes, the deck rolls, the hull works, the whale sounds). Nothing in this packet
  addresses how that reaches a player through a fixed-camera diorama; that is a real and
  unaddressed problem, flagged in the spec's remaining-evidence section.
