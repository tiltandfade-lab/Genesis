## Urban Art Motif — d20

| d20 | Motif | What it looks like (and what it’s good for) |
|---:|-------|----------------------------------------------|
| 1  | **Boundary Lines & Ward Marks** | Painted lines, brass studs, chalk sigils at thresholds. Misalignment = hidden access, bribed passage, or secret route. |
| 2  | **Civic Processions** | Reliefs/posters of parades, rites, marches. One figure’s gesture points to a latch, alley, or meeting spot. |
| 3  | **Beast Signage** | Shop signs with local animals (boar, gull, eel). Foreshadows turf, guards, or hazards tied to that “house.” |
| 4  | **Knots & Rigging** | Rope knots on piers, cranes, awnings. Knot pattern encodes a simple signal or timing clue. |
| 5  | **Street Map Fragment** | Old ward map, faded route lines, sewer diagram. Missing section implies a hidden cut-through or underworks entry. |
| 6  | **Tools of the Trade** | Murals of hammers, keys, scales, quills. Tool implies the interaction method (lever, lock, ledger, stamp). |
| 7  | **Bell & Lantern Code** | Bell symbols, lantern colors, window shutters. Code hints patrol timing or safe approach. |
| 8  | **Public Tallies** | Chalk counts, strike marks, ration boards. Fresh marks = recent activity, shortages, or heat in the area. |
| 9  | **Ordinance & Prohibition** | Warning placards, “NO ENTRY,” fines, taboo icons. The forbidden side often hides shortcuts or contraband. |
| 10 | **Chains, Locks, & Seals** | Wax seals, lock plates, padlocks, chains. Keyhole motif signals a false lock, real latch, or “official” bypass. |
| 11 | **Masks & Faces** | Festival masks, carved faces, theater motifs. A missing/defaced face hides a compartment, note, or key marker. |
| 12 | **Mirror Glass & Brass** | Polished windows, brass trim, reflective tiles. Reflections reveal tails, hidden writing, or seams in a wall. |
| 13 | **Overpaint & Graffiti Layers** | Old signage under new paint, tags over murals. Scraped layer reveals older route, faction mark, or true name. |
| 14 | **Trade Stamps & Brands** | Cargo marks, guild stamps, registry seals. Points to a smuggler cache, counting house, or underworks link. |
| 15 | **Vines & Ironwork** | Decorative vines, wrought spirals, trellis patterns. The “vine line” leads to a hinge, loose bar, or concealed slit. |
| 16 | **Water & Drain Lines** | Salt bloom, drip stains, runoff paths. Reveals drainage logic, hidden voids, or a culvert entrance. |
| 17 | **Mildew & Mold Rosettes** | Bloom patterns on walls/cloth. Growth indicates airflow from a hidden room or tunnel. |
| 18 | **Fire Scars & Glass Veins** | Burn marks, heat-cracked stone, melted glass streaks. Veins “point” toward vents, weak walls, or sealed doors. |
| 19 | **Old Laws Under New Signs** | Older plaques overwritten by newer edicts. The old layer reveals original function, owner, or hidden exemption. |
| 20 | **The City’s Bruise** | Repeating dark stain bands on stone (soot, purple lichen, alchemical spill). Where it stops marks a boundary, ward line, or “safe” threshold. |
