# Lane 1 — The breadth sweep: what else could "contested fortress" be?

## The question, stated honestly

The name *Contested Fortress* invites one image — an army outside, defenders inside, ladders
on the wall. If that image is wrong, or is only one case among many, the whole site gets
built to serve a minority of the demand. This lane enumerates the design space before
anything is designed, then keeps only what survives the ontology test.

The ontology test is guidelines step 0.5 plus `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` §2's hard
tell: **ask what persists when the state ends.** If the place goes back to being itself, the
reading was a *state* (a transform, bin c) and does not belong to Site 9's own identity. If
the place could not exist without the reading, it is structural.

Site 9's classification is already fixed by `GOLDEN-SITE-ONTOLOGY-ENGINE-MARRIAGE.md` §6:
**scale/relationship stress case**, primary proof *"large defense plus at least two
populations and active fronts."* So a candidate reading only earns a place if it stresses
**scale**, **relationship**, or both. A reading that merely dresses a fortress differently
belongs to the host vernacular (Site 1's military-masonry family) and to the transform
stack, not to Site 9.

---

## The twelve candidate readings

### 1. Active assault — the ladders-on-the-wall siege

The default image. Attackers outside a continuous defensive envelope, defenders on it.

**Test.** When the assault ends, the fortress is a fortress again. This is a *state* over a
military-masonry host. It also fails the Genesis premise badly: a party of adventurers is
not an army, and a level whose premise is "two armies fight" makes the player a spectator.

**Verdict: REJECT as the site's identity.** Retain as one licensed occupancy/condition state
(and note that `Urban Segment Approach` row 18, *The Fortress-Prison Siege Line*, already
rolls exactly this as an *approach* — an obstacle to get through, not a level to inhabit).

### 2. Split occupation — attackers hold the outer ward, defenders hold the inner

Historically the normal *outcome* of an assault on a multi-ward castle, because the
architecture is built to produce it. Multi-ward castles nest their baileys deliberately so
that a breach of the outer defences leaves the garrison a further line: the outer or lower
bailey holds the garrison and its ancillary buildings, the inner ward holds the hall and the
lord's quarters, and if the outer works fall the defence continues from the keep
([Wikipedia — Outer bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_bailey);
[Wikipedia — Inner bailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_bailey);
[Exploring Castles — Concentric castles](https://www.exploring-castles.com/castle_designs/concentric_castles/)).

**Test.** The reading requires two enclosures, a front between them, and two populations
with attributed ground. Remove any of those and the site is a Guard Post at odd scale. It is
structural.

**Verdict: KEEP — this is the core reading.** It is simultaneously a scale case (two
enceintes exceed one tactical window) and a relationship case (two populations, one front).
Crucially, the architecture **pre-draws the fronts**: the fallback line is a design
intention, not an accident of combat.

### 3. Cohabitation — the works outlived the war and someone else moved in

A fortress whose military purpose has lapsed while its walls have not, now sheltering a
population that did not build it. Historically ordinary: fortified towns and refuge
enclosures existed precisely to hold a population that was not the garrison
([Wikipedia — Refuge castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refuge_castle)).

**Test.** Nothing "ends" and restores this. The relationship *is* the place. A hamlet paying
rent to an absent lord for the right to live inside his curtain wall is not a fortress in a
state; it is a different kind of settlement.

**Verdict: KEEP — and the roller audit (lane 4) shows this is the *dominant* live
expression**, not a curiosity. It stresses relationship without requiring violence, which
matters enormously for a game whose census says most play is not a pitched battle.

### 4. Condominium — two powers garrison the same works by treaty

Joint occupancy under agreement: two claimants, both legitimate, both armed, sharing gates,
stores and schedules.

**Test.** This is `LayeredControl`'s predicate almost word for word — two persistent
simultaneous claims with a consequential difference in authority, access, custody or
taxation (`GOLDEN-SITE-ONTOLOGY-ENGINE-MARRIAGE.md` §5.1).

**Verdict: REJECT as Site 9's own identity; it is Site 8 applied to a fortress host.** Retain
as a compose case — and it is a good one, because Site 8 must prove itself on at least two
unlike hosts and a fortress is maximally unlike a tavern.

### 5. Mutiny — the garrison splits against itself

One population divides; the front runs through the barracks.

**Test.** Same as 4: it is layered control, then open conflict, over one host. Its interest
is entirely in *who* rather than *where*.

**Verdict: REJECT as identity; KEEP as an occupancy card** that can produce reading 2's
geometry without an external attacker.

### 6. Refuge — the countryside is inside and the garrison is outnumbered

A refuge enclosure is not permanently occupied; it is a rampart-girt retreat the local
population runs to, large enough to hold their stores and their animals, and it exists for
exactly the days it is needed ([Wikipedia — Refuge castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refuge_castle)).
The livestock question is not decorative: at Kerak, besiegers ate the townspeople's animals
that had been left in the ditch ([Wikipedia — Siege of Kerak (1183)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kerak_(1183))).

**Test.** Two populations with genuinely different obligations — one defends, one shelters,
and the second is *bigger*. Nothing about the geometry restores itself when the crisis ends;
the enclosure is sized for the crowd it is meant to hold.

**Verdict: KEEP as a first-class variant.** It supplies a non-combat contest — space, water,
food, sanitation, authority — over the same works, and it makes the honest-empty-space law
load-bearing rather than aesthetic: the empty ward exists *because* it must one day be full.

### 7. Fortified town — the enceinte contains a civilian settlement

Walled towns garrisoned and provisioned to hold both a garrison and a refugee population
were a deliberate policy instrument, not an accident.

**Test.** Two vernaculars in one envelope (military masonry outside, domestic fabric inside)
and two populations by design. Structural. But note it overlaps Site 10's urban fabric.

**Verdict: KEEP with a boundary.** The wall and its fighting surfaces belong to Site 9; the
streets, frontages and market inside belong to Site 10. Site 9 owns the *envelope and its
relationship to what it encloses* — not a second town generator.

### 8. Under construction — contested before finished

Works half-built, scaffolding, an incomplete circuit with real gaps.

**Verdict: REJECT as identity; KEEP as a condition card.** It is a construction state, and a
cheap and excellent one, because an unfinished circuit is a *legible* second route.

### 9. Undermined — the siege goes underground

Sapping and countermining; sally ports in concentric works were tunnelled between inner and
outer walls specifically so the garrison could get at enemy sappers
([Wikipedia — Sally port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_port)).

**Verdict: KEEP as a licensed sub-relation, not as identity.** Its value is that it is a
legitimate seam to Site 5's excavated vernacular and to the dungeon walk, which — per lane 4
— is where most of the demand actually arrives. A fortress with a mine gallery under it is
one site expressed through two walk families.

### 10. Slighted — deliberately destroyed so it can never be held again

Not decay: masonry mined and dropped on purpose, producing split, leaning wall masses rather
than tumbled rubble (see `images/e2-corfe-keep-slighted.jpg`).

**Verdict: REJECT as identity; KEEP as the degradation path,** and note it is the cleanest
available boundary with Site 3. A slighted fortress that nobody claims is Site 3. A slighted
fortress that two parties still want is Site 9 with damage applied.

### 11. Gate-as-fortress — the whole site compressed into one gatehouse

A gatehouse is a contested fortress folded into one building: a barbican projecting forward
with its own walls, loops and portcullises funnels attackers into a confined killing ground
before they reach a second identical set at the gatehouse proper, under enfilading fire from
loops and murder holes overhead
([Exploring Castles — Gatehouse](https://www.exploring-castles.com/castle_designs/gatehouse/);
[Castles Catalog — Murder hole](https://castlescatalog.com/glossary/murder-hole/)).

**Verdict: KEEP — as the small rung of the ladder.** This is how Site 9 reaches down toward
Site 1 without becoming it: the same sequence, one building, no second population required
yet.

### 12. Non-masonry expressions — hillfort, pā, earthwork, timber

Multivallate hillforts follow the contours with successive banks and ditches
([Wikipedia — Hillfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillfort)); Māori pā use palisades,
banks, ditches and defensive terracing on headlands and hilltops, and — unlike European
hillforts — routinely incorporate food storage pits and a water source inside the defended
area ([Wikipedia — Pā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%81);
[Ruapekapeka — pre-musket pā](https://www.ruapekapeka.co.nz/maori-warfare/pre-musket-maori-pa/)).

**Verdict: KEEP as the cultural axis, not as separate readings.** The pā finding is
load-bearing for Genesis: *storage and water inside the defended area* is the thing that
makes a two-population fortress survivable, and it is a rollable functional requirement
rather than a style. It also directly supports the refuge reading (6).

## The distinction from Site 8, stated plainly

Site 8 asks **"who really controls this?"** — its currency is recognition, concealment,
knowledge and permission, and its signature structural buy is concealed pieces (false wall,
trapdoor). Its tension survives at any map size, and it can be entirely latent.

Site 9 asks **"who holds which ground, right now?"** — its currency is attributed territory,
a visible front, and a defensive envelope that both sides can read. Nobody is hiding. Its
tension *requires* enough materialised space to have two sides of something.

The clean test: **remove the concealment and Site 8 collapses; remove the second population's
ground and Site 9 collapses.** A besieged castle with a traitor in the gatehouse is both, and
that is a compose the engine should support — not evidence that the two are the same case.

## Declared gaps in this lane

- All sources are general-audience secondary literature (encyclopaedia and enthusiast sites).
  They are adequate for enumerating the design space and inadequate for any quantitative
  claim. **No number from this lane should reach a spec.**
- The non-European sweep returned usable material on hillforts and pā and returned
  **nothing** on brochs, dzongs or kasbahs. Those remain unexamined, and the cultural axis is
  therefore thinner than it should be.
- Nothing here is primary archaeology, and no site plan was measured. Every spatial statement
  in this lane is relational, not dimensional.
