### Dungeon Lore Art (d30)

Roll the same number as **Dungeon Lore Content**. The art physically depicts the lore entry at that number.

*The medium varies. The content is always the revelation.*

---

**Origin (1–5)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 1 | A carved diagram of the room's walls, rendered from the inside — arrows pointing inward, not out. Not a map of the dungeon. A blueprint of containment. |
| 2 | A frieze showing two processions — one older and beneath, one newer on top — moving in opposite directions toward the same altar. |
| 3 | A single enormous panel depicting one frozen moment: figures mid-action around an object or threshold. No before. No after. Just this. |
| 4 | Carvings of figures clearly identifiable as the current occupants — by their tools and garb — standing before these walls in unmistakable awe. They look like arrivals, not builders. |
| 5 | A surface once covered in writing, now completely smooth. The effort of erasure is more visible than any image ever was. |

**The Threat's True Nature (6–10)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 6 | A layered fresco: the oldest layer shows something entirely different here — different figures, different purpose. The current occupants painted over it. Not well enough. |
| 7 | A portrait series of the dungeon's occupants, each rendered looking in the same direction — not at the viewer, not at each other. At something behind the artist. |
| 8 | A ledger-style inscription, column after column of notation, depicting transactions or tallies that bear no relationship to the dungeon's apparent function. |
| 9 | A frieze that stops mid-scene. The figures approach a specific threshold and the carving simply ends. No artist's mark. No finish. |
| 10 | A sequence of portraits — different faces, different eras, different dress — all in identical postures of supplication before the same central figure, which does not change. |

**The Previous Cycle (11–15)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 11 | A painting of figures in this exact room, from this exact angle, clearly not the current occupants. They are doing what the party is doing. The painting is old. |
| 12 | A wall covered in marks at chest height — initials, dates, symbols — the accumulated record of everyone who reached this room. Several overlap. |
| 13 | A portrait of the current faction's leadership. Beneath the paint, older faces — same poses, different people. Someone has been repainting this wall for generations. |
| 14 | A triptych: left panel shows something being bound. Center panel shows it dormant. The right panel is blank — the third scene was never painted, or was removed. |
| 15 | A circular diagram divided into segments like a calendar. Most are marked. The party's arrival places them near the end of the current turn. |

**The Cost (16–20)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 16 | An instructional diagram — clinical, annotated, step by step — depicting a procedure that is clearly not mechanical. The annotations are in a careful, unhurried hand. |
| 17 | A series of portraits of the same figure at regular intervals. In each successive image, something is subtly less — detail, definition, presence. |
| 18 | A single portrait rendered with more care than anything else in this dungeon. Beneath it, an inscription in a personal hand. The figure is looking directly at whoever stands here. |
| 19 | A map — not of this dungeon, but of somewhere above ground. Specific locations are marked. The marks are recent. |
| 20 | A mechanism diagram showing a continuous process with no off switch. One component is annotated in a different hand: *already running.* |

**The Place Itself (21–25)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 21 | The oldest visible art in the dungeon — clearly predating everything else — depicting this exact chamber as it appeared then. The comparison to now is instructive. |
| 22 | A map of this dungeon that bears no resemblance to the dungeon the party has navigated. Either the map is wrong, or what the party is in is much smaller than the real thing. |
| 23 | Architectural sketches of the dungeon's walls, columns, and floors — but each sketch includes a figure that is not attached to the structure. It is part of it. |
| 24 | A diagram of a continuous process with no human figures anywhere in it. Everything depicted is structural or environmental. It is clearly still operating. |
| 25 | A long horizontal frieze spanning the full wall, divided into equal segments, each depicting a state of this dungeon. The last marked segment is close to the right end. |

**What's Coming (26–30)**

| d30 | Art |
|-----|-----|
| 26 | A depiction of an arrival — a single figure approaching from outside, rendered in a style completely unlike everything else here, as if drawn by a different hand entirely. |
| 27 | A threshold scene: figures on one side, something on the other, separated not by a wall but by a line. The line is annotated with a single word or symbol. |
| 28 | The same scene depicted twice, side by side — first the current occupants at work, then what follows. Apparently painted by the same hand, at the same time. |
| 29 | A painting of the entrance to this dungeon from outside, depicting figures entering. The figures match the party's composition in a way that is not coincidental. |
| 30 | A single image covering the entire far wall: the dungeon as it will look when it is finished. Some elements match the current state. Others do not exist yet. |
