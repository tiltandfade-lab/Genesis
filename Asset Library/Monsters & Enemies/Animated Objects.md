*p. 16 MM 2024*

Animated objects are crafted with potent magic to follow the commands of their creators. When not commanded, they follow the last order they received to the best of their ability, and can act independently to fulfill simple instructions. Some animated objects — including many created in the Feywild — might converse fluently or adopt a persona, but most are simple automatons.

**Constructed Nature:** An animated object doesn't require air, food, drink, or sleep. The magic animating it is dispelled when it drops to 0 hit points. An animated object reduced to 0 hit points becomes inanimate and is too damaged to be of further use.

Animated Armor is the most common form encountered in dungeons and estates: an empty steel shell that clanks and grinds as it moves, scripted with rudimentary speech to demand passwords or deliver warnings. The Flying Sword is the most aggressive — a weapon that fights with the confidence of a warrior that can't be injured. The Rug of Smothering is the most insidious, disguised as ordinary furnishing until it silently rolls a victim to death.

_Medium Construct, Unaligned_

- **Armor Class:** 18 (natural armor)
- **Hit Points:** 33 (6d8 + 6)
- **Speed:** 25 ft.
- **STR:** 14 (+2) | **DEX:** 11 (+0) | **CON:** 13 (+1) | **INT:** 1 (-5) | **WIS:** 3 (-4) | **CHA:** 1 (-5)
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison, psychic
- **Condition Immunities:** Blinded, charmed, deafened, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned
- **Senses:** Blindsight 60 ft. (blind beyond this radius), passive Perception 6
- **Languages:** —
- **Challenge:** 1 (200 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Antimagic Susceptibility:** The armor is Incapacitated while in the area of an *antimagic field*. If targeted by *dispel magic*, the armor must succeed on a Constitution saving throw against the caster's spell save DC or fall Unconscious for 1 minute.

**Actions**

- **Multiattack:** The armor makes two Slam attacks.
- **Slam:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +4 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 5 (1d6 + 2) bludgeoning damage.

_Large Construct, Unaligned_

- **Armor Class:** 12
- **Hit Points:** 27 (5d10)
- **Speed:** 10 ft.
- **STR:** 17 (+3) | **DEX:** 14 (+2) | **CON:** 10 (+0) | **INT:** 1 (-5) | **WIS:** 3 (-4) | **CHA:** 1 (-5)
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison, psychic
- **Condition Immunities:** Charmed, deafened, exhaustion, frightened, paralyzed, petrified, poisoned
- **Senses:** Blindsight 60 ft. (blind beyond this radius), passive Perception 6
- **Languages:** —
- **Challenge:** 2 (450 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Antimagic Susceptibility:** As Animated Armor.
- **False Appearance:** While motionless, the rug is indistinguishable from a normal rug.

**Actions**

- **Smother:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +5 to hit, reach 5 ft., one Medium or smaller creature. _Hit:_ 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage. If the target is a Medium or smaller creature, the rug can give it the Grappled condition (escape DC 13). Until the grapple ends, the target has the Blinded and Restrained conditions, is suffocating, and takes 10 (2d6 + 3) bludgeoning damage at the start of each of its turns. The rug can smother only one creature at a time. While grappling the target, the rug halves the damage it takes (rounded down), and the target takes the same amount of damage.

### d10 The Last Order (What Command Is It Following?)

Animated objects are only as sensible as the last instruction they received. The original owner may be dead, decades gone, or simply forgetful. Roll to determine what standing order the object is executing — often to absurd, dangerous, or tragic effect.

| d10 | The Last Command | Campaign Tone & Execution |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **1** | **"Let no one pass."** | **Classic Dungeon:** The straightforward guard command. It has been executing this order without deviation for 200 years. The room it's guarding has long since been looted. It does not know this. |
| **2** | **"Protect my research."** | **Investigation:** The armor protects a specific table, chest, or shelf. Whatever was on it is gone — but the armor's persistent guarding implies the original owner considered it extremely valuable. |
| **3** | **"Keep the fire burning."** | **Environmental / Absurd:** The flying sword has been attempting to cut firewood for a fireplace that has been cold for a century, aggressively attacking anything that comes near the hearth. |
| **4** | **"Bring it to me."** | **Chase:** The rug was commanded to retrieve a specific item — perhaps a letter, a ring, or a book — and has been silently, patiently stalking anyone carrying it for years. It does not stop. |
| **5** | **"No one touches the master."** | **Social Trap:** The armor was ordered to protect a specific person. That person is now dead and lying in a chair in the corner. The armor will violently defend the corpse from anyone who tries to move it. |
| **6** | **"Serve the guests."** | **Dark Comedy:** The rug has been commanded to "seat" guests. It enthusiastically attempts to roll itself around visitors and carry them to the dining table, interpreting its smothering attack as hospitality. |
| **7** | **"Attend the ceremony."** | **Atmospheric:** The armor stands at perfect attention in the middle of a ruined great hall, awaiting a ceremony that ended in massacre 300 years ago. It won't attack unless touched. It has been waiting here since then. |
| **8** | **"Kill any undead."** | **Ally (Temporary):** The armor is aggressively hunting the undead — including, possibly, a party member who has been affected by necrotic corruption or a temporary curse it interprets as undeath. |
| **9** | **"Play the music."** | **Fey / Unsettling:** The flying sword has been commanded to play a specific song on a specific instrument. The instrument is long destroyed. The sword has been attempting to replicate the tune by striking different metal surfaces in the dungeon, perfectly, forever. |
| **10** | **"Do not wake the master."** | **Horror:** The rug is posted outside a sealed chamber door. It silently and immediately smothers anyone who knocks, speaks loudly, or attempts to open the door. Whatever is inside has been sleeping undisturbed for a very long time. |

### d10 What the Object Guards (The Cache)

Animated objects are stationed by someone who wanted to protect something. That thing is still here. Roll to determine what justified the expense of a magical guardian.

| d10 | What's Protected | Investigation Hook |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **1** | **A locked iron strongbox** | Contains 300 gp and a deed to a property the party may now technically own. |
| **2** | **A fragile glass orb** | A *crystal ball* — cracked and barely functional. Produces distorted, flickering images of a location the party doesn't recognize. |
| **3** | **A plain leather journal** | The personal diary of a mage. The last entry discusses the cost of animating the armor and expresses deep regret about a decision they made. The entry cuts off mid-sentence. |
| **4** | **An antique weapon rack** | Empty. Whatever weapons were here were taken long ago. The armor was left behind when its master fled, and it has been guarding an empty rack ever since. |
| **5** | **A sealed letter** | Addressed to someone by name. That name belongs to a notable NPC the party knows — or a faction leader they're about to meet. |
| **6** | **A mirror** | The only mirror in the dungeon. A DC 13 Arcana check reveals it functions as a one-way scrying window into a specific room nearby, still active. |
| **7** | **Nothing at all** | Whatever was here has been gone for decades. The original owner probably died before they could give the order to stand down. |
| **8** | **A spell scroll** | *Animate Objects* — the only surviving copy of the specific ritual variant used to create this object. Worth significant gold to the right buyer. |
| **9** | **A preserved humanoid hand** | Wearing a heavy signet ring. The hand has been kept perfectly preserved by the same magic animating the room. The ring bears a noble's crest. |
| **10** | **Another animated object** | Still in its box. Inert. Awaiting a command that will never come — unless a PC speaks one. |
