# SITE 6 PRISON/CUSTODY — 2026-07-27 GAP-CLOSE SYNTHESIS

## 2026-07-28 adversarial disposition

`PR-DOC-10` remains a valid custody doctrine candidate. `PR-DOC-11` Ward-and-Surety is
classified as an external disposition/social-liability provider, not a custody doctrine:
it can guarantee appearance and consequences but cannot replace the controlled threshold,
property chain, holding, service, counterplay and release obligations. Care/asylum is likewise
a distinct care host unless actual prevented exit activates the full custody lifecycle.

## 1. What this pass was asked to do, and what it actually did

The campaign brief asked for two things: close the measured-breadth gap (cross-cultural
custody architecture, historical capacity/dimension data), and draft the next doctrine pair
beyond Keeper-House/Ledger-and-Shift. Both are done:

- `MEASURED-REFERENCES.md` adds four independent, dated, sourced custody traditions outside the
  original packet's Anglo-American/British evidence spine — Qing China (a measured 47,000 m²
  compound), Edo Japan (a measured ~8,700 m² jail with real capacity/staffing/classification
  figures and a translated-into-reach architectural floor plan), a matched pair of
  custody-without-architecture traditions (English frankpledge, Japanese gonin-gumi), and an
  honestly-recorded partial/negative finding for precolonial Africa and Mesoamerica.
- `DOCTRINE-PAIR-TWO.md` drafts `PR-DOC-10 — Yamen Magistrate Custody` and
  `PR-DOC-11 — Ward-and-Surety Custody` as a matched second comparison pair, built directly from
  that research, using the same card schema and comparison-receipt discipline the first pair
  used, and explicitly flags the one load-bearing question the pair raises against Site 6's own
  REQUIRED invariants (§4 of that file).

`FFT-COHORT-COMPARISON.md` and `BREADTH-SWEEP.md` were produced as well because they are load-
bearing parts of THE DEPTH LAW this campaign's brief invokes by name, not because the campaign
brief's two named asks required them directly.

## 2. Honest result

The accepted Site 6 promise, form-family table, ruled PR1/PR2/PR3 build order, and both existing
doctrine cards survive this pass unchanged. Four things are genuinely new:

1. **Two non-Anglo-American custody traditions now have real measured/quantified evidence**
   (yamen compound area and organization; Tenmachō jail area, walls, moat, capacity, staffing,
   classification, and a reachable architectural plan) where the original packet had only
   Fremantle/Robben Island/Australian-convict text citations for its non-European breadth — and
   those were themselves colonial institutions, not independent traditions.
2. **A second, structurally opposite matched doctrine pair now exists**: one card
   (`PR-DOC-10`) pushes custody *maximally toward* the seat of power (fused judicial/custodial/
   administrative/residential authority in one compound); the other (`PR-DOC-11`) pushes custody
   *maximally away from* any dedicated architecture at all (a distributed social/legal
   obligation with zero purpose-built footprint). Together they bracket the entire design space
   the first pair's two civic-jail variants sit inside the middle of.
3. **The invariant question was useful and is now resolved by adversarial review**:
   `PR-DOC-11` is an external disposition/social-liability provider, not a custody doctrine.
4. **The FFT gap is closed honestly, not favorably** — Site 6 gets the Camp study's "zero direct
   hits" result, not the Mine study's "a whole named cohort" result. Two real findings
   (Golgorand Execution Site for the disposition edge; Thieves Fort for the faction-seized-prison
   occupancy silhouette) exist, but no tactical cell-block cohort does, because FFT's 121-map
   corpus does not contain one. Reporting this as a full FFT closure would be dishonest; it is
   reported as a partial, targeted closure instead.
