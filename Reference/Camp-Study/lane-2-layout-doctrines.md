# Lane 2 — Camp layout doctrines

**The question.** Site 2's occupancy axis RULES three composition profiles — *military: ordered
rows, tent hierarchy, multiple towers* · *refugee/shanty: dense irregular, borrowed walls* ·
*bandit: screened from the route*. This lane asks whether real camp layout actually behaves that
way, and what the generator would have to hold to produce each profile.

---

## 1. The four doctrines the evidence actually shows

Looking across the images, camps do not vary along one "ordered ↔ disordered" axis. They vary along
**two independent axes**, and the RULED profiles sit at three corners of the resulting square.

- **Axis 1 — what the plan is FOR:** *ordered by a commander* (rows/grid) versus *ordered by the
  community itself* (ring/cluster) versus *ordered by the ground* (frontage/organic).
- **Axis 2 — what the camp faces:** *inward* (ring, defended centre) versus *outward* (frontage,
  addressing a route) versus *nothing* (grid, indifferent to context).

|  | inward | outward | indifferent |
|---|---|---|---|
| commander-ordered | Roman camp *(rows facing the intervallum void)* | — | modern refugee grid |
| community-ordered | tipi/ger ring | — | — |
| ground-ordered | bandit screen | waystation frontage · shanty street | shanty sprawl |

The camp brief's ring-vs-frontage distinction is therefore **the right primary axis** — the evidence
supports it as the structural split. What the evidence adds is that "ordered rows" is *not* a third
value on that axis: **a Roman camp is a ring doctrine too.** See §2.

## 2. Roman marching camp — the ordered grid is really a ring with a hollow rim

*Evidence: `l2-roman-fortress-plans.jpg` (Cybulski, "Castra Romana" — three camp plans, a vallum
reconstruction, a gate reconstruction, and vignettes of the troop tent, the timber tower, the
granary).*

What the plate shows, and what matters:

- **Playing-card outline** — a rectangle with rounded corners, four gates, one on each side.
- **Two cross-streets** — the *via principalis* (transverse) and the *via praetoria*, meeting at the
  centre; the *principia*/forum, *quaestorium* and commander's quarters sit at that crossing.
- **The intervallum** — a **clear ring of empty ground, ~200 ft wide, between the rampart and the
  nearest tents.** This is the single most transferable fact in the lane: the Roman camp's defining
  spatial gesture is *a reserved void*, not a wall. The tents form a dense field; the void is what
  makes the field read.
- **Ditch-and-bank** — *fossa* at least 3 ft deep / 5 ft wide, typically a 2:1 width-to-depth
  ratio; *agger* about 2.4 m wide × 1.8 m high, thrown up from the ditch spoil, topped with a
  stake palisade (*vallum*). In Genesis units: **a 1h-deep ditch and a 1h-high bank** — a two-piece
  GROUND assembly, exactly as the brief's deferred earthwork anticipates.
- **The timber tower vignette (*turris lignea*)** — a square lashed-timber tower with an open railed
  deck, standing on the palisade line. This is a direct historical warrant for the camp brief's
  **occupancy-licensed lashed watch platform** and for its count-scaling: the plate shows towers as
  a repeating element of the perimeter, not a singular landmark.
- **The troop tent vignette (*tentoria*)** — the papilio, see lane 1 §1.6.

**Consequence for the profile.** A generated "military encampment" that is only rows of tents is
missing the two things that make the reference read: **the reserved perimeter void** and **the
central cross-street with the command tent at its junction**. Both are negative-space facts, which
lines up exactly with FFT relational rule 7 ("quiet ground is active composition").

## 3. Military tent rows in practice — rows are a service diagram, not a grid

*Evidence: `l1-bell-fenton-rows-01.jpg`, `l1-bell-fenton-light-03.jpg`, `l2-fenton-naval-brigade.jpg`
(Crimea, 1855), `l2-cavalry-rows.jpg` (Fort Lawton, 1900).*

- Rows in the photographs are **loose, not gridded** — consistent spacing along the row, ragged
  between rows, and they bend to follow ground. The Naval Brigade camp curves along a shallow
  valley floor.
- **Every row has a service line in front of it.** At Fort Lawton the horse picket line runs the
  full width of the camp in front of the tents; in the Crimean plates it is a line of barrels,
  kits, cook-fires and stacked arms. **The row is a doubled thing: shelter line + working line.**
  A generator that lays down only tents will produce something that reads as a tent shop, not a
  camp.
- **Hierarchy is legible by what is NOT a tent.** In the Naval Brigade plate the officer's marquee
  is bigger, differently shaped, and has a clear apron; in the 4th Division plate, dug-in
  earth-banked huts sit among the tents. The camp is never one repeated object.
- **The camp sits in a clearing bounded by forest or by a valley lip** (Fort Lawton is a conifer
  clearing; Sebastopol a shallow bowl). This is direct support for the brief's banked §10.3
  dimension: **forest pocket and clearing**.
- **Scale reads through repetition and haze.** In `l1-bell-fenton-light-03.jpg` the camp recedes to
  the horizon as a texture. For a tactical map that means: a few dozen tents in the play space, and
  the impression of hundreds handled by the composed edge (FFT relational rule 8).

## 4. Ring encampments — the tipi and ger evidence

*Evidence: `l1-tipi-great-camp-02.jpg`, `l2-tipi-village-hill.jpg`, `l1-ger-camp-01.jpg`,
`l2-mongol-ring-miniature.jpg`.*

- The Grabill photograph shows a very large camp that is **not** a single tidy circle: it is a
  loose field of cones with **local clusters** (family groups) and clear lanes between them. The
  ring is a *social* fact expressed at cluster scale, not a drafting-compass fact at camp scale.
- `l2-tipi-village-hill.jpg` shows the alternative: a **line along a ridge**, wagons drawn up in
  front. Terrain overrides the ring whenever terrain has an opinion.
- Two rules that survive at gameplay scale and cost nothing:
  1. **All shelters in one camp face the same way** (tipi doors east). This single constraint is
     what makes a scatter read as one people.
  2. **The ring has a named centre and named positions** — the honoured place, the chief's lodge.
     This maps onto the camp's fire-at-the-centre capsule and gives the roller a reason to place
     the *best* tent at a specific bearing rather than at random.
- The ger camp photo shows the alternative small-scale form: a **short row of drums along a track
  edge**, all doors the same way. Small nomad camps in the images are almost never rings — the ring
  is a large-camp/gathering form.

**Consequence.** "Ring" in the camp brief should be read as *inward-facing with a claimed centre*,
not as *circular*. A four-tent camp cannot make a circle; it can absolutely make an inward-facing
cluster around a fire. That preserves the topology at rung 0 where the tent count is small.

## 5. Refugee camps — planned grid versus organic growth, in one frame

*Evidence: `l2-zaatari-aerial.jpg`.*

- The Za'atari aerial shows both states simultaneously: **a planned orthogonal grid of shelter
  plots** and, threading through it, a **dense commercial spine** (the "Champs-Élysées" market
  street) where the grid has been eaten by accretion, plus blocks where units have been dragged out
  of their plots into courtyard clusters.
- The planning literature describes exactly this: the grid model (UNHCR/Sphere handbooks, in use
  since the 1970s) is criticised as reading "too military-like" and as failing to support community
  formation; camps then transform toward socio-culturally driven clusters with emergent central
  spaces — which in turn produces blocked streets and undefined leftover space.
- **The generator lesson is the transformation, not either end state.** A refugee/shanty camp that
  reads true has *both*: a legible original order, and visible violations of it. That is a
  history/condition fact, not a layout fact — which puts it in the same column as maintained
  overgrowth at the guard post.

## 6. Informal settlement — density gradient, borrowed walls, and the walkway spine

*Evidence: `l2-shanty-honduras.jpg`, `l2-shanty-barisal.jpg`, and, from lane 4, FFT MAP048 (Slums
of Zarghidas) and MAP032 (Slums in Dorter).*

- **Density is a gradient, not a value.** In the Honduras hillside the shacks are dense at the
  bottom/along the road and thin out uphill into isolated units on cut pads. Genesis-usable form:
  *density falls off with distance from the access edge* — one parameter, and it produces the whole
  read.
- **Terrain does the composition.** Each shack sits on its own small cut terrace; the hillside is
  the elevation hierarchy. This is the camp brief's "terrain owns the high ground" ruling, arriving
  from the urban side too.
- **Borrowed walls and stilts.** Barisal shows units built off a host wall, off pilings, and off
  each other, with **a plank walkway acting as the street**. The walkway is the strongest single
  piece: it is circulation, it is elevation, and it is a tactical corridor.
- **Roof material is the entire palette.** Corrugated sheet, thatch, tarp, plank — mixed within
  metres. The §8 makeshift set already names these; the finding is that the *mixing* is the read,
  so the material picker must be per-unit, not per-camp.

## 7. Screened / enclosed camps — the bandit profile's evidence

*Evidence: `l2-camp-lawton-shebangs.jpg` (Sneden's 1864 prisoner-of-war camp plan),
`l1-bedouin-wadirum-01.jpg` (woven screen corridor), and FFT MAP091 Thieves Fort from lane 4.*

- The Camp Lawton plan is the clearest **screened-camp diagram** available in the public domain: a
  stockade rectangle enclosing 44 acres, a "dead line" inside it, **sentry boxes spaced around the
  outside**, one entrance gate, a brook crossing the enclosure, guard quarters and officers'
  housing *outside* the stockade, and the prisoners' shelters ("shebangs") shown as irregular
  clusters inside. The relations it teaches:
  - a **single controlled entrance** where the road meets the enclosure;
  - a **perimeter of repeated watch points** rather than a continuous defended wall;
  - **an inner setback** (the dead line) — again a reserved void doing the compositional work;
  - **water crossing the site** as an amenity and a weakness at once.
- `l1-bedouin-wadirum-01.jpg` supplies the cheap version: a **free-standing woven screen run** that
  makes a corridor. For "bandit: screened from the route," a screen run plus a bend is a complete
  answer — the camp is not walled, it is *hidden*, which is a sightline fact, not a fortification
  fact.

## 8. Mapping the doctrines onto the RULED composition profiles

| RULED profile | doctrine it draws on | the pieces it needs | the negative-space rule it needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **military: ordered rows, tent hierarchy, multiple towers** | Roman camp + 19c tent rows | uniform troop tent (bell or papilio) · officer marquee/pavilion · lashed watch platform ×N · service line props (picket line, stacked arms, barrels) · optional ditch+bank | **reserved perimeter void** (intervallum) + **central cross-street** with the command tent at the junction |
| **refugee/shanty: dense irregular, borrowed walls** | informal settlement + refugee-camp transformation | lean-to bay against host wall · scavenged plank/tarp roof variants · plank walkway run · crate stacks · terrace cut pads | **density gradient falling away from the access edge**; leftover undefined pockets are a feature |
| **bandit: screened from the route** | screened enclosure + screen run | screen/windbreak run · crude barricade · one controlled gap · lookout on terrain (not a tower unless licensed) | **the route must be visible from the camp and the camp not visible from the route** — a sightline reservation, not a wall |
| *(unruled, but the evidence keeps offering it)* **caravan/service: frontage** | lane 3 | see lane 3 | apron in front, quiet behind |

## 9. Findings (plain English)

1. **The Roman camp's signature is an empty ring, not a wall.** A ~200 ft clear *intervallum* sat
   between the rampart and the nearest tents. The ordered-military read depends on that reserved
   void at least as much as on the rows.
2. **Real tent rows are loose and follow the ground.** They bend along valleys and ridges; only the
   spacing *within* a row is regular. A perfect grid will read as modern, not martial.
3. **Every military row is doubled: a shelter line and a working line in front of it** (picket line,
   cook fires, stacked kit). Tents alone do not read as a camp.
4. **A camp is never one repeated object.** Officer marquees, dug-in huts, wagons, and stores break
   the field. This is where the tent-hierarchy ruling earns its keep compositionally, not just
   narratively.
5. **"Ring" means inward-facing with a claimed centre, not circular.** Historic camps cluster by
   family and lane; the true circle is a large-gathering form. Small camps still read as rings
   because everything faces the fire.
6. **One free rule makes a scatter read as one people: everything faces the same way.** Tipi doors
   east; ger doors south. It costs a single bearing on the roll.
7. **Terrain overrides the doctrine whenever terrain has an opinion** — a ridge produces a line, a
   hillside produces terraced clusters, a valley floor produces a curve.
8. **Planned camps and organic camps are the same camp at different ages.** Za'atari shows a grid
   with a market spine eating through it. The truest generated refugee camp carries a legible
   original order plus visible violations of it — a condition/history fact.
9. **Informal density is a gradient keyed to the access edge**, and the plank walkway is the street.
   One parameter and one piece cover most of the shanty read.
10. **The screened camp is defined by sightline, not by fortification** — a screen run, a bend, one
    controlled gap, and repeated watch points. The Camp Lawton plan also supplies an "inner
    setback" line, which is the same reserved-void trick as the intervallum.
11. **The ditch-and-bank the brief defers is a two-piece GROUND assembly at Genesis scale** — a
    1h ditch and a 1h bank, from one excavation. It never becomes a foundation, so it does not
    violate the surface-anchored capsule.
12. **The lashed timber tower is historically a repeating perimeter element**, which supports the
    camp brief's count-scaled licence rather than a single hero tower.
