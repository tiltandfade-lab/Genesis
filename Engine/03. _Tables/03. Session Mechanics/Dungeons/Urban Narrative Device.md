# Urban Narrative Device

A d36 table of social complications and hidden truths in NPC encounters. Each entry describes what's actually happening beneath the surface of an urban social interaction, what the party is likely to assume, and how the DM can leverage that gap to create narrative tension.

| d36 | Device | Core Situation | Player Misread | DM Leverage |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Informant's Agenda | This NPC offers information freely — but what they choose to share steers the party toward the informant's real goal | Players assume the information is neutral or helpful | The pattern of what they volunteer vs. what they omit reveals their actual interest |
| 2 | Unwilling Informant | The NPC knows something valuable but fears retribution if they help openly; they drop hints and clues but deny direct involvement | Players think the NPC is evasive or unreliable | The NPC's relief or panic when the party acts on partial information shows what they really wanted |
| 3 | Faction Proxy | This NPC represents a faction's interests without the party realizing which faction pulls their strings | Players see them as independent or loyal only to themselves | The NPC's genuine shock or satisfaction when faction consequences arrive reveals their true allegiance |
| 4 | The Debt Collector | The NPC appears to be offering help or friendship, but every interaction is accumulating a debt the party will eventually be called to repay | Players think they're building goodwill or genuine relationships | The NPC's formal shift from friendly to creditor when collection time comes reshapes the entire relationship |
| 5 | Hidden Patron | An NPC has been anonymously funding or supporting the party's goals, and reveals themselves only when that leverage becomes useful | Players assume they're solving problems through their own effort | The revelation that they've been helped all along recontextualizes their victories and creates obligation |
| 6 | The Reputation Merchant | This NPC's interest in the party stems entirely from how knowing them will affect their own social standing | Players assume the NPC cares about the party's goals or wellbeing | When the NPC abandons them mid-crisis because it's become socially risky, the transactional nature becomes clear |
| 7 | Moral Exemption Seeker | The NPC subtly positions themselves as aligned with the party's values, hoping to access the party's moral authority to justify their own dubious actions | Players accept them as genuinely principled | When the party discovers what the NPC actually did (or wants to do), they realize they've been used as moral cover |
| 8 | The Compromised Official | This NPC holds legitimate authority but is secretly beholden to criminal or hostile interests; they appear to help but are actually compromised | Players trust them because of their office or reputation | Their sudden refusal to act (or active obstruction) when ordered reveals their true masters |
| 9 | Family Obligation | The NPC is helping or hindering the party not because of personal conviction but because of family duty, debt, or shame | Players interpret their actions as ideological or personal preference | When family pressure forces them to betray or abandon the party, the real motivation surfaces |
| 10 | The Double Life | This NPC presents one identity publicly and lives an entirely different life in secret; the party knows only one side | Players see them as coherent and trustworthy based on partial knowledge | Discovery of their secret identity (or worse, having it revealed publicly) forces a crisis of trust |
| 11 | Competitive Ally | The NPC appears to be helping the party, but is actually racing them toward the same goal to claim credit or reward | Players see them as teammates | When they move to cut the party out entirely, the competitive nature becomes undeniable |
| 12 | The Convenient Enemy | An NPC the party suspects or opposes is actually innocent; a third party has orchestrated evidence to point the party's violence or investigation at them | Players assume the obvious suspect is guilty | When the real culprit is revealed (often too late), the party realizes they were manipulated into attacking the wrong target |
| 13 | Penitent Villain | This NPC is genuinely reformed or remorseful about past crimes, but the party doesn't know about those crimes and treats them with suspicion they've earned | Players distrust them for reasons they don't fully understand | When their past surfaces, the party must decide whether genuine reformation is possible |
| 14 | The Marked Target | An NPC the party befriends or helps is marked for death or ruin by powers the party doesn't yet know exist; helping them drags the party into a larger conflict | Players think they're making a simple ally | The inevitable arrival of the NPC's enemies (and their interest in the party) reshapes the entire situation |
| 15 | Blackmail Victim | This NPC is being coerced or controlled through blackmail; they appear to be choosing their actions but are actually trapped | Players assume they're acting on principle or self-interest | When the NPC reveals (or the party discovers) the blackmail, they must decide whether to help the NPC get free |
| 16 | The Underground Entrepreneur | An NPC appears to be a legitimate merchant or professional, but their real wealth and power comes from illegal markets or criminal enterprise | Players interact with them as law-abiding citizens | When the party encounters their criminal operation, they must decide what they know and whether to report it |
| 17 | Religious Authority as Tool | A priest, oracle, or spiritual leader uses their moral position to influence the party toward specific goals; they may believe in their cause, but their real loyalty is to a higher authority | Players see them as honestly serving their faith | When that authority's true goals conflict with the party's, the gap between public doctrine and private obedience becomes clear |
| 18 | The Thwarted Guardian | An NPC who genuinely wants to protect someone or something is prevented from doing so by forces they can't openly resist; they signal for help indirectly | Players miss the subtle warnings or assume the NPC is failing through incompetence | When the party realizes the NPC was desperate and caged all along, their previous inaction becomes tragic |
| 19 | Class Divide Shame | An NPC from a lower social station pretends to be higher, or vice versa; they're ashamed of their true position and will overcompensate if exposed | Players take their stated status at face value | When their true social standing is revealed (by accident or necessity), their desperation to hide it becomes a complication |
| 20 | The Proxy Sacrifice | This NPC has been quietly positioned to take the fall for someone else's crimes or failures; they don't yet realize it but will be convenient to blame | Players see them as innocent (or guilty) based on evidence | When they're suddenly scapegoated, the party realizes the entire situation was manufactured |
| 21 | Jealous Protector | An NPC's assistance comes with hidden strings — they want to maintain control over someone or something the party is trying to help or free | Players think the NPC is unselfishly invested in the party's success | When the NPC's true possessiveness emerges, it reframes their earlier help as manipulation |
| 22 | The Counterfeit Authority | An NPC claims official standing, credentials, or power they don't actually possess; they're either delusional or deliberately fraudulent | Players accept their authority at face value | When their fraud is exposed, every decision made based on their authority must be reconsidered |
| 23 | Divided Loyalty | This NPC genuinely cares about multiple people or factions with conflicting interests; they've been trying to protect all sides and will inevitably fail one of them | Players assume they're committed to a single cause | When they're forced to choose, they'll betray whichever side they were least equipped to fully serve |
| 24 | The Stigmatized Secret | An NPC is hiding something about themselves (a disease, a forbidden relationship, a shameful birth, magical weakness) that would destroy their social position if revealed | Players see them as straightforward and unencumbered | When their secret emerges, it explains otherwise inexplicable behavior and creates unexpected vulnerability |
| 25 | Economic Desperation | This NPC appears to be helping the party from principle or friendship, but is actually motivated by the need to pay debts, feed family, or avoid destitution | Players interpret generosity as goodwill | When the party learns the NPC is one bad week from ruin, it recontextualizes every sacrifice they've made |
| 26 | The Useful Accident | An NPC's incompetence, carelessness, or bad luck has accidentally created an opportunity the party can exploit; the NPC doesn't realize they've helped | Players assume either deliberate assistance or pure chance | When the NPC discovers their role in the party's success, they may demand compensation, or their guilt may bind them closer |
| 27 | Revenge's Patient Servant | An NPC is helping the party (or hindering them) as part of a long-term plan to get revenge on someone else; the party is a tool, not the purpose | Players think the NPC's motivation centers on them | When the revenge actually happens, the NPC abandons or reveals the party's involvement as collateral damage |
| 28 | The Stolen Identity | An NPC has taken on a false name, history, or social position; they're hiding from something or someone and fear exposure | Players know them only by their false identity | When their past catches up with them, the party must decide whether they knew the "real" person at all |
| 29 | Competing Rescuer | Multiple NPCs are trying to help the party (or save someone the party cares about) without coordinating; their interference creates chaos | Players see each helper as independent and aligned | When their efforts collide and cause actual harm, the party realizes no one was actually in control |
| 30 | The Opportunist's Mask | This NPC wears whatever mask the party needs them to wear (friend, ally, neutral, enemy) purely to access resources or opportunities; they have no real convictions | Players believe in their apparent alignment or position | When their true nature emerges (usually when it's no longer profitable to pretend), they're revealed as hollow |
| 31 | Complicit Authority | An NPC in a position of power is aware of injustice or crime but turns a blind eye because addressing it would create personal or political cost | Players assume officials are either corrupt or honestly ignorant | When evidence of their knowledge surfaces, it becomes a choice between exposure and silence |
| 32 | The Redemption Seeker | An NPC is desperately trying to atone for past actions or failures by helping the party, but their past victims or enemies may view the party as complicit in the NPC's escape | Players see them as reborn or reformed | When the NPC's past victims demand justice, the party must decide whether they've become accessories to unpunished crime |
| 33 | Hidden Competition | Two NPCs the party trusts are secretly competing with each other; one has positioned themselves with the party to monitor or undermine the other | Players see each NPC as separately motivated | When their competition erupts, the party realizes they've been positioned as pawns in a longer game |
| 34 | The Necessary Villain | An NPC commits genuinely harmful acts for what they view as justified reasons (protecting someone, preventing worse harm, enforcing community law); the party may initially agree, then realize the cost | Players see the NPC's actions as moral or necessary | When the full scope of harm becomes clear, the moral calculus shifts and the NPC becomes harder to categorize |
| 35 | Inheritance of Debt | An NPC has inherited obligation, debt, or enmity from a previous generation and must act against the party (or with them) to settle what they didn't create | Players assume the NPC's hostility or alliance is personal | When the NPC explains the inherited burden, it humanizes their opposition or reveals they never wanted to be involved |
| 36 | The Synchronized Reveal | Multiple NPCs reveal conflicting information, hidden identities, or betrayals at the same moment; the party can't process fast enough to respond coherently | Players assume a linear narrative with one truth emerging | When multiple contradictory truths emerge simultaneously, it becomes impossible to separate lies from fact until much later |
