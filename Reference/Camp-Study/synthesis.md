# Camp Study — synthesis

> **Authority note (2026-07-25 audit):** this is the research record, not the live
> implementation contract. Current dispositions—including fact-weighted tent selection
> and the dominant-center-claim rule—live in `docs/GOLDEN-SITES-CATALOG.md`.

## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The camp's forms are researchable and they resolve much more cleanly than "a bunch of extruded
triangles." **Nine tent traditions collapse into three silhouette genera** — cone, drum, span (plus
a dome that is a span pulled to the ground) — and culture is carried by three cheap geometric facts:
*what happens above the apex*, *whether there is a vertical wall*, and *whether the plan is round or
a strip*. Layout resolves equally cleanly: camps do not vary along one tidy/untidy axis but along
**what the plan is for** and **what it faces**, which means the brief's ring-vs-frontage split is
structurally correct, and the RULED military/shanty/bandit profiles each turn out to depend on a
*negative-space* rule as much as on pieces. The waystation typology survives the brief's
offers-never-commands capsule completely intact — even a fortified caravanserai controls only its
own doorway. The FFT corpus, checked honestly, **contains no camps at all**, so Site 2 is the first
golden site with no FFT composition to learn from; what it does offer is a clearing grammar and
three surrogate maps that each solve one piece of the problem in another material. And one relation
was independently confirmed by four separate sources: **reserve the centre, and put exactly one
object in it.**

## 1. Tent-form typology — forms → culture-channel candidates

Full extraction in lane 1; silhouettes at one scale in `tent-form-silhouettes.svg`. Grid: cell = 5
ft, h = 2.5 ft.

| form | genus | plan × height | **the signal (must survive the camera)** | geometry | paint | status variant | culture-channel candidate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bell / Sibley | CONE skirted | ~4 cells × 4–5h | base skirt break + bare point | cone, skirt break, door slit | seams, weathering, unit marks | none — it *is* the troop unit | disciplined · institutional · military |
| Wedge / Roman papilio | SPAN prism | **2 × 2 cells × 2h** | straight ridge + flat gable ends | prism, gable opening | leather seams, mud hem | size + plan position | legionary · ordered · imperial |
| Bedouin black tent | SPAN low | 4–10 × 2 cells × 2–3h | scalloped low ridge + **open front** | span plane, back wall, pole line | woven stripe, divider curtain | **length** (pole count) + curtain | desert · pastoral · hospitality |
| Tipi | CONE tall | ~4 cells × 6h + crown | **projecting pole crown** | tilted cone, crown, flap poles | painted band, flaps, soot | **paint + ring position** | plains · migratory · hunter |
| Lavvu | CONE squat | ~3 cells × 3h + stub | squat bowed profile | fat cone, stub splay | sag, seams, soot | none | northern · subsistence · egalitarian |
| Ger / yurt | DRUM | ~4 cells × 3h (2h wall) | wall/roof break + stove pipe | drum, break, **door frame**, pipe | hem band, meander, rope bands | **scale** + ring position | steppe · horse-nomad · rooted tribal |
| Pavilion (round) | CONE walled | 5 cells × 4–5h | tall wall + finial + pennant | wall, cone, finial | gores, valance, heraldry | **is** the hierarchy | courtly · feudal · officer |
| Marquee (ridge) | SPAN tall | 4 × 8 cells × 4h | broad flat roof + awning | roof plane, walls, awning | panel seams, banners | **is** the hierarchy | courtly · institutional · command |
| Wickiup / bender | DOME | ~2 cells × 2–3h | door arch in a shaggy dome | dome, arch | thatch, patchwork, soot | none — expedient | makeshift · poor · monstrous |

**How status actually works, across cultures.** Only one of the three mechanisms is "make it
bigger":

- **(a) Scale** — ger. One mesh, two scales.
- **(b) Paint + position** — tipi. The chief's lodge is the same cone with paint, standing at a
  named bearing in the ring. *This is the mechanism that fits the geometry/paint law best, and it
  pushes the status information into the LAYOUT.*
- **(c) A different form entirely, standing in its own cleared apron** — Roman/European. Troop
  wedge versus commander's pavilion. *This is the mechanism most legible at gameplay scale, and it
  is what the brief's "troop tent / officer tent" split already assumes.*

## 2. Layout-doctrine sheet, mapped to the RULED composition profiles

| RULED profile | doctrine + evidence | pieces it needs | **the negative-space rule it needs** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **military — ordered rows, tent hierarchy, multiple towers** | Roman camp plan (Cybulski plate) + 19c tent rows (Crimea, Fort Lawton) | uniform troop tent ×N · officer marquee/pavilion · lashed watch platform ×N · **service-line props in front of every row** (picket line, stacked kit, barrels) · optional ditch+bank | **reserved perimeter void** (the ~200 ft *intervallum*) + **a central cross-street with the command tent at the junction** |
| **refugee / shanty — dense irregular, borrowed walls** | informal settlements (Honduras, Barisal) + refugee-camp transformation (Za'atari) + FFT MAP048/032/040 | lean-to bay against a host wall · scavenged plank/tarp/corrugated roof variants (**picked per unit, not per camp**) · plank walkway run (multi-level) · crate stacks · cut terrace pads | **density gradient falling away from the access edge**; small irregular leftover pockets are the quiet ground at this density |
| **bandit — screened from the route** | Camp Lawton stockade plan + Bedouin screen run + FFT MAP091 Thieves Fort | screen/windbreak run · crude barricade · one controlled gap · lookout on terrain | **a sightline reservation, not a wall**: the route must be visible from the camp and the camp not visible from the route. Plus an **inner setback line** if enclosed |
| *(unruled but repeatedly offered by the evidence)* **caravan / service — frontage** | lane 3, all four types | see §3 | apron in front, quiet behind; ornament only at the threshold |

**Three layout findings that cut across all profiles.**

1. **Real rows are loose and follow the ground.** Spacing is regular *within* a row and ragged
   between rows; rows bend along valleys and ridges. A perfect grid reads as modern, not martial.
2. **Every row is doubled — a shelter line and a working line in front of it.** Tents alone read as
   a tent shop, not a camp.
3. **One free rule makes a scatter read as one people: everything faces the same way.** One bearing
   on the roll. Confirmed by tipi practice, ger practice, and FFT's Public Cemetery.

## 3. Waystation typology + the service-yard grammar note

**The ladder of asking.** well/spring (offers water, asks nothing) → way shelter (offers roof, asks
nothing) → coaching inn (offers service, asks money) → caravanserai (offers safety, asks money;
**closes its own gate at night, never the road**). The control column is empty the whole way up.
**The brief's offers-never-commands capsule survives the entire real typology.**

**The service-yard grammar** (eight relations, stated for a composition compiler):

1. **Apron before threshold** — widened, hardened, wear-marked ground between route and mass. No gate.
2. **One entrance, oversized, ornamented** — decoration is spent on the threshold, nowhere else.
3. **The yard is a reserved void with one central object** — fountain, well, fire, mounting block.
4. **Service ranges are repeated identical cells** on one to three sides; four only if fortified.
5. **The animal side and the human side separate** — a licensed asymmetry that reads as function.
6. **A climbable working vertical exists and is not a tower** — wall stair, gallery stair, ladder.
7. **Wear is asymmetric and points at the road** — the cheapest possible expression of the type.
8. **Nothing checks you** — no barrier, no arrow slit facing the route, no commanded threshold.

**The single strongest structural finding in lane 3:** the coaching inn's **carriage arch is
geometrically the guard post's gate arch with the control relation deleted.** Same piece, opposite
relation. That is a real argument that the waystation assembles from inherited pieces rather than
earning its own module — i.e. evidence for the prediction already on record in the module test.

## 4. What the evidence says about the banked rulings

### Confirmed, with evidence

| banked ruling | what confirms it |
|---|---|
| Tent MASSES are geometry, ALL decoration is paint | Caerlaverock pavilion (plain cone + gores = the whole status read); ger (plain drum + hem band); sheikh's tent (plain span + patterned curtain). Status is 60–80% paint on an unchanged mass in all three traditions. |
| Tent FORMS are culture-expressive | Nine traditions produce three genera differing in apex, wall and plan aspect — genuinely distinguishable at distance without hue shifts. |
| Terrain owns the high ground | Informal settlements terrace into hillsides; FFT wilderness maps make terrain the dominant mass; Fenton's camps sit in bowls. Camps do not build elevation. |
| Fire at the centre / inward ring | **Four independent confirmations of "reserve the centre, put one object in it"**: caravanserai fountain, FFT Public Cemetery's central object in an open avenue, the druid grove's idol in a basin, the camp's own fire ring. |
| Apron-without-gate | The whole lane-3 typology; and the carriage arch as the gate arch minus control. |
| Guest-family property (camps roll inside hosts) | The druid grove is the pattern shipped: a hosted refugee population, host-capped, with its own traders and its own front door, and guest/host friction as the story. |
| Shanty quarter = the waystation's urban sibling | FFT's slum maps are visibly the same site type as its trade cities, one rung down. |
| Occupancy-licensed lashed watch platform, count-scaled | Cybulski's *turris lignea* is a repeating perimeter element on a palisade, not a singular landmark. FFT Germinas Peak proves lashed timber can be a primary visual subject. |
| Camps leave traces, not ruins | The well stop has no building at all — only trodden ground, spill and radial approach paths. Use-marks *are* the site at low rungs. |
| Forest pocket / clearing as the §10.3 dimension | Fort Lawton's camp sits in a conifer clearing; FFT's wilderness block is exactly this grammar. |

### Two places where the evidence pushes back

**(i) "Ring" reads as *circular*, and it should read as *inward-facing with a claimed centre*.**
Historic camps cluster by family into lanes and pockets; the true drawn circle is a
large-gathering form, and terrain overrides it whenever terrain has an opinion (a ridge produces a
line; a hillside produces terraced clusters). A four-tent rung-0 camp cannot make a circle — but it
can absolutely face the fire. Restating the topology as *inward-facing with a claimed centre*
preserves the ruling at the small tent counts the camp will actually roll.

**(ii) The ger contradicts the permanence ladder.** It has a vertical wall, a rectangular door
frame and a chimney; it reads as a *building*, not as borrowed shelter. A rung-0 camp full of gers
will not read as "borrowed a clearing." Either the ger is reserved for rooted/tribal occupancies,
or the ladder gains a named exception. Nothing else in lane 1 has this problem.

### Two genuine tensions the study surfaced but cannot resolve

- **Ingress.** Lanes 2–3 say *one controlled entrance* (caravanserai, coaching arch, stockade gate).
  Lane 5 says *at least three ways in, priced differently* (conversation / risk / skill check). Both
  are correct for their site type; the camp has to pick.
- **The camp has no interior.** Both touchstones escalate through physical layers — outer yard →
  interior → inner room. A tent is a mass, not a room, so the camp cannot escalate inward.

## 5. THE FOUNDER PACKET

Ten questions, plain English. Adam rules; nothing below is a recommendation dressed as a decision.

**Q1 — Which tent forms enter the kit first?** The evidence says three forms cover the widest
ground because they are three different *shapes*, not three flavours: the **skirted cone** (bell —
the cheapest "ordered military rows" read), the **low open-fronted span** (Bedouin — the only new
mass in the set, and the best tactical piece: cover, concealment, and a room you can shoot into),
and the **shaggy dome** (wickiup — the makeshift/poor/monster form that also earns at the lair and
the shanty quarter). Do you want those three, a different three, or more?

**Q2 — Does the Roman troop tent get a place because the arithmetic is free?** A Roman
*contubernium* tent is 10 Roman feet square — almost exactly **2 × 2 combat cells at 2h high**. It
is the only form in the set that lands exactly on the grid, which would make ordered military rows
cost no fractional geometry at all. Worth adding as a fourth, or is the bell tent enough?

**Q3 — Culture → form bindings: are these the right pairs?** Proposed candidates for
CultureVisualConstitution: *institutional/military* → skirted cone or wedge · *desert/pastoral* →
low span · *steppe/horse-nomad and rooted tribal* → drum (ger) · *plains/migratory* → tall cone with
pole crown · *northern/subsistence* → squat cone · *courtly/feudal* → walled pavilion + ridge
marquee · *makeshift/poor/monstrous* → dome. Any you want swapped, cut, or held back?

**Q4 — Is the ger allowed at rung 0?** It has a wall, a door frame and a chimney, so it reads as a
building rather than as borrowed shelter, which fights the permanence ladder. Reserve it for rooted
or tribal occupancies, give the ladder a named exception, or let it in anywhere?

**Q5 — Do the cone forms share one mesh, or ship separately?** Tipi and lavvu are the same genus and
are the least distinguishable pair in the set. If they share a parameterised cone they are cheap but
risk reading as one form with a slider; if they ship separately they need deliberate divergence
(straight profile + tall crown versus bowed profile + stub crown). Which, or do we cut one?

**Q6 — Guy lines.** They are load-bearing to the read of pavilions and wedges, and they are exactly
the thin geometry the low-poly economy forbids. Candidate compromise: **a few short stake stubs plus
a ground decal of radiating stake marks — no rope geometry at all.** Accept, or drop guy lines
entirely?

**Q7 — When does the earthwork arrive?** At Genesis scale the Roman ditch-and-bank is only a
**two-piece GROUND assembly — a 1h ditch and a 1h bank from one excavation** — and it never becomes
a foundation, so it does not break the surface-anchored capsule. The military composition profile is
noticeably thin without it (the reserved perimeter void is what makes the Roman camp read). Admit it
with the military occupancy now, or hold it deferred as banked?

**Q8 — How many ways into a camp?** The historical service and enclosure evidence says **one
controlled entrance**; the occupied-site touchstones say **three routes priced differently** — a
conversation at the front, a risk (a log, a gap in the screen), and a skill check that buys
surprise. Which model does the camp roll?

**Q9 — Does the camp get anything you can go *inside*?** Both touchstones escalate outer yard →
interior → inner room, and a tent is a mass, not a room. Three candidates: (a) the vertical
substitutes for the horizontal — knoll, wagon top, licensed platform, and the camp accepts a
shallower arc; (b) exactly one volume is enterable (the officer pavilion, or the wagon); (c) the
camp is deliberately a shallow site and depth belongs to hosts. Which?

**Q10 — Promote "reserve the centre, put one object in it" to a camp composition law?** Four
independent sources agree — the caravanserai fountain, FFT's Public Cemetery, the druid grove's
idol, and the camp's own fire ring. It would join the guard post's relational rules as the camp's
first taste rule, and it would come with a countable check (a rolled camp whose centre is occupied
by more than one object fails validation). Promote, or leave as a working default?
