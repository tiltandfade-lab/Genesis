*p. 78 MM 2024*

Copper dragons are incorrigible pranksters, joke tellers, and riddlers who dwell in rocky uplands. Despite their "good" alignment, they possess a fiercely covetous, miserly streak. They are extremely proud and become highly dangerous if their tricks are not appreciated or if their hoard is approached.

**Campaign Integration Hooks:**

- **The Extortionist Prankster:** A vital mountain pass or ravine is being blocked by a Copper Wyrmling. It has used its acid to weaken rock formations, creating mudslides and rockfalls to trap merchant wagons. It refuses to let anyone pass without paying a "toll" consisting of fine art, rare metals, or an incredibly good joke.
- **The Unhelpful "Hero":** The party tracks a notorious bandit to a cave, only to find the bandit stripped of his loot and glued to the ceiling in a patch of hardening mud. The wyrmling claims the bandit's stolen goods as its own "rightful treasure" and absolutely refuses to hand over the quest items the party needs unless they agree to a high-stakes riddle contest.

### DM Tip: Managing a Campaign-Long Companion

If the PCs succeed and earn the wyrmling's loyalty, you have to balance its presence at the table. A Copper Wyrmling is a potent combatant for low-level parties (with an AC of 17 and an Acid Breath weapon).

- **Keep it flavorful, not optimal:** The dragon shouldn't solve the PCs' problems for them. In combat, it might prioritize blinding enemies with mud, taunting the biggest threat, or using its _Slow_ breath weapon to play keep-away, rather than just optimizing damage.
- **The Cost of Friendship:** It demands interaction. If the PCs ignore it, don't tell it jokes, or refuse to engage with its pranks, it will wander off to find "more interesting" friends until they win it back.
