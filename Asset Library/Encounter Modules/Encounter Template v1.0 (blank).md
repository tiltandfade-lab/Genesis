# ENCOUNTER TITLE
> Give the encounter a short, evocative title based on its most memorable image, obstacle, social tension, or hidden truth.
>
> Good title patterns:
> - **The [Object] [Divider / Gate / Mask / Stair / Knot / Curtain / Marker]**
> - **The [Color / Material / Mood] [Thing]**
> - **The [False Surface]**
> - **The [Public Scene]**
>
> Aim for **2–4 words**.

---

# RUN THIS IN 10 SECONDS
> Give the GM the fastest possible operational summary.
>
> Formula:
>
> **A [type of obstacle / situation] blocks [goal]. The PCs can [approach 1], [approach 2], or [approach 3], but [main pressure] makes delay or carelessness dangerous.**
>
> This should be readable in one breath.

---

# LIKELY FLOW
> Give the GM a suggested play rhythm without railroading the scene.
>
> Use **4–5 beats max**:
>
> 1. **First impression** — what the PCs notice immediately
> 2. **First move** — what they are likely to test or engage
> 3. **Complication** — what deeper layer or pressure emerges
> 4. **Decision point** — what meaningful choice the scene presents
> 5. **Resolution / escalation** — what usually happens next
>
> Keep each beat to **one short line**.

# ROLE / PRESSURE / TAGS

## Role
> What is this scene **doing** in the adventure?
>
> Choose 1–3:
> - Diversion
> - Discovery
> - Control
> - Delay
> - Access
> - Warning
> - Resource
> - Social Friction
> - Threat
> - Transition
> - Misdirection
>
> **Guideline:** one primary role, one secondary role, one optional flavor role.

## Pressure
> What makes the scene active instead of decorative?
>
> Examples:
> - Low (escalates only if PCs push)
> - Moderate (lingering costs time / stealth / position)
> - High (scene is already unstable)
> - Social attention
> - Route friction
> - Hidden surveillance
> - Time-sensitive opportunity
>
> **Guideline:** pressure should answer:
> **“What gets worse if the PCs engage carelessly?”**

## Tags
> Add fast-reference tags for retrieval and reuse.
>
> Use 4–7 tags from categories like:
> - location: urban, alley, rooftop, market, dockside
> - mode: social, discovery, stealth, combat-risk, hazard
> - function: route-control, clue-node, soft-gate, public-friction
> - special texture: cover, faction, contraband, coded-message, crowd

# ONE-SENTENCE PROMISE
> In one sentence, tell yourself what kind of play this encounter offers.
>
> Formula:
>
> **A/An [situation or blockage] becomes a [play-space or tension], where the PCs can [approach 1], [approach 2], and possibly [approach 3/payoff]—without [combat/exposure/escalation] unless they push.**

> This is the **core player fantasy** of the node.

# GM READ-ALOUD: ON APPROACH
> **Use when the PCs first enter the scene.**
>
> Include:
> - route geometry / space shape
> - 1 sensory cue
> - 1 lighting cue
> - 1 movement-affecting feature
> - 1 suspicious or notable object
> - 1 active social or environmental focal point
>
> Keep it **4–7 sentences max**.
> Write it so it can be read verbatim **or** paraphrased easily.

---

# GM READ-ALOUD: ON CLOSER INSPECTION
> **Use when the PCs slow down and focus on the real clue surface**  
> (the object, wall patch, sigils, crowd behavior, hidden marker, art, mechanism, etc.).
>
> This box should:
> - deepen the mystery
> - reveal texture that matters
> - hint at the truth without fully resolving it
>
> Keep it to **2–5 sentences**.

---

# GM READ-ALOUD: ON ESCALATION
> **Use when the scene turns**  
> (noise, suspicion, patrol arrival, magical backlash, crowd shift, spirit flare, etc.).
>
> This box should:
> - change the emotional temperature
> - make the consequence feel immediate
> - clarify that the scene is no longer static
>
> Keep it to **2–5 sentences**.

---

# GM READ-ALOUD: ON SUCCESS *(OPTIONAL)*
> **Use when the obstacle yields or the party gains clear advantage.**
>
> This box should:
> - reward success with sensory payoff
> - signal transition to the next node
> - optionally hint at what lies beyond
>
> Keep it short and satisfying.

# WHAT THE PLAYERS NOTICE IMMEDIATELY (FAST LIST)
> List **4–6 obvious, actionable observations**.
>
> Good categories:
> - what blocks or redirects movement
> - what looks suspicious
> - what can be used
> - what increases exposure
> - what implies a hidden mechanism
> - what reveals social tension
> - what suggests cover / stealth / elevation
>
> **Rule:** these should be things the PCs can act on **without rolling first**.

---

# SCENE TRUTH (WHAT’S REALLY GOING ON)
> This section answers:
> - What is the surface read?
> - What is actually happening?
> - What is the hidden stake?
>
> Write:
>
> **This is actually [real situation], not just [surface appearance].**
>
> Then define:
> - who benefits from the scene as it currently stands
> - who is controlling perception
> - whether the obvious clue is real, partial, or a decoy
> - what the players can uncover if they push deeper
>
> If useful, include **2–4 interpretation branches**:
> - innocent version
> - criminal version
> - faction version
> - bait / decoy version
>
> This is where your weird details become meaningful instead of random.

---

# CONTROL DIALS (SYSTEM-NEUTRAL)
> Add **1–3 simple scene meters** that let you track changing state without overbuilding mechanics.
>
> Good dials:
> - Attention
> - Route Friction
> - Suspicion
> - Instability
> - Heat
> - Crowd Mood
> - Alarm
> - Position
>
> Each dial should have:
> - a clear range
> - a plain-language meaning
> - a trigger for when it rises
>
> Format:
>
> **[Dial Name] (0–X):** what it measures  
> • 0 = ...  
> • 1 = ...  
> • 2 = ...  
> • 3 = ...
>
> **Guideline:** use dials only if they help you run the scene faster.

---

# INTERACTABLES (GM CONTROL PANEL)
> Include **3–5 interactables** max.
>
> Each interactable should answer:
> - what it is
> - why it matters
> - how players might use it
> - what the cost/risk is
> - whether it contains a twist, clue, or environmental shift
>
> Format:
>
> **[Object / Feature Name]:** short functional description  
> • Use: what players can do with it  
> • Cost: what goes wrong, what it consumes, or what attention it draws  
> • Twist: optional hidden function / alternate read / linked clue
>
> **Rule:** every interactable must support at least one of:
> - movement
> - stealth
> - clue discovery
> - social leverage
> - route alteration
> - escalation

---

>
> For each, define:
> - role / identity slot
> - what they want **right now**
> - what leverage works on them
> - one visible tell
>
> Format:
>
> **[NPC Name / Role]**
> • Wants:  
> • Leverage:  
> • Tell:
>
> **Guideline:** keep them tactical, not biographical.
> Leave identity open if the module is meant to be plug-and-play.

# MODULE TABLES

>
> Recommended recurring tables:
> - **d6 Obstacle Functional Reality**
> - **d6 Hazard Form**
> - **d6 Standout Object Truth**
> - **d6 Escalation**

>
> **Guideline:** these tables should be encounter-specific, not generic filler.

# PAYOFFS (CHOOSE 1–2)

>
> Categories:
> - rumor
> - clue

> - contact
> - hidden route
> - stash / contraband / resource

# ESCALATION
> If the PCs push too hard, roll or choose from the encounter’s **d6 Escalation** table.
>
> Good escalation results:
> - authority arrives
> - watchers intervene
> - route seals
> - crowd becomes hostile or obstructive
> - hidden actor reveals themselves
> - combat risk appears
> - the clue moves or goes cold
>
> **Guideline:** escalation should transform the scene, not merely punish curiosity.

# DESIGN CHECK (USE BEFORE FINALIZING)
> Before you run or publish the encounter, confirm:
>
> - **What is the scene’s actual job?**
> - **What pressure makes it live?**
> - **What can players do immediately?**
> - **What is hidden beneath the surface?**
> - **What happens if they push too hard?**
> - **What future node does this point toward?**
> - **Can a tired GM run this after reading only the top third of the card?**
>
> If not, the card still needs tightening.
