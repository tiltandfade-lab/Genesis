## 3. Ranked "what a site generator must supply" list

Ranked by frequency × how load-bearing the invention was for actual play (not just flavor):

1. **Sightline/cover geometry for stakeout, stealth, and ambush** (7 findings, highest load-bearing).
   Every social-stealth PC turn that worked ("watch the door," "hide in the crowd," "read who's
   tailing me") needed the DM to invent a specific gap, alley, stall, or crowd-density on the spot.
   This is the single most load-bearing structural gap: a generator needs a standard vocabulary of
   *cover elements* (stall-gaps, doorway nooks, crowd-density, dead-end lanes) attachable to any
   settlement site, not just golden sites.

2. **Elevation + unstable terrain as tactical/dramatic ground** (7 findings, very high load-bearing).
   Two separate sets (05, 09) independently invented the *same* trick — bait an enemy onto a
   narrow, unstable edge (a plank between pilings; a tripline at a slick glass-lip) and use the
   terrain itself as the kill. Vantage points ("the high dry span," "the resin-gate choke") were
   also invented purely to let a low-stat PC "read" a whole area at once. A generator should ship
   named vantage points and at least one collapsing/slick/narrow hazard per site as standard kit.

3. **Institutional interior with a custody/cell + single-corridor structure** (Site 6 match, 3
   findings across set-08/10/12, but essentially one location reused for 20+ turns).
   This is the cleanest golden-site hit in the whole marathon — the counting-house cell, corridor,
   and warden's stairs did real, sustained work (an entire pressure-theme's worth of scenes). It
   confirms Site 6 Prison/Institution as demonstrably demanded, not speculative.

4. **Water/flood hazard as recurring dramatic architecture** (5+ findings across sets 03/05/07/09).
   Rising water, drowning rooms, failing spans, and a "glass that keeps its dead" are not one-off —
   they are the spine of Sella's entire back half. No golden site currently models a
   flood/drowning-disaster site; this is a standing demand with no home.

5. **Small provable/discoverable props that answer investigation probes** (8 findings: dead-drop
   twine, courier chit, tally board, carved tree-bark, resin-sealed deed).
   Every time a player "read" or "investigated" a place, the DM had to invent one concrete,
   examinable object to reward or deny the check. A generator that pre-seeds 1–2 such objects per
   site (with a truth and a false-read) would remove a recurring DM improvisation burden.

6. **Light as a danger/state telegraph** (4 findings: crater-glow, closing spore-ring, dream-glass,
   drowned-port glow).
   Used consistently as the *mechanism* for telegraphing danger honestly (a binding design law
   already in this repo) — lighting states worth generating alongside props, not just for mood.

7. **Fast establishing exteriors for waystations/institutions/markets** (6 findings).
   High frequency, lower load-bearing — mostly one or two sentences of texture (roof shape,
   material, what's parked outside). Cheap to supply, but supplied constantly; a generator's
   "exterior blurb" library would absorb most of this for free.

8. **A vertical shaft/well as a dungeon-style descent point grafted onto an otherwise mundane
   building** (2 findings, set-09) — a narrow but real demand: players expect *some* buildings to
   have a below (cellar, well, sunken floor) that leads to a different register of danger/secrecy.

## 4. Coverage verdict

**Visited / needed this marathon:**
- **Site 6 — Prison/Institution**: visited hard and repeatedly (the entire Rennick counting-house
  arc, sets 08/10/12). This is the one site the transcripts prove is directly demanded by play.
- **Site 10 — Urban Institution**: visited (Harrowfield's Consortium factor-house, set-02/04).
- **Site 2 — Camp/Service**: touched lightly (the Drover's Rest waystation, set-02; the drover's
  campfire, set-06).
- **Site 7 — Natural Lair**: touched (the haunted Bioluminescent Grove, set-06) — a good fit,
  though the Grove's "haunting" is a narrative front, not a monster's physical lair, so the match
  is thematic more than structural.

**Never touched at all:**
- **Site 1 — Guard Post**: only referenced as prior-session backstory ("High-Harrow's under-gate")
  — never actually played inside the marathon transcripts.
- **Site 3 — Dormant/Abandoned**, **Site 4 — Monastery/Commune**, **Site 5 — Mine/Workshop**,
  **Site 8 — Infiltrated/Layered**, **Site 9 — Fortress/Gatehouse**, **Site 11 — Mixed-scale/titan**:
  zero appearances of any kind.

**Place-kinds play demanded that NO golden site covers:**
1. **The Shimmering Maw itself** — a whole settlement built over/around a fused-glass crater that
   is quasi-sentient ("the well is owed," "it calls," "it takes"). This is the single biggest
   structural gap: an entire campaign's home base with no golden-site analog at all. It's not a
   guard post, not a camp, not an urban institution in the civic-building sense — it's a hybrid
   settlement-plus-natural-hazard that every set had to keep re-inventing piecemeal (alleys, warrens,
   the Tree, the crater's glow, the flood).
2. **A flood/drowning disaster site** — rising water, collapsing spans, drowned bodies as terrain
   and as evidence. Recurs across four separate sets (03/05/07/09) as the actual engine of danger
   and consequence; nothing in the twelve sites models it.
3. **A permanent poor-quarter/warren** (the Refugee Quarter) — distinct from Site 2's *temporary*
   camp-on-borrowed-ground; this is a settled, permanent slum with its own social machinery
   (dead-man rooms, fire-circles). Site 2's brief explicitly says it owns the temporary case; this
   gap is real and unclaimed by any of the twelve.
4. **A small vertical shaft/well built into an otherwise ordinary structure** (the well-house) —
   a "mini-dungeon" access point grafted onto a mundane building, used twice as a plot-critical
   hiding/retrieval mechanism.

## 5. Honesty note

- **Borderline classifications**: several findings straddle categories on purpose (the glass-lip
  ambush is both an elevation-hazard and a sightline/tradecraft beat; the Traitor's Tree is both
  light and material). Each was tagged by its single most load-bearing role for the frequency
  table, so the counts undercount cross-category richness slightly rather than double-count it.
- **Thematic vs. structural site-12 (Anomalous/Living) matches**: the crater/glass and the
  fog-contagion both read as a "place that is itself alive/hungry" in the fictional sense the
  catalog's Site 12 describes — but neither is ever staged as a buildable *scene* with rooms,
  props, or a floor plan; they stay atmosphere and narrative pressure. I logged this as a thematic
  parallel only, not a real coverage hit, since it would mislead the demand-evidence purpose to
  count it as proof Site 12 got "played."
- **What I did not find**: no scene in any of the 11 files reads as a Guard Post, Monastery,
  Mine/Workshop, Fortress, Dormant ruin, Infiltrated/Layered site, or Mixed-scale/titan site of any
  kind — I checked specifically for these and came up empty, which is itself the finding (see
  Coverage verdict).
- **Volume processed**: all 11 transcripts read in full, ~570 KB / ~1565 lines total (skim-read
  per the brief, not word-by-word, but no file was skipped or partially read). `tables.js`/
  `tables.json` were never opened, per the standing rule.
