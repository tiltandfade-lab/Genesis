## What the pixels showed

### FD-1 — Reclamation is a surface mosaic on unchanged geometry **[seen]**

`22860.png` is a woodland map whose floor is a *checker of stone slab tiles and grass tiles at
the same height*. The paving is being taken back by turf, and it costs nothing: no geometry
changes, only which material a flat tile carries.
`46747cb01da69ee6b1bb4e125bdb5183.jpg` (four rotations of one wooded map) shows the same
device on terraces and channel edges.

This is the same finding as the Lair study's LC-8 ("breakdown is painted, not modelled") and
LC-1 ("irregularity lives in the riser texture"), arriving from the *reclamation* direction
instead of the cave direction. Three independent inductions now agree.

**Transfer.** Genesis's OVERGROWN and BURIED vectors should be **material-role mosaics on the
existing tile field** for anything that is not a route. Geometry is spent only where the
change is walkable, climbable, or blocking.

### FD-2 — One intact vertical landmark carries the silhouette; the low mass carries the damage **[seen]**

`03c0a2c50df2483ad9ba9c4a6993a19d.png` is a church-and-houses cluster on a small green mound.
The tower is intact ashlar with spirelets; the nave has an *unroofed bay* with exposed
rafters and a gap in the wall face beside it; loose stones lie spread on the grass around the
base. So even in FFT's own vocabulary, damage is **selective and localised** while one tall
element stays whole and does the composing.

That is exactly the `DEFAULT — damage is selective` invariant in the Site 3 concept, and it
is exactly the guard-post cohort's "one primary spatial sentence" rule surviving the move
into a damaged host.

**Transfer.** The dormant generator should *choose a survivor* — one tall, intact,
recognisable element — before it chooses what to break.

### FD-3 — The rubble apron is a ground-material spread, not a mesh scatter **[seen]**

Same frame: the loose stone around the church base is painted onto the grass as an irregular
region of stone-coloured ground with a handful of raised blocks, not as fifty rock meshes.
This matches the Lair study's LC-8 anti-rule ("no modelled small rock") and matches the real
photograph D1-01, where a rubble apron reads as a tonal band at the wall foot.

**Transfer.** Rubble is **geometry where it is a route (a cone or ramp) and paint where it is
only a look (a spread)**. That sentence is inherited verbatim from Lane 4 of the Lair study
and it survives the move into Site 3 unchanged.

### FD-4 — Dark, unowned ground is composed as broad quiet mass with two dominant flanks **[seen]**

`078_2_3.gif` is a dusk/night basin: dark water or marsh across a broad flat floor, two large
tan rock masses at left and right, sparse bare vertical accents (dead trees or stakes) on the
flat, grass banks around the rim, and the whole thing floating on black. Almost the entire
playfield is quiet ground.

**Transfer.** This is the honest-empty-space law (`GOLDEN-SITE-ONTOLOGY-ENGINE-MARRIAGE.md`
§4.3) in the exact register Site 3 needs. A dormant site is *supposed* to be mostly empty.
The renderer must not scatter props to prove the map was used, and the FFT precedent says it
does not have to.

## What the written cohort adds

### FD-5 — The corpus's ruin map interlocks the ruin with terrain and route **[documented]**

`FFT-TO-GENESIS-RELATIONAL-SHAPE-GRAMMAR-STUDY.md` on **MAP037 — Ruins Outside Zaland**: one
tall ruined gateway is the unmistakable dominant landmark; the route passes directly through
it; grass shoulders, rock slopes, trees and patchwork ground frame the passage; the ruin is
interlocked with terrain and road, not an isolated prop; large void/edge regions and sparse
trees keep the silhouette readable.

Its own Genesis lesson was written for the *guard post* ("do not import the map's ruined
state wholesale; translate the chronological layering"). Read from Site 3's side, the lesson
reverses and becomes a positive instruction:

**Transfer.** A dormant site must remain **interlocked with its route and terrain**. The
ruined gateway earns its place because the road still goes through it. A ruin standing beside
an irrelevant path is the Site 3 equivalent of the guard post's "watch hut beside an
irrelevant path" rejection.

### FD-6 — Natural = no ceiling; dug-by-culture = an unlit overburden mass **[documented]**

Lair Lane 4's LC-2 and LC-4: no map in the natural-cave cohort models an overhead surface,
but the Goland mine (built underground) models a large overhead rock mass rendered as an
unlit black vault over a lit gallery.

**Transfer — this is the discriminator Site 3 needs for the crypt.** A crypt, catacomb,
undercroft or cellar is *dug by culture*. It therefore inherits the **unlit overburden mass**
treatment, not the natural-cave no-ceiling treatment. That is how a dormant built interior
reads as enclosed without a lid occluding the fixed camera, and it is consistent with the
cutaway law.

### FD-7 — Interiors open on two sides; a modelled arch is a mass, not a lid **[documented]**

LC-5: the Underground Book Storage maps keep tall back walls on the two far sides and open the
two camera-facing sides completely; one goes further and models barrel-vault arcades as
free-standing arch masses rising off the floor — a ceiling implied by its springings.

**Transfer.** The undercroft/vault crypt should be built as **pier lines plus arch masses**,
open camera-side. My own read of D3-01 (St Felix) independently shows a real arcade arch
standing free with sky through it. Two sources, same shape.

### FD-8 — Labyrinths are cut by removing tiles **[documented]**

LC-6: FFT's cave labyrinths in plan are filled tile fields pierced by voids; the voids are
the walls; the structure is subtractive.

**Transfer.** The catacomb gallery network is the ideal case for this posture, and it agrees
with DA-9 from the image lane: cut voids survive. A crypt generator should be **subtractive
over a solid block**, not additive from wall runs.

### FD-9 — The rotation obligation is worse for Site 3 than for anyone else **[documented]**

LC-10 is the Lair study's flagged transfer risk: FFT maps are authored for one preferred
camera; two of four rotations of a typical cave map are near-unreadable, and Genesis inherits
an obligation FFT does not have.

**Transfer, and a warning specific to this site.** Dormant sites *increase* the risk, because
their read depends on silhouette (ragged wall heads against sky, openings as voids, one
surviving landmark) and silhouette is the most rotation-sensitive property there is. A ruin
that composes beautifully from the authored angle can become an unreadable pile from the
opposite one. This belongs in the adversarial case for the first proof.
