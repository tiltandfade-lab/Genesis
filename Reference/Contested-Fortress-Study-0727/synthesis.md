# Synthesis — what the research says Site 9 actually is

## The one-paragraph answer

A Contested Fortress is a defensive work **larger than the people currently inside it**, in
which at least two populations hold attributed ground and a readable line runs between them.
Its identity is not the siege; the siege is one licensed condition. Its identity is the
relationship between a set of nested or layered defensive lines and the populations that have
ended up on different sides of them — and the fact that the works are big enough that no
single tactical window can contain the whole argument.

## The nine findings

### 1. The demand is for an interior, not a silhouette

Fifteen of a hundred `dungeon-type` rows carry Military Fortification, and **all fifteen
describe corridors, chambers, stairs and stores** — "geometric corridors with arrow slits,"
"defensive chokepoints every few steps," "guard posts overlook empty corridors." The census
puts that archetype at **14.62 %** of dungeon walks. The demand arrives through the *dungeon
walk* and asks for a fortification **inside**.

Independently, FFT reached the same answer: its most complete fortress is Bethla Garrison,
built as **five separate maps** — sluice, granary, south wall, north wall, and a council
chamber — and the map actually *named* "Bethla Garrison" is an interior room with a table and
banners.

**Consequence:** Site 9's first build should not be a castle exterior.

### 2. A fortress is played as a set of named windows, and the windows are named for objectives

Bethla's five maps are the materialization-window law shipped in a commercial game. Each
window is a distinct operating circuit of one persistent site, and each is named for the thing
being fought over — the water control, the stores, a wall segment, the command room.

**Consequence:** the retained fixture for Site 9 should be a **window set on one site id**,
not one hero board. This is the property that makes Site 9 a scale case rather than a big
Guard Post.

### 3. The wall is two battlefields

FFT built the south wall of Bethla from inside (a broad walk with kiosks on it, parapets both
sides, stairs to a lower court) and the north wall from outside (a tower face with two ranks
of loops across an open boulder-scattered field). Lane 2's Marqab pair shows the same thing in
photographs: a wall-walk in the foreground, the inner works above, and a strip of bare
overlooked ground between them.

**Consequence:** the wall is not a boundary object. It is **terrain with two faces**, and a
generator that only supports the outside view has built half the site.

### 4. The site's dominant *live* state is cohabitation, not siege

Thirteen of a hundred `place-master-setting` rows are fortification settlements, and the
pattern in their own prose is overwhelming: **Thorngate Keep** — "a border garrison … now
housing a growing civilian quarter inside the old killing ground"; **The Borrowed Wall** — "a
hamlet sheltering inside the curtain-wall of a castle that was never theirs, paying rent to a
lord no living soul has met"; **The Garrison-Wife Town** — dependents who outlasted the army.
`place-history` adds "a garrison town that outlived its war and learned to farm."

The live engine's fortresses are almost never under attack. They are **inherited, colonised,
drifted, and shared.**

**Consequence:** this is the single largest correction the research makes. Building Site 9 as
a siege generator would serve a minority of the rolls the engine actually produces.

### 5. The entrance is lengthened on purpose, in three unrelated materials

Beaumaris offsets its outer gate from its inner gatehouse so the way in must travel the narrow
ring between two walls. Marqab makes the attacker climb a long walled ramp under the inner
works. Maiden Castle overlaps earth banks so the entry doubles back on itself. Three
materials, one intention: **make the way in long, turning, and overlooked.**

FFT does the same with a broken causeway across a moat (Igros) and a climbing terraced stair
(Riovanes).

**Consequence:** the lengthened entrance is a `REQUIRED`-grade relation candidate, and it is
material-independent — which is exactly what a cross-cultural site family needs.

### 6. The gate is a sequence, and its machinery is a staffed room

Castle Rushen shows bridge → arch → portcullis → timber door → second arch in one frame.
Castle Rushen's portcullis chamber shows the crew: two figures on a lever over the floor slot,
a third shooting from the window in the same room.

And the engine already has both, unwired: **`Urban Area Type` row 195 Gatehouse** (20′ × 30′
with a *10′ × 20′ murder-hole gallery on the floor above*) and **row 196 Barbican** (30′ × 40′
with a *10′ × 10′ heavy portcullis control room*).

**Consequence:** "operate the gate" is an interior objective with a position, a crew and its
own defenders. And Site 9's most specific room programs are **already authored** — the job is
to wire them, not to invent them.

### 7. Most of a fortress is quiet ground, and buildings line the wall

Caerphilly's stone core is a fifth of its site; the rest is water and platform. Krak sits above
an enormous bare glacis. Maiden Castle's interior is a featureless plateau. Corfe's ruin stands
above an empty grass slope. Beaumaris's inner ward — the most defended space in a concentric
castle — is an **empty lawn** with the ranges pressed against the curtain. FFT's Banished Fort
occupies about a quarter of its plate.

The engine agrees: `urban-scene-frame` 156 gives a Keep Courtyard of **80′ × 80′** whose only
listed content is a parade ground and four corner turrets.

**Consequence:** honest empty space is not a licence Site 9 may use; it is a **requirement**
Site 9 must prove. And the placement rule is concrete: **structures line the enceinte, the
centre stays open.**

### 8. The defensive edge is culturally free at constant cover class

Golconda's parapet is a rank of tall arched merlons each carrying loops and a corbelled
drop-box, stacked in two ranks — the same cover job as a European crenellation with an
entirely different silhouette. `urban-scene-frame` 206 already costs crenellation at
**three-quarters cover**.

**Consequence:** Site 1's ruled defensive-edge equality generalises upward without amendment.
Site 9 inherits it and adds the *second rank* case — a firing level above a firing level.

### 9. The site 8 boundary is clean, and it is about concealment

Site 8 asks *who really controls this* — recognition, permission, concealment, knowledge; its
structural buy is false walls and trapdoors; it can be entirely latent. Site 9 asks *who holds
which ground right now* — attributed territory, a visible front, a defensive envelope both
sides can read.

**The test: remove the concealment and Site 8 collapses; remove the second population's ground
and Site 9 collapses.** A besieged castle with a traitor in the gatehouse is both, and that
compose is worth supporting — a fortress is maximally unlike a tavern, which is precisely the
second host Site 8 needs to prove itself on.

## What the research changed about the site

| the site *before* research | the site *after* research |
|---|---|
| A big castle exterior with an army outside it | A **fortification interior window set** on one persistent site, whose dominant live state is **cohabitation**, not assault |
| One hero board | A **set of named windows** chosen by objective — gate, wall segment, stores, command room, water control |
| Walls as a boundary | Walls as **two-faced terrain** with a route on top and a front beside them |
| Site 1's kit at bigger scale | Site 1's kit **plus** the lengthened entrance, the multi-barrier gate with a staffed machinery room, the wall-walk-as-route, and attributed ground |
| Quiet space as a permission | Quiet space as a **proof obligation** |
