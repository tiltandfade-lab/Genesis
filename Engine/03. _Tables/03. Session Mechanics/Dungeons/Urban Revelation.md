| d20     | Urban Revelation                                                                                                                                                                      |
| :------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1**   | **The crime was committed by the person who reported it** — they needed the investigation to proceed for reasons that had nothing to do with justice.                                 |
| **2**   | **The victim orchestrated their own misfortune** — to frame an enemy, extract sympathy, or create a window for another scheme to operate unimpeded.                                   |
| **3**   | **The city guard is complicit** — not all of them, but enough of the chain of command that "justice" would require burning down the institution the party was asked to serve.         |
| **4**   | **The problem was never the crime; the real issue is the territory dispute** — two factions were using this incident as cover to redraw neighborhood boundaries in blood.              |
| **5**   | **An NPC the party trusted has been feeding false information** — not from malice, but because they were deceived by someone higher up the food chain.                                |
| **6**   | **The threat was a distraction** — while the party investigated, someone else accomplished the true objective elsewhere in the city.                                                 |
| **7**   | **The "missing" person left voluntarily** — they were hiding from someone the party thought was a victim or innocent bystander.                                                      |
| **8**   | **The solution creates a worse crisis** — solving this problem removes a constraint that was keeping something larger in check; the party's victory has an expiration date.           |
| **9**   | **A city official commissioned the crime themselves** — not for the stated reason, but to justify a crackdown on a specific neighborhood or demographic.                              |
| **10**  | **The conspiracy is smaller and more personal than it seemed** — not a ring or organization, but two or three people locked in a vendetta, pulling the city into their private war. |
| **11**  | **The culprit was hired by someone above them** — and that person was hired by someone else; the chain goes higher than the party can currently reach.                               |
| **12**  | **The city's laws and history are the real antagonist** — the crime and all its complications are inevitable products of the way power is structured here.                            |
| **13**  | **A faction has been subtly steering the party's investigation** — not controlling it, but influencing which clues come to light and which stay buried.                              |
| **14**  | **The witness everyone trusted is the one with the greatest motive** — their account was technically true, but constructed to implicate the wrong person.                             |
| **15**  | **The misdirection was deliberate** — someone wanted the party to suspect Person A so that Person B could operate freely on the other side of the city.                              |
| **16**  | **The local hero or beloved figure is the true antagonist** — the party's investigation has implicated someone the neighborhood will not accept as guilty.                            |
| **17**  | **The crime has been repeating for years, always blamed on rotating scapegoats** — the party just found a new one before the usual cycle could turn.                                |
| **18**  | **The victim and the perpetrator are working together** — the "crime" was a performance, and the party was always meant to be the audience, not the investigator.                    |
| **19**  | **The root cause is institutional rot so deep that no single arrest will fix it** — the party has caught a symptom while the disease spreads in branches they haven't looked at.   |
| **20**  | **Everyone involved has a legitimate grievance** — there is no villain here, only competing claims of justice; the party's judgment will satisfy no one.                             |
