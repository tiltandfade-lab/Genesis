# SITE 3 — DORMANT / ABANDONED PLACE

## Build-order ruling

The conceptual foundation is retained. Full research, asset listing, and clay proof
move late—preferably last—because Site 3 consumes the operating families built by the
other Golden Sites. It becomes the portfolio integration test: can every finished host
remain itself after shutdown, neglect, damage, reclamation, or deliberate closure?

One early technical rule remains active: abandonment transforms the host's recorded
facts, ids, and components; it never swaps the host for a generic ruin prefab.

## The plain-English promise

You arrive somewhere that used to work and no longer does. The place should tell you
what people once did there, suggest why that activity stopped, and make the silence
matter to how you explore it now.

The player should feel that they are reading the remains of a real place rather than
walking through a generic pile of ruins.

## What makes it a site family

Site 3 is not one architectural style. It is a transformation family applied to an
existing host: a guard post, waystation, camp structure, lair, monastery range, market
frontage, mine, prison, or later site.

It owns the rules and pieces that explain how a functioning place becomes dormant,
neglected, failed, reclaimed, or deliberately closed. The host still supplies the
original culture and architectural identity.

## Proposed invariants

- **REQUIRED — former purpose remains readable.** A player should be able to form a
  useful idea of what the place was for.
- **REQUIRED — the stopped state has a cause.** Removed supplies, blocked access,
  unfinished work, weather damage, deliberate closure, collapse, or another recorded
  fact explains why it no longer operates.
- **REQUIRED — the present condition changes play.** It must alter access, safety,
  information, shelter, salvage, visibility, or another concrete interaction.
- **REQUIRED — host truth survives.** Original surfaces, routes, ownership history,
  culture, and component provenance are transformed rather than replaced by a generic
  ruin prefab.
- **REQUIRED — no stable new operator owns the place.** Passing scavengers, animals,
  weather, or an encounter are legal. A functioning replacement population promotes
  the site toward Site 8, infiltrated/layered control.
- **DEFAULT — damage is selective.** Most of the place remains coherent enough to make
  the important failure legible.
- **LICENSED — supernatural persistence.** Haunting, magical stasis, cursed closure, or
  impossible preservation appears only when realm and history facts support it.

Simply removing NPCs, scattering rubble everywhere, adding vines, or applying a grey
filter does not satisfy the site.

## Identity, culture, and circumstance

### Site identity

The visible relationship between former function and present absence is the site's
identity.

### Cultural expression

Culture affects how a place is shut, mourned, stripped, repaired, marked as forbidden,
or left for return. It also remains visible in the host's construction. Culture does
not decide whether abandonment always produces one particular rubble or overgrowth
mesh.

### Local circumstance

Climate, material, elapsed time, terrain, disaster, maintenance history, evacuation
speed, wealth, scavenging pressure, and former function decide how the site decays.

## Five-expression family

1. **Small — suspended shelter.** An intact hut, watch shelter, or service shed with
   its work stopped and its useful supplies deliberately removed.
2. **Ordinary — neglected waystation.** The recommended first proof: a once-working
   roadside stop whose service relationships remain obvious while maintenance, warmth,
   provisions, and safe passage have begun to fail.
3. **Large — dead institutional wing.** An inherited monastery, prison, market, mine,
   or fortress portion with repeated rooms falling out of service in a legible pattern.
   The scale belongs to the host; Site 3 supplies the condition transformation.
4. **Degraded — breached and reclaimed.** Collapse or erosion creates an accidental
   route while vegetation, water, snow, sand, or animals occupy the unattended space.
5. **Unusual — deliberately closed.** A culture has sealed, dismantled, marked,
   memorialized, cursed, or ritually retired the place rather than merely walking away.

## Recommended first build

### Operating waystation → dormant waystation

Build one small roadside waystation twice from the same committed host:

1. its last functioning state; and
2. its dormant state after a rolled cause.

Keep the host's footprint, ids, culture, and original purpose stable. Transform only
the facts caused by abandonment. This makes it possible to judge whether the system
is telling a history or merely decorating a ruin.

This first build should teach us:

- how condition changes a functioning assembly without erasing it;
- how a former route becomes blocked, unsafe, or accidentally bypassed;
- how missing supplies and cold or disabled fixtures communicate absence;
- how selective collapse, breach, rubble, and repair pieces attach to host geometry;
- how culture remains visible after the occupants leave; and
- how an unmaintained night scene stays readable without unexplained practical lights.

## First visual proof

Show matched operating/dormant captures from the fixed camera:

- daylight clay comparison;
- dressed comparison with the same host ids;
- motivated night comparison—the dormant site has no maintained flame unless a current
  fact owns it;
- human, small, and large standee checks;
- original entry plus one condition-created blockage, hazard, or breach;
- one salvage or information objective;
- two changed abandonment causes; and
- one adversarial case where excessive damage threatens to erase the former function.

## Proof → MVP → Ideal

### Proof

One operating/dormant waystation pair, two changed causes, one adversarial over-damage
case, and a retained receipt showing exactly which host facts changed.

### MVP

Condition transforms for small exterior and small interior hosts; suspended, neglected,
breached, and reclaimed states; selective rubble/breach kit; culture-owned closure
marks; readable abandoned lighting; and promotion to Site 8 when stable replacement
occupancy appears.

### Ideal

The transformation family works across all twelve Golden Sites, multiple elapsed-time
bands, climate-specific decay, repair and reactivation, large repeated institutions,
Grounded through Mythic causes, and visible historical layers without destroying
canonical host truth.

## Return condition

Resume full concepting after the remaining host families exist. At that point, choose
the first operating/abandoned comparison for maximum portfolio coverage rather than
assuming the waystation is still the best host.
