## Why this sweep exists

Site 3 is classified as a **cross-host transform**, not a host program
(`GOLDEN-SITE-ONTOLOGY-ENGINE-MARRIAGE.md` §6; `SETTLED-LIFE-SITES-PROGRAM.md` §2). The
temptation with a transform is to write it as an adjective — "ruined", "overgrown", "grey"
— and then build one ruin prefab. The sweep's job is to find out how many genuinely
different things "the place stopped working" can be, so that the spec factorizes the state
instead of naming it.

The sweep found **eight distinct source literatures** that each describe a different
mechanism. Only two of them are about decay. That is the finding.

## The eight readings, and what each contributes

### 1. Abandonment is a *decision*, and the decision is legible (behavioural archaeology)

Schiffer's formation-process framework separates *cultural* from *natural* modification of a
site, and gives three terms Genesis can use directly:

- **curate behaviour** — removing objects from a site and transporting them elsewhere in
  anticipation of future use;
- **de facto refuse** — usable or repairable material culture left behind at abandonment; its
  composition is determined by whether abandonment was rapid or gradual and whether return
  was anticipated;
- **draw-down** — the tendency not to replace worn or broken items once people know they are
  about to move.

**Genesis consequence.** *What was taken versus what was left is the primary evidence of how
and why the place stopped.* An orderly, anticipated withdrawal strips the valuable and
portable and leaves the heavy and cheap. A panicked or violent end leaves valuables on the
floor. A gradual decline leaves everything worn out and nothing replaced. This is an
information layer available before any combat, and it is *free* — it costs a per-object flag,
not a mesh.

Sources: [Michael Brian Schiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Brian_Schiffer) ·
[Toward the Identification of Formation Processes](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-antiquity/article/abs/toward-the-identification-of-formation-processes/9B745F18A337FA1F3E130A0760237FDB) ·
[Abandonment processes in prehistoric pueblos](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/abandonment-of-settlements-and-regions/abandonment-processes-in-prehistoric-pueblos/BCF66B551F3A972574EA68FDF7777424)

### 2. Decay has a fixed order, and it starts at the roof (building pathology)

The order is not aesthetic, it is physical: roof failure admits water; water rots the
timbers and destabilises floors; plants colonise the wet fabric; plants bring birds and
animals; floors sag, walls bow, the frame stops carrying load, and the building returns to
rubble. Material choice sets the *clock*, not the *sequence* — a timber-framed house may
hold together roughly 50 years, concrete 50–100, stone well over a century, but a caved-in
roof collapses any of those far sooner.

**Genesis consequence.** The decay ladder is *causal and ordered*, which means a generator
can validate it. A collapse without a source above it is a rejection. Overgrowth on a
building whose roof is sound is a rejection. Material changes the elapsed-time band that
maps to a rung, not the rung order.

Sources: [Why Do Abandoned Buildings Decay So Quickly? (ScienceABC)](https://www.scienceabc.com/eyeopeners/why-do-abandoned-buildings-decay-so-quickly) ·
[Getty CI — Condition Assessment: Material and Building Pathology](https://www.getty.edu/conservation/publications_resources/teaching/ea_material.pdf)

### 3. Ruins are quarries, and robbing is a *human* vector with its own logic (spolia)

Abandoned, ruined or undesirable structures are excellent quarries: pre-cut slabs,
thresholds, lintels, columns and capitals, already shaped, already transported. Late-antique
and medieval builders ran an industry on it — there were guilds of dismantlers
(*subrutores*), warehouses of recovered material, and resale lists. Reuse was also
*symbolic*: incorporating a prestigious fragment claimed legitimacy or displayed triumph.

**Genesis consequence.** Robbing is not decay. It is selective, intelligent, and it targets
exactly the elements that would otherwise survive longest — dressed stone, lintels, metal
fixings, timber. It therefore *inverts* the survivability ladder at chosen points, and it
leaves a distinctive negative trace (a robbed-out footing, a lintel-less opening that then
collapsed, a doorway with its jambs gone). It is also a live faction hook: someone is
quarrying this place *now*, and they have a cart, a route, and an opinion about the party.

Sources: [The Paradox of "Spolia"](https://www.labrujulaverde.com/en/2025/12/the-paradox-of-spolia-the-reuse-of-building-materials-in-late-antiquity-and-the-early-middle-ages/) ·
[Spolia — Peter Sommer Travels](https://www.petersommer.com/blog/archaeology-history/spolia) ·
[Wells Cathedral spolia case study](https://www.archiwest.co.uk/post/wells-cathedral-spolia)

### 4. Closure can be a *ritual act*, and it preserves rather than degrades

Termination or closure rituals are archaeologically well attested: subterranean structures
ritually "closed" by burning roofs and depositing special objects; intact cooking vessels
placed deliberately in storage containers as a final respectful mark at room abandonment;
burnt offerings, valuables placed on floors, vessels purposefully destroyed in patterned
ways. Termination deposits signify the closure of communication with supernatural powers at
an abandoned building.

**Genesis consequence.** This is the *opposite* of neglect and must be a separate axis. A
ritually closed place is **sealed, furnished, and intact** — the valuables are still there
because leaving them was the point. The gameplay problem inverts: the difficulty is getting
in and deciding whether to violate the closure, not surviving the collapse. It also supplies
the culture layer with real work: how a culture shuts a place is a strong, cheap cultural
signature.

Sources: [Reverential abandonment: a termination ritual at El Palmar (Antiquity)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/reverential-abandonment-a-termination-ritual-at-the-ancient-maya-polity-of-el-palmar/CC2C7A3307927BB1AC8548E2D16E9FE0) ·
[Plotting abandonment: a ritual deposit at Cerro Baúl](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0278416518300692) ·
[Not Seeing Is Believing: Ritual Practice and Architecture at Çadır Höyük](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/12/8/665)

### 5. Industry has a formal *dormant-but-alive* state: care and maintenance

Mining and process industry distinguish **care and maintenance / mothballing** (production
stopped, site actively managed so it stays safe and stable and can restart) from
**decommissioning** (permanent end; everything not destined to remain is removed) from
**closure/sealing** (shafts backfilled, built-in components removed where feasible or sealed
over where not). Mothballing is *expensive* — idled assets still need inspection, corrosion
protection, and maintenance staff.

**Genesis consequence.** This is the missing rung at the top of the ladder. A place can be
**stopped but held**: nothing rots, the doors are locked, someone walks the site once a
month, and the machinery is greased and wrapped. That is a completely different level from a
roofless shell, it is *more* dangerous socially (somebody still owns it) and *less* dangerous
physically, and no existing Genesis site covers it. It also names the transform's reverse
verb: **restart**.

Sources: [Care and maintenance (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Care_and_maintenance) ·
[Mine closure: decommissioning and reclamation (GTK)](https://mineclosure.gtk.fi/mine-closure-consists-of-decommissioning-and-mine-site-reclamation/) ·
[Mothballing — a guide](https://www.maintenanceandengineering.com/2020/04/23/mothballing-a-guide/) ·
[Backfilling shafts](https://www.darda.de/en/knowledge/backfilling-shaft)

### 6. Reclamation is a *succession*, with a name and a predictable order

Ruderal ecology — from Latin *rudus*, rubble; the term was popularised studying the plant
communities that arrived in the ruins of post-war Berlin — describes the disturbance-tolerant
strategy: fast growth, annual/biennial life cycles, huge seed output, high dispersal, first
to cover bare ground. Ruderal vegetation is the first sign of secondary succession.
Successional change in vegetation then alters fauna composition, abundance, and
predator–prey dynamics.

**Genesis consequence.** Overgrowth is not a texture, it is a *timed sequence with a
substrate preference*: bare disturbed ground and horizontal ledges first, joints and
verticals next, canopy last. And the ecology change is what legitimately brings animals in —
the animal occupant is a *consequence* of the plant stage, not a separate roll.

Sources: [Ruderal Resilience (Frontiers in Built Environment)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/built-environment/articles/10.3389/fbuil.2022.769357/full) ·
[Ruderal plants and ecological succession](https://livetoplant.com/ruderal-plants-and-their-role-in-ecological-succession/) ·
[Barn owl dietary shift following partial field abandonment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11394017/)

### 7. Reoccupation is real, common, and it *ends* the transform

Roman amphitheatres were not left empty. At Arles the open spaces and sturdy walls became
the foundation of a small fortified community — nearly 200 houses within the walls, plus
chapels and four towers, inhabited into the early 19th century. At Lucca the *cunei* were
converted into dwellings and the oval became an urban square, passing through fortress,
warehouse and prison on the way.

**Genesis consequence.** This is exactly the boundary the invariant already draws: *no stable
new operator owns the place.* A settled reoccupation is not a dormant site — it is a new host
program wearing an inherited shell, and it belongs to Site 8 (layered control) or Site 10
(urban institution). Site 3 owns only the *transient* claimants: scavengers, animals, a
camp in one wing, a robbing crew with a cart. Naming this from the literature makes the
invariant defensible rather than arbitrary.

Sources: [Arles, Roman and Romanesque Monuments (UNESCO)](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/164/) ·
[The Roman Amphitheatre in Lucca](https://www.luccaapartmentsandvillas.co.uk/blog/roman-amphitheatre-lucca/) ·
[The Roman Amphitheater of Arles](https://www.thearchaeologist.org/blog/the-roman-amphitheater-of-arles-a-monument-of-antiquity-and-modernity)

### 8. Why places stop is mostly *slow and economic*, not dramatic

Deserted medieval village scholarship is blunt: most desertions are economic — marginal land
failing, soil exhaustion, climatic deterioration from c. 1200, enclosure through the 15th–16th
centuries, rivers changing course or silting, coastal erosion, windblown sand. Plague
hastened an already-running decline rather than causing most desertions; most DMVs actually
became deserted in the 15th century, after the mid-14th-century Black Death.

**Genesis consequence.** The cause table must be weighted toward the mundane. "A dragon ate
them" is a licensed Mythic-band result, not the default. The default causes are *the road
moved*, *the seam ran out*, *the water failed*, *the lord enclosed it*, *the rent rose*, *the
war took the men*. Those causes are also better hooks, because they leave a *counterparty* —
somebody benefited.

Sources: [Deserted medieval village (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserted_medieval_village) ·
[Causes of Desertion — Beresford's Lost Villages](https://dmv.wordpress.hull.ac.uk/index.php/villages/causes-of-desertion/) ·
[Abandoned village (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserted_village)

## Funerary — a separate reading, because the census demands it

Subterranean Crypt is 21.3 % of dungeon rolls (`docs/intel/walk-census.md` §2a). It is the
single largest bucket in the census and it was mapped to Site 3 on the reasonable but
untested ground that "funerary/tomb reads as the abandoned/condition family". The sweep says
that mapping is **half right**, and the half that is wrong is load-bearing.

**Funerary architecture is a host program with its own operating model.** The vocabulary is
specific and mechanical:

- **loculi** — rectangular burial niches cut lengthwise into gallery walls, stacked several
  tiers high, sealed with marble slabs, tiles or bricks;
- **arcosolia** — arched recesses, the higher-status version;
- **cubicula** — small burial chambers off the galleries; **hypogeum** for a private
  rock-cut burial place;
- **ossuary / charnel house / dead house** — a graded chain: bodies kept until the flesh has
  gone, then bones removed to a bone house.

That chain — receive, prepare, deposit, seal, commemorate, later exhume and translate to
bulk storage — is an *operating model*, with roles, capacities, property flows and failure
modes. A crypt with a living cult is a maintained institution, not a dormant place; the
Hallstatt charnel image in Lane 2 is the proof (painted, dated, named skulls under candles).

**And tombs have their own adversary, with their own route.** Most Valley of the Kings tombs
were robbed within a century of sealing. Burial chambers were deliberately sited and passages
filled with debris to prevent theft; robbers responded by tunnelling — some were stonecutters
who knew which bedrock was soft enough to cut, and evidence across the Old, Middle and New
Kingdoms shows crews working patiently over long periods to drive a tunnel into a tomb they
believed was rich. Robbery is detectable from the state of the seals and the disturbance of
the contents.

**Genesis consequence — the single biggest gameplay find in the sweep.** A funerary site
comes with **two structurally different routes for free**: the *ceremonial way* (designed,
generous, sealed, watched, socially and ritually expensive) and the *robbers' way* (narrow,
unlit, unsupported, capacity-limited, undesigned, often one-way). That satisfies the
strategic-gameplay law's "at least two viable approaches with different advantages, risks and
consequences" without inventing anything.

Sources: [Loculus (architecture)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loculus_(architecture)) ·
[Structure of the Catacombs in Rome](https://www.catacombsociety.org/the-structures-of-the-catacombs/) ·
[Catacombs (Encyclopedia.com)](https://www.encyclopedia.com/social-sciences-and-law/anthropology-and-archaeology/archaeology-general/catacombs) ·
[Tomb Robbing in Ancient Egypt (World History Encyclopedia)](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/1095/tomb-robbing-in-ancient-egypt/) ·
[Tomb Robberies — Theban Mapping Project](https://thebanmappingproject.com/articles/tomb-robberies) ·
[Hidden History of Tomb Robbing (Smithsonian)](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/discover-the-hidden-history-of-tomb-robbing-in-ancient-egypt-180984895/)

## Sunken — a separate reading, because Sunken Estate is 10.3 %

Drowned settlements behave differently from dry ruins. At Ladybower the churches, pubs,
farms, schools and homes of Derwent and Ashopton went under in 1943, including the 1672 manor
Derwent Hall; the remains of buildings, roads and bridges become visible again when the water
recedes. One structure survived by being *moved* — the 17th-century packhorse bridge was
rebuilt stone by stone upstream — and the church spire was left standing as a memorial, broke
the surface in 1947, and was then demolished.

**Genesis consequence.** Water is a *level datum*, not a texture. It sorts the site by
height: everything below the datum is preserved-but-inaccessible; everything at the datum
carries a band of altered material; everything above weathers normally. Drawdown is a
first-class *state change* that opens the map — and it is a clock the player can sometimes
push (breach the sluice, block the leat, wait for the dry season). Note also that the engine's
own `dungeon-type` row for Sunken Estate already says "Original Purpose: Manor house /
Basement levels" — the table has already committed to the below-grade half being the
playable half.

Sources: [The Secret Drowned Villages Under Ladybower](https://letsgopeakdistrict.co.uk/the-secret-drowned-villages-under-ladybower-reservoir/) ·
[Drowned villages of the Peak District](https://www.thestar.co.uk/retro/21-haunting-images-capture-drowned-villages-flooded-to-create-peak-district-reservoir-5229248) ·
[Ancient villages now sitting on the bottom of reservoirs](https://ruralhistoria.com/2023/09/04/ancient-villages-now-sitting-on-the-bottom-of-reservoirs/)

## Declared gaps in this sweep

1. **No non-European vernacular decay evidence.** Every image in Lane 2 and most written
   sources are British, Italian, Egyptian or North American. Mudbrick, rammed earth, timber-
   only, and stilt/raft construction decay on completely different clocks and in different
   orders (mudbrick slumps into its own footprint; a thatch-and-timber house can vanish
   entirely). The elapsed-time bands proposed in the spec are therefore **stone-and-timber
   biased and should be treated as one construction card, not the law**.
2. **No measured decay-rate data.** The "five years of ageing per year empty" figure quoted in
   the popular source is not a measurement and is not used in the spec. The elapsed bands are
   ordinal, not numeric.
3. **No cold-climate or desert case.** Permafrost, snow-load collapse, sand burial and salt
   weathering each reorder the ladder; none were researched here.
4. **Reoccupation is documentary only** — no image evidence in this packet (see the image-gap
   table in `images/LICENSE-LEDGER.md`).
5. **The 55-count wilderness Site-3 mapping in the census is a disclosed keyword heuristic**
   (walk-census §4 caveat 5), not a founder ruling. The two dungeon numbers (21.3 % and
   10.3 %) come from `walk.setup.type` and are exact.
