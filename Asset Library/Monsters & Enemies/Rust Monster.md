*p. 261 MM 2024*

The rust monster is one of the most dreaded creatures in any dungeon — not because of its combat power, which is mediocre, but because of what it does to the party's equipment. A rust monster encounter that ends without anyone losing a weapon or a set of armor is a successful rust monster encounter. The creature itself is basically harmless to unarmored targets. Against a heavily-armored fighter with a magical longsword, it is an existential threat.

The key to running a rust monster well is to make the threat *feel* real before the encounter. The party should have context: they know metal equipment is vulnerable, they know the rust monster targets the highest-quality items first (drawn by the scent of refined metal), and they know nonmagical items are destroyed on contact while magical items must survive Constitution-like saves. When the rust monster enters, the fighter's player feels a spike of genuine panic. That's the encounter doing its job.

Rust monsters are not malicious — they are hungry. They eat metal the way a goat eats tin cans: compulsively and enthusiastically. They have no interest in creatures except as obstacles between themselves and the iron smell of good equipment. A party that manages to offer them unneeded metal gear can sometimes distract a rust monster entirely without combat.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Rust monsters leave a very specific trail.

- **The Oxidized Metal:** Any metal in a rust monster's habitual territory develops orange-brown corrosion unusually fast. Iron brackets, chains, and hinges in rust monster areas are eaten through and crumbling. DC 12 Investigation to identify the pattern as abnormal for the age of the metal.
- **The Rusted Weapon:** Finding a weapon or piece of armor reduced to a crumbling orange lump tells the story without text. A DC 11 History check can identify what the weapon was from the remaining shape. Someone fought a rust monster here and lost their gear.
- **The Missing Iron:** Dungeon fixtures in rust monster territory are missing their iron fittings entirely — the rust monster consumed them at the source. A door with no hinges, a portcullis that's been eaten away, iron wall sconces reduced to stumps.

### 1. Rust Monster

A scuttling, antennae-waving menace that eats metal and senses refined ore like a bloodhound.

_Medium Monstrosity, Unaligned_

- **Armor Class:** 14 (natural armor)
- **Hit Points:** 27 (5d8 + 5)
- **Speed:** 40 ft.
- **STR:** 13 (+1) | **DEX:** 12 (+1) | **CON:** 13 (+1) | **INT:** 2 (−4) | **WIS:** 13 (+1) | **CHA:** 6 (−2)
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 11
- **Languages:** —
- **Challenge:** 1/2 (100 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Iron Scent:** The rust monster can pinpoint, by scent, the location of ferrous metal within 30 ft.
- **Rust Metal:** Any nonmagical weapon made of metal that hits the rust monster corrodes. After dealing damage, the weapon takes a permanent −1 penalty to damage rolls. If its penalty drops to −5, the weapon is destroyed. Nonmagical ammunition made of metal that hits the rust monster is destroyed after dealing damage.

**Actions**

- **Bite:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +3 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 5 (1d8 + 1) piercing damage.
- **Antennae:** The rust monster corrodes a nonmagical ferrous metal object it can see within 5 ft. of it. If the object isn't being worn or carried, the touch destroys a 1-foot cube of it. If the object is being worn or carried by a creature, the creature can make a DC 11 Dexterity saving throw to avoid the rust monster's touch. On a failed save, the object takes a permanent −1 penalty to the AC it offers. Armor reduced to an AC of 10 or a shield that drops to a +0 bonus is destroyed. If the object touched is a held metal weapon, it is subject to the Rust Metal trait.

### 2. Rust Monster Swarm (Rusting Pack)

Three or four rust monsters working in proximity — not truly cooperative but simultaneously targeting different party members. The combined iron scent reaction creates chaotic movement as each creature pursues a different metal source.

_Use 3–4 individual rust monster stat blocks in the same encounter._

**Encounter Note:** When running multiple rust monsters, assign each one a priority target based on the metal quality/quantity each PC carries. A wizard in robes is of no interest; a paladin in full plate is the highest priority. Use this to create dramatic tactical choices: does the fighter hold position to protect the party, or withdraw to protect their armor at the cost of leaving allies exposed?
