*p. 332 MM 2024*

Zombies occupy the lowest rung of the undead hierarchy — mindless, rotting husks driven by a single, dim compulsion to destroy the living. Where skeletons are at least obedient, zombies are barely controllable. They shamble, they pile up, they absorb punishment that would kill anything else, and they keep dragging themselves forward. A single zombie is pathetic. Twenty zombies with Undead Fortitude is a nightmare.

The horror of zombies is not their power but their origin. Every zombie was a person. Unlike skeletons, where the identity is stripped away, zombies often wear their former lives on their decomposing faces — old uniforms, farmer's tools still clutched in dead hands, children's clothes. A necromancer who raises zombies from a local village is making a statement: *I will turn your dead against you.*

Ogre zombies are particularly dangerous. The necrotic energy that animates a massive body doesn't translate to precision — ogre zombies are even stupider and slower than their humanoid counterparts — but their raw durability and reach make them valuable siege tools for a necromancer who needs a door broken down.

### Campaign Integration: Investigation & Clues

Zombies signal an active, local necromancy problem — they don't keep indefinitely without maintenance.

- **The Smell:** Zombies smell. Badly. A party can usually detect a zombie presence before they see one. Persistent stench of rot in an area that should be dry and clean is a reliable indicator.
- **The Drag Marks:** Zombies don't lift their feet properly. They leave distinctive drag marks and broken nails in the dirt or stonework wherever they walk. A DC 11 Survival check confirms the tracks and estimates how many.
- **The Fresh Dead:** Finding a zombie that's *too fresh* — barely days old — means someone raised it recently and is still in the area. Old, desiccated zombies suggest an abandoned site; fresh ones mean an active threat.

### 3. Zombie Plague Carrier

A zombie whose corpse has festered into something particularly foul — the rot has become infectious. Shambling through close quarters with one of these is a serious health risk.

_Medium Undead, Neutral Evil_

- **Armor Class:** 8
- **Hit Points:** 22 (3d8 + 9)
- **Speed:** 20 ft.
- **STR:** 13 (+1) | **DEX:** 6 (−2) | **CON:** 16 (+3) | **INT:** 3 (−4) | **WIS:** 6 (−2) | **CHA:** 5 (−3)
- **Saving Throws:** WIS +0
- **Damage Immunities:** Poison
- **Condition Immunities:** Exhaustion, Poisoned
- **Senses:** Darkvision 60 ft., passive Perception 8
- **Languages:** understands all languages it knew in life, but cannot speak
- **Challenge:** 1/2 (100 XP) | **Proficiency Bonus:** +2

**Traits**

- **Undead Fortitude:** If damage reduces the zombie to 0 hit points, it must make a Constitution saving throw with a DC of 5 + the damage taken, unless the damage is radiant or from a critical hit. On a success, the zombie drops to 1 hit point instead.
- **Festering Aura:** Any creature that starts its turn within 5 ft. of the plague carrier must succeed on a DC 12 Constitution saving throw or be **Poisoned** until the start of its next turn.

**Actions**

- **Slam:** _Melee Weapon Attack:_ +3 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target. _Hit:_ 4 (1d6 + 1) bludgeoning damage. On a hit, the target must succeed on a DC 12 Constitution saving throw or contract **Zombie Rot** (see below).

**Zombie Rot:** A creature afflicted with Zombie Rot gains one level of Exhaustion at the end of each long rest unless cured by a *lesser restoration* spell or similar magic. It is not contagious beyond the initial exposure.

### d10 The Rot Scene (Investigation & Clues)

| d10 | Discovery | Investigative Hook |
|:----|:----------|:------------------|
| **1** | **The Feeding Trough** | A long stone trough is filled with rotting offal. The zombies were being maintained — whoever ran this place was feeding the undead to slow their decomposition. |
| **2** | **The Bite Mark** | A dead body found in the room has a distinctive bite pattern from a zombie. A DC 13 Medicine check identifies that the victim was bitten *after* they were already dead — the zombie was feeding on a corpse, not killing. Implies it was recently animated from something that died naturally. |
| **3** | **The Branded Dead** | Every zombie in the area has the same brand on its left forearm — a merchant's mark or a cult symbol. These people were enslaved before they were killed. |
| **4** | **The Child's Drawing** | A crude drawing is pinned to the wall — stick figures, a house, a sun. One of the zombies in the room is child-sized. Someone in this dungeon has done something unforgivable. |
| **5** | **The Ledger** | A necromancer's log tracks the zombies by number, not name. The entries stop abruptly three weeks ago. The last entry reads "containment failing." |
| **6** | **The Survivor Account** | A barely-alive prisoner in the room whispers that the zombies were locals — kidnapped from nearby farms over the past month. They can name the necromancer responsible. |
| **7** | **The Resistance** | One zombie behaves slightly differently — it hesitates, it twitches, it doesn't immediately attack. A DC 15 Arcana or Insight check suggests a sliver of will is still fighting the animation. *Lesser restoration* or a *dispel magic* at 3rd level might free it — revealing a witness. |
| **8** | **The Rotter's Trail** | Drag marks and rotting flesh scraps lead from this room to a larger chamber beyond. Something *very large* was recently animated here and dragged out. |
| **9** | **The Mass Grave** | A concealed floor panel opens onto a pit filled with bodies in various stages of decomposition — a supply depot of future zombies. Some of the bodies at the bottom are still fresh. |
| **10** | **The Mirror** | A full-length mirror hangs in the zombie-infested room. Zombies don't react to reflections. The mirror is enchanted — a scrying focus. Someone has been watching through it. |
