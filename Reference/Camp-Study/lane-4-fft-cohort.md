# Lane 4 — The FFT cohort: what the corpus actually offers the camp

## 0. Method and view semantics

**A useful discovery about the corpus, recorded for the next study.** The five views are not five
isometric rotations. **View 0 is a near-orthographic top-down plan; views 1–4 are the four
isometric rotations.** That makes view 0 a free plan-diagram of every map in the game — the single
best asset in the archive for reading topology, route corridors, and mass placement. The guard-post
study's cohort work leaned on the isometrics; the plan view is faster for exactly the questions the
camp is asking.

**Sampling.** 46 map ids read at view 0 (systematic coverage of every terrain and settlement family
in the corpus, exhaustive across the wilderness block 71–91), plus 11 additional isometric views on
the strongest candidates — **57 images total.** Ids listed in §4.

---

## 1. The plain answer: the corpus contains NO camps

**There is not a single tent, bedroll, campfire-with-shelters, encampment, bivouac, or waystation
in the 121-map corpus.** Not one. The nearest things to a camp are a prison, a graveyard, and two
slums. `fft_map_list` confirms it by name as well as by eye: the 121 display names contain no
"camp," "tent," "inn," "waystation," "caravan," or "post."

This is not an accident of sampling; it is a design fact about Final Fantasy Tactics. The game's
map set is built from **settlements, fortifications, religious buildings, interiors, and named
wilderness** — the *permanent* world. Temporary occupation is not a place type in FFT.

**So the lesson the corpus offers is the one the brief anticipated: FFT's open-clearing and
forest-pocket terrain grammar** — the camp's banked §10.3 dimension — **plus three surrogate maps
that each solve one piece of the camp problem in another material.** Both are reported below.

---

## 2. The clearing and forest-pocket grammar (the actual lesson)

Read across the wilderness block (71–91), FFT's outdoor maps behave with unusual consistency.

**2.1 — A clearing is defined by what surrounds it, never by what is in it.**
MAP085 *Mandalia Plains* is the purest case: an almost flat green field, and the entire composition
is a scatter of pale rock spires standing on it. Remove the rocks and there is no map. The rocks are
doing four jobs at once — **cover, sightline break, silhouette punctuation, and edge definition** —
and none of them is decoration. MAP084 *Bariaus Hill* does the same with a denser boulder field;
MAP081 *Grog Hill* does it with a paved route cross instead.

**2.2 — Vertical punctuation comes from the boundary, and it is allowed to leave the frame.**
MAP080 *Araguay Woods* is the strongest example in the corpus: five enormous tree trunks rise
straight out of the play surface and **exit the top of the frame entirely**, reading as black
columns. The playable ground beneath is a modest, gently modelled forest floor. FFT is completely
comfortable letting boundary objects be taller than the camera. MAP074 *Sweegy Woods* uses conifers
at the corners for the same purpose at smaller scale.

**2.3 — The forest pocket is a bowl with a raised centre.**
Sweegy Woods, Araguay Woods and Yuguo Woods all put a modest raised mass at or near the centre of a
lower surround, framed by trees at the corners. The dominant/supporting/quiet mass hierarchy holds,
but at outdoor sites the *dominant mass is terrain*, and the corners are reserved for framing
verticals.

**2.4 — Quiet ground is enormous.**
In every wilderness map the majority of surface area carries nothing. Mandalia Plains is perhaps 70%
empty grass. This is the strongest possible confirmation of relational rule 7, and it is the single
most important number for a camp: **a camp that fills its clearing has already failed.**

**2.5 — Water and rock do the edge work.**
MAP087 *Bariaus Valley*, MAP088 *Finath River*, MAP083 *Zirekile Falls*: a river region cuts the map
and the composed edge is where the water leaves. MAP086 *Doguola Pass* and MAP090 *Germinas Peak*
use rock walls the same way. Camps sited beside water inherit the edge for free.

**2.6 — Outdoor maps still have architecture, and it is always small, always timber, always
circulation.** Doguola Pass has plank stairs and a small bridge; Germinas Peak has an entire lashed
timber catwalk system climbing between rock spires; Bariaus Hill has small stonework. FFT never
leaves a wilderness map completely unbuilt — it inserts **one small human-made circulation object**
to prove the place is used.

## 3. The three surrogates — the maps that solve a piece of the camp problem

### 3.1 MAP100 *Public Cemetery* — the camp's geometry problem, already solved

This is the most valuable single map in the corpus for Site 2, and it is not a camp.

It is a broad flat grass clearing containing **about a dozen small repeated stone masses**
(tombs), arranged in **two loose rows flanking an open central avenue**, with a low rubble line
crossing the field, one small red-roofed structure at the centre, and **four tall dark conifers
placed asymmetrically as vertical punctuation**.

That is *structurally identical to a camp*: many small identical shelters, a claimed centre, a
reserved circulation void, and framing verticals. The lessons transfer directly:

- **Repeated small masses read as one place when they share an orientation and a rhythm.** Every
  tomb faces the same way. (Compare lane 2's "everything faces the same way.")
- **The rows are loose, not gridded** — they read as ordered without reading as drawn.
- **The centre is left open and given one object.** Exactly the fire-at-the-centre capsule.
- **Four tall trees carry the whole vertical composition** while the built objects stay under
  knee height. A camp of 2–3h tents in a clearing has the same profile problem and can use the
  same answer.
- **The masses are far apart.** Spacing between tombs is roughly equal to their own width or more.

### 3.2 MAP048 *Slums of Zarghidas* (with MAP032 *Slums in Dorter*, MAP040 *Slums in Goug*) — the shanty profile, already solved

Zarghidas is a stack of timber-and-plaster boxes on stepped terraces above water, with **plank
walkways threading between them at several levels**, small green pockets left over between
buildings, and roofs of many different pitches and colours packed together. Dorter's slum is the
same read in brown timber and mud; Goug's is the same in grey stone and dark timber.

- **Density is expressed by roof adjacency**, not by ground coverage. Roofs touch and overlap.
- **The walkway is the street**, and it is at multiple levels — circulation is the tactical
  substance of the map.
- **Left-over green pockets** appear between buildings, irregular and small. Those pockets *are*
  the quiet ground at this density.
- **Borrowed walls are visible**: units share walls and lean on each other and on retaining faces.
- This is the closest FFT gets to the camp brief's urban-hosted "shanty quarter," and it validates
  the ruling that the shanty quarter is the waystation's urban sibling — Zarghidas' slum is
  unmistakably *the same site type* as its trade city, one rung down.

### 3.3 MAP091 *Thieves Fort* and MAP090 *Germinas Peak* — the lashed-timber vernacular

- **Thieves Fort**: a dense block of plaster and heavy timber standing over water, approached by
  **plank walkways on piles**, with the timber laid on in visibly ad-hoc slabs and a small green
  court trapped inside. It is a *bandit occupancy* rendered as construction: not fortification, but
  **accretion and screening**. The camp brief's bandit profile has a direct FFT ancestor here.
- **Germinas Peak**: rock spires linked by an entirely **lashed timber catwalk, stair and bridge
  system** with open railed decks. This is the closest thing in the corpus to the camp's
  occupancy-licensed **lashed timber watch platform**, and it proves FFT is willing to make raw
  timber circulation a primary visual subject.

### 3.4 Two negative surrogates worth naming

- **MAP063 *Golgorand Execution Site*** — a masonry-walled grass yard with one large timber
  assembly (scaffold + stair) in the middle. It is the **yard-with-one-object** grammar lane 3
  extracted from caravanserais, arriving from the other direction. Useful for the waystation.
- **MAP093/094 *Broke Down Shed*** — two tiny single-room interiors, wooden and stone. They show
  how little FFT needs for a "shed": one room, one opening, one floor. The waystation's standing
  shed does not need to be more than this.

## 5. Findings (plain English)

1. **FFT has no camps.** Zero tents, zero bivouacs, zero waystations across 121 maps. The game maps
   only the permanent world. Site 2 is therefore the first golden site with **no FFT precedent to
   copy the composition from** — which raises the stakes on lanes 1–3 and on Adam's taste pass.
2. **View 0 of the corpus is a top-down plan, not a fifth isometric.** That is a free plan-diagram
   for every map in the game and the fastest way to read topology. Worth recording for future
   studies.
3. **The clearing grammar is the real prize.** A clearing is defined by its boundary, not its
   contents; roughly 70% of a wilderness map is quiet ground; and rock/tree clusters do cover,
   sightline, silhouette and edge simultaneously.
4. **FFT lets boundary verticals leave the frame.** Araguay Woods' tree trunks exit the top of the
   image as black columns. Genesis's camp could use exactly this for a forest pocket — enormous
   trunks at the envelope, nothing tall inside.
5. **Public Cemetery is the camp's composition problem already solved in stone.** Repeated small
   identical masses, loose rows either side of an open centre avenue, one central object, four tall
   asymmetric conifers carrying the vertical. Copy the *relations*, never the map.
6. **Spacing is generous.** In the cemetery the gaps between masses are at least as wide as the
   masses. A camp packed tighter than that will read as a village.
7. **The slum maps are the shanty profile, complete.** Multi-level plank walkways as streets, roofs
   touching, leftover green pockets, borrowed walls. They also confirm the ruling that the shanty
   quarter is the urban sibling of the waystation — the slum and the trade city are visibly the same
   site type one rung apart.
8. **Thieves Fort is the bandit occupancy rendered as construction** — accretion and screening
   rather than fortification. And **Germinas Peak proves FFT will make raw lashed timber a primary
   subject**, which is the licence the camp's watch platform needs visually.
9. **FFT always inserts one small human-made circulation object into wilderness** — a plank stair, a
   bridge, a paved cross. The camp's "customary ground" rung (fire stones, cleared pads, a cache)
   is the same instinct: prove the place is used.
10. **Water and rock supply the composed edge for free.** A camp sited in a river bend or against a
    rock wall inherits its envelope and needs less invented boundary.
11. **The corpus includes debug/test maps** (id 119 is a checkerboard). Any future automated pass
    over the archive should exclude 116–120 rather than treat them as content.
