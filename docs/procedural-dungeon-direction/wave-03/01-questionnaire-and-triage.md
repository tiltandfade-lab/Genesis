#### Inherited laws Wave 3 may deepen but not silently contradict

- **Semantic authority precedes geometry.** Rolls, walks, site programs, histories, owners, and graph edges author
  intent and truth. A deterministic compiler realizes them; a renderer only projects the compiled result.
- **The five-foot mechanical grid survives.** Boundaries may gain subslots and visual objects may gain local
  transforms, but rendering resolution does not rewrite gameplay space.
- **Function, builder/history, current use, and condition remain distinct.** Empty rooms may be meaningful; rare
  incompatibility is valuable only when a legal explanation survives.
- **Reusable structural stamps plus signature bundles are accepted.** Rotation/mirroring are ordinarily licensed;
  stretching follows declared fixed/ranged/bay/path/organic/variant policies rather than arbitrary scaling.
- **Portals, circulation, interaction fronts, supports, hazards, and tactical clearance are first-class
  reservations.** Later decoration cannot accidentally consume them.
- **Creature scale does not enlarge every dungeon.** Connected scale domains and explicit transitions carry mixed
  inhabitants, vehicles, crowds, and guises.
- **Discovered architecture never rerolls.** Persist the compiled plan; explicit receipts alone change it.
- **Bounded constructive solving comes first.** Hard validity precedes soft composition; backtracking, candidate
  scoring, degradation, and plain-language unsatisfied diagnostics precede speculative ML or universal SMT.
- **Wave 10 projection law is binding.** BattleMat/SceneTray, EngagementLens, cards, accessibility, provider-
  neutral DM narration, and visual fallbacks consume canonical architecture; they do not become a second owner.
- **Current no-cash phasing is binding.** One retained Mac dungeon proof comes first; broader corpora, specialized
  expertise, paid compute, and human evidence activate only through named triggers.

### 12.3 Recovered local design evidence

#### Scale-domain sketch

The remembered sketch is already accepted in `FOUNDATION.md` section 2.9. Architecture and creature scale constrain
each other; builder-first architecture, inhabitant-shaped architecture, natural selection, and repurposed mismatch
are different states. Compatible connected domains use wider portals/corridors, taller ceilings, larger steps, and
explicit transition rooms. Incompatible occupants are rare and require a legal explanation such as guise,
squeezing, amorphous movement, breach, an external lair, a trapped resident, servants, or recent invasion. Adam's
answer reaffirms this law and adds colonies/occupation as a clear repurposed-mismatch example.

#### What GaneshaDx contributes—and what it does not

The GPL-3.0 GaneshaDx repository remains research-only; no code or assets are imported. Its useful lesson is a data
and proof boundary, not a Genesis layout algorithm: tactical terrain tiles own positions, heights, slopes,
thickness/surface classes, and passability while decorative polygons are a separate projection. Its greybox
reconstruction and four diagonal views support Genesis's one-way `canonical data -> mesh` pipeline and four-yaw
acceptance gate.

The missing Genesis level-design step is still `TacticalCompositionPlan`: select one legal authored-feeling
arrangement inside `SpatialPlanV2` without inventing or dropping graph edges. The accepted sequence is:

```text
rolls / walk / venue program
  -> SpatialIntent
  -> SpatialPlanV2 legality
  -> TacticalCompositionPlan
  -> SurfaceAssemblyPlan
  -> BattleMap / SceneTray / TownTray
  -> governed shots, materials, sprites, and DM-seat facts
```

GaneshaDx strengthens the representation and QA case. It does not decide how Genesis should place a shrine, route,
stair, ambush shelf, and giant-scale occupation into one coherent new site. That remains a bounded constructive
solver plus composition-scoring problem.

### 12.4 Material and variation research finding

Adam's layered-art instinct is correct, with one important separation: architecture materials, reusable 3D props,
and pixel-art figures do not all want the same treatment.

Adobe Substance 3D Designer graphs produce reusable 2D material outputs; graph instances and exposed parameters
allow one weathering or material graph to create controlled variants, including one exposed control driving several
nodes. Substance 3D Painter Smart Materials are reusable layer stacks whose generators and filters respond to
baked mesh maps such as normal, world-space normal, ambient occlusion, curvature, position, and material ID. This
is why grime can collect in corners and edge wear can follow geometry. Decals are separately bounded multi-channel
stamps suited to cracks, stains, soot, blood, signs, repair seams, and impacts.

The resulting Genesis variation stack is:

1. **Canonical composition:** different graph realization, region shapes, scale domains, portals, routes,
   elevations, and landmark relationships prevent layout clones.
2. **Generated identity art:** create new sprites, trim/component strips, props, decals, and culture motifs when a
   genuinely new silhouette, noun, culture, material family, or signature demands them.
3. **Parameterized material families:** deterministically vary base color/value, normal, roughness, height/AO,
   grain, joint pattern, motif density, wetness, and age inside the licensed material/culture family.
4. **Geometry-aware condition masks:** use curvature/edge, cavity/AO, world-up, height, exposure, contact, leak,
   traffic, and support data to place wear coherently on reusable meshes and generated architecture.
5. **Source-backed decals and overlays:** mount cracks, soot, moss, blood, repairs, heraldry, and other localized
   history only when their canonical source or a bounded presentation-weathering rule licenses them.
6. **Trim and component assembly:** reuse bands, corners, caps, apertures, supports, and panels while geometry owns
   silhouette and connection; deterministic selection, transforms, and placement prevent atlas reuse from becoming
   a cloned site.

For generated disposable architecture, Painter-style per-mesh hand painting is the wrong runtime dependency.
World/local projection, trim sheets, semantic masks, decals, and geometry-derived fields should carry most of the
variation. A Substance/Material-Maker/Blender-style graph belongs in an **offline foundry** that exports admitted,
versioned maps and metadata; play must not require the authoring tool or run high-resolution authoring graphs.

For pixel sprites, a normal/height map can enrich lighting, but bitmap-to-normal inference is approximate: painted
shadows may be misread as physical depth. Genesis should admit reviewed companion channels—material/region id,
height/normal, roughness, wear/condition, emissive, palette/motif masks—and reject halo, pillow, identity, silhouette,
or pixel-scale failures. Material mutation cannot silently turn one creature, outfit, culture, or damage fact into
another.

The current no-cash lane should begin with open tooling and simple deterministic image/mask operations. Material
Maker is an open-source node-based PBR material authoring tool with export support for common game engines; Blender
also provides reusable shader/geometry node graphs and baking. Adobe Substance is a strong funded workflow and a
useful architectural reference, but it is a separate paid toolchain rather than a prototype dependency.

  and [exposed parameters](https://helpx.adobe.com/ie/substance-3d-designer/using/exposing-parameters.html)
- [Adobe Substance Painter Smart Materials](https://helpx.adobe.com/si/substance-3d-painter/using/materials-smart-materials.html),
  [mesh-map baking](https://helpx.adobe.com/hu/substance-3d-painter/using/baking.html), and
  [decals](https://helpx.adobe.com/substance-3d-community-assets/desktop/the-different-asset-types-on-substance-3d-community-assets/decals.html)
- [Adobe Bitmap 2 Material outputs](https://helpx.adobe.com/substance-3d-b2m/main-features/outputs.html) and
  [Automation Toolkit rendering](https://helpx.adobe.com/substance-3d-sat/command-line-tools/sbsrender.html)

### 12.5 Revised ten-question deep-dive batch

The complete response turns the remaining material into ten connected decisions. Each question below uses **B** as
the recommendation; Adam may answer `1-10` with letters, amendments, or “recs are solid.” Prototype means the first
retained Mac proof, playable MVP means the integrated pre-alpha floor, and ideal means the promoted feature goal.

#### 1. P3.1 - what “canonical” and “render-only” mean

In a ruined dwarven hall, suppose a load-bearing basalt column blocks movement, grants cover, supports a balcony,
and has a chipped fluted surface with moss in its cavities.

- **A - every visible feature is canonical.** Each chip, flute, moss clump, bevel, and stone seam becomes stored
  world data. The DM can refer to anything visible, but persistence, saves, simulation, and authoring cost explode.
- **B - layered truth contract (recommended).** The column's id, footprint/volume, material, structural role,
  support links, condition, cover/blocking, attachment sockets, and mechanically meaningful break state are
  canonical. Tessellation, bevel smoothness, UVs, tiny chips, shader grain, and bounded moss distribution are
  render-only projections. A render-only choice may make truth legible but cannot change movement, support, or
  available actions. If a chip becomes a handhold or the moss proves a leak, it is promoted through a receipt.
- **C - renderer owns appearance and mechanics approximate it.** Cheapest initially, but the board can show a gap,
  ledge, or support the rules do not recognize and the provider-neutral DM seat cannot speak reliably.

Prototype B stores cells/elevation, boundaries, portals, blocker/cover class, one material/structure id, and one
break state while rendering primitive geometry. MVP adds semantic zones, support/attachment links, major condition
states, and truthful DM-seat action facts. Ideal adds broad material/force responses and finer promoted geometry.
Cost: low-to-medium prototype, medium MVP, high ideal; the benefit is one durable authority seam.

**Question 1:** Is B the right rule—mechanically meaningful shape/state is canonical, while micro-surface realization
is derived until explicitly promoted?

#### 2. P3.2/P3.9 - how no-clone visual breadth is produced

Two tombs built by the same drowned culture should share recognizable stonework and funerary motifs without
sharing a finished layout or identical decay.

- **A - generate bespoke finished art for every site and modification.** Maximum nominal novelty, but expensive,
  slow to admit, difficult to keep deterministic, and likely to generate contradictory materials or mechanics.
- **B - five-layer diversity stack (recommended).** Canonical composition creates unique spatial relationships;
  generated art fills real identity/silhouette/culture gaps; parameterized material graphs create controlled family
  variants; trim/component kits assemble legal structures; source-backed masks/decals carry weather and history.
  A seed plus manifest records every choice. Repetition becomes cultural vocabulary, not cloned composition.
- **C - fixed finite art kit with tint/noise only.** Cheap and stable, but culture families and repeated sites will
  become visibly samey well before the dream target.

Prototype B uses one generated material/trim family, a few admitted decals/masks, and deterministic parameters with
open/free tools. MVP grows several culture packs and history layers behind manifests. Ideal adds an automated
generated-art/material foundry, richer companion channels, and artist review/funded Substance-class tooling if it
earns its cost. Cost: medium pipeline/QA, potentially high ideal asset production; no cash dependency in prototype.

**Question 2:** Accept B as the anti-clone architecture, including the rule that a new sprite is generated for a
real identity/silhouette gap—not for every stain, chip, or color change?

#### 3. P3.3 - how “everything eventually has mechanics” scales

The same hall contains columns, loose blocks, hairline cracks, carved faces, dust, and individual moss specks.

- **A - simulate every visible element independently from the prototype.** Closest literal interpretation, but it
  makes the first room a physics research project and postpones a playable game.
- **B - universal structural citizenship, tiered mechanics (recommended).** Every meaningful structure has stable
  identity plus generic material, volume, support/attachment, condition, and force-response seams. Prototype proves
  one column/door/bridge family; MVP gives major structures generic break/burn/move/support responses; the ideal lets
  sufficient force affect every meaningful column and architectural citizen. Subordinate surface detail remains
  owned by that citizen unless promoted.
- **C - only bespoke hero objects are mechanical.** Fastest, but most visible architecture stays inert forever and
  contradicts the desired physical world.

The provider-neutral DM seat can say “the basalt column is load-bearing and already cracked” under B, offer legal
actions, and narrate a committed result. It cannot promise that an arbitrary dust mote is simulated. Cost: medium
schema/fallback work, high ideal physics/content/QA, controlled by generic responses before bespoke behaviors.

**Question 3:** Accept B's boundary between meaningful structural citizens and subordinate surface detail?

#### 4. P3.4 - provenance-first scale domains

An ogre colony occupies an old human mine whose haul tunnel opens into a larger quarried nave.

- **A - current occupants always resize the whole site.** Easy access, but erases architectural history and makes
  all dungeons large and bland.
- **B - builder provenance plus explicit adaptation (recommended).** Original builders set the baseline domains.
  Current occupants are normally compatible or have made licensed adaptations—breaches, widened portals, ramps,
  exterior quarters, servant routes. Rare incompatibility is preserved as a narrated and mechanical constraint.
- **C - place occupants freely and explain overlap in prose.** Cheap, but breaks pathing, combat, and world trust.

Prototype B supports small/medium and one large domain with one mismatch receipt. MVP adds domain transitions and
core movement profiles. Ideal supports the full scale/vehicle/guise/breach ecology. Cost: medium solver/content
work; high ideal testing, but it prevents universal oversizing.

**Question 4:** Does B fully capture your recovered sketch, including colonies as repurposed mismatch, or is there
another normal exception beyond explicit adaptation/causal explanation?

#### 5. P3.5 - graph-to-space realization after GaneshaDx

The canonical graph says: gate -> flooded nave -> reliquary; the nave also connects to a high choir reached by a
stair, and the reliquary has one secret return edge.

- **A - place hand-authored room stamps directly from graph nodes.** Useful as input vocabulary, but tends toward
  room-chain repetition and cannot by itself compose shared elevation masses or shaped negative space.
- **B - staged legality then composition (recommended).** `SpatialPlanV2` embeds legal cells/volumes and preserves
  every stable edge; `TacticalCompositionPlan` compares a bounded set of legal region/elevation/route/landmark
  arrangements; `SurfaceAssemblyPlan` builds geometry/material mounts. Ganesha-style tiles and four-yaw greyboxes
  become representation and QA tools, not generation authority.
- **C - generate a beautiful mesh first and recover gameplay space from it.** Visually tempting, but reverse
  inference can lose edges, clearance, scale, and deterministic mechanics.

Prototype B annotates one existing room without moving cells, then composes one retained room. MVP embeds a small
multi-room graph with bounded backtracking and plain-language failures. Ideal adds richer stacked surfaces,
candidate scoring, and broad site boundaries. Cost: medium-to-high engineering; Ganesha reduces conceptual risk but
does not remove the solver work.

**Question 5:** Accept B and the finding that GaneshaDx validates the boundary/QA approach but does not solve the
Genesis layout algorithm?

#### 6. P3.6 - reservations and competing claims

A chapel needs a clear entrance procession, altar interaction face, secret hatch, load-bearing buttress, staircase,
combat deployment area, and a camera-readable landmark.

- **A - one fixed global priority list.** Simple, but every exception becomes a fragile special case.
- **B - typed reservation ledger (recommended).** Hard invariants, owner-exclusive claims, compatible/shared
  claims, and soft composition preferences have explicit phase owners and conflict diagnostics. A stair footprint
  cannot be decorated away; a route may share camera readability; an altar apron may conditionally overlap combat
  space if both owners declare compatibility.
- **C - place everything and repair collisions afterward.** Quick for a demo, but repairs silently erase purpose or
  create unreachable interactables.

Prototype B has hard cells plus named exclusions. MVP adds claim types, compatibility, priority, and diagnostics.
Ideal adds richer multi-level/time-state claims and tooling. Cost: medium engineering, low art cost, major debugging
payoff.

**Question 6:** Accept B as a technical default, with Adam returning only when two desirable player experiences
truly conflict?

#### 7. P3.7 - how structural stamps may combine

A culture's buttress bay, wall aperture, corner shrine, balcony, and repair scaffold all want the same wall run.

- **A - allow arbitrary overlap, then hide seams with art.** Produces impossible support, doubled walls, blocked
  openings, and history noise.
- **B - typed sockets and legal transforms (recommended).** Stamps declare mount surfaces, sockets, footprint/
  clearance/support, fixed/ranged/bay/path deformation, repeat/mirror rules, compatible nesting, and seam ownership.
  The composer selects a bounded legal assembly; materials and decals cannot legalize bad geometry.
- **C - use only large monolithic room stamps.** Easy to validate but returns to authored-template clones and weak
  recombination.

Prototype B proves a wall bay plus opening/trim/support. MVP adds nested assemblies and history modifications. Ideal
adds broader organic/path deformation and authored tooling. Cost: medium asset metadata and validation, high ideal
library breadth.

**Question 7:** Accept B as the technical rule for reusable architecture without cloned finished rooms?

#### 8. P3.8 - when solve failure becomes fiction

The roll calls for an active giant shrine inside an intact narrow halfling burrow.

- **A - always return something by silently relaxing constraints.** Few hard failures, but lies about scale and
  history.
- **B - hard/soft/degradable contracts with licensed catastrophe (recommended).** Preserve topology, reachability,
  scale truth, required function, and safety. Backtrack, choose alternate stamps, resize soft envelopes, or split a
  domain in a recorded order. The mismatch becomes an intentional scenario only if a canonical owner licenses a
  breach, invasion, trapped idol/occupant, guise, external shrine, or similar cause; otherwise report unsatisfied.
- **C - fail immediately on the first conflict.** Honest but brittle and wastes recoverable rolls.

Prototype B uses bounded attempts and a simple trace. MVP adds ordered alternatives and player-safe degradation.
Ideal adds broad scenario-bearing catastrophe owners and smarter candidate comparison. Cost: medium solver work;
catastrophe content is high breadth and therefore promotion-gated.

**Question 8:** Accept B, and is “catastrophe must have a causal owner plus real mechanics” the right boundary
between memorable impossibility and a generator bug?

#### 9. P3.10/G3.1 - action-created spatial facts

A fighter embeds an axe in the column, ties a rope to it, and a companion later hangs from the rope while the room
is shown in SceneTray and then BattleMat.

- **A - let each renderer place a convincing version independently.** Visually easy, but the axe/rope/actor drift
  between views and the DM seat cannot know load or reach.
- **B - stable local attachments and receipts (recommended).** The axe binds to a canonical structure/surface id
  with local position/orientation, support/load, condition, source action, and persistence/expiry. Rope and actor
  reference that attachment. Only an authorized topology/structure mutation receipt can break the column or open a

### 12.7 Generated follow-up: procedural cultural visual identity

Yes: a culture pack can be a **compiled cache**, not authored canon. The recommended owner is a persisted
`CultureVisualConstitution` (conceptual name, not frozen schema) created once when a fictional culture becomes
canonical:

```text
causal culture/world facts + bounded rolls
  -> CultureVisualConstitution (stable semantic style grammar)
  -> SiteCultureImprint claims (who built/used/occupied/altered what, when)
  -> visual obligations by channel and visibility
  -> procedural geometry/material/motif resolution
  -> admitted generated or library payloads where exact art is still needed
  -> persisted ids, versions, hashes, provenance, and truthful fallback
```

The constitution should describe constraints and ranges rather than a finished atlas:

- causal inputs: environment/substrate/resources, species/scale, production and survival, institutions/doctrine,
  technology/magic, realm conditions, contacts, historical shocks, and taboos;
- signature invariants: a few recurring proportions, rhythms, shapes, construction joints, palette/value roles,
  motif operators, light treatment, or maintenance behaviors that make the culture recognizable;
- bounded variation: legal material substitutions, color ranges, density, motif transforms, clean/worn/repaired
  states, and explicit forbidden combinations;
- surface/structure grammar: portal/frame/column/roof/trim relationships, repetition cadence, joinery, tooling,
  finish, and named mount families;
- portable expression: clothing/dress cues, containers, symbols/glyphs, tools, furnishings, signage, heraldry,
  and exact generated sprites/components when needed;
- historical behavior: how this culture extends, repairs, reinforces, defaces, reuses, buries, or preserves older
  work; and
- knowledge/visibility: which impressions are plainly visible, concealed, erased, disputed, or recognizable only
  after learning.

A site's culture relationship then determines what must appear. A **builder** normally affects geometry,
construction, primary material, portal/trim rhythm, and old signature marks. A later **occupant or colony** affects
adaptations, dressing, thresholds, repairs, portable objects, and overlays without repainting the site's origin out
of existence. A **trader/influence** may justify imported goods or pigment but not local columns. A hidden or erased
culture creates evidence obligations without leaking secret identity through labels.

The compiler should emit a `SiteCultureImprint` receipt with culture id, relationship, epoch, strength, scopes,
required/allowed channels, visibility/knowledge class, source facts, and satisfaction evidence. A materially
present culture may never disappear because exact art is missing: a procedural minimum using palette/material,
geometry/rhythm, motif/trim, repair practice, or a bounded marker must satisfy the imprint before optional bespoke
art. Conversely, a culture name alone does not license coating every surface with its motif.

Material Maker fits this model unusually well. Named parameters and locked seeds can compile one constitution into
related clean, weathered, repaired, wet, soot-marked, or regionally substituted material outputs. Its command-line
export can later batch admitted `.ptex` recipes into PNG channel sets. Shape/trim/glyph grammars handle structural
identity; generative AI supplies missing exact sprites/components; neither needs to be the culture owner.

This is consistent with procedural architectural shape-grammar research: a compact context-sensitive rule set can
generate large families of coherent architectural detail while enforcing adjacency and structural relations. It
also avoids a Cartesian authored `culture x purpose x realm x history` pack library.

#### Recommended prototype-to-ideal ladder

- **Prototype:** generate and persist one constitution with material/palette, one motif grammar, one structural
  rhythm, one repair behavior, and explicit exclusions. Project it into one builder site and one later-occupied site
  using a small Material Maker graph plus procedural trim/motif fallback. Prove recognition without cloned layout.
- **Playable MVP:** several procedural cultures; mixed-origin sites; typed imprint obligations; culture-aware
  geometry/material/trim/dressing; cached admitted micro-packs; knowledge-safe projection; save/remount and
  deterministic fallback.
- **Ideal:** cultural evolution, schism, diaspora, colonization, trade diffusion, revival, and hybrid schools as
  versioned episodes; automatic foundry generation; broader sprite/dress/object/language expression; player-earned
  recognition; funded human art/cultural review where evidence justifies it.

Cost is medium for the semantic constitution/imprint system, medium-high for procedural grammar and toolchain, and
high for the ideal evolving culture ecology. The approach saves authored combinatorial cost while adding coherence,
admission, and QA obligations.

#### Executive finding

The research **confirms and sharpens Option B**. No current library found owns Genesis's whole problem: canonical

The recommended architecture remains:

```text
SpatialIntent with stable node/edge ids
  -> bounded constructive SpatialPlanV2 candidates
  -> hard legality, topology, scale, clearance, support, and reservation rejection
```

The important refinement is an explicit **solver adapter boundary**. Genesis owns the problem model, candidate
contract, deterministic seed/budget, diagnostics, and final decision. Optional graph-layout or exact-solver helpers
may propose coordinates or answer a narrow satisfiability question, but they cannot own graph edges, fiction,
degradation, tactical quality, or committed geometry.
