#patron-archetype

> **Who employs, sponsors, or sends the PC** — the archetype of the figure (or faction) behind the work. Rolled when the player takes on a recurring backer, accepts a standing arrangement, or the DM needs to know *who's pulling the threads* behind a job rather than inventing a patron whole-cloth. Each row carries four loaded columns: the **archetype**, the **kind of work** it assigns, what it **gives** (pay / access / protection / gifts), and the **strings** — the cost that comes due. The strings are the point: a patron without a hook is just a paymaster. Fork ceiling — direction-setting, never an instant catastrophe; the catch tightens over time.
>
> **DESIGN NOTE (for the orchestrator, not a DESIGN.md edit):** A *patron* is almost never a new codex entity — it's a **role an existing NPC or Faction record plays.** Recommend a `patronRole` object on the `dm` (and a sanitized echo on `fields`) of an existing `npc:` or `faction:` codex record: `{ archetype, work, offers, strings }` rolled from this table, plus typed `links[]` to bind the relationship — `serves` (PC → patron) and/or `owes` (PC → patron when the strings are a debt the PC carries; the codex auto-mirrors it as `owed-by` on the patron), `controls` (patron → a resource it grants access to), `wants` (patron → the thing the work serves). When the patron is an organization, attach it to a `faction:` record and let `member-of` / `leads` carry the org structure already in the codex vocabulary (`src/world/codex.js`). A brand-new entity is warranted only for a patron who has no other presence in the world (a faceless letter-and-purse contact) — and even then, mint a thin `npc:` record so it can recur, remember, and turn. Anti-drift win: "who sent you, and what do they want back" stops being re-improvised per job and becomes a stamped, recurring relationship the script holds.
