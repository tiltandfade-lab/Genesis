# DOCTRINE PAIR TWO — Site 6 Prison/Custody

The campaign brief asks for "the NEXT doctrine pair beyond Keeper-House/Ledger-and-Shift." The
existing packet already named eight further candidates (`PR-DOC-03` through `PR-DOC-09`), all
single cards, several `RESEARCH-NEEDED`. Rather than promote one of those in isolation, this
pass drafts a genuinely new **matched pair** — following the same "must change at least four
correlated facts" law the first pair used — built specifically from this campaign's
measured-breadth research (`MEASURED-REFERENCES.md`). The proposed pairing lets the same
comparison-receipt discipline expose whether both cards actually belong to the custody family.
The 2026-07-28 review found that they do not.

The two new ids are `PR-DOC-10` and `PR-DOC-11` (ids 03–09 remain exactly as the original packet
left them — nothing here renames or supersedes them).

## 1. Card law (restated, unchanged)

A custody doctrine is a correlated answer to the same institutional questions. It is not an
ethnic label, moral alignment, palette, prop set, or named prison mesh.

Every promoted card must change at least: one geometry or organization fact; one access or
interaction fact; one property/record or service fact; and one operating consequence, failure
mode, or counterplay target.

The following remain invariant across every card, including these two:

- one committed topology for outside and captured starts;
- a named authority claim and current operator;
- controlled thresholds and truthful force surfaces;
- exact person/property identities and causal custody;
- finite disposition or equivalent pressure;
- service provenance;
- precommitted social/legal, routine/procedural, covert/secret, and force/disruption
  possibilities; and
- `rollerLineage` / `sourceRollRefs`.

**Flagged immediately, not smoothed over:** `PR-DOC-11` below tests two of these invariants
("controlled thresholds and truthful force surfaces") against a real historical doctrine that
has *no* threshold or force surface in the ordinary sense. §4 states this stress explicitly
rather than quietly declaring compliance.

## 2. `PR-DOC-10 — Yamen Magistrate Custody`

status: **PROPOSED — ADVERSARIAL-REVIEWED CANDIDATE; NOT FOUNDER-RULED OR SELECTED FOR PROOF**
best fit: Rung B/C; degrades to Rung A as a single-official household-office hybrid
evidence spine: Neixiang County Yamen Museum (Henan, Qing-dynasty); Wikipedia "Yamen"; the
William Alexander 1805 cangue engraving; `MEASURED-REFERENCES.md` §1

**Doctrine sentence:** lawful custody is one permanent function of the seat of judicial and
administrative power itself — the jail is a wing of the same compound that holds the court, the
magistrate's residence, and the government's records, not a separate building answering to it.

| axis | correlated answer |
|---|---|
| authority | the magistrate personally, as the crown's appointed judge, administrator, and jailer in one office; authority is displayed by the compound's own gate/hall sequence rather than a separate commission document |
| organization | three named staff classes divide custody-adjacent labor by function rather than by shift: bodyguard/order-keeping staff, gate/prison/warehouse guards, and public-facing arrest/summons/tax runners; none is dedicated solely to the jail |
| classification | prisoners are held in the same compound's cell wing regardless of charge severity at Rung A/B; separate holding by case type is a Rung-C promotion, not a default |
| geometry | front gate → public courtyard → court hall (same axis the magistrate's own residence sits behind); the jail wing opens off a secondary courtyard reached past the hall, not off the street; no separate public jail-facing threshold exists — visitors to the jail pass through the same gate sequence as litigants |
| circulation | one shared front-gate sequence serves petitioners, prisoners under escort, and the magistrate's own household; the jail's "public edge" and the court's public edge are the same edge |
| access | staff-role tokens (rank, uniform, duty roster) rather than individual keys dominate; the magistrate's personal authority can override any procedural barrier at will, correctly modeling that concentrated power as both a strength (fast resolution) and a vulnerability (a single corrupt or absent magistrate degrades the entire institution, echoing `PR-DOC-01`'s keeper-crisis failure mode but from the top of the hierarchy rather than a mid-level custodian) |
| property / records | case records, court records, and prisoner property records are compiled by the same clerical staff in the same records hall — a genuinely different organizational fact from either existing card, where property/evidence records and court records are already separated staff functions |
| services | household and institutional services overlap even more than `PR-DOC-01`'s keeper-house: the same kitchen, well, and staff quarters serve the magistrate's family, the court staff, and the jail |
| routine | court sessions, tax collection, and jail routine are scheduled around the same compound's daily rhythm rather than three independent schedules |
| force | restraint is frequently **portable and worn** (the cangue) rather than only architectural — a person under cangue can be marched publicly through the settlement as a disposition-in-progress, not only held behind a cell door. This licenses "custody that leaves the building" as an ordinary, doctrine-correct event, not an escape |
| legitimate counterplay | petition the magistrate directly (the same office that holds the person can release them, with no separate release authority to appeal to); expose or exploit the low, informally-supplemented pay of runners (a sourced, structural corruption vector — bribe a runner, not just "a guard"); exploit the compound's single shared gate sequence; intercept or influence a public cangue procession |
| failure | a corrupt, absent, or deposed magistrate does not just impair the jail — it impairs the court, the tax function, and the household simultaneously, because they are one office. This is a stronger, more totalizing single-point-of-failure than `PR-DOC-01`'s keeper crisis, and should be weighted as a rarer, higher-stakes occupancy state rather than an ordinary "corrupt gaol" variant |
| promotion | Rung C first separates the jail's own public-facing intake from the court's public-facing petition desk — the compound's first true internal subdivision — before it separates property/evidence the way `PR-DOC-02` does at the same rung |
| readable signals | one continuous walled compound with a single formal gate; a court-hall silhouette visually dominant over the jail wing; runners in distinct but unofficial-looking dress; a cangued figure visible in public space away from the compound; shared household/institutional service traces (well, kitchen, granary) inside the same walls as the cells |

**Forbidden shortcut:** treating this as "`PR-DOC-01` keeper-house with a different palette and
a court hall glued on." The load-bearing difference is that the *court itself* is the custodial
authority with no separate keeper role at all — petition, trial, sentencing, and release all run
through the one office that also holds the keys, which `PR-DOC-01`'s keeper (answerable to a
court he does not sit on) does not model.

## 3. `PR-DOC-11 — Ward-and-Surety Custody`

status: **RECLASSIFIED — NOT A CUSTODY DOCTRINE; external disposition/social-liability
provider candidate; see §4**
best fit: Rung A only; does not promote to Rung B/C without ceasing to be this doctrine
evidence spine: English frankpledge/tithing (Anglo-Saxon period, regularized under Cnut);
Tokugawa Japan's gonin-gumi (formalized c. 1643–1725); corroborating (not primary) evidence from
precolonial African restitution/banishment practice per the sources reached in
`MEASURED-REFERENCES.md` §5

**Doctrine sentence:** lawful custody is a legal and social obligation distributed across an
ordinary residential group — there is no dedicated building; the group itself is collectively
liable to produce the held person, and the enforcement mechanism is collective fine and
reputational exposure rather than a wall.

| axis | correlated answer |
|---|---|
| authority | a rotating or elected group leader (tithing-man / *gonin-gumi* leader), answerable to a higher civil authority (sheriff's "view of frankpledge"; village *nanushi*/*shōya*) who periodically audits the group rather than operating the custody day to day |
| organization | the group — nominally ten households (frankpledge) or five (gonin-gumi), both sourced as varying in practice — is the custodial unit; there is no dedicated staff, no keeper, no guard roster |
| classification | none by cell or block — the held person remains embedded in their own or a group member's household; "classification" is instead which group a person belongs to, fixed well before any offense occurs |
| geometry | **there is no dedicated custodial geometry.** The held person occupies ordinary domestic space belonging to themselves or a group member. The only architecture this doctrine contributes to Site 6 is negative: it proves a legal fixture can exist with zero purpose-built footprint |
| circulation | ordinary village/neighborhood circulation; no separate public, staff, or prisoner routes exist because there is no separate custodial space to route between |
| access | anyone in the group has "access" in the ordinary sense of living alongside the held person; the meaningful access control is legal, not spatial — who may demand the person's production, and to whom the group must answer |
| property / records | property is not confiscated or centrally stored under this doctrine; a person's goods remain with their household. The only "record" this doctrine generates is the group's own roster and its periodic audit at the "view of frankpledge" or equivalent |
| services | none are provided by a custodial authority — the household's or group's own ordinary food, shelter, and water suffice, because custody is not physical confinement |
| routine | the group's routine *is* the held person's routine; there is no separate meal/yard/count schedule. The only routine event unique to this doctrine is the periodic external audit (the sheriff's biannual "view of frankpledge" is a sourced, dateable event type) |
| force | **the doctrine has no physical force surface.** Its enforcement is collective liability: if the group cannot produce the accused, the whole group is fined; reputational and social pressure ("fear of losing face," per the sourced gonin-gumi study) is described in the evidence itself as the *primary* mechanism, more effective than the threat of official punishment |
| legitimate counterplay | persuade, bribe, or intimidate individual group members rather than a single keeper or guard; exploit the gap between the group's *legal* liability and its *practical* ability to physically stop a determined escape (the doctrine's evidence explicitly states there was no police force to prevent flight — only consequence after the fact); expose group corruption or internal conflict; time an escape around the audit cycle |
| failure | the group's collective fine is the sourced failure consequence for the group; for the individual, the sourced consequence of flight is outlawry / continued liability rather than an immediate physical recapture — a genuinely different failure shape from every other card, where failure is measured in alert states and breached barriers |
| degradation | this doctrine does not degrade to a smaller Rung — it is already the smallest possible custody expression by construction. It also does not promote to Rung B/C: the moment a dedicated custodial building, staff, or cell exists, the situation has left this doctrine and become `PR-DOC-01`, `PR-DOC-02`, or another card |
| readable signals | no purpose-built structure to point a camera at; the tell is *social* — a person visibly going about ordinary household/village life while a specific neighbor or group is visibly, publicly answerable for them (a period-appropriate marker: a shared meal, a joint appearance before a visiting official, or an audit-day gathering) |

**Forbidden shortcut:** treating this as "`PR-DOC-01` keeper-house but the keeper is the whole
village" — the keeper-house doctrine still has a building, a key, and a wall. This doctrine has
none of those, by design, and that absence is the entire point being tested.

## 4. The invariant stress, stated plainly for Codex

Site 6's working spec lists as `REQUIRED`: *"custody boundary. At least one controlled
threshold separates a held population from free exit, with a named operator or active
replacement system"* and *"force has truth. Bars, doors, anchors, walls, chains, wards, and
restraints have material, support, lock, durability, noise, time, and consequence facts."*

`PR-DOC-11` satisfies the **named operator** half of the first invariant (the tithing-man /
group leader is a real, sourced, named role) but does **not** satisfy "controlled threshold" in
any spatial sense, and its "force" is legal/social liability with no material, lock, or
durability facts at all — collective fine and reputational exposure are real, sourced,
consequential mechanisms, but they are not the kind of fact the second invariant's list
(material/support/lock/durability/noise) anticipates.

Two honest ways were originally named. The 2026-07-28 adversarial review selects the narrow
reading for the working spec:

1. **Narrow reading:** `PR-DOC-11` is out of Site 6's scope as written — it is a *disposition
   alternative* (like exile or ransom) rather than a custody doctrine, because Site 6's own
   accepted definition requires "a controlled threshold" the doctrine cannot supply. Under this
   reading, the card should be filed as an occupancy/disposition hook (comparable to how
   `PR-DOC-07` Hostage/Surety is currently kept as an occupancy overlay rather than a promoted
   doctrine), not a sibling to `PR-DOC-01`/`PR-DOC-02`.
2. **Broad reading:** Site 6's REQUIRED invariants should be read (or explicitly amended) to
   admit a **social/legal threshold and a legal/reputational force surface** as valid
   alternate realizations of "controlled threshold" and "force has truth," the same way the
   working spec already admits wards and impossible materials as valid alternate realizations
   of ordinary walls under the `LICENSED` strange-containment class. Under this reading,
   `PR-DOC-11` is a legitimate doctrine and the invariant wording needs one clarifying sentence,
   not a new exception.

**Disposition:** retain the broad reading above as rejected design history. File
`PR-DOC-11` as an external disposition/social-liability provider; it may compose with a
custody host but cannot satisfy or replace its lifecycle.

## 5. Comparison receipt

The same discipline the first pair used (`culture-doctrine-cards.md` §3), applied to this pair.
Because `PR-DOC-11` has no dedicated geometry, several rows read as "none" rather than a
building-shaped answer — which is itself the comparison's point, not a gap in the table.

| proof dimension | `PR-DOC-10` Yamen Magistrate | `PR-DOC-11` Ward-and-Surety |
|---|---|---|
| primary authority target | the magistrate's single fused office | the group's collective liability and its external auditor |
| organizational bottleneck | one person holding judicial + custodial + administrative power | the group as a whole; no single bottleneck person exists |
| public-to-secure form | one shared gate sequence serving court and jail alike | none — no public/secure distinction exists |
| property form | records compiled by the same clerical staff as court records | none — property never leaves the household |
| staff movement | three named runner classes, informally paid, functionally overlapping | none — no staff exists |
| social/legal plan | petition the magistrate directly; exploit runner corruption | persuade, bribe, or intimidate individual group members; exploit audit timing |
| routine plan | court/tax/jail schedule sharing one compound rhythm | the held person's own ordinary household routine; the periodic external audit |
| covert plan | the shared gate sequence; runner corruption; cangue-in-transit interception | escape between audits; exploit the gap between legal liability and physical prevention |
| force plan | defeat or evade the single fused office's authority (concentrated, therefore both fragile and dangerous) | there is no force surface to defeat — the "force plan" against this doctrine is simply leaving, which the doctrine cannot physically stop, only legally punish afterward |
| signature failure | a corrupt/absent/deposed magistrate collapses court, tax, and custody together | the group's collective fine and reputational loss when it cannot produce the accused |

Palette, heraldry, and material remain supporting signals for `PR-DOC-10`. For `PR-DOC-11`,
even palette and material are inapplicable in the ordinary sense — its "supporting signal" is
social visibility, not decoration, which is itself worth flagging as a genuinely new kind of
doctrine-card row this comparison format was not originally built to hold.

## 6. What this closes, and what it deliberately does not

**Closes:** the campaign brief's request to test a next doctrine pair with real,
cross-cultural, dated, sourced evidence. The comparison earns one retained custody candidate
and one boundary rejection; it does not earn a second promoted pair.

**Does not close:** founder ruling; selection of `PR-DOC-10` for proof; a clay proof; or the
residual `RESEARCH-NEEDED` status of `PR-DOC-07`/`PR-DOC-08`. The invariant question is
resolved narrowly in §4: a social-liability provider may compose with custody but cannot
replace its controlled threshold and force truth.
